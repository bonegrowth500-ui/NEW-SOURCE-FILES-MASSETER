# 10 · Lifetime Value
*Part III — Start, Extend, Run, Grow*

**The shift:** from *"The sale is the finish line"* to *"The first program is the start of a measured relationship, and the back end is designed for the months when outcomes appear."*

It's graduation day, and Dan *(composite, Struggler)* has his week-12 re-assessment, the end-of-program verdict, open beside his baseline. He logged [60] of [84] days, just over his adherence threshold. [His posture-habit marker], one of the pre-agreed measures his verdict runs on, crossed its threshold in week [9]. [His body-composition measure] improved at each of its last two readings. Nothing is automatic yet: on most of the days he didn't log, [his weekly habit block] didn't happen either. It's real progress on a record that could still go either way.

Run his next three months two ways. In the first, the Program, your 12-week flagship, simply ends. Nobody reads his logs, and the skipped days double. By his next capture date he's guessing again, with a better story about why. In the second, his record keeps a reviewer and a calendar: lighter review while his markers still move, re-captures on set dates, and a written answer to the question every graduate carries out the door. Did it hold?

The second version is where the business compounds: re-captures past week 12 exist only if someone schedules them, and they're where your proof and your referral asks come from. This module designs that back end, counts what it's worth per client, and prices each seat in care minutes, the review, call, and milestone time it takes from your week.

## 1. The LTV Stack

What a client is worth to you depends on what happens after week 12, because his habits settle and measured change shows in the months after the Program ends.

### The Program ends before his habits settle

Habits take a median of about two months to become automatic. The spread runs from days to most of a year. The changes an adult can measure, in body composition and posture habits, mostly take months to show in matched captures. So a 12-week Program graduates many clients mid-build, like Dan: markers moving, habits not yet running on their own.

Operators misread that gap in two opposite ways. One counts value at the sale, so graduation is the last touch: clients drift, and the re-captures that would have become proof never happen. The other renews everyone. This category sold its buyers endless tips and one more device, so a renewal offered to every graduate reads as the same pattern. A graduate with no trend to protect is paying for hope.

### Lifetime value is a multiplier that costs minutes

Lifetime value is one term of the Demand Equation: eligible adults, those who could pay from their own income or savings without new credit, times conversion, lifetime value, and margin, capped by care minutes (Module 1). At lifetime values of $1.7–3.0k, an eligible lead is worth about $25–210, and the most you'd pay for one is about a third of that, so a loosely counted lifetime value makes you overpay for reach.

Every back-end seat also spends care minutes. A Program seat costs about 18–20 care minutes a client-week in the Growing stage, about $8–30k a month, and about 13–15 at Scaling, past about $30k. At capacity those minutes are the cap. A back end that earns less per care hour than the Program can then grow revenue while shrinking the number of Program seats your week allows.

### Count four layers, with the minutes beside each

So count what a client is worth in four layers, with the care time each costs beside it. The Program counts at its price. Round Two, a maintenance block for graduates with measured momentum, and the Hold, a measurement subscription for graduates, count per graduate, so multiply each by the share of clients who graduate. The Hold counts at the months members actually keep it. Premium counts only where it's real: Private seats, your premium seat with fixed deliverables, sold while minutes are spare or at parity, earning at least what a Program seat earns per care hour. That count is the **LTV Stack**:

**LTV = Program + Round Two per graduate + Hold months + premium, with care minutes counted against each.**

| Layer | How it's counted | Opening prices | Proof prices | Care time against it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Program | Price per client | $1.5–2.2k | $2.4–3.2k | ~18–20 min per client-week while Growing; ~13–15 at Scaling |
| Round Two | ~$0.8–1.2k × ~10–26% of graduates × the share who graduate | Same | Same | A line of its own: ~0.35–0.4 h a week at the target steady states |
| Hold months | $39–79 a month × months kept × a 20–40% take, per graduate | Same | Same | ~0.25–0.6 h a week across all members, rising by stage |
| Premium | Private seats while minutes are spare, or at parity | Founding seats ~$4–6k | Private only at parity | A line of its own |
| Per client, before referrals | | ~$1.7–3.0k | ~$2.6–3.8k | |

Most of the spread is price: at planning takes, the back end adds a few hundred dollars a client. That's why price is the first profit lever once seats bind and lifetime value the second. What the back end adds that price can't is the record past week 12. It's also the lever that serves his result, as long as each seat costs minutes in proportion to what it earns.

### Rebuild the stack every quarter

Rebuild the stack at your quarterly review, per start, from your own records. Use the planning ranges until about 30 graduates, then your own take and churn, since a ratio built on fewer events is mostly noise. Keep Round Two per graduate: counted per offer, the same take looks one and a half to two times as big. Leave referrals and training partners out, because each is a new client with a stack of his own.

When a layer sits below its range, fix its design or its moment, never its qualification. Watch the other edge too. If Round Two's take runs above the top of its range, or the share told "you don't need Round Two" falls, for two 30-event windows, audit your momentum reads before you count the money. A stack inflated by loose reads points you at the wrong layer, and an honest one names the layer worth fixing.

## 2. Round Two, the Maintenance Format

Round Two protects a trend his record already shows. So it's sold on measured momentum alone, it's lighter than the Program because protecting a trend takes less review than starting one, and a graduate buys it once.

### Measured momentum is a reading test

Measured momentum is read from his marker sheet at the week-12 re-assessment, and it has two parts. His adherence is at or above his threshold, and at least one marker is still improving across its last two readings. The test is the same for every client and printed on every sheet from baseline day, so no offer depends on how you feel about his week 12. His record must also have moved: a graduate paid under the non-response clause, the partial refund when no marker reached its threshold despite his effort, hears no offer.

Each part has a job. Adherence comes first because a maintenance block needs habits to maintain, and a reading can rise for reasons that have nothing to do with the work. Two readings come second because one good week is noise, while two in a row is a trend more review can protect. So his enthusiasm doesn't qualify him, and neither does a capture that looks better to him or his ability to pay.

Dan qualifies: his [60] of [84] days clear his threshold, [his posture-habit marker] sits at threshold, and [his body-composition measure] improved at both of its last readings. Plan on about 50–65% of graduates showing measured momentum and 20–40% of those buying, about 10–26% of all graduates. The take moves with adherence across the start and with a final fortnight designed as the Program's peak.

A graduate takes Round Two once. A second block follows only if Round Two's own week-12 read shows measured momentum and he asks for it; otherwise his next step is the Hold or a clean finish. A block that renews itself on a schedule is the endless-tips pattern with a review attached.

### The format is lighter on purpose

The format is the Program's rhythm at maintenance strength. He gets a written review of his check-in every second week, and a seat on the weekly group call if he wants one. For a graduate of Program Async, the same Program without the live call, Round Two stays async at the same price. He re-captures to your Capture Standard, the matched conditions every capture follows, at Round Two's own weeks 6 and 12. The review stays proactive, because in behavior-change research, contact the coach initiates goes with better results, and support that waits to be asked adds little.

Price it at about $0.8–1.2k, well under the Program, because it carries half the review cadence and two capture reads. He pays in full or in up to three of your own installments inside its 12 weeks, at cost.

### Full intensity is a separate path

Full intensity has its own condition. A graduate's week-6 read, the written mid-program read, documented a misdirection and changed the lever. By week 12 the corrected lever shows early movement: its marker improved at both readings since the change, on a record with a marker at threshold. That's the misdirected verdict, one of the three honest reasons a stall happens, corrected. After about six weeks on the right lever, maintenance would under-serve him, so he gets weekly review, the call, and both written reads. Who pays depends on where the misdirection came from, which the week-6 read records before any offer exists:

| The week-6 read recorded | What that means | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| A misdirection from outside your plan | Nothing in his intake or his logs showed it: [a routine he never disclosed or logged] | Full-intensity Round Two at week 12, priced as a Program |
| A miss that was yours | A signal in his intake or logs you didn't act on; a plan step you set wrong | Corrective weeks at no charge: the weeks the error cost, up to 6. Maintenance Round Two afterward only on measured momentum |

When you can't tell which it was, count it as yours. The service guarantee, your promise on turnaround, reads, and deliverables, has to be collectable by a client who did the work, and a doubt settled in your favor isn't. Every corrective week goes into the claim rate your Dated Record, the dated public log of your process, publishes. The Path and Timeline Card, the one page he reads before paying, prices both versions of Round Two (Module 6), and your guarantee terms already say a miss of yours is corrected free.

### It's offered at a measured peak, after every check

Round Two is offered at a measured peak between the week-10 review and the week-12 re-assessment: a measurement moment with his adherence and at least one marker at threshold, so no refund decision is open. How the offer is argued, from his record against the timeline he accepted before paying, is the Renewal Case (Module 22). Nothing is offered at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit.

Before any payment, the checkout runs what every paid step runs. First comes the affordability question, word for word: "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?" Then the Fit Check's second tier asks the plain questions that pause a sale when a signal shows. A no ends money talk and sets the pause route, a content-free tag that holds every marketing send and blocks checkout for 60–90 days, then asks permission once. He keeps the free next step graduation already gave him: his record and a quarterly date of his own. From 30 graduates, publish the share told "you don't need Round Two" beside the share offered it, because that line is what makes the offer credible.

**When the signals disagree.** Say Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, asks for Round Two before you've offered anything. He can pay without a second thought, his week-12 captures look sharper to him, and he wants the precision of more reviewed weeks. His adherence clears his threshold and [his posture-habit marker] sits at threshold, but no marker improved across its last two readings: moved and holding, with no measured momentum. Three signals say yes, and the one written before money was on the table says no, so the sheet decides. Captures are observations under conditions, and ability to pay is a condition of every paid step, never a qualification for this one.

Tell him plainly that he doesn't need Round Two, and why: there's no trend for more review to protect. What he's asking for is proof that it lasts, the Hold's job, delivered as a quarterly written review and offered after the same two checks. If he pushes ("I'll pay for the weeks anyway"), the answer holds, because a client who later saw he'd been sold maintenance with nothing to maintain wouldn't say it served him. Round Two held to its test sells on the record, and every man it's offered to can see why.

## 3. The Hold

The Hold sells one answer, whether what moved has held. It earns its monthly price only while it keeps delivering that answer on a calendar.

### A measurement subscription comes first

The question every graduate carries out the door, did it hold?, can't be answered by a mirror. Change here is slow and confounded. Weight swings, a haircut, a tan, and for men in their early 20s the last of their growth all redraw a face in a photo. Matched captures on a calendar are what separate what held from what the light did. The Hold gives him that calendar in place of the daily mirror check.

Until about 30 alumni are in it, the Hold is a measurement subscription with two paid deliverables. One is a re-capture to your Capture Standard every quarter, on a date set at graduation. The other is one written review a quarter, inside a stated turnaround: what held, what slid, and the one adjustment worth making. He sends [one week of logs a month] for that review to read, because behavior is measured often and appearance rarely. Members also stay on the Canon Lane, the free email lane that repeats your core claims to every subscriber outside a pause (Module 26), listed on the card as free.

Once about 30 alumni are in, the alumni room opens, the Hold's paid room with a prompt each week and fixed rules. Existing members get it at their current rate, and joining it is their choice. Before that point, a room of [five] members reads as a failed product, and its moderation would come out of review minutes.

For you, the Hold is where months 4–12 get re-measured. With his separate consent those re-captures become the long-timeline, observed ranges you publish, shared back with members in aggregate.

### The Hold is a slow line, priced from its minutes

Price the Hold inside $39–79 a month from what its minutes cost at your Program's rate, never from how light it feels. At the steady state that earns $25k a month, that's about $59 for around 20 members with no room, and that price holds only with a templated review. At the $50k steady state it's about $69 for around 45 members with the room open, and rising. Plan on 20–40% of graduates taking it at the week-12 review. The take rises when his record has moved and the card is plain, and the line fills slowly:

| Months after your first graduation | 6 | 12 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold members, at ~8 graduates a month, a ~30% take, and ~6% monthly churn | ~12 | ~21 | ~31 |

Membership churn typically runs 4–8% a month, faster in open, cheap groups and slower in curated ones. Some churn is success. A member whose re-captures have shown it holding for [two or three] quarters, and who leaves, got what he came for.

### Opt-in at the review is the default

People tend to overestimate how often they'll use a membership they pay for. So a subscription can earn for months from a man who stopped using it. To a buyer who has been sold to before, a forgotten charge reads as the grift he came here to escape. Defaults are also a robust effect, moderate to large in pooled studies and smaller for consequential choices, which is why a hidden one is out. Reid *(composite operator)* met both at once. He added a pre-ticked Hold month to every Program checkout, and sign-ups looked strong. In month [5], two members who didn't recognize the charge disputed it, a count a processor notices at solo volume.

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Opt-in at the week-12 review | From your first graduation | A lower take, since each member chooses with his record in hand |
| A disclosed Hold phase agreed at purchase | A later test, once about 30 graduates have given you opt-in take and churn | A higher take bought with a default's pull, so it runs only under strict conditions |

**Default: opt-in at the review.** A man who chooses the Hold with his week-12 record open is buying measurement he wants, and he'll still say so when he reads his statement in month five. Test the disclosed phase no earlier than about 30 graduates, and under four conditions. It sits on the Path and Timeline Card and at checkout as a separate yes. It starts after a moved verdict and no other. It bills first after a reminder with a one-click exit. And the test ends if disputes or complaints rise above the opt-in version's.

### Terms make it safe to buy

The Hold's deliverables card is one page stating what a member gets, when, and from whom, with its billing terms: a reminder before each charge and a one-click exit. The service guarantee covers every Hold deliverable, and when a week runs long, new members wait rather than a review slipping.

The Hold is offered at graduation to a man whose record moved and at Round Two's week-12 re-capture, never after the clause, a flat read, or an exit. A member who hasn't had Round Two can hear about it at a quarterly re-capture that passes the momentum test. The same affordability question and Fit Check run before the first charge. A checking or fixation signal means no Hold, because its core is a capture, and that man gets a referral and reading-only content instead. A subscription a skeptic can leave in one click asks him to trust nothing he can't undo, and that's what lets you sell it plainly.

## 4. Community Options

A paid room earns its place only when its members arrive with a record and its minutes are budgeted before it opens. That's why the default is the alumni room, and why a free community never opens, at any stage.

### Rooms go wrong fast in this niche

Every pressure your door screens out comes back in an unscreened room. In self-selected samples, most of the category's hardcore audience is under 18. Rating culture arrives with the first "rate me" post, and the Struggler's comparison-photo habit turns a room into a place to measure faces. Distress shows in public, where one man's "everything is ruined" reaches the whole room before it reaches you. Device promotion finds any open room this category has.

The moderation that answers those pressures has to be yours. Room posts are client content, and routing help, the task-billed helper who moderates your public comments, never sees client content (Module 11). Moderation also comes out of the same care hours as review. So every room gets a line in minutes before it opens, and a job that line pays for. A free community fails both tests: it spends care hours on unpaid attention and pulls in the underage viewers and rating talk your door exists to keep out.

### Each option gets a job and a minute budget

Community Options, the paid-room choices for alumni and members, come down to three:

| Option, and its job | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| The alumni room: keeps measuring alive between quarterly re-captures, among men with records | About 30 alumni are in the Hold | ~0.2 h a week at the $50k steady state: one templated prompt and two fixed moderation passes, with safety replies inside them; paid for inside the Hold |
| An in-cohort peer space: contact between calls for one start | A start asks for it and its reviews are running inside their turnaround | [Two] fixed passes a week, [~10] min in all, from that start's care minutes; it closes at graduation |
| An open paid membership: a room for verified adults keeping their own record with your tools | Rarely: from Growing, and only if its moderation line passes parity | $29–59 a month at the 8–15% monthly churn open groups run, the heaviest moderation, and steady pressure from underage viewers |

**Default: the alumni room.** Every member arrives with a record, the Fit Check behind him, and 12 weeks of the norms, so standing rests on process and two passes a week keep it safe. Until about 30 alumni are in the Hold, the default is no room at all, since the review is the product.

The open membership's buyer is a verified adult who keeps his own record with the Self-Serve System and wants a room of others doing the same. It reviews nobody's record, its checkout runs the age attestation, the affordability question, and the Fit Check, and no content routes a first-time viewer to it. Open it only once its moderation line earns what a Program hour earns, a point that at its churn and price often never comes.

### Room rules have consequences, and distress comes first

The room's members are adults who measure instead of guess, and the rules keep standing tied to that practice. Handles are the default, and cameras are optional. No face is posted, compared, or rated, his own included, and nobody gives medical, dental, or structural advice. Alumni status is a dated fact of graduation, and any board follows Quiet Commitments, where commitments go to the reviewer and sharing stays opt-in inside a closed group (Module 17). A board ranks only timestamps, logs sent and re-captures kept on their dates, never self-reported days, posting volume, or looks. It opens to a member only after his fit window, freezes while his exit window is open, and never mentions who left.

Distress comes before every other rule. A post that reads as distress is hidden from the room at once, and the member is pointed to [the help line] straight away for anything that can't wait. You reply to him personally the same day, and a referral trigger, such as jaw pain, bite concerns, snoring, or fixation, becomes a referral conversation.

Other breaks follow a fixed ladder, because members of a paid room are owed predictable rules. The first break gets the post removed and a private note. A second within [three months] means [two weeks] of read-only. A third, or any post of another person's face, a rating, or a pitch, ends the membership, with the unused part of the month refunded. Say a member posts a side-by-side of his jaw and asks whether it's "better". You take it down, tell him privately that captures go to his reviewer, and put his question into his next written review. Nobody is corrected in public, because the Dignity Check asks whether he leaves more capable or more defective.

## 5. The Training-Partner Seat and Coming Back

A training partner and a returning graduate both arrive knowing the work, and each still buys on his own record, through his own door.

### A pair costs two seats and saves none

The Training-Partner Seat is pair enrollment: two adults in one start, each through his own door. It also arrives at graduation, when a graduate introduces the man he trains with. It fits this niche because many men here keep the pursuit private, and a training partner is often the one person who already knows. He shares the gym, the schedule, or the flat, so he sees a skipped week days before it reaches a log. Referred clients also tend to stay longer and be worth more, though you plan acquisition without them.

The minutes decide the rest. Each partner gets his own baseline, markers, reviews, and reads, and only the group call is shared. So a pair costs exactly two Program seats: at 18–20 care minutes a client-week in Growing, about 3.6–4 care hours each over 12 weeks, or 7–8 for the pair. That's why there's no pair discount: the pair saves you no minutes, and a second seat priced below what recent buyers paid breaks the rule that nobody pays less for the same seat.

Both seats come from the start's real cap. With one left, the pair takes the next start together, or the partner who's ready enrolls alone. Open the seat only once starts fill and the group call has split by stage, past about 12–15 concurrent clients, because a pair in a founding group of four is half the room.

Each partner answers the age attestation, the affordability question, and the Fit Check himself, in private, and neither learns the other's answers, verdict, exit, or refund unless he shares it. Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)* asks to put his gym partner's seat on his own card, and the answer is the partner's own checkout, since money that isn't his routes him to the Starter Path, the free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now. When a client's partner turns out to be Jordan *(composite, minor)*, 16, there's no seat; he gets the education lane, public content and an orthodontist referral, with nothing collected. Offering the seat is a private ask at a measured peak, like every back-end ask.

### Coming back starts with his record

A graduate who returns has the skills, so the question is what held, and his record answers it before any offer. Nothing pulls him back, since this business sends no win-back sequence, and a way back he triggers himself respects the "done" he said. He books a fit conversation like any buyer, sets a fresh capture beside his week-12 set on his own device, and brings his logs and marker readings since:

| His record shows | The read | One recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Markers near their week-12 readings, one habit slid, dates kept | The lever held; one habit needs restarting | [That habit block] restarted on his own, with his quarterly date |
| Markers near their week-12 readings, dates missed | The lever held; the calendar didn't | The Hold, after the two checks |
| The marker that moved has slid back with the habits | The lever needs the work again | The Program at the next start and the published price |
| He left through the non-response clause | The lever didn't move for him | Never the same plan; a new goal starts at the door |

**Default:** the smallest step his record supports, because he already has the skills and should pay only for review his record still needs. Step up to the Program only when the marker that moved has slid back. Round Two stays off the table, since measured momentum needs adherence he hasn't logged. The Self-Serve System, the paid kit for keeping your own record without review, is never pitched as his route back (Module 4).

Sam *(composite, Ambivalent)* declined the Hold and went quiet through his busy season. [Seven] months later he replies to a Canon Lane email: "Season's over. Can I come back?" His readings put [his posture-habit marker] still at threshold, [his weekly habit block] stopped in month [3], and he kept one of [two] quarterly dates. The lever held and the calendar didn't, so the recommendation is the Hold. The affordability question and the Fit Check run before the first charge, and he joins. The fresh 12 weeks he half expected stays unsold, because his record doesn't call for it.

## 6. What the Back End Costs in Minutes

At capacity a back-end seat keeps its place only by earning what a Program seat earns per care hour, and the honest arithmetic says Round Two and the Hold get there by design, not by default.

### Parity turns into a minute budget

Round Two costs a fraction of a Program seat's minutes: a review every second week, two capture reads, and a call seat that was running anyway. The Hold costs one written review a quarter per member, plus the room's line once it opens. Each gets its own line in Seat Math, the monthly seat caps drawn from measured care minutes, and when a line is full the offer waits (Module 6).

What those lines may cost comes from the Parity Rule: a back-end seat has to earn at least what a Program seat earns per care hour, or it's sold only while minutes are spare (Module 13). Measure against what your Program earns once minutes bind. At Scaling that's about $1,250–1,400 per care hour with the Program at the 25–34 ceiling, about $3.5–3.9k, while a $3.1k Program earns about $900–1,190. Turned around, the rule is a budget:

**Minutes a back-end seat may cost = what it earns ÷ your Program's revenue per care hour.**

### The arithmetic says both need design

Per care hour here is a month's revenue divided by the weekly line times about 4.33. Every figure comes from the steady states' own lines:

| Seat, steady state | Brings in a month | Care line | Per care hour | Against ~$1,250–1,400 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Round Two, $25k | ~$1.5k | ~0.35 h a week (~1.5 h a month) | ~$990 | Below; about level with a $3.1k Program |
| Round Two, $50k | ~$2.8k | ~0.4 h a week (~1.7 h a month) | ~$1,600 | Above |
| The Hold, $25k, no room | ~$1.2k (~20 × $59) | ~0.4 h a week (~1.7 h a month) | ~$680 | Well below |
| The Hold, $50k, room open | ~$3.1k (~45 × $69) | ~0.6 h a week: ~0.4 reviews, ~0.2 room | ~$1,190 | Just below |

Say it plainly: at the $25k steady state Round Two sits below the Scaling benchmark, so it sells only while minutes are spare, and Seat Math caps its seats. Two levers close most of the gap. A templated biweekly review shrinks its line. A price nearer the top of $0.8–1.2k raises what the line earns: at about $1.2k, the same line brings about $1.8k a month, about $1,200 a care hour.

The Hold is further off. At $25k its line works out to roughly 15 minutes a review, and at $59 the budget allows about 8: $177 a quarter against $1,250–1,400 an hour. Template the review to fit before selling another seat. At $50k the line must carry the room too. About 45 members come to roughly 3.5 reviews a week. At about 7 minutes each that's about 0.4 hours, which leaves about 0.2 for the room's prompt, its two passes, and the safety replies inside them. At $69 that still lands just under parity, which sits nearer $72–80 a month, so new members' price steps toward the top of the range while alumni keep theirs.

Reid shows what skipping the budget costs. He writes each quarterly review from scratch, in about [25] minutes, at [$39] a month. A member's [$117] a quarter buys about [10] minutes at his Program's [$700] per care hour, so every review he writes takes time from a Program seat. The review compares matched captures and three monthly log weeks, the part of this business only you can do. So template the writing, and never the reading.

The card states your time per member, and a member's questions about his record go into his next quarterly review, since an unpriced minute in the room comes out of a Program review. Budgeted in minutes, the back end grows with your alumni while your week stays the size you designed.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Your first graduates are few, so the constraint is definition rather than volume: the momentum test, both cards, and the corrective-week rule must exist before the first week 12. The default move is printing the test on every founding sheet and pricing the back end on the Path and Timeline Card before anyone pays. The trap is gratitude, offering Round Two to every founding client because he took a chance on you.

**Growing.** Round Two's minutes still sit inside your review line, so the constraint is visibility: you can't yet see what a back-end seat costs. The default move is timing back-end reviews apart from Program reviews for one quarter. The trap is reading a take or a churn rate off [eight] graduates, when anything short of about 30 is mostly noise.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind, and each Price Step, your scheduled price rise, lifts what a Program hour earns, so a back-end price that passed parity last year can fail it now. The default move is rechecking both back-end prices whenever the Program steps, with alumni keeping their rates. The trap is the room turning into a second Program, with members asking there for review their card doesn't include.

## Standard Check

- **Round Two on a test, with the honest no.** The card prints the momentum test, the price, and the full-intensity and corrective-week terms, and a graduate who fails the test hears that he doesn't need it (*Present the price*). Nothing is offered at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit (the line on vulnerability).
- **No hidden default in the Hold.** Two itemized deliverables, the free lane shown as free, opt-in at the review, a reminder before every charge, and a one-click exit (*Present the price*) mean a member reading his statement in month five still says it served him (the Informed-Client ceiling).
- **Standing from the record, safety first.** Alumni status is a dated fact of graduation, boards rank timestamps and freeze while an exit window is open, no face is posted, and a distress post is hidden while help comes first (*Build identity on evidence*; the line on shame).
- **One door per partner.** Each partner answers every check himself, and no minor is ever seated (the line on vulnerability).
