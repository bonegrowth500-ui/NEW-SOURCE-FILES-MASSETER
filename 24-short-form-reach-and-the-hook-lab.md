# 24 · Short-Form: Reach and the Hook Lab
*Part VI — The Ecosystem*

**The shift:** from *"Short-form is for going viral"* to *"Short-form is the early reach engine and a noisy lab, judged by eligible-adult yield and confirmed in long-form."*

Two of your shorts go up on the same Tuesday. The first answers whether mewing works, and by Friday it has [~400k] engaged views, the ones that watched past the first seconds. Follows jump by [~2,000], and the comments fill with rating requests, several from boys who say they're 15. [Three] adults walk through your door, the owned path that starts with your self-assessment. The second opens on a 27-year-old who has spent a year on [his routine] and can't tell if any of it works. It gets [~9k] engaged views, sends [seven] adults through your door, and [one] books a fit conversation, the free first call that ends in one recommendation.

Your dashboard calls the first one the win. Your calendar says it was the second. The first found the category's youngest viewers, who engage fastest and can't buy, and the second found a man in the middle of his stall. Neither result says much about next Tuesday, because one week can't separate a framing that works from one that got lucky.

This module runs short-form as two things at once. In the early months it's your main reach engine, judged by the eligible adults it sends you, the legal adults who could pay without new credit. All along it's a lab for framings. Nothing there counts until it wins twice and then wins clearly in long-form.

## 1. The Early Reach Engine and the Default Platform Set

In your first four months, short-form is the main way strangers find you. Its feeds put each piece in front of people who have never heard of you, and its worth is counted in eligible adults at your door.

### The man you want doesn't search for you

Your buyer hides the pursuit. He rarely comments or tells a friend, and when he searches, he types the category's words, which the youngest viewers type too and a new channel can't rank for yet. So long-form, which compounds through search and suggested feeds, reaches him slowly in your first months. Short-form doesn't wait for his search. Each piece goes first to a small group of strangers and widens when they stay, so a short about a 27-year-old's stall can find the man living it in week one.

Reach binds first, and month 3 is decided by conversations and the routes that produce them. So short-form is one of the labeled sources in the Founding Sprint, your first conversations without an audience, each booking tagged with its route (Module 9). The early design week gives it about 3.5 hours for 4–7 native pieces a week, its largest content line.

### The door is the scoreboard

A short view is worth far less than a long-form view. Plan on roughly 0.1–1 raw lead per 1,000 short-form views, on each platform's own count, against 2–5 per 1,000 engaged long-form views early on. Only 30–70% of raw leads are eligible adults, and packaging moves that share more than the choice of platform does. In the samples that exist, most of this category's hardcore audience is under 18, and rating and comparison content pulls them in fastest. So views and follows, read alone, are the numbers most likely to mislead you. The one that doesn't is eligible-adult yield: eligible leads per unit of reach, by source.

Two habits raise it. The first is adult-situation framing, the short-form end of the Age-Up Dial, the packaging choices that lower the minors' share (Module 23). A short opens on the 27-year-old who has guessed for a year, or the man whose headshot is about to go on the company site. It never opens on an idealized jaw, a rating, or two faces set side by side.

The second habit is routing. Every profile's link goes to your self-assessment, whose age fork sends anyone under 18 to the education lane, public content and an orthodontist referral, before anything is stored. Where a platform lets a short link a long-form piece, link the one that pays its point off, since the man who taps it has chosen a longer look at you.

### Shorts first, Reels second, TikTok on proof

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts only | Your editing budget covers one native pass per piece | No Reels for your Instagram router, so fewer adults reach you by DM |
| Shorts, then Reels | Early, with long-form running and Instagram as your router | A second native pass per piece |
| Shorts, Reels, and TikTok | A labeled trial matches Reels on eligible adults per editing hour over about 30 door completions | A third pass, the longest route to your door, and no feed into long-form or your router |

**Default: Shorts first, Reels second.** Shorts feed your long-form channel, on the platform that reaches more US adults than any other and nearly all of those aged 18–29. Few viewers follow a short into long-form, but the ones who do meet your decision-stage pieces and your pitch. Reels feed your Instagram router, on a platform that reaches about half of US adults, where men who won't comment in public send a keyword in private and get your door link back. TikTok reaches a little over a third of US adults and feeds neither owned asset, so the adults it sends are its whole output.

So TikTok joins only on that match, which is the one comparison every platform shares, since your door counts eligible adults the same way everywhere. A trial is an experiment: it runs only on slack under 25 hours, for [8–12] weeks or about 30 door completions, whichever comes first, and it's the first thing cut when a week runs long. The set answers to the Platform Count Rule: run only as many platforms as your editing budget serves, and drop one before long-form slips (Module 25). When the budget can't serve both, Reels go before Shorts, and the keyword replies keep routing the Reels already up.

**Shorts feed long-form, Reels feed the router, and TikTok earns its place on yield.**

### Minor exposure is weighed honestly

None of the three is an adult feed, since teens use all of them heavily, so Shorts first rests on what happens after the view. A Shorts viewer can walk into long-form, where decision-stage topics thin out the youngest. Platforms also tend to hold back feature-comparison content from teen accounts, which your packaging never makes anyway, and those rules change, so re-check them each quarter. On every platform, packaging and the door's age fork do the protecting, because no setting keeps a 15-year-old out of your comments.

**When the signals disagree.** In week [3] of a TikTok trial, one piece runs to [40 times] the trial's usual views and adds [~3,000] follows in a weekend. The platform's age data and your fork agree that most of them are under 18, and the piece's label shows [four] door completions, [one] of them eligible. Views say move your week to TikTok. The trial was built to answer that question, and one piece can't.

So the decision is already written. Your hours stay where they are, and nothing moves from Shorts, Reels, or long-form. You make no sequel to ride the spike, and its framing gains no win, because trial pieces are cut from framings that already won their pairs and the lab counts wins only in pairs. The young follows will tilt the trial's next audiences, so set the spike aside and read only the pieces posted after it. End the trial early if their adult share at the fork trails Reels'; otherwise it runs its window, and a window that closes short of 30 completions is a no. Hide the rating requests under your comment policy, and give anyone who says he's under 18 the public education-lane answer, with no DM and no route to the door.

## 2. The Shorts Job Selector

Every short gets one job before it's made, and it's judged only by that job's number. The number every feed shows first counts everyone, and the youngest viewers, who can't buy, pile into it fastest.

### One leaderboard pulls every piece younger

Put every short on one leaderboard of views, and the winners are the pieces the youngest viewers engage with, since they engage first. The pieces built to send adults to your door look like losers and get dropped. The number you steer by chooses what you make next, so a views leaderboard slowly hands your packaging to 15-year-olds.

### Each platform keeps its own count

The count itself moves under you. A view counted from a piece's first frame counts every 15-year-old who scrolled past, and platforms redefine their public counts without asking you. So give each platform one fixed count: engaged views on YouTube, and on Reels and TikTok the count each one reports, named in the metric and dated whenever it changes. Read a pair of pieces only within one platform, and compare platforms only on eligible adults per editing hour, which your door counts the same way everywhere. That's Denominator Discipline, a denominator you can hold still under every rate (Module 12).

### Four jobs, each with its own number

A long-form piece carries two jobs at once, and the Two-Job Scorecard judges it on reach and on belief by eligible-adult yield. A short has seconds, so it carries one:

| Job | What the piece does | Judge it by | Its one ask (Module 18) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | Puts an adult situation in front of strangers | Adult share at your door's age fork, by label; then taps into long-form and follows per 1,000 of the platform's count | The next piece, or a follow |
| Lead | Gives a man with a live question his next step | Door starts per 1,000 of the platform's count, through a link or keyword tagged to the piece; eligible leads confirm over about 30 door completions | The door |
| Proof | Shows your standard working: how you capture, your dated log, a sample review, a consented clip | Profile visits, follows, and taps into long-form per 1,000 | The long-form piece behind it, or the door |
| Offer | Tells a warm viewer the destination, the plan, the price, and where to go | Offer-page visits from the piece | The offer page, on surfaces followers see first |

Reach, lead, and proof pieces sit on the Stranger rung of the Warmth Ladder, the rule that matches each asset's ask to its viewer's warmth, so they ask only for the next piece, a follow, or the door. The offer job belongs to viewers who have already come back. It waits for surfaces that reach followers first, such as Stories once you run native Instagram, and for your offer page. A feed tests every piece on strangers first, many of them minors, so no price ever runs there, and even on follower surfaces a follow counts as coming back only when adult-framed pieces earned it.

**One job per short, one number per job.**

That's the **Shorts Job Selector**: name each short's job before you make it, and judge it by that job's number. It keeps a lead piece from being killed for low views, and a spike from being mistaken for demand.

### One point makes four pieces

Take one defensible point from your Canon, your 5–7 core claims in fixed words (Module 14): "A record doesn't read itself; review turns it into a decision." As a reach piece, it opens on a situation in words, eight months of logs in a notes app that nobody has read, and points to the next piece. As proof, it walks through a sample weekly review labeled as a composite, then points to the long-form piece behind it. As a lead piece, the same opening ends on the door: "If you're an adult and can't tell what's moved, the free self-assessment in my profile sorts out where you are."

The offer piece names the destination before the mechanics: being taken seriously, at ease in photos and rooms, knowing instead of hoping. Then comes the obstacle, months with nothing measured, and the plan: the Program, 12 weeks of written review of your week, with captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12 taken the same way every time. Claim 7 places the destination beyond the face: "Most of how people read you was never about your jaw." The piece says who it's for and who it isn't, states [$price], and sends him to the page for the next start, since a date spoken into a piece goes stale.

Tag each piece when you plan the batch: its job, its link, its rung, and the Canon claim it carries. Read each job's number as a count at your weekly review. A rate replaces its planning range only after about 30 events stand behind it, so an early lead piece is read by its door starts, and door completions pool across pieces until about 30 exist. Early, most pieces take the reach or lead job, and proof starts with your own process, adding client clips only once consent exists.

## 3. The Two-Win Rule

Short-form is also a lab for framings, and a single win there is mostly noise. So a framing counts only after it wins twice and then wins clearly in a long-form title test.

### Why one win proves little here

A framing is the situation and promise a piece opens with. "27 and asking if it's too late" and "What can an adult still change?" can carry the same point to different people. In this niche the framing decides who stops scrolling: the adult in the middle of his stall, or the 15-year-old who wants a rating. Short-form makes a cheap, fast lab and a noisy one, and this category adds noise of its own:

- A piece's first audience is a small seed of strangers, many of them under 18, so a win can mean one young pocket liked it.
- Buyers decide in clusters, after payday, a wedding, or a work photo, so a week measures your calendar as much as your framing.
- The bone question pulls views whatever the framing, so a winner on that topic may be the topic winning.
- Counts are small. At roughly 0.1–1 raw lead per 1,000 views, a piece with [5,000] views sends between none and five people to your door, so a pair judged on completions compares zero with one.

### A win needs a floor, a margin, and an adult audience

Pair two framings of the same point in the same week, on the same platform, with the same job and length, so the framing is the only thing that differs. Where a platform lets you trial a piece with non-followers first, use that slot for pairs, since it holds the audience steadier. Judge the pair on events you can pin to each piece: taps on the piece's own link where the platform gives it one, or messages with a keyword unique to the piece, counted per 1,000 of that platform's count.

Set two lines before the pair runs. Below a floor of about 30 counted events across the pair, it's no result, logged as such, and neither framing gains or loses. Above the floor, a framing wins only by beating its pair by a margin you fix in advance, [such as half again as many events per 1,000], and a tie is never a win. Its adult share, read at your door's age fork across the labeled door starts its pieces send, has to hold at or above your running share. Door completions confirm a lead framing once about 30 have pooled across its pieces.

A framing that wins twice, in different weeks, has earned a long-form test. Be plain about what that proves. Small wins pass lucky framings more often than you'd guess, because each pair is a small sample of strangers on one day, so two wins only nominate a framing and the long-form test decides.

### Confirmation happens in long-form

A short-form win measures a stranger's thumb in the first second. A long-form title measures an adult deciding to give you part of his evening, in front of an older and warmer audience, so it's an independent test run on the viewers who buy. Put the framing into the title and thumbnail of the next long-form piece whose topic fits, and run it against your current default in your long-form platform's own title test. The useful kind judges versions by watch time, so a framing that wins the click and loses the viewer loses. Only a clear winner counts, and "performed the same" or "inconclusive" is a no.

**Win twice in short-form, then clearly in long-form, or it's still a guess.**

That's the **Two-Win Rule**: a short-form framing counts only after it wins repeatedly and is then confirmed in a long-form test. A confirmed framing becomes a packaging default, and it still answers to yield. If the pieces titled with it draw a younger audience at the fork over the next window, it loses its place.

### How noise fools you

| What you saw | What it can be | The check |
|---|---|---|
| One piece at [10 times] your usual views | A young pocket, a trend, a lucky seed | Its adult share at the fork, and how its pair did |
| A framing that won on views | The youngest viewers engaging first | The job's number |
| A winner on the bone question | The topic pulling, whatever the framing | A pair on the same topic |
| A 3-to-1 win in door starts | Four people | The floor: no result |
| Three wins you remember | Five losses you don't | A written record of every pair |
| A jump the week a platform recounted views | A new denominator | The platform's fixed count, with the break dated |

Reid *(composite operator)* runs pairs on Reels and skips both lines. His first pair, ["what nobody tells you at 22"] against ["what I'd measure at 22"], ends [3] door starts to [1] on about [4k] views each, and he calls it a win, though under the floor it was no result. His second pair ties on keyword messages, and he gives it to the framing he liked. Two "wins" in, he skips the long-form test and retitles his next [three] long-form pieces in the new pattern. Their clicks hold, their watch time slips, and the objection pieces that had been booking fit conversations book [two] fewer that month. When he finally tests the pattern on a fourth piece, the verdict is "performed the same". He spent a month of titles on a framing that never beat anything, and the rule would have logged one no result, one tie, and left his titles alone.

### Run the lab inside the batch

Test at most two framings at a time, each in one pair a week, inside the 4–7 pieces you already make. Keep a hook log of every pair: the date, the platform, the point, both framings, the counted events, the margin, and each framing's adult share at the fork. A framing that loses two pairs leaves the lab, and one that wins two waits for the next long-form piece whose topic fits. Test framings, never stakes. Pieces get faster and tighter in pace, never hotter in what they threaten, so no shame variant, rating, or idealized jaw enters a pair, whatever it might win.

## 4. The Native Tax, Derivatives, and Reels Craft

Every platform charges a tax on content that looks imported, so pay it on purpose. Post one clean master of your own work with a light native pass for each platform, and fund full re-cuts only where they've proved they pay.

### Imports get kept out of the feed

This category's feeds are crowded with reposts: aggregator accounts recycling other people's transformation photos, with no dates and no consent. Platforms keep reposted and watermarked material out of the feeds that reach strangers, and they rework their originality checks often, so re-verify the current rules each quarter. Your own original footage, posted clean, stays eligible, and never reposting anyone else's footage keeps you visibly apart from the aggregators. Viewers make the same call faster, scrolling past a letterboxed clip or another app's captions as an import.

That cost of belonging is the **Native Tax**: every platform charges for non-native content, so size the adaptation honestly. Three options cover the sizes:

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| One clean master, a light pass per platform | Your own footage, with no other app's logo on it | Its own first line, on-screen text, cover, and caption per platform |
| A full re-cut per platform | That platform's re-cut pieces have beaten the light pass twice on the same job's number | Editing hours out of your 3.5 a week |
| The same file everywhere, watermark and all | Never | Kept out of the feeds that reach strangers |

**Default: one clean master, cross-posted with a light native pass.** The tax that matters is detection plus the imported look. A clean master with its own first line, text, cover, and caption clears both for the least editing. Switch a platform to full re-cuts only when its re-cut pieces beat the light pass twice on the same job's number, which is the Two-Win logic applied to your editing budget.

### Create once, cut many

Short-form rides the long-form batch and the editor you already have, so one filming session feeds both surfaces and every cut comes back to you before it's scheduled. Before you film, mark each script's defensible points, the ones true as a single sentence, and shoot the strongest again as vertical takes. Each derivative, a short clip cut from the long-form piece, goes out as one clean master with its pass for each platform. It takes the rung of the surface it's posted to, so a clip cut from a decision-stage pitch loses its price and checkout link before it reaches a feed. Some moments cut well, and some never do:

| Cuts well | Never cuts |
|---|---|
| A core claim, said whole in its fixed words | The pitch, whose price belongs on warm surfaces |
| A capture fact shown on lamps or a capture grid | A before/after segment, or any face comparison |
| A stall told back from anonymized patterns | An outcome story without its full count |
| A teardown's steelman and its sharp point, together | A debated point whose caveat won't fit its sentence |
| A destination line with its perspective line | Anything a commenter under 18 wrote |

A clip is a claim in your name, so the clip brief in this module's templates gives your editor the point, its caveat, the Canon claim, the job, and the ask. When your week can't pay a platform's tax, the Platform Count Rule drops the platform before long-form pays it.

**One master per point, a light pass per platform, your approval on every cut.**

### Reels craft for Instagram

On Instagram, a stranger who likes a Reel usually opens your profile before he follows or taps, and in this category your grid tells him in a second whether you're one more transformation account. The adults who want more tend to write by DM rather than comment. Four parts of the craft follow from that.

**Covers.** Make the grid read as a library of adult decisions: one plain line per cover, in the same type and frame. Never use a face pair or your own jaw angle, since your face carries trust and is never evidence for the method. Pin the pieces that route, starting with the Honest Answer, your straight public answer to the bone question.

**Text.** Many viewers watch with the sound off, so the on-screen text carries the one point by itself, in the words he'd use. Canon claims stay word for word, and a caveat stays on screen as long as its claim does, clear of the app's buttons, since a covered caveat is a missing one.

**Pacing.** The category's fastest Reels race to a face, and yours race to the point. Cut pauses and repeats, never the caveat or a claim's fixed words. The first line names a situation, the payoff lands in the same piece, and the last line makes one ask, which is the Payoff Test, every curiosity device paid off in the same asset (Intro).

**The send-to-a-friend test.** A send shows that a stranger valued the piece enough to pass it on, and in this niche that's rare, because the man you want hides the pursuit. So design for the send, then test it: would an adult forward this to a friend, and would the friend feel recognized rather than rated? A piece that names a situation, like eight months with nothing measured, passes. So does one that arms him against a grift pattern, because forwarding it makes the sender look careful. A piece whose forward says "this is about your face" fails, however many sends it would earn, because the man receiving it never asked.

## 5. The Clip Context Check and the One-Defensible-Point Rule

Every clip you publish is a new claim in your name, so it carries its own caveat or it doesn't ship. And every short is built on one point that's true as it stands.

### A cut sentence is a new claim

A long-form piece earns its claims in order: the question, what the evidence says, how strong it is, and only then the point. Cut a few seconds from it and the point travels alone. "Some adults report their jaw looks different" loses "that's debated, and I don't sell it" and becomes a promise the category's worst channels would make. The feed strips the rest of the context too, and the first frame travels alone as a thumbnail. A clip missing its caveat is the exact screenshot a hostile critic looks for, and the man who sees only the clip never sees the minute that qualified it.

That's the job of the **Clip Context Check**: every extracted clip is an original claim, so put the caveat back or drop it. Find the sentence that qualified the point in the source. If the caveat fits inside the on-screen sentence, the clip ships, because the point is now true as it stands: "Some adults report their jaw looks different; that's debated, and I don't sell it." If the caveat needs a minute of its own, the point goes back to long-form, where the minute fits. Canon claims go out word for word, and your editor flags while you decide.

**Put the caveat back or drop the clip.**

### One true point per short

A short has room for one point, and a viewer carries away one sentence. If that sentence needs a second one to be true, the second is the one he'll lose. So each short follows the one-defensible-point rule, one true point per short piece that needs no caveat. A caveat short enough to live inside the sentence keeps the rule, since the sentence is then true as stated, while a point that needs a tier explained, a count, or an "except" belongs to long-form:

| The point | True as stated? | The move |
|---|---|---|
| "Photos taken under different light can't be compared." | Yes, in every case | Ship it, shown on lamps or a capture grid |
| "Behavior gets measured every week; appearance gets captured rarely, the same way every time." | Yes: a process fact you control | Canon claim 3, word for word |
| "Some adults see their jaw change." | No: it's debated, and alone it reads as a promise | Put "that's debated, and I don't sell it" inside the sentence, or leave it to long-form |
| "Your stall isn't genetic." | No: nobody can say that about every stall | Claim 2: "Most stalls we see are direction problems: months of real effort with no map and nothing measured. Measuring is how you'd know if yours is." |
| "Mewing works if you stay consistent." | No: a structural claim nobody can support | Never |

### One sharp point at a practice

A short suits *Fight ideas, not people* at its pointed strength, because one defensible point can carry an edge when it's aimed at a practice. Cut it from your quarterly teardown of a published ad, and bring only the steelman and the point. The ad never appears, and neither does any pair. Show the tell on the lamps themselves, or on a consenting volunteer in one continuous shot as the light moves, and point to your Capture Standard, the matched conditions every capture follows (Module 6):

> **You:** "There's a jaw-device ad going around, and I won't show it. The man in it may well have worked hard, and wanting what he wanted is fair. Now watch the light on my volunteer: overhead, it carves shadows along the jaw; in front, it fills them in. Look at that ad again, and you're comparing two lamps. Before you buy a device, ask for dated photos taken the same way. My Capture Standard is in my profile, so hold me to the same test."

Notice the move: the steelman in one line, one sharp point at the practice, then a check he can run on anyone, you included. It repairs Guide, because he learns the test your own proof has to pass: the Honest-Evidence Test, the tells of manufactured proof taught so buyers can judge anyone (Module 16). The same edge fits three more practices, rating culture, tip farms, and the "it's all genetics" shrug, each in the hook patterns below.

> **Theo** *(composite, Burned Struggler)*: "So you're the one honest guy. Convenient."
>
> **You:** "Don't take my word for it. My Capture Standard and my dated log are public, so check them first. If they hold up, the free self-assessment in my profile is the next step."

The line you never say is "Look at that guy's jaw, it's obviously fake," which comments on the features of a man who never consented. Keep the edge on the lamp.

### Client results travel with their counts

Consented client clips put a checkable result in a real man's words. *Name the destination boldly* licenses them in organic short-form, so keeping them off it under-sells the men who earned them. They follow the Proof Portability Gradient, which sets where each kind of proof may travel. Contextualized clips run in organic short-form with range and denominator on screen, and outcome proof never runs in ads (Module 27).

None exists in month 1, since consent comes after the fit window, his full refund in the first 14–21 days. It comes only at a measured peak, a reading where his adherence and at least one marker have reached their thresholds. Process testimonials arrive from about week 7 of your first client. Outcome clips wait for the proof milestone, 10 or more graduates with consented process testimonials, and carry a small-sample label.

Before/after pairs never appear in a short. They stay in long-form and your site library, where the whole Context Stack, the six items that make a result checkable, fits. A short carries his words, his markers, his context, and his counts, and its first frame shows a situation, never a face:

```
First frame: his situation in words, "[24, shift work, eight months of guessing]"
Claim: "[marker type] reached its threshold by week 12"
What else changed: [weight ±~X kg] · [a new job with a longer commute]
Timeline: baseline [date] · week 12 [date] · markers only, no captures shown
Range: [A–B] across [n] graduates · a small sample
Count: started [N] · exited [n] · graduated [n] · no marker at threshold [k]
His words, under [his initials]: "[For the first time I know what's working and what isn't.]"
Perspective: "Most of how people read you was never about your jaw."
On screen throughout: Observed among clients, not caused.
Ask: the free self-assessment in my profile
```

## 6. Cadence by Stage

Short-form gets its most hours while long-form has no search traffic, and the lab moves at long-form's pace, because every framing it nominates needs a long-form piece to be confirmed.

| Stage | Short-form a week | Pieces and the lab | Who cuts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early, under about 50 eligible leads a month | About 3.5 h | 4–7 native pieces; two framings in pairs; about two long-form tests a month | You film; an editor cuts; you approve |
| Growing | About 1.25 h | [3–5] pieces, mostly derivatives; one pair a week; up to three tests a month | A freelancer cuts; you approve |
| Scaling | About 0.5 h | [2–4] derivatives; one pair when a long-form slot fits | Freelancers cut; you approve every clip |

### The Canon rotation sets the batch

Each batch walks the Canon in order: one claim per piece, cycling through all seven, so at 4–7 pieces a week each claim comes round every week or two. Claim 1, the Honest Answer, goes in every piece built on the category's search terms, whatever the rotation says, and when a myth resurfaces, the claim that answers it leads for about two weeks. The pairs sit inside the rotation, two framings of the same claim in the same week. So the lab never costs the Canon a slot, and a buyer who sees ten of your shorts hears most of your claims in their fixed words.

### The week-3 shift pauses the lab's confirmations

The Founding Sprint's week-3 source check can collide with the lab. If your warm network and replies give fewer than 2 held conversations a week by week 3, about 2 hours a week move from long-form into short-form batches and Permission-First Replies, public answers on other threads with a DM only after he says yes. That leaves long-form about half an hour a week, so the next piece slips and the lab's confirmations wait with it. Take the cost with open eyes, since conversations decide month 3 and a framing can wait a month for its test. The shift adds no platform, so the Platform Count Rule still holds.

Keep the Honest Answer up and every link pointing at it while the shift runs, and let two-win framings queue in the order they won. The hours go back to long-form once your weekly count reaches the Sprint's target of 3–6 held conversations. If week 12 arrives with fewer than about 15 held conversations, the shift stays, short-form and replies take more of your slack up to 25 a week, and the small adult-only paid test opens at months 3–4.

### The hours follow compounding

Once long-form draws its own search and suggested traffic, an hour there buys more adults than an hour of shorts: 2–5 raw leads per 1,000 engaged long-form views against roughly 0.1–1 per 1,000 short-form views, plus a clickable path and your pitch. So Growing's design week moves short-form to about 1.25 hours, and Scaling's to about half an hour of approvals. Revenue can call you Growing while your volume says Early, and the binding constraint decides. Keep the Early line while eligible leads run under about 50 a month.

### Overload cuts come in a fixed order

Past 25 hours, the TikTok trial goes first, with the other experiments. After X and native Instagram posts, short-form above its stage line goes next, Reels before Shorts, while the keyword replies keep routing the Reels already up. The stage line itself sits inside the Protected Content Minimum, content hours client work can never eat (Module 11). A short that spreads is never a reason to add hours or a platform, only a candidate for the lab, where it needs two wins like any other framing.

**Short-form leads while long-form is young, and the lab keeps long-form's pace.**

**Week 2. First wins.** A beats C [24] to [11] on messages per 1,000, over the floor and past his fixed margin. On Shorts, B beats D [28] to [12] on taps into long-form. Both framings' adult shares at the fork hold near his running [~60%].

**Week 4. The queue fills.** At [seven] pieces a week, B beats C [26] to [13] for its second win and joins the queue, and C, with two losses, leaves the lab.

## Checklist: The Clip Context Check

Run it on every clip before it's scheduled. A yes on every line ships it.

1. Does the clip's point stay true without the sentence before or after it in the source? → Put the caveat inside the on-screen sentence, or send the point to long-form.
2. Does every caveat, tier, or "debated" from the source stay on screen as long as its claim does? → Put it back, or drop the clip.
3. Is the Canon claim this piece carries quoted word for word? → Paste it from your Canon.
4. Is the clip free of client results, or does each carry the whole Context Stack on screen, from at least 10 graduates and labeled a small sample until 30? → Cut the result.
5. Is the clip free of client material, or does all of it match the scope he signed, words only unless he ticked more? → Cut it.
6. Is every frame free of before/after pairs and face comparisons, with an adult situation in the first? → Re-cut it.
7. Does the ask fit a feed's Stranger rung, the next piece, a follow, or the door, with no price and no comment keyword? → Change the ask.
8. Is it bound only for organic feeds, or, if it's going into an ad, is it process proof alone? → Pull every client result from the ad.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Reach binds, and short-form is its main source. The default is the full batch on Shorts and Reels, walking the Canon with two framings in pairs. The trap is reading the first month's pairs as results. Most sit under the floor at founding volume, and "no result" is the honest read.

**Growing.** Long-form's own traffic is compounding, so short-form's job narrows to the lab and derivatives. The default is one pair a week, with each two-win framing sent to the next long-form slot that fits. The trap is forcing a framing onto a topic it doesn't fit, so the test scores a broken title and a good framing leaves the lab.

**Scaling.** Your approval time binds, because freelancers cut most clips and every one still passes you. The default is a fixed approval slot with the check run on every clip. The trap is letting a clipper's pace set your claims, since one stripped caveat at volume undoes a year of the Honest Answer.

## Standard Check

- **One defensible point, caveats intact.** Each short makes one point true as stated, with any caveat inside its sentence, or the point goes to long-form (the line on structural claims).
- **No pair, rating, or young bait.** No before/after pair, face comparison, idealized-jaw hook, or rating enters any short or pair, whatever it would earn, and minors in comments get the education lane in public (the line on vulnerability).
- **Results travel with their counts.** Consented clips run in organic short-form with range, denominator, and "observed, not caused" on screen, and never in ads (*Name the destination boldly*, inside the line on fabricated proof).
- **The edge goes on the lamp.** Teardown shorts show no one's face: a steelman line, the tell on lamps or a consenting volunteer, and one sharp point at the practice (*Fight ideas, not people*).
