# 19 · The Sales Conversation
*Part V — Selling and Transformation*

**The shift:** from *"Closing is pressure or luck"* to *"I close by contract, with force aimed at the situation and routes for burned and insecure buyers."*

Near the end of a fit conversation, a 24-year-old with about eight months of [his routine] behind him, nothing measured, says the six words every operator in this category hears weekly:

> **Dan** *(composite, Struggler)*: "Makes sense. I need to think about it."

Most operators answer in one of two ways. One says "No pressure," sends nothing, and never hears from him again. The other cuts the price for today, and a man used to the category's countdown discounts hears what he expected. Both lose the sale. The first also fails him, because a good-fit buyer left to guess is let down as surely as one who was pushed.

A contract-based close hears the same words from a different place. Earlier, Dan agreed the call would end in a clear yes or a clear no, and he named what another year of guessing would cost him. So you ask what he's weighing, find the missing belief, and answer it from his record. If he still wants time, he leaves with a date on a real start.

## 1. The Contract and the Arc

A close in this category holds when it works as a contract: a structure he agrees to at the start, run in a fixed order, ending in a decision you both named before it began.

### He punishes the move he uncovers

Your buyer has met the free "jaw analysis" that turns into a device pitch halfway through, so he knows your call is a sale too. What he holds against you is a move he uncovers, because one hidden move tells him the whole call was staged.

So the structure comes first, out loud: what you'll ask, the straight answer about what can and can't change, and one recommendation at the end, whether a program, the free path, "don't buy", or a referral. A clear yes and a clear no both end it well, and laid out like that, it leaves his guard nothing to find. "No is fine" helps only when it's structurally true: a real free path, money talk that ends at "I can't afford it", and fixed follow-up.

### Each step stands on the one before

Each step needs the one before it. Taken out of order, a step turns into pressure:

| Step | What you do | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Contract | Agree the structure and both endings | A pitch he uncovers |
| 2. Excavate | His months, checks, and conclusion | Selling before a named need |
| 3. Summary | Goal, stake, and gap, confirmed | The wrong goal |
| 4. Recommend | One option, from his reasons | A menu that stalls him |
| 5. Price | Real tiers top first, price once, silence | Apology, discount, decoy |
| 6. Affordability | The question, word for word, about his pick | Selling past his means |
| 7. Ask | "Do you want to start on [date]?" | A good-fit buyer unasked |
| 8. Sort | Up to two probes per real objection | The wrong objection answered |
| 9. Decision Date | His date to decide by, on a real point | "I'll let you know" |
| 10. Day one | Baseline day, his first review, booked | A yes that cools |
| 11. Recap | In writing within 24 hours | A decision from memory |

That's **Close by Contract**, the close that agrees its structure first and runs in order to the ending it named. Run it on every live selling conversation, in 20–30 minutes, or about 45 in your founding months while research rides along.

**Agree the ending first, earn each step, and leave with day one, a clear no, or a date.**

### The floor holds on a live call

The Informed-Client Test, the check every tactic passes in both directions (Intro), holds from the door's checks until a stop rule fires. Its ceiling asks whether a client anywhere in your range, low end included, would still say the call served him. Its floor says a screened, good-fit buyer left to talk himself out of the right decision was failed too, since he goes back to guessing while the next device waits.

Force means his stake, one confident recommendation, an explicit ask, up to two probes per real objection, and a real date. Pressure means asking after a clear no, reopening money after "I can't afford it", selling into distress, invented urgency, or shame. The free jaw analysis runs on embedded commands and false takeaways, which work only while he can't see them.

## 2. Excavation and the Implication Question

Excavation earns the recommendation: before you name anything you sell, he tells you what he tried, what it cost him, and what he wants, in words you'll hand back.

### He talks first, about what he did

Describe the program before he has named a need and you've handed him something to object to. Reading the buyer and adapting to him is among the best-supported predictors of selling well. Here the reading starts with his months: what he did, how he checked it, what happened around month 3, and what he concluded. Ask what he did, because last month is a record and next month is a guess.

His answers also set his route and surface the stops. A past purchase that promised structural change marks a burned buyer, who gets the verification kit, a sample written plan and sample weekly review, and no probes. A run of recent purchases, each dropped for the next, is a fit-check signal instead, and it pauses the sale. Talk about his worth, or regular checking of his face, puts him on the Dignity Route, the way of selling that keeps force off his worth. Jaw pain, snoring, or a bite concern means a referral note first, and checking many times a day ends the sale.

What he bought shows how he was sold before, so it stays in your notes, out of the summary, the recommendation, the price, and the recap. A price argued from money already lost is the category's recovery pitch.

### The ladder climbs to his life, then stops

Run the Destination Ladder, two questions that climb from the feature he names to the life behind it and one that grounds it (Module 2). Stop once the answer leaves his face. "A sharper jaw" becomes "I'd stop dodging the camera at work", and the grounding question finds the quarterly team photo.

### He names the stake

Waiting looks free here, because outcomes are slow and another month seems to cost only a month. Stakes move people by small to moderate amounts, mostly when they believe they can act, and framing a cost as a loss or a gain changes almost nothing. So the stake has to be accurate and his, since a cost he says out loud is one he can't argue with later:

> **You:** "If the next 12 months look like the last [eight], what does that cost you?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer, however long it takes.]*

That's **the Implication Question**: he names the cost of waiting in his own words, and you reflect it. On the standard route, say back his months, money from here on, and the moments he's missed, plainly. If he says it's fine, go back to a cost he named earlier and name it: "You said [his words]. Another year of guessing is another year of that." Then future-pace the record, leaving his face out:

> **You:** "Picture next [March] two ways: another year of photos you can't compare, or a record that tells you what moved."

**Ask what another year like this one costs, hold the silence, and hand his words back.**

Two routes narrow it. On the Dignity Route the stake holds only time, money from here on, and guessing, with no missed moment said back. A burned buyer hears one forward question, and his answer stands.

### The summary is the hinge

End excavation with three things in his words: where he wants to be, what waiting costs him, and what was missing. On the Dignity Route the destination stays at knowing instead of guessing, and being at ease, and the cost at time, money from here on, and guessing. Ask whether you got it right, and wait. His yes makes everything after it his own argument.

## 3. Recommendation, Price, and the Spoken Affordability Line

Recommend one thing for the reasons he gave you, show every real tier top first, say the price once, and ask the affordability question about the tier he picks.

### He gets one recommendation, argued from his summary

A menu hands the decision back to a man who came because eight months of guessing couldn't make it, and the recommendation is the judgment he's paying for. So recommend one thing, argued from his summary. It's usually the Program, your 12-week flagship, and otherwise Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables; the Starter Path, your free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now; "don't buy"; or a referral.

Before any price, walk the Path and Timeline Card, the page that shows the whole path and its likely cost before payment (Module 6), yes before no. His log shows within two weeks whether the work is happening, and at week 12 he'll know what moved, while jaw change from habits is debated and unpromised.

### Every real tier shows first, and the price is said once

Show the real tiers, top first, say which you recommend, and state its price once, both forms on one line: $[2,400] in full, or three payments of $[820], $[2,460] in total. A buyer with steady pay and no lump sum then hears a price he can meet. The premium tier anchors honestly only because someone buys it; a tier built to make the next look cheap is a decoy he spots. Then stop talking. The first words after a price are usually an apology, a justification, or a discount, each a sign you doubt the number.

That's **Premium First, Price Once**: the real tiers premium-first, one recommendation, the price once, then silence. The honest anchors are the delivery math he can count and the value of months not wasted, another year of guessing beside a year in which he knows. Surgery, filler, hourly rates, an invented "total value", and what he already spent stay away from the price.

### The affordability question comes after the tiers

Then comes the question he'll hear before every payment you take. Once he has named the tier he'd pick, ask it word for word, about that tier:

> **You:** "I ask everyone this before any payment. Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

It follows the tiers because by then he has seen every real option, so a no can't be met with a cheaper one. A yes lets the checkout run the Fit Check, the plain-language check for signs that buying could hurt him (Module 5), before any pay button. A no ends money talk: the Starter Path once, tool-free, and the pause route, the content-free tag that holds every marketing send and blocks checkout for 60–90 days, then asks permission once. There's no plan, no lower tier, and no Program Async, the tier without the live call. What he says aloud sets the same tag a checkout answer would.

### A plan is its own paid step

If he picks the plan, the question comes again, word for word, because the plan is a second paid step. Plans at Cost, the rules that keep a plan from becoming a loan, allow only your own installments, at most three, each within about a third of his monthly take-home, and none after a Fit Check signal. The Layered Guarantee, your written promises on the work you control, gets one line in the recommendation, "[We guarantee the work we control, and we pay on time.]", and its terms when he asks what happens if nothing changes (Module 7).

## 4. The Objection Map

Fourteen objections decide most calls in this niche, each points at one missing belief, and each is answered with the evidence for that belief instead of a longer pitch.

### Every objection names a link

A yes rests on six beliefs, Cause, Range, Vehicle, Guide, Self, and Now, and a no usually means one is missing: that's the Belief Chain (Module 14). Raise the loudest objection yourself. Say the Honest Answer's spoken version, your straight answer to the bone question (Module 3), on every call right after the summary, and he never has to defend "adults can't change". The objection map files the niche's deciding objections by link, beside the reply that loses each one:

| His words | Link | What never does |
|---|---|---|
| "Adults can't change anyway." | Range | "Some guys do see jaw changes" |
| "It's all free on YouTube." | Vehicle | Knocking free channels |
| "Why not an orthodontist?" | Range, Guide | Knocking clinicians |
| "I've seen faked before/afters." | Guide | A better before/after |
| "What if nothing changes?" | Guide | A promise about his face |
| "I need to think about it." | Now, or a hidden link | A discount, a deadline, a third probe |
| "How much of your time do I get?" | Vehicle | Minutes, hourly rates, "total value" |
| "Who sees my photos?" | Guide | "Nobody's looking at you" |
| "How many minutes a day?" | Self | A smaller number than the truth |
| "I always quit." | Self | "Most people quit too" |
| "How long, and what's the total?" | Range, Guide | A date for his face |
| "Can I stop paying?" | Guide | "You're locked in" |
| "I need to ask my parents" or "my partner" (an adult) | Now, once the money is his | Pitching the parent or partner |
| "I need to ask my parents" (a minor) | None: a stop rule | "Come back when you're 18" |

### The Real-Objection Sort finds the one he has

"I need to think" is this category's all-purpose cover, and under it sits one of three things: a doubt he hasn't voiced, a fear of deciding wrong, or a polite no. A repair aimed at the wrong one reads as pressure, so sort before you answer, starting with what he's weighing. An unvoiced doubt gets its link's check question and evidence from his record. Fear of deciding wrong is a Guide doubt, answered with the guarantee's terms. A polite no gets named so it can end cleanly: "Is that a no? That's a good outcome too," then the clear-no line.

That's **the Real-Objection Sort**: separate the stated objection from the real one, and resolve each he raises with up to two probes. Two is the limit, because a third question stops sorting and starts wearing him down. A burned buyer's "I need to think" gets no probe at all.

**Sort, check, repair with his evidence, and return to the ask.**

### The Pushback Signal changes the route

Sometimes he stops raising doubts and argues for staying put: the busy season, the genetics he's sure of, the free videos he'll get to. Stop persuading, because a rebuttal makes him argue harder, and each sentence he spends on the status quo rehearses it. Try this instead:

> **Sam** *(composite, Ambivalent)*: "I'll start after the busy season. It's not going anywhere."
>
> **You:** "Fair. You've got a real season coming. Can I push back on one part?"
>
> **Sam:** "Go on."
>
> **You:** "You said last year's season ended in [October], and nothing started then either. What would be different after this one?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

That's **the Pushback Signal**: when he argues for the status quo, reflect it, ask permission, and challenge it with his own evidence. A concrete answer, like overtime that eats the plan's minutes, moves his decision to the first start after it, and a vague one keeps the next start. Either way it ends at the ask. Never reply "there's always a reason to wait", which argues where his own words would do it.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a good-fit buyer says yes to the affordability question, then needs to think. The sort finds Self, you spend both probes, repair it with his record, and ask again, and he needs to think. Every fit signal says yes, and his answer says not today.

First rule out a burned buyer you missed. Then ask once whether it's the same doubt or a new one. A new doubt earns its own two probes, while the same doubt means they're spent, so name what you see, once, and let the calendar hold the decision:

> **You:** "It sounds like you believe the plan and doubt you'll be the one who keeps it. That's worth deciding on a real date. The [March] start's last day to join is [March 13]. What date will you decide by?"

No discount, no third probe, no date off the calendar. The recap and his check-in carry it from there.

## 5. State Routing, the True Thing, and Stop Rules

The arc stays fixed for every buyer: his state sets the route through it, the Dignity Route changes where force points, and a stop rule ends it.

### His state sets the route through the arc

Buyer States, the four situations a buyer can arrive in, are read in his words at every contact, since a state can shift inside one call. The offer and the arc stay fixed while the route changes:

| State | Lead with | What changes | His date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Struggler | Cause, then Self | His months read back; the true thing aimed at the missing measurement | The next start's last day to join |
| Burned Struggler | Guide | The kit and terms first; one forward stake question; no probes | The start he chooses |
| Optimizer | Vehicle, Guide | Deliverables, turnaround, and privacy before price | The next start, or late entry now |
| Ambivalent | Now | His own reasons, and "what changes then?" | The next start, or the first after a real constraint |
| Insecurity-led, over any state | The Dignity Route | Stakes kept to time, money from here on, and guessing | Unchanged |

That's **State Routing**: one arc and one offer, with the route through them set by his state. When states mix, burned comes first, because nothing else lands until he has checked you.

### The Dignity Route turns his worth question toward the guessing

Some buyers describe themselves where others describe a situation, and to them a reflected missed moment is evidence against their worth. They take the Dignity Route, which changes where force points and never what you recommend (Module 15). On a call it changes the reflection, the summary, and where the hard true thing lands. Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, a consultant, opened by asking about turnaround and privacy:

> **Adrian:** "I look wrecked on every client call. Is this even worth it for someone like me?"
>
> **You:** "That's a hard way to feel, and it makes sense after [six] months with no answer. You've put in time and money, and you still don't know what's working. That's the guessing, and the guessing is the problem. It's also the part we can end."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

The client calls he named never come back in the reflection, the summary, or the recap, even though they're his goal, and his summary says he wants to know instead of guess, and to be at ease. The recommendation holds: a Private seat at $[5,000] with [recorded walkthroughs and 24-hour written replies], "We never read or score your face", the price once, the question, and the ask.

### The hard true thing lands on the situation

Force belongs here once his months are on the table and he has agreed with your summary. Then say it plainly:

> **You:** "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem, not you."

It lands because it names a waste he already feels, gives it a cause he can change, and lifts the blame off him, so he can hear the plan. Aimed at his face or his worth, the same force is shame, which is why Adrian heard it aimed at the guessing.

### Every stop has one line, said word for word

A clear no ends the ask. A protective stop ends the sale to protect him: money he said he can't spare, distress, a fit-check signal, or a minor. Each stop has one line, and nothing after it carries a price, an offer, or a date. Protective stops leave nothing beyond "stopped: stop rule", a minor's leaves nothing at all, and their recordings are deleted unscored.

| What happens | The line | Then |
|---|---|---|
| A clear no | "Understood. That's a clear no, and I won't ask again today. Your recap still comes within 24 hours." | A recap with no price, date, or ask; his link may be logged |
| "I can't afford it", or a no to the question | "Understood, and thanks for saying it straight. Here's the Starter Path, free: logs and self-checks, without the review. The door opens whenever you write." | The pause route |
| A fit-check signal, such as a booked procedure or a run of recent purchases | "Thanks for telling me. That's something we talk through before any payment, so nothing gets paid today. I'll put in writing what the program can and can't do for you, and we'll talk again in three days or more." | The signal pause and pause route; if he enrolls, it's through a single-use link with no plan, opening after 72 hours |
| Distress, fixation, or checking many times a day | "I'm going to stop talking about the program, because what you've just told me matters more. That one's not mine, so let's talk about who to see and what to tell them." | A referral conversation and note, crisis resources if acute, reading-only content, the pause route |
| He's a minor | "Thanks for telling me. I work only with adults, so I'm ending the call here and deleting what you've shared, and anything you paid comes back. For questions about a face that's still growing, an orthodontist is the right person." | Everything deleted; nothing kept |

## 6. Close, Decision Date, and Follow-Up

Every call ends in one of three places, day one booked, a clear no, or a Decision Date, and what happens after it is fixed before the call starts.

### Day one is booked before you hang up

How firmly a man commits as a call ends tends to predict what he does, and "I'll sort it this week" is weaker than "Monday at 7". On a yes, send the checkout while you're talking, with the Card and the Expectation Document, the fuller written terms of the work, above the pay button, and book baseline day. A signal at checkout starts the signal pause instead, with no payment that day.

### The Decision Date sits on a real point

When he needs time, he leaves with the Decision Date, his personal date to decide by. It falls on or before the last day to join of the start he's deciding for: the next start by default, and a later one when a burned buyer picks it or a real constraint dates it. Late entry through each start's second week keeps the next one within about 2–4 weeks, and an announced Price Step, a scheduled rise stated at least 30 days ahead, is said once if it lands first (Module 8). The date rests on honesty and planning, never on deadline pressure, whose evidence is contested.

### Follow-up holds his date, then lets him go

This category follows up by chasing: the "just checking in" run, the cart email that multiplies, the credit that suddenly expires. So the Follow-Up Rule is fixed before the call, and every touch is one he was told about: a written recap within 24 hours, one check-in on his Decision Date, and one close-the-loop message, then regular email only, and nothing while the pause route holds.

Say Sam leaves with [March 9]. The recap goes out that evening, and the check-in on [March 9] names his date, the start, and its last day to join, then meets silence. Silence on a date he chose usually means the date slipped or the answer is a no he didn't want to say, and another touch turns either into pressure. So the close-the-loop goes two days later with the next start's dates, and nothing follows it: no second check-in, no "seats are filling", no credit. After a protective stop none of this runs; he gets the Starter Path, the referral note, or the signal pause's written expectations, once.

### The scorecard names the step to fix first

Memory keeps the moments of a call that stung, so a close improves only on a record. Record every call he consents to, score one recording a week against the call scorecard, and score your last 30 held conversations monthly. Keep an objection log by link, in his words, never with his fit-check answers or what he said about his face or worth, quoted only with his consent; it feeds the Conversation-to-Content Loop (Module 18). That's the Selling-Skill Loop: recorded, scored calls and an objection log, with protective-stop calls never mined.

A weak score is one item failing across several recordings, or a close rate under its range over 30 held conversations, and the fix starts at the earliest failing step, since each rests on the one before. In month [11], Cole *(composite operator)* sees item 3 fail on [four] of his [eight] scored recordings, as he fills the silence after the price with "and that includes…", and item 5 fail on [two], a third probe. He fixes the silence first, because a price defended sounds doubted and his third probes were chasing that doubt. The offer and price stay as they are, and by month [14] his last 30 close at [35%]. Refunds and disputes get the same trace, back to the promise and path used on their call.

## Worked Example: Dan, From Contract to Day One

Cole is in Growing, month [14]: proof milestone met, the Program at $[2,400] in full or three payments of $[820], and one Private seat open while minutes are spare. The [March 2] start's last day to join is [March 13]. Dan booked from his result page after a yes to the affordability question beside the price range.

**Minutes 0–12. The read: a Struggler on the standard route.** Dan agrees to the contract and the recording. Excavation turns up about eight months of [his routine], bathroom photos every few weeks, and [about $300] on [an app and a chewing gadget] never sold as bone change. His conclusion is "maybe it's just genetics". He describes his situation, never his worth, so the standard route runs, led by Cause and Self. The ladder climbs from "a sharper jaw" to "I'd get picked to present to the regional team". Left alone: the Program, his face, and the [$300], which stays in the notes.

**Minute 13. The stake, named directly.** Cole asks the Implication Question and lets [about ten] seconds of silence run.

> **Dan:** "Another year of hanging back when they pick who presents. And more money on whatever looks like the answer that month."
>
> **Cole:** "So waiting costs you another year of hanging back, and more money on answers you can't check. Another year of guessing costs another year."

The stake is lived, forward, and his, so it's said back plainly, gadget money left out.

**Minute 15. The summary, the straight answer, the true thing.** Dan confirms the summary: taken seriously at work, the cost he named, and the missing baseline and review. The Honest Answer's spoken version follows word for word, as on every call, then the true thing from section 5.

**Minute 18. One recommendation, the tiers, the price.** Cole recommends the Program from [March 2], because Dan wants to know what's moving and nobody has read his record, and walks the Card, yes before no. The tiers come top first: a Private seat at $[5,000] with [recorded walkthroughs and 24-hour written replies], then the Program at $[2,400] in full, or three payments of $[820], $[2,460] in total. [About eight] seconds of silence pass before Dan says, "The Program." He says yes to the affordability question, asked word for word about it.

**Minute 21. The sort finds Guide.**

> **Cole:** "Do you want to start on [March 2]?"
>
> **Dan:** "I need to think about it."
>
> **Cole:** "Fair. What would you be weighing?"
>
> **Dan:** "Whether I'm paying for one more thing that doesn't work."
>
> **Cole:** "What would you need to see from me to trust how this works?"
>
> **Dan:** "A way out if nothing happens."
>
> **Cole:** "For [14] days from baseline day, one line gets you a full refund. Log at least [80%] of days, and if no marker reaches its threshold by week 12, [a fixed share inside 25–50%] comes back in cash within 7 days. Given that, does starting on [March 2] still fit what you want?"
>
> **Dan:** "Yes. Can I do the three payments?"

That was fear of deciding wrong, a Guide doubt, so the repair was the guarantee's terms, then back to the ask. Left alone: a third probe, a discount, and "most people love it".

**Minute 24. The plan step, and day one.** The plan is its own paid step, so Cole asks the question again, word for word, and Dan says yes, from his pay; each $[820] payment sits under a third of his take-home. At checkout he attests, answers the question, and clears the Fit Check. He pays the first installment and books baseline day for [March 2, 7 pm]. The recap leaves out the [$300]. Cole's time was about 45 minutes all-in, and his close rate sits inside the 25–45% range with proof.

**The same words from Theo.** Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, sells for a living and bought [a device and a course] that promised structural change, so his door answers tagged him Burned Struggler. Cole put the terms first, took his answer to one forward stake question as given, then recommended the Program and ran the tiers, the question, and the ask. Theo said he needed to think.

> **Cole:** "Of course. Here's what I'd check: a sample written plan, a sample weekly review, and the guarantee terms, all in your recap. The next start is [March 2], last day to join [March 13], and the one after is [April 6]. Which do you want to decide for, and by when?"
>
> **Theo:** "April. I'll decide by [April 10]."

No sort and no probe, and the device money stays in the notes. On [April 8] Theo writes back with two questions about the non-response clause, then a yes.

**A stop rule, mid-call: Maya.** Maya *(composite, welcome, not targeted)*, 28, a product designer, saw the price range on the booking page. [Nine] minutes in, asked why now, she says she's been waiting for a pay-later option to cover it, so Cole asks the question about that range, word for word.

> **Maya:** "Not without pay-later, no."
>
> **Cole:** "Understood, and thanks for saying it straight. Here's the Starter Path, free: logs and self-checks, without the review. The door opens whenever you write."

The rest of the call answers her questions about the free path, with no lower tier, no plan, and no date. The pause route is set, the recording is deleted, and the record reads "stopped: stop rule".

**What it shows.** The same six words got two answers because the route decided what they meant: a Guide doubt the guarantee's terms could settle, and due diligence the kit could serve. Maya's stop came from her own words, and nothing after it carried a price.

## Scripts: The Call Outline and the Fourteen Answers

### The call outline

Quoted lines are word for word; brackets hold his details.

1. **The contract.**

> **You:** "Here's how this goes, about [30] minutes. I'll ask how you've gone about this so far, then tell you straight what can and can't change for an adult. Then I'll recommend one thing, whether that's a program, the free Starter Path, not buying, or someone else to see, and give you any price. A clear yes and a clear no are both good outcomes, and either way you get a written recap within 24 hours. Can I record this for my notes? It's deleted after [30] days, and no is fine."

2. **Excavate.** "Walk me through the last [eight] months." · "How did you check whether it was working?" · "What happened around month 3?" · "Why now?" · Then the ladder.

3. **The stake and the summary.** The Implication Question from section 2, its silence held, then:

> **You:** "So waiting costs you [his words]. Let me check I've got it. You want [his destination]. You've put [eight] months in with nothing measured, and what's been missing is a baseline and someone reading your record. Did I get that right?"

On the Dignity Route, [his destination] is "to know instead of guess, and to be at ease".

4. **The straight answer, then the recommendation.** The Honest Answer's spoken version comes first, word for word.

> **You:** "Then I'd recommend [the Program] from [March 2], because [his reasons]. Here's the whole path: [the Card, yes before no]. There are two real options, top first. Private is $[5,000], with [recorded walkthroughs and 24-hour written replies]. The Program is $[2,400] in full, or three payments of $[820], $[2,460] in total, and it's the one I'd recommend for you."
> *[Stop. Say nothing until he speaks.]*

State seat status here only when it could change his start: "This start has [8] seats, and [5] are taken."

5. **The question about his pick, then the ask.**

> **You:** "I ask everyone this before any payment. Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"
> *[His yes.]*
>
> **You:** "Do you want to start on [March 2]?"

6. **Sort, date, day one.** "Fair. What would you be weighing?" → the link's check → his evidence → "Given that, does [the Program from March 2] still fit what you want?" A plan gets the question again. If he needs time: "The [March] start's last day to join is [March 13]. What date will you decide by?" On a yes, book baseline day.

7. **A stop, at any step.** Its line from the stop-rule table in section 5, word for word.

The two marked silences are where calls leak. The pushback variant comes right after the price:

> **Dan:** "That's a lot."
>
> **You:** "It's a real amount. Is it that it isn't comfortable, or that you're not sure it's worth it?"

If it isn't comfortable, that's a no, and the money stop line follows. If it's the worth, the link is Vehicle: the deliverables and the year he doesn't spend guessing, then the affordability line about his pick, then the ask. Never say "If price is the issue, I can do something for you today", a discount that teaches every later buyer to wait.

### The fourteen answers, by link

Each answer returns to the recommendation and the ask, unless a stop line has fired.

1. **"Adults can't change anyway."** *Range.*
> **You:** "There's no good evidence that habits change the shape of an adult's bone, and I don't sell that. Some things are debated, and I'll tell you where the evidence is thin. A lot does change and can be measured: your habits, your body composition, how you carry yourself, your grooming, how you're photographed. If what you want is to be at ease in photos and to stop guessing, that's where the work is. Which of those are you working on, and how would you know?"

2. **"It's all free on YouTube."** *Vehicle.*
> **You:** "Most of it is, and some of it's good. What it can't do is read your week. A record doesn't read itself; review turns it into a decision."

3. **"Why not an orthodontist?"** *Range, Guide.*
> **You:** "If your bite or your jaw's shape is the question, an orthodontist is who to see, and I'll tell you what to ask. Mine is your habits, measurement and weekly review, which run alongside their care and replace none of it."

4. **"I've seen faked before/afters."** *Guide.*
> **You:** "So have I. Ask six things of any result, mine included: Matched? Dated? What else changed? Which rung? Out of how many? Where are the rest? My face is not evidence for the method. The record is, published on the dates I committed to."

5. **"What if nothing changes?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "By week 6 your record shows what's moving, and by week 12 it can tell you a lever doesn't move for you. Log at least [80%] of days, and if no marker reaches its threshold, [a fixed share inside 25–50%] comes back in cash within 7 days. You can also leave in the first [14] days, or at week 6 once you've done the work. I don't guarantee how your face looks."

6. **"I need to think about it."** *Now, or a hidden link.*
> **You:** "Fair. What would you be weighing?"

Then the sort. A burned buyer gets his date and no probe.

7. **"How much of your time do I get?"** *Vehicle.*
> **You:** "You get 12 written reviews within [48] hours of each check-in, 12 group calls, a written read at week 6, a re-assessment at week 12, and three matched captures: judgment on your record, and a year you don't spend guessing."

8. **"Who sees my photos?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Only me. Captures sit at [where] and are deleted when you ask. Join under a handle, camera off; the charge reads [a discreet name]; nothing is published without consent you can withdraw."

9. **"How many minutes a day?"** *Self.*
> **You:** "[Minutes a day] on [habit blocks], about 10 minutes for the weekly check-in, and one [60]-minute call a week. Walk me through a normal weekday, and let's find where they'd go."

10. **"I always quit."** *Self.*
> **You:** "What happened the last time?"

If he couldn't tell it was working: "You didn't quit because you're weak. You quit because nothing showed you it was working. On the Program someone reads your week, every week."

11. **"How long, and what's the total?"** *Range, Guide.*
> **You:** "Your log shows within two weeks whether the work is happening, and at week 12 you'll know what moved. Jaw change from habits is debated, so I don't promise it. Over [9] months it's $[2,400] for the Program alone, $[3,447] with Round Two, a lighter follow-on block, and some months of the Hold, our measurement subscription, and $[4,947] if an outside correction needs a full second block."

12. **"Can I stop paying?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Yes. In the first [14] days, one line gets you a full refund. At week 6, if you've done the work, you can stop, get back what you paid for the weeks left, and owe nothing more. A failed payment pauses the program, with no fees, until you pay or cancel forward."

13. **"I need to ask my parents," or "my partner."** *Now, once the money is his.*
> **You:** "Whose money would it be?"

Their money gets the money stop line, and the pause route only if he couldn't pay it himself. His own money, with their view wanted or shared with a partner, makes a joint decision: "It all goes in your recap so you can go through it together. What date do you want to decide by?" No probes.

14. **"I'm 17. I'd need to ask my parents."** *A stop rule.* The minor's stop line, deletion, and a refund of anything paid, with no offer and no "later".

## Checklist: The Call Scorecard

Answer from your recordings and your last 30 held conversations. Protective-stop calls are never scored.

1. Did the contract name both good endings and the free path before any question about him? → No: open with the contract line.
2. Did he name the stake and confirm your summary before any recommendation? → No: ask the Implication Question, then summarize.
3. Was the price said once, both forms on one line, away from what he'd already spent, then silence? → No: cut what followed the number.
4. Was the affordability question asked word for word after the tiers, about his pick, and at any plan step? → No: fix it before your next call.
5. Did each real objection get one or two probes, none if he was burned, then the ask again? → None fails the floor; a third is pressure.
6. Did the route hold: the kit and his date if burned, no missed moment said back on the Dignity Route, every stop in its words? → No: replay where it slipped.
7. Did he leave with baseline day booked, a plain no, or a date on or before his start's last day to join? → No: find where it stalled.
8. Across your last 30 held conversations, did 15–35% enroll before proof, or 25–45% with it? → Below: replay three no's against items 1–7 before touching the offer.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Conversations bind, and every call is also research, so the founding call runs about 45 minutes and discloses both jobs before he books (Module 9). Run the full arc and score weekly, because your first 30 held conversations teach your close faster than any script. The trap is protecting the research by skipping the ask, which leaves a good-fit adult without a recommendation or a price.

**Growing.** Selling hours bind. Free calls go to uncertain buyers and those near a high-ticket decision under the Call Cap, the weekly limit on free conversations, and the overflow gets a Decision Assessment, your paid, credited written plan, which leads its own call. The trap is letting the credit into the close or the recap, where money he already paid becomes a deadline; it's stated once, in his written plan.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind and 50–70% of enrollments come without a call, so live calls go to Private and high-uncertainty buyers (Module 20). The trap is carrying the arc's words onto async surfaces without its stop lines.

## Standard Check

- **The question after the tiers.** Asked about his pick once every real tier is shown, the affordability question turns a no into the Starter Path and the pause route, never a cheaper tier: the line on vulnerability, held where a downsell is easiest.
- **Two probes, then a real date.** *Close* at full strength asks every good-fit buyer, probes each real objection once or twice, and ends on day one or a Decision Date on a real point (*Use real dates*).
- **Forward money only.** *Name the stakes* reflects money from here on; what he already spent stays out of the summary, the price, and the recap (*Present the price*).
- **Stop lines, word for word.** Nothing after one carries a price, an offer, or a date, and a protective stop leaves nothing beyond "stopped: stop rule".

## Quick Reference

**In one line.** Agree the ending first, let him name the stake, recommend once, price once, ask, and leave with day one, a clear no, or a Decision Date.

**Takeaways**
- The contract comes first, because a buyer who knows the structure has nothing to uncover.
- The affordability question follows the tiers and asks about his pick, so a no never meets a cheaper tier.
- Every objection names a link: sort it, probe once or twice, repair it with his record, ask again.

**The arc.** Contract → excavate → summary → one recommendation → tiers, price once, silence → the affordability line → ask → sort → Decision Date → day one → recap.

**The objection map.** Range: adults, orthodontist, timeline · Vehicle: free content, your time · Guide: fake proof, no change, privacy, stopping · Self: minutes, quitting · Now: "I need to think" · money first: parents, partner · a minor: stop.

**Stops and follow-up.** A clear no ends the ask; money he can't spare, distress, a fit-check signal, or a minor ends the sale, leaving nothing beyond "stopped: stop rule". Then one recap, one check-in, one close-the-loop.
