# 19 · The Sales Conversation
*Part V — Selling and Transformation*

**The shift:** from *"Closing is pressure or luck"* to *"I close by contract, with force aimed at the situation and routes for burned and insecure buyers."*

Near the end of a fit conversation, a 24-year-old with about eight months of [his routine] behind him, nothing measured, says the six words every operator in this category hears weekly:

> **Dan** *(composite, Struggler)*: "Makes sense. I need to think about it."

Most operators answer in one of two ways. One says "No pressure," sends nothing, and never hears from him again. The other cuts the price for today, and a man who has scrolled past the category's countdown discounts hears exactly what he expected. Both lose the sale. The first also fails him, because a good-fit buyer left to guess is let down as surely as one who was pushed.

A contract-based close hears the same words from a different place. Earlier, Dan agreed the call would end in a clear yes or a clear no, and he named what another year of guessing would cost him. So you ask what he's weighing, find the missing belief, and answer it from his record. If he still wants time, he leaves with a date on a real start. This module is that close, with its routes and the lines that stop it.

## 1. The Contract and the Arc

A close in this category holds when it works as a contract: a structure he agrees to at the start, run in a fixed order, ending in a decision you both named before it began.

### He punishes the move he uncovers

Your buyer has met the free strategy call that turns into a pitch halfway, and the friendly chat that ends in a checkout link. He knows a call about a paid program is a sale. What he holds against you is a move he uncovers, because one hidden move tells him the whole call was staged, and he discounts everything you said before it.

So the structure comes first, out loud: what you'll ask, the straight answer about what can and can't change, one recommendation and a price at the end, and two good endings, a clear yes or a clear no. Laid out, it leaves his guard nothing to find. "No is fine" helps only when it's structurally true: a real free path, money talk that ends at "I can't afford it", and follow-up fixed in advance.

### Each step stands on the one before

Each step needs the one before it. Taken out of order, a step turns into pressure:

| Step | What you do | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Contract | Agree the structure and both endings | A pitch he uncovers |
| 2. Excavate | His months, checks, and conclusion | Selling before a named need |
| 3. Summary | Goal, stake, and gap, confirmed | The wrong goal |
| 4. Recommend | One option, from his reasons | A menu that stalls him |
| 5. Price | Real tiers top first, price once, silence | Apology, discount, decoy |
| 6. Affordability | The question, word for word, about his pick | Selling past his means |
| 7. Ask | "Do you want to start on [date]?" | A good-fit buyer unasked |
| 8. Sort | Up to two probes per real objection | The wrong objection answered |
| 9. Decision Date | His date to decide by, on a real point | "I'll let you know" |
| 10. Day one | Baseline day, his first review, booked | A yes that cools |
| 11. Recap | In writing within 24 hours | A decision from memory |

That's **Close by Contract**, the close that agrees its structure first and runs in order to the ending it named. Run it on every live selling conversation, in 20–30 minutes, or about 45 in your founding months while research rides along.

**Agree the ending first, earn each step, and leave with day one, a clear no, or a date.**

### The floor holds on a live call

The Informed-Client Test, the check every tactic passes in both directions (Intro), applies on every call. Its ceiling asks whether a client anywhere in your range, the low end included, would still say the call served him after seeing what you did. Its floor says that letting a screened, good-fit buyer who can pay talk himself out of the right decision fails him too. The floor applies once he's through the door's checks, and only until a stop rule fires.

Force is a stake in his words, one confident recommendation, an explicit ask, up to two probes per real objection, and a real date. Pressure is asking again after a clear no, reopening money after "I can't afford it", selling into distress, invented urgency, or shame. Embedded commands, false takeaways, and an assumptive close fail the ceiling, since each works only while he can't see it.

## 2. Excavation and the Implication Question

Excavation earns the recommendation: before you name anything you sell, he tells you what he tried, what it cost him, and what he wants, in words you'll hand back.

### He talks first, about what he did

Describe the program before he has named a need and you've handed him something to object to. Reading the buyer and adapting to him is among the best-supported predictors of selling well. Here the reading starts with his months: what he did, for how long, how he checked it, what happened around month 3, and what he concluded. Ask what he did, because last month is a record and next month is a guess.

His answers also set his route and surface the stops. A past purchase that promised structural change marks a burned buyer. Talk about his worth, or regular checking of his face, puts him on the Dignity Route, the way of selling that keeps force off his worth. Jaw pain, loud snoring, or a bite concern means a referral note before anything is sold, and checking many times a day ends the sale.

What he bought is history. It shows how he was sold before, so it stays in your notes and out of the summary, the recommendation, the price, and the recap. A price argued from money already lost is the recovery pitch this category runs on burned men.

### The ladder climbs to his life, then stops

Run the Destination Ladder, two questions that climb from the feature he names to the life behind it and one that grounds it (Module 2). Stop once the answer leaves his face. "A sharper jaw" becomes "I'd stop dodging the camera at work", and the grounding question finds the quarterly team photo.

### He names the stake

Waiting looks free in this niche. Outcomes are slow, so another month seems to cost only a month, and a man can defer for a year without ever deciding to. Stakes move people by small to moderate amounts, mostly when they believe they can act, and framing a cost as a loss instead of a gain changes almost nothing. So the stake has to be accurate and his, since a cost he says out loud is one he can't argue with later:

> **You:** "If the next 12 months look like the last [eight], what does that cost you?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer, however long it takes.]*

That's **the Implication Question**: he names the cost of waiting in his own words, and you reflect it. On the standard route, say back his months, money from here on, and the moments he's missed, plainly. Once he has described the cost, name it directly: "Then another year of guessing costs you another year." Future-pace the record and leave his face out: 12 more months of guessing beside 12 in which he knows what's moving.

**Ask what another year like this one costs, hold the silence, and hand his words back.**

Two routes narrow it. On the Dignity Route the stake holds only time, money from here on, and guessing, with no missed moment said back. A burned buyer hears one forward question, and his answer stands as given.

### The summary is the hinge

End excavation with three things in his words: where he wants to be, what waiting costs him, and what was missing. Ask whether you got it right, and wait. His yes makes everything after it his own argument, and a correction helps, since a recommendation built on a wrong summary is a pitch to someone else.

## 3. Recommendation, Price, and the Spoken Affordability Line

Recommend one thing for the reasons he gave you, show every real tier top first, say the price once, and ask the affordability question about the tier he picks.

### One recommendation, argued from his summary

A menu hands the decision back to a man who came because eight months of guessing couldn't make it, and the recommendation is the judgment he's paying for. So recommend one thing, argued from his summary. It's usually the Program, your 12-week flagship, and otherwise Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables; the Starter Path, the free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now; "don't buy"; or a referral.

Before any price, walk the Path and Timeline Card, the page that shows the whole path and its likely cost before payment (Module 6). Say what 12 weeks deliver, that his log shows within two weeks whether the work is happening, and that jaw change from habits is debated and unpromised.

### Every real tier, top first, and the price once

Show the real tiers, top first, say which you recommend, and state its price once. The premium tier anchors honestly only because it's real, a seat someone buys, while a tier built to make the next look cheap is a decoy this buyer spots at once. Then stop talking. The first words after a price are usually an apology, a justification, or a discount, each a sign you doubt the number, while silence lets him do the math in months of his own pay.

That's **Premium First, Price Once**: the real tiers premium-first, one recommendation, the price once, then silence. The honest anchors are the delivery math he can count and the value of months not wasted, another year of guessing beside a year in which he knows. Asked how much of your time he gets, he hears deliverables with dates, since minutes invite a per-minute price. Surgery, filler, hourly rates, an invented "total value", and what he already spent stay away from the price.

### The spoken affordability line

Then comes the question he'll hear before every payment you take. Once he has named the tier he'd pick, ask it word for word, about that tier:

> **You:** "I ask everyone this before any payment. Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

It follows the tiers because by then he has seen every real option, so a no can't be met with a cheaper one. A yes lets the checkout run the Fit Check, the plain-language check for signs that buying could hurt him (Module 5), before any pay button. A no ends money talk. He gets the Starter Path once, tool-free, and the pause route, the tag that stops sales and date sends for 60–90 days before asking his permission again.

Nothing after a no carries a price, an offer, or a date: no plan, no lower tier, and no Program Async, the tier without the live call. Money that isn't his gets the same free path. What he says aloud sets the same tag a checkout answer would, which silences the abandoned-cart email too.

### Plans and the guarantee in their places

A plan comes only after a yes, stated as one total: $[2,400] in full, or three payments of $[820], $[2,460] in total. Plans at Cost, the rules that keep a plan from becoming a loan, allow only your own installments, at most three, each within about a third of his monthly take-home, and none after a Fit Check signal. The plan is its own paid step, so the question comes again. The Layered Guarantee, your written promises on the work you control, gets one line here and its full weight when he asks what happens if nothing changes (Module 7).

## 4. The Objection Map

Fourteen objections decide most calls in this niche, each points at one missing belief, and each is answered with the evidence for that belief instead of a longer pitch.

### Every objection names a link

A yes rests on six beliefs, Cause, Range, Vehicle, Guide, Self, and Now, and a no usually means one is missing: that's the Belief Chain (Module 14). The cheapest objection is the one you raise first. Give the Honest Answer, your straight public answer to the bone question (Module 3), before he asks, and he never has to defend "adults can't change". The objection map files the niche's deciding objections by link, with the evidence that answers each:

| His words | Link | What answers it | What never does |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Adults can't change anyway." | Range | The Honest Answer, word for word | "Some guys do see jaw changes" |
| "It's all free on YouTube." | Vehicle | Review of his record | Knocking free channels |
| "Why not an orthodontist?" | Range, Guide | Their scope, yours, and a referral on any trigger | Knocking clinicians |
| "I've seen faked before/afters." | Guide | Six questions for any result, yours included | A better before/after |
| "What if nothing changes?" | Guide | The Layered Guarantee's terms | A promise about his face |
| "I need to think about it." | Now, or a hidden link | The sort; a burned buyer gets the kit and his date | A discount, a deadline, a third probe |
| "How much of your time do I get?" | Vehicle | Deliverables with dates | Minutes, hourly rates, "total value" |
| "Who sees my photos?" | Guide | Coach-only captures, a handle, a discreet billing name | "Nobody's looking at you" |
| "How many minutes a day?" | Self | The real time, fitted to his week | A smaller number |
| "I always quit." | Self | His logged week; review that comes to him | "Most people quit too" |
| "How long, and what's the total?" | Range, Guide | The Card's timeline and [9]-month total | A date for his face |
| "Can I stop paying?" | Guide | Refund windows; payments that stop; no fees | "You're locked in" |
| "I need to ask my parents" or "my partner" (an adult) | Eligibility, then Now | Whose money first; a joint decision gets the recap and his date | Pitching the parent or partner |
| "I need to ask my parents" (a minor) | None: a stop rule | The call ends; data deleted; any payment refunded | "Come back when you're 18" |

### The Real-Objection Sort finds the one he has

"I need to think" is this category's all-purpose cover. Under it sits a doubt he hasn't voiced, a decision he's afraid to get wrong, or a polite no, each needing different evidence. A repair aimed at the wrong one reads as pressure. So sort before you answer: one probe to find what he's weighing, one to check the link it points at, then evidence from his record, then back to the recommendation and the ask.

That's **the Real-Objection Sort**: separate the stated objection from the real one, and resolve each he raises with up to two probes. Two is the limit, because a third question stops sorting and starts wearing him down.

**Sort, check, repair with his evidence, and return to the ask.**

A Self doubt often needs the hard true thing, said with force: "You didn't quit because you're weak. You quit because nothing showed you it was working." A burned buyer's "I need to think" is due diligence, so it gets the verification kit, a sample written plan and a sample weekly review, and a date he chooses, with no probe.

### The Pushback Signal changes the route

Sometimes he stops raising doubts and argues for staying put: the busy season, the genetics he's sure of, the free videos he'll get to. That's the signal to stop persuading, because a rebuttal makes him argue harder, and each sentence he spends on the status quo rehearses it. Reflect his case, ask permission, then challenge it with his own evidence:

> **Sam** *(composite, Ambivalent)*: "I'll start after the busy season. It's not going anywhere."
>
> **You:** "Fair. You've got a real season coming. Can I push back on one part?"
>
> **Sam:** "Go on."
>
> **You:** "You said last year's season ended in [October], and nothing started then either. What would be different after this one?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

That's **the Pushback Signal**: when he argues for the status quo, reflect it, ask permission, and challenge it with his own evidence. A concrete answer, like overtime that eats the plan's minutes, moves his decision to the first start after it, and a vague one keeps the next start. What you never say is "there's always a reason to wait", which argues with him where his own words would do it. Either answer ends at the ask.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a buyer clears the door, names his stake, confirms your summary, and says yes to the affordability question. At the ask he needs to think, the sort finds Self, and you spend both probes, repair it with his logged week, and ask again. He says he needs to think. Every fit signal says yes, and his answer says not today.

First rule out a burned buyer you missed, since a past purchase that promised structural change moves him to the kit and his own date. Then ask once whether it's the same doubt or a new one. A new doubt earns its own two probes. The same doubt means the probes are spent, so name what you see, once, and let the calendar hold the decision:

> **You:** "It sounds like you believe the plan and doubt you'll be the one who keeps it. That's worth deciding on a real date. The [March] start's last day to join is [March 13]. What date will you decide by?"

No discount, no third probe, and no date that isn't a real point. The recap and one check-in on his date carry it from there.

## 5. State Routing, the True Thing, and Stop Rules

The arc stays the same for every buyer: his state sets the route through it, the Dignity Route changes where force points, and a stop rule ends it.

### His state picks the route

Buyer States, the four situations a buyer can arrive in, are read in his words at every contact, since a state can shift inside one call. The offer and the arc stay fixed while the route changes:

| State | Lead with | What changes | His date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Struggler | Cause, then Self | His months read back; the true thing aimed at the missing measurement | The next start's last day to join |
| Burned Struggler | Guide | The kit and terms first; one forward stake question; no probes | The start he chooses |
| Optimizer | Vehicle, Guide | Deliverables, turnaround, and privacy before price | The next start, or late entry now |
| Ambivalent | Now | His own reasons, and "what changes then?" | The next start, or the first after a real constraint |
| Insecurity-led, over any state | The Dignity Route | Stakes kept to time, money from here on, and guessing | Unchanged |

That's **State Routing**: one arc and one offer, with the route through them set by his state. A burned buyer hears your confidence as the pitch that sold him before, so his checking gets the kit, one firm recommendation, and a date he picks.

### The Dignity Route, in practice

Some buyers describe themselves where others describe a situation, and to them a reflected missed moment is evidence against their worth. They take the Dignity Route, which changes where force points and never what you recommend (Module 15). Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, a consultant, opened by asking about turnaround and privacy. [Ten] minutes in he said, "I look wrecked on every client call. Is this even worth it for someone like me?"

That's a question about his worth, so the route runs over his Optimizer route. You validate first, keep the stake at time, money from here on, and guessing, and leave the client calls he named out of every reflection, even though they're his goal. The ladder stops at knowing instead of guessing. The rest runs unchanged: tiers top first, a Private seat at $[5,000] for its speed and privacy, the price once, the question, and the ask.

### The hard true thing, said with force

Force belongs here once his months are on the table and he has agreed with your summary. Then say it plainly:

> **You:** "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem, not you."

It lands because it names a waste he already feels, gives it a cause he can change, and lifts the blame off him, which is what lets him hear the plan. Aimed at his face or his worth, the same force is shame. On the Dignity Route it aims at the guessing: "[Six] months of effort, and nothing to show you what it's doing. The guessing is the problem, and it's the part we can end."

### Stop rules, said out loud

Four things end the call, each with one line said word for word, and nothing after a stop line carries a price, an offer, or a date. The three that protect him, money, distress, and a minor, leave only "stopped: stop rule", with the recording deleted unscored.

| What happens | The line | Then |
|---|---|---|
| A clear no | "Understood. That's a clear no, and I won't ask again today. Your recap still comes within 24 hours." | A recap with no ask; its link in your log |
| "I can't afford it", or a no to the question | "Understood, and thanks for saying it straight. Here's the Starter Path, free: logs and self-checks, without the review. The door opens whenever you write." | The pause route; "stopped: stop rule" |
| Distress, fixation, or checking many times a day | "I'm going to stop talking about the program, because what you've just told me matters more. That one's not mine, so let's talk about who to see and what to tell them." | A referral conversation and note, crisis resources if acute, reading-only content, the pause route; "stopped: stop rule" |
| He's a minor | "Thanks for telling me. I work only with adults, so I'm ending the call here and deleting what you've shared, and anything you paid comes back. For questions about a face that's still growing, an orthodontist is the right person." | Everything deleted; nothing kept |

## 6. Close, Decision Date, and Follow-Up

Every call ends in one of three places, day one booked, a clear no, or a Decision Date, and what happens after it is fixed before the call starts.

### Day one is booked before you hang up

How firmly a man commits at the end of a conversation tends to predict what he does next, and "I'll sort it this week" is a weaker start than "Monday at 7". So on a yes, send the checkout while you're still talking, with the Card and the Expectation Document, the fuller written terms of the work, above the pay button. Book baseline day before you hang up. A signal at checkout starts the signal pause, no payment that day, expectations in writing, at least 72 hours to cool off, and no plan, and day one waits.

### The Decision Date sits on a real point

When he needs time, he leaves with the Decision Date, his personal date to decide by. It sits on or before the next real decision point: a start's last day to join, or an announced Price Step, a scheduled rise stated at least 30 days ahead (Module 8). With late entry through each start's second week, a real point is always within about 2–4 weeks. The date rests on honesty and planning, since the evidence that deadlines push people is contested. A credit he has paid for appears once, as a term in his written plan, and never beside his date.

### Follow-up is fixed in advance

The Follow-Up Rule sets everything after the call: a written recap within 24 hours, one check-in on his Decision Date, one close-the-loop message, then regular email only, and nothing while the pause route holds. Fixed in advance, follow-up can't slide into chasing. After a protective stop he gets the Starter Path or the referral note once, and nothing more.

### The Selling-Skill Loop

Your close improves on a loop you can check. Record every call he consents to, and score one recording a week against the call scorecard. Keep an objection log by link, in his words but never his fit-check answers or what he said about his face. It feeds content through the Conversation-to-Content Loop, which moves each answer earlier in his path (Module 18). Trace every refund and dispute to the promise and path used on its call, and change one thing at a time, judged over about 30 held conversations. That's the Selling-Skill Loop: recorded, scored calls and an objection log, fed by every call that no protective stop ended.

>

## Scripts: The Call Outline and the Fourteen Answers

### The call outline

About 20–30 minutes, or 45 in the founding months. Quoted lines are word for word; brackets hold his details.

1. **The contract.**

> **You:** "Here's how this goes, about [30] minutes. I'll ask how you've gone about this so far, then tell you straight what can and can't change for an adult. If something I sell fits, I'll recommend one thing and give you the price. A clear yes and a clear no are both good outcomes, and either way you get a written recap within 24 hours. Can I record this for my notes? It's deleted after [30] days, and no is fine."

2. **Excavate.** "Walk me through the last [eight] months." · "How did you check whether it was working?" · "What happened around month 3?" · "Why now?" · Then the ladder, stopping once his answer leaves his face.

3. **The stake and the summary.**

> **You:** "If the next 12 months look like the last [eight], what does that cost you?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*
>
> **You:** "So waiting costs you [his words]. Let me check I've got it. You want [his destination]. You've put [eight] months in with nothing measured, and what's been missing is a baseline and someone reading your record. Did I get that right?"

4. **The recommendation and the price.** If Range came up, the Honest Answer's spoken version comes first, word for word.

> **You:** "Then I'd recommend [the Program] from [March 2], because [his reasons]. Here's the whole path: [the Card's lines]. There are two real options, top first. Private is $[5,000], with [its deliverables]. The Program is $[2,400], and it's the one I'd recommend for you."
> *[Stop. Say nothing until he speaks.]*

5. **The question about his pick, then the ask.**

> **You:** "I ask everyone this before any payment. Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"
>
> **You:** "Do you want to start on [March 2]?"

Add seat status only when it could change his start: "This start has [8] seats, and [5] are taken."

6. **Sort, date, day one.** "Fair. What would you be weighing?" → the link's check question → his evidence → "Given that, does [the Program from March 2] still fit what you want?" If he needs time: "The [March] start's last day to join is [March 13]. What date will you decide by?" On a yes: "Let's book baseline day now."

7. **A stop, at any step.** Its line from the stop-rule table in section 5, word for word, and nothing after it.

Every line does one job in Close by Contract's order, and the order keeps each line safe. The price follows a summary he confirmed, the question follows the tiers so a no can't meet a cheaper one, and the ask follows the question. The two marked silences are where calls leak, because he has to speak first.

The pushback variant comes right after the price:

> **Dan:** "That's a lot."
>
> **You:** "It's a real amount. Is it that it isn't comfortable, or that you're not sure it's worth it?"

If it isn't comfortable, that's a no, and the affordability stop line follows. If it's the worth, the link is Vehicle: the deliverables and the year he doesn't spend guessing, then the ask again. The line you never say is "If price is the issue, I can do something for you today", a discount that teaches every later buyer to wait.

### The fourteen answers, by link

Each answer returns to the recommendation and the ask, unless a stop line has fired.

1. **"Adults can't change anyway."** *Range.*
> **You:** "There's no good evidence that habits change the shape of an adult's bone, and I don't sell that. Some things are debated, and I'll tell you where the evidence is thin. A lot does change and can be measured: your habits, your body composition, how you carry yourself, your grooming, how you're photographed. If what you want is to be at ease in photos and to stop guessing, that's where the work is. Which of those are you working on, and how would you know?"

2. **"It's all free on YouTube."** *Vehicle.*
> **You:** "Most of it is, and some of it's good. What it can't do is read your week. A record doesn't read itself; review turns it into a decision."

3. **"Why not an orthodontist?"** *Range, Guide.*
> **You:** "If your bite or your jaw's shape is the question, an orthodontist is who to see, and I'll tell you what to ask. My work runs alongside theirs and replaces nothing they do."

4. **"I've seen faked before/afters."** *Guide.*
> **You:** "So have I. Ask six things of any result, mine included: Matched? Dated? What else changed? Which rung? Out of how many? Where are the rest? My face is not evidence for the method. The record is, published on the dates I committed to."

5. **"What if nothing changes?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "By week 6 your record shows what's moving, and by week 12 it can tell you a lever doesn't move for you. Log at least [80%] of days, and if none of your markers, set on baseline day, reaches its threshold, [a fixed share inside 25–50%] of what you paid comes back in cash within 7 days. The first [14–21] days carry a full refund. Done the work and want out at week 6? You get back what you paid for the weeks left. I don't guarantee how your face looks."

6. **"I need to think about it."** *Now, or a hidden link.*
> **You:** "Fair. What would you be weighing?"

A burned buyer gets the kit and his own date instead, as Theo did.

7. **"How much of your time do I get?"** *Vehicle.*
> **You:** "You get 12 written reviews within [48] hours of each check-in, 12 group calls, a written read at week 6, a re-assessment at week 12, and three matched captures. You're paying for judgment on your record, and a year you don't spend guessing."

8. **"Who sees my photos?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Only me. Captures are stored at [where] and deleted when you ask. Join under a handle with your camera off; the charge shows as [a discreet name]; nothing is published without consent you can take back."

9. **"How many minutes a day?"** *Self.*
> **You:** "[Minutes a day] on [habit blocks], about 10 minutes for the weekly check-in, and one [60]-minute call a week. Walk me through a normal weekday, and let's find where they'd go."

10. **"I always quit."** *Self.*
> **You:** "What happened the last time?" *[Let him answer.]* "You didn't quit because you're weak. You quit because nothing showed you it was working. This week you logged [six] of [seven] days with nobody reading them."

11. **"How long, and what's the total?"** *Range, Guide.*
> **You:** "Your log shows within two weeks whether the work is happening, and jaw change from habits is debated, so I don't promise it. Over your first [9] months the likely total runs from $[2,400] for the Program alone to $[3,447] with Round Two, a lighter follow-on block, and a few months of the Hold, our measurement subscription."

12. **"Can I stop paying?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Yes. Leave in the first [14–21] days for a full refund, or at week 6 if you've done the work, and your remaining payments stop. If a payment fails, the program pauses with no fees until you carry on or stop."

13. **"I need to ask my parents," or "my partner."** *Eligibility, then Now.*
> **You:** "Whose money would it be?"

If it's his parents' money: "Then I'd hold off, since I only take money that's comfortable from your own income or savings. Here's the Starter Path, free, and the door opens whenever you write back." If he couldn't pay it himself, that's a no, and the pause route is set. Money shared with a partner gets: "It all goes in your recap so you can decide together. What date works for you?" No probes.

14. **"I'm 17. I'd need to ask my parents."** *A stop rule.* The minor's stop line, deletion, and a refund of anything paid, with no offer and no "later".

> Subject: Your recap from [date]
>

>
> My recommendation: [the Program] from [March 2], because [his reasons]. Price: $[2,400] [or three payments of $[820], $[2,460] in total]. Start: [March 2]. Last day to join: [March 13].
>
> [Day one: baseline day, (date, time).] / [Your Decision Date: (March 9). I'll check in that day.]
>

Leave out any assessment credit, stated once as a term in his written plan, plus what he already spent, his fit-check answers, and anything about his face. On the Dignity Route the stake line holds only time, money from here on, and guessing.

**The check-in, on his Decision Date.**

> "Today's the date you set for [the Program from March 2], and the last day to join is [March 13]. Yes, no, or a question? Any of the three is a good answer."

**The close-the-loop, if he hasn't replied [two days] later.**

> "I'll close this out so it isn't sitting in your inbox. If the timing changes, the next start is [April 6], and its last day to join is [April 17]. Reply any time."

## Checklist: The Call Scorecard

Score one recorded call a week, and your last 30 held conversations monthly. A call that money, distress, or a minor ended is never scored.

1. Did the contract name a clear yes and a clear no as good outcomes before any question about him? → No: open with the contract line.
2. Did he name the stake in his own words and confirm your summary before you recommended anything? → No: ask the Implication Question, hold the silence, and summarize first.
3. Was the price said once, away from anything he'd already spent, with silence until he spoke? → No: cut what you said after the number.
4. Was the affordability question asked word for word, after the tiers, about his pick? → No: fix it before your next call.
5. Did each real objection get two probes at most, then a return to the ask? → No: mark where the third probe or the missing return came.
6. Did he leave with baseline day booked, a plain no, or a date on or before the next last day to join? → No: the floor failed; find where the call stalled.
7. Across your last 30 held conversations, did 15–35% enroll before proof, or 25–45% with it? → Below: replay three no's against items 1–6 before you touch the offer.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Conversations bind, and every call is also research, so the founding call runs about 45 minutes and discloses both jobs before he books (Module 9). Run the full arc every time and score weekly, since your first 30 held conversations teach your close faster than any script. The trap is protecting the research by skipping the ask, which leaves a good-fit adult who helped you learn without a recommendation or a price.

**Growing.** Selling hours bind. Free calls go to uncertain and high-intent buyers under the Call Cap, the weekly limit on free conversations, and the overflow gets a Decision Assessment, your paid, credited written plan, which leads its own call. The trap is letting the credit into the close or the recap, where it turns money he already paid into a deadline.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind and 50–70% of enrollments come without a call, so live calls go to Private and high-uncertainty buyers (Module 20). The trap is carrying the arc's words onto async surfaces without its stop lines.

## Standard Check

- **The question after the tiers.** Asked about his pick once every real tier is shown, the affordability question turns a no into the Starter Path and the pause route, never a cheaper tier: the line on vulnerability, held where a downsell is easiest.
- **Two probes, then a real date.** *Close* at full strength asks every good-fit buyer, probes each real objection twice at most, and ends on day one or a Decision Date on a real point (*Use real dates*). A burned buyer gets the kit and his own date instead.
- **Forward money only.** *Name the stakes* reflects money from here on; what he already spent stays out of the summary, the price, and the recap (*Present the price*).
- **Stop lines, word for word.** Nothing after one carries a price, an offer, or a date, and a stop for money, distress, or a minor leaves only "stopped: stop rule".

## Quick Reference

**In one line.** Agree the ending first, let him name the stake, recommend once, price once, ask, and leave with day one, a clear no, or a Decision Date.

**Takeaways**
- The contract comes first, because a buyer who knows the structure has nothing to uncover.
- The affordability question follows the tiers and asks about his pick, so a no never meets a cheaper tier.
- Every objection names a link: sort it, probe twice at most, repair it with his record, ask again.

**The arc.** Contract → excavate → summary → one recommendation → tiers, price once, silence → the affordability line → ask → sort → Decision Date → day one → recap.

**The objection map.** Range: adults, orthodontist, timeline · Vehicle: free content, your time · Guide: fake proof, no change, privacy, stopping · Self: minutes, quitting · Now: "I need to think" · money first: parents, partner · a minor: stop.
