# 19 · The Sales Conversation
*Part V — Selling and Transformation*

**The shift:** from *"Closing is pressure or luck"* to *"I close by contract, with force aimed at the situation and routes for burned and insecure buyers."*

Near the end of a fit conversation, a 24-year-old who has put about eight months into [his routine], with nothing measured, says the six words every operator in this category hears each week:

> **Dan** *(composite, Struggler)*: "Makes sense. I need to think about it."

Most operators answer in one of two ways. One says "Of course, no pressure," sends nothing, and never hears from him again, so he goes back to guessing until another channel sells him the next gadget. The other cuts the price for today, and a man who has scrolled past the category's countdown discounts hears exactly what he expected. Both lose the sale. The first also fails him, because a good-fit buyer left to guess is let down as surely as one who was pushed.

A contract-based close hears the same words from a different place. Earlier in the call Dan agreed it would end in a clear yes or a clear no, and he named what another year of guessing would cost him. So you ask what he'd be weighing, find the belief that's missing, answer it from his own record, and ask again. If he still wants time, he leaves with a date set on a real start. This module is that close, run in order, with its routes for burned and insecure buyers and the lines that stop it.

## 1. The Contract and the Arc

A close in this category holds when it works as a contract: a structure he agrees to at the start, run in a fixed order, ending in a decision you both named before it began.

### He punishes the move he uncovers

Your buyer has met the free strategy call that turns into a pitch halfway through, and some have met its mirror image, the friendly chat that ends in a checkout link. He knows a call about a paid program is a sale and doesn't hold that against you. What he holds against you is a move he uncovers, because one hidden move tells him the rest of the call was staged, and he discounts everything you said before it.

So the structure comes first, out loud. Announcing that you'll change his mind would raise his guard, while laying out what happens leaves his guard nothing to find. "No is fine" persuades little on its own, and it earns its place only when it's structurally true: a real free path for anyone who shouldn't buy, money talk that ends at "I can't afford it", and follow-up fixed in advance.

> **You:** "Here's how this goes, about [30] minutes. I'll ask how you've gone about this so far, then tell you straight what can and can't change for an adult. If something I sell fits, I'll recommend one thing and give you the price. A clear yes and a clear no are both good outcomes, and either way you get a written recap within 24 hours. Can I record this for my notes? It's deleted after [30] days, and no is fine."

### Each step stands on the one before

A summary needs his words, a recommendation needs a summary he confirmed, and an ask needs to know the money is comfortable. Run the steps out of order and each one turns into pressure:

| Step | What you do | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Contract | Agree the structure, and that a yes or a no both end it well | A pitch he uncovers halfway |
| 2. Excavate | His months, how he checked them, what he concluded | Selling before he has named a need |
| 3. Summary | His goal, the cost he named, what was missing, confirmed | A recommendation aimed at the wrong goal |
| 4. Recommend | One option, argued from his reasons | A menu that stalls him |
| 5. Price | Real tiers top first, the price once, silence | Apology, discount, decoy |
| 6. Affordability | The question word for word, about his pick | Selling past his means |
| 7. Ask | "Do you want to start on [date]?" | A good-fit buyer left unasked |
| 8. Sort | Up to two probes per real objection | Answering an objection he doesn't have |
| 9. Decision Date | On or before the next real decision point | "I'll let you know" |
| 10. Day one | Baseline day, his first review, booked on the call | A yes that cools overnight |
| 11. Recap | In writing within 24 hours, in his words | A decision made from memory |

That's **Close by Contract**, the close that agrees its structure first and then runs in order to the ending the contract named. Said as one line:

**Agree the ending first, earn each step, and leave with day one, a clear no, or a date.**

Run it on every live selling conversation, from the free fit conversation to a call about the premium seat. A fit conversation runs 20–30 minutes, or about 45 in your founding months while research rides along. With prep and the recap it costs about an hour early and about 45 minutes once it's routine.

### The floor holds on a live call

The Informed-Client Test, the check every tactic passes in both directions (Intro), gets its hardest use on a call. Its ceiling asks whether a client anywhere in your range, the low end included, would still say the call served him after seeing exactly what you did. Its floor says that letting a screened, good-fit buyer who can pay talk himself out of the right decision fails him too, because he goes back to guessing. The floor applies once he's through the door's checks and while no stop rule has fired, and a stop rule always wins.

Force is a stake in his words, one confident recommendation, an explicit ask, up to two probes on each real objection, and a real date. Pressure is asking again after a clear no, reopening money after "I can't afford it", selling into distress, invented urgency, or shame. Embedded commands, the false takeaway ("I'm not sure you're ready"), and the assumptive close that books day one before his yes all fail the ceiling, because each works only while he can't see it.

## 2. Excavation and the Implication Question

Excavation earns the recommendation: before you name anything you sell, he tells you what he tried, what it cost him, and what he wants, in words you'll hand back to him.

### He talks first, about what he did

Describe the program before he has named a need and you've handed him something to object to. Reading the buyer and adapting to him is among the best-supported predictors of selling well, and here the reading starts with his months: what he did, for how long, how he checked it, what happened around month 3, and what he concluded. Ask what he did, never what he'd do, because a plan for next month is a loose guide and last month is a record.

His answers do three jobs. They give you the stall pattern your recommendation will argue from. They set his route, since a past purchase that promised structural change marks a burned buyer, and talk about his worth or regular checking of his face puts him on the Dignity Route, the way of selling that keeps force off his worth. And they surface the stops while they're cheap: jaw pain, loud snoring, or a bite concern means a referral note before anything is sold, and checking many times a day ends the sale.

What he bought is history. Write it in your notes, since it shows how he was sold before, and leave it there, out of the summary, the recommendation, the price, and the recap. A price argued from money he has already lost is the recovery pitch this category runs on burned men.

### The ladder climbs to his life, then stops

Run the Destination Ladder, two questions that climb from the feature he names to the life behind it and one that grounds it, stopping once the answer leaves his face (Module 2). "A sharper jaw" becomes "I'd stop dodging the camera at work", and "Where would you notice it first?" gives you the quarterly team photo. Write the top rung in his words, since the recommendation and the recap both argue from it.

### He names the stake

Waiting looks free in this niche. Outcomes are slow, so another month seems to cost only a month, and a man who believes that can defer for a year without ever deciding to. Stakes move people by small to moderate amounts, mostly when they already believe they can act, and framing a cost as a loss instead of a gain changes almost nothing. So the stake has to be accurate, and it has to be his, because a cost he says out loud is one he can't argue with later:

> **You:** "If the next 12 months look like the last [eight], what does that cost you?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer, however long it takes.]*

That's **the Implication Question**: he names the cost of waiting in his own words, and you reflect it. On the standard route, say back his months, money from here on, and the moments he's missed, plainly. Once he has described the cost, name it directly: "Then another year of guessing costs you another year." Future-pace the record and leave his face out of it: 12 more months of guessing beside 12 in which he knows what's moving.

**Ask what another year like this one costs, hold the silence, and hand his words back.**

Two routes narrow it. On the Dignity Route the stake holds only time, money from here on, and guessing, with no missed moment said back. A burned buyer hears one forward question, and his answer stands as given.

### The summary is the hinge

End excavation with three things in his words: where he wants to be, what waiting costs him, and what was missing. Ask whether you got it right, and wait. His yes makes everything after it his own argument. A correction helps you, so fix the summary before you recommend, since a recommendation built on a wrong summary is a pitch to someone else.

## 3. Recommendation, Price, and the Spoken Affordability Line

Recommend one thing for the reasons he gave you, show every real tier top first, say the price once, and ask the affordability question about the tier he picks.

### One recommendation, argued from his summary

A menu feels respectful and works against him. Options mostly stall people when they're complex and the chooser is unsure what he wants, which describes a man eight months into not knowing which lever matters. So recommend one thing and argue it from the summary he just confirmed. It's usually the Program, your 12-week flagship. It can be Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables; the Starter Path, the free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now; "don't buy"; or a referral.

Before any price, walk the Path and Timeline Card, the one page that shows the whole path and its likely cost before payment (Module 6). Say what 12 weeks deliver, that his log shows within two weeks whether the work is happening, that body composition and posture habits show over months, and that jaw change from habits is debated and unpromised. A price heard after that is the price of a path he has already seen.

### Every real tier, top first, and the price once

Show the real tiers, top first, say which one you recommend, and state its price once. The premium tier anchors honestly only because it's real, a seat someone buys with fixed deliverables, and a tier built to make the next one look cheap is a decoy this buyer spots at once. Then stop talking. The first words after a price are usually an apology, a justification, or a discount, and each tells him you doubt the number you just set, while silence lets him do the math in months of his own pay.

That's **Premium First, Price Once**: show the real tiers premium-first, recommend one, state the price once, and hold the silence. The anchors that stay honest are the delivery math he can count and the value of months not wasted, another year of guessing beside a year in which he knows. Surgery and filler, hourly rates, an invented "total value", and what he already spent stay away from the price.

When he asks how much of your time he gets, answer in deliverables with dates: 12 written reviews, each within [48] hours of his check-in, 12 group calls, a written read at week 6, a re-assessment at week 12, and three captures taken the same way each time. Minutes describe your cost and invite a per-minute price. Deliverables describe what he'll hold.

### The spoken affordability line

Then comes the one question he'll hear before every payment you ever take. After the silence, once he has said which tier he'd pick, ask it word for word, about that tier:

> **You:** "I ask everyone this before any payment. Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

It comes after the tiers because by then he has seen every real option, so a no can't be met with a cheaper one. A yes lets the checkout run the Fit Check, the plain-language check for signs that buying could hurt him (Module 5), before any pay button appears. A no ends money talk: he gets the Starter Path once, tool-free, and the pause route, the tag that stops sales and date sends for 60–90 days before asking his permission again.

Nothing after a no carries a price, an offer, or a date: no plan, no lower tier, no Program Async, the cheaper tier without the live call, and no "when things settle down". Money that isn't his gets the same free path. What he says out loud sets the same tag a checkout answer would, so no abandoned-cart email reaches a man who told you no on a call.

### Plans and the guarantee in their places

A plan comes only after a yes, stated as one total: "$[2,400] in full, or three payments of $[820], $[2,460] in total." Plans at Cost, the rules that keep a plan from becoming a loan, allow your own installments only, at most three, each within about a third of his monthly take-home, and none after a Fit Check signal. The plan is its own paid step, so the question comes again, word for word. The Layered Guarantee, your written promises on the work you control, enters the recommendation as one line and gets its full weight when he asks what happens if nothing changes (Module 7).

## 4. The Objection Map

Fourteen objections decide most calls in this niche, each points at one missing belief, and each is answered with the evidence for that belief instead of a longer pitch.

### Every objection names a link

A yes rests on six beliefs, Cause, Range, Vehicle, Guide, Self, and Now, and a no usually means one of them is missing: that's the Belief Chain (Module 14). The cheapest objection to handle is the one you raise first, so give the Honest Answer, your straight public answer to the bone question (Module 3), before he asks, and he never has to defend "adults can't change". A man arguing for his doubt is rehearsing it. The objection map files the niche's deciding objections by link, each with the evidence that answers it:

| His words | Link | What answers it | What never does |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Adults can't change anyway." | Range | The Honest Answer's spoken version, word for word | "Some guys do see jaw changes" |
| "It's all free on YouTube." | Vehicle | Review of his record, which free content can't give | Running down free channels |
| "Why not just see an orthodontist?" | Range, Guide | What they do, what you do, a referral on any trigger | Running clinicians down |
| "I've seen faked before/afters." | Guide | Six questions for any result, yours included | A better before/after |
| "What if nothing changes?" | Guide | The Layered Guarantee, in its own terms | A promise about his face |
| "I need to think about it." | Now, or a hidden link | The sort; for a burned buyer, the kit and his date | A discount, a deadline, a third probe |
| "How much of your time do I get?" | Vehicle | Deliverables with dates, and the months not wasted | Minutes, hourly rates, "total value" |
| "Who sees my photos?" | Guide | Coach-only captures, a handle, camera off, a discreet billing name | "Nobody's looking at you" |
| "How many minutes a day?" | Self | The real time, fitted to his week | A smaller number than the truth |
| "I always quit." | Self | His logged week, and review that comes to him | "Most people quit too" |
| "How long, and what's the total?" | Range, Guide | The Card's timeline and its [9]-month total | A date for his face |
| "Can I stop paying?" | Guide | Refund windows, payments that stop, no fees | "You're committed once you sign" |
| "I need to ask my parents" or "my partner" (an adult) | Eligibility, then Now | Whose money first; a joint decision gets the recap and his own date | Pitching the parent or partner |
| "I need to ask my parents" (a minor) | None: a stop rule | The call ends, his data is deleted, anything paid is refunded | "Come back when you're 18" |

### The Real-Objection Sort finds the one he has

"I need to think" is this category's all-purpose cover. Under it sits a doubt he hasn't voiced, a decision he's afraid to get wrong, or a polite no, and each needs different evidence. Answer the stated version with a longer pitch, a discount, or a deadline and you'll likely miss the link, and a repair aimed at the wrong link reads as pressure. So sort before you answer: one probe to find what he's weighing, one to check the link it points at, then evidence from his record, then back to the recommendation and the ask.

That's **the Real-Objection Sort**: separate the stated objection from the real one, and resolve each he raises with up to two probes. Two is the limit, because a third question stops sorting and starts wearing him down.

**Sort, check, repair with his evidence, and return to the ask.**

A Self doubt often needs the hard true thing, said with force: "You didn't quit because you're weak. You quit because nothing showed you it was working." A burned buyer's "I need to think" is due diligence, so it gets the verification kit, a sample written plan and a sample weekly review, and a date he chooses, with no probe at all.

### The Pushback Signal changes the route

Sometimes he stops raising doubts and starts arguing for staying put: the busy season, the genetics he's sure of, the free videos he'll get to. That's the signal to stop persuading, since a rebuttal makes him argue harder and every sentence he spends on the status quo rehearses it. Reflect his case, ask permission, then challenge it with his own evidence:

> **Sam** *(composite, Ambivalent)*: "I'll start after the busy season. It's not going anywhere."
>
> **You:** "Fair. You've got a real season coming. Can I push back on one part?"
>
> **Sam:** "Go on."
>
> **You:** "You said last year's season ended in [October], and nothing started then either. What would be different after this one?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

That's **the Pushback Signal**: when he argues for the status quo, reflect it, ask permission, and challenge it with his own evidence. A concrete answer, like overtime that eats the plan's minutes, moves his decision to the first start after it, with the free path in between. A vague one keeps the next start, so the destination holds while the route bends.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a buyer clears the door, names his stake, confirms your summary, and says yes to the affordability question. At the ask he needs to think, the sort finds Self, and you spend both probes, repair it with his logged week, and ask again. He says he needs to think. Every fit signal says yes, and his answer says not today.

First rule out a burned buyer you missed, since a past purchase that promised structural change moves him to the kit and his own date. Then ask once whether it's the same doubt or a new one. A new doubt earns its own two probes. The same doubt means the probes are spent, so name what you see, once, and let the calendar hold the decision:

> **You:** "It sounds like you believe the plan and doubt you'll be the one who keeps it. That's worth deciding on a real date. The [March] start's last day to join is [March 13]. What date will you decide by?"

No discount, no third probe, and no date that isn't a real point. The Follow-Up Rule carries it from there.

## 5. State Routing, the True Thing, and Stop Rules

The arc stays the same for every buyer: his state sets the route through it, the Dignity Route changes where force points, and a stop rule ends it.

### His state picks the route

Buyer States, the four situations a buyer can arrive in, are read in his words and read again at every contact, because a state can shift inside one call. The offer and the arc stay fixed, and what changes is which links you lead with, what comes before anything vivid, and whose date it is:

| State | Lead with | What changes in the arc | His date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Struggler | Cause, then Self | His months read back; the true thing lands on the missing measurement | The next start's last day to join |
| Burned Struggler | Guide | The kit and guarantee terms first; one forward stake question; no probes | The start he chooses |
| Optimizer | Vehicle, Guide | Deliverables, turnaround, and privacy before price | The next start, or late entry into the one just begun |
| Ambivalent | Now | His own reasons, and "what changes then?" | The next start, or the first one after a real constraint |
| Insecurity-led, over any state | The Dignity Route | Stakes kept to time, money from here on, and guessing | Unchanged |

That's **State Routing**: one arc and one offer, with the route through them set by his state. A burned buyer, one who already paid for a promise of structural change, hears your confidence as the pitch that sold him before. So you support his checking with the kit and the guarantee terms, make one firm recommendation, and let him choose the date, and his receipts stay in your notes.

### The Dignity Route, in practice

Some buyers describe themselves where others describe a situation, and for them a reflected missed moment becomes evidence against their worth. They take the Dignity Route, which changes where force points and never what you recommend (Module 15). Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, a consultant, opened by asking about turnaround and privacy. [Ten] minutes in he says, "I look wrecked on every client call. Is this even worth it for someone like me?"

That's a question about his worth, so the route runs over his Optimizer route. You validate first: it's a rough way to feel after months with no answer. The stake stays at time, money from here on, and guessing, and the client calls he named never come back in a reflection, even though they're his goal. The ladder stops at knowing instead of guessing. The rest runs unchanged: the tiers top first, a Private seat at $[5,000] recommended for its speed and privacy, the price once, the question word for word, and the ask.

### The hard true thing, said with force

Force belongs here once his months are on the table in his own words and he has agreed with your summary. By then you've earned the right to say it plainly:

> **You:** "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem, not you."

It lands because it's aimed at the situation. It names a waste he already feels, gives it a cause he can change, and lifts the blame off him in the same breath, which is what lets him hear the plan that follows. Aimed at his face or his worth, the same force is shame. On the Dignity Route it keeps its force and aims at the guessing: "[Six] months of effort, and nothing to show you what it's doing. The guessing is the problem, and it's the part we can end."

### Stop rules, said out loud

Four things end the call, and each has one line you say word for word, short enough to say steadily when the moment is hard. Nothing after a stop line carries a price, an offer, or a date. The stops that protect him leave nothing beyond "stopped: stop rule", and a call that ends on one is never scored, because its recording is deleted.

| What happens | The line | Then |
|---|---|---|
| A clear no | "Understood. That's a clear no, and I won't ask again today. Your recap still comes within 24 hours." | A recap with no ask in it |
| "I can't afford it", or a no to the question | "Understood, and thanks for saying it straight. Here's the Starter Path, free: logs and self-checks, without the review. The door opens whenever you write." | The pause route; "stopped: stop rule" |
| Distress, fixation, or checking many times a day | "I'm going to stop talking about the program, because what you've just told me matters more. That one's not mine, so let's talk about who to see and what to tell them." | A referral conversation, a written note, crisis resources if it's acute, the pause route; "stopped: stop rule" |
| He's a minor | "Thanks for telling me. I work only with adults, so I'm ending the call here and deleting what you've shared, and anything you paid comes back. For questions about a face that's still growing, an orthodontist is the right person." | Everything deleted; nothing kept |

## 6. Close, Decision Date, and Follow-Up

Every call ends in one of three places, day one booked, a clear no, or a Decision Date, and what happens after it is fixed before the call starts.

### Day one is booked before you hang up

How firmly a man commits at the end of a conversation tends to predict what he does next, and "I'll sort it this week" is a weaker start than "Monday at 7". So on a yes, send the checkout while you're still talking, with the Card and the Expectation Document, the fuller written terms of the work, ahead of the pay button. The checkout runs the age attestation, the question, and the Fit Check, and then you book baseline day on the call. A signal at checkout starts the signal pause, no payment that day and at least 72 hours to cool off, and day one waits for it.

### The Decision Date sits on a real point

When he needs time, he leaves with the Decision Date, his personal date to decide by, set on or before the next real decision point: a start's last day to join, or an announced Price Step, a scheduled rise stated at least 30 days ahead (Module 8). Late entry runs to the end of a start's second week, so a real point sits within about 2–4 weeks of any call, and you never have to invent one. The date rests on honesty and planning, since a decision with no day to land on drifts to later, and the evidence that deadlines push people is contested. A credit he has paid for is never the reason: it appears once, as a term in his written plan, and never beside his date.

### Follow-up is fixed in advance

The Follow-Up Rule sets everything after the call: a written recap within 24 hours, one check-in on his Decision Date, and one close-the-loop message, then regular email only, and nothing while the pause route holds. Fixing it keeps follow-up from sliding into chasing, and it tells the buyer exactly what a no costs him in messages, which is nothing. After a protective stop he gets the Starter Path or the referral note once, and nothing more.

### The Selling-Skill Loop

Your close improves on a loop you can check. Record every call he consents to, and each week score one recording against the call scorecard. Keep an objection log by link, in his words but never his fit-check answers or anything he said about his face, and let it feed content through the Conversation-to-Content Loop, which moves each answer earlier in his path (Module 18). Trace every refund and dispute to the promise and path used on its call. Change one thing at a time and judge it over about 30 held conversations, since fewer can't separate a better close from a better month.

That's the Selling-Skill Loop: recorded, scored calls and an objection log that sharpen the close one change at a time. A call a stop rule ended is never mined, so it feeds no score, no log, and no content.

## Worked Example: Dan, From Contract to Day One

Cole (composite operator) is in Growing, month [14]. His proof milestone is met, the Program sells at $[2,400], one Private seat is open while his minutes are spare, and the [March 2] start's last day to join is [March 13]. Dan, 24, works full-time in logistics. He booked from his result page after answering yes to the affordability question beside the public price range, and he kept the free week of logging while he waited: [6] of [7] days.

**Minutes 0–12. The read: a Struggler on the standard route.** Cole opens with the contract, word for word, and Dan agrees to it and to the recording. Excavation brings the signs: about eight months of [his routine], checked by bathroom photos "every few weeks, when I remember" and rarely otherwise; two restarts, each dropped around week 3; [about $300] on [an app and a chewing gadget], neither sold as bone change; and a conclusion, "maybe it's just genetics". He describes his situation, never his worth, and nothing he bought promised structure, so the standard route runs, led by Cause and Self. The ladder climbs from "a sharper jaw" to "I'd stop dodging the camera at work and get taken seriously in meetings", grounded in the quarterly team photo. Left alone: any word about the Program, any comment on his face, and the [$300], which goes in the notes as history.

**Minute 13. The stake, in his words.** Cole asks the Implication Question and lets [about ten] seconds of silence run.

> **Dan:** "Another year of ducking that photo. And I'd probably buy whatever the next video tells me to."
>
> **Cole:** "So waiting costs you another year of ducking that photo, and more money on things you can't check."

The stake is lived and forward, and Dan said it, so it's said back plainly. The gadget money stays out, since it's gone and his stake is about the year ahead.

**Minute 15. The summary, the straight answer, the true thing.** Cole summarizes the destination, the stake, and what was missing, a baseline and someone reading his record, and Dan confirms it. Because Dan raised genetics, Cole gives the Honest Answer's spoken version word for word, and Dan answers its closing question himself: body composition and how he's photographed, and nothing he has now would tell him. Then the true thing, with force: "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem, not you."

**Minute 18. One recommendation, the tiers, the price.** Cole recommends the Program from [March 2], because Dan wants to know what's moving and nobody has ever read his record. He walks the Card: reviewed check-ins every week, captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12, a written read at week 6, and a log that shows within two weeks whether the work is happening, with jaw change from habits debated and unpromised. Then the tiers, top first: a Private seat at $[5,000] with [a weekly one-to-one call and replies within a day], and the Program at $[2,400], the one he recommends. Then nothing, for [about eight] seconds, until Dan says, "The Program." Cole asks the affordability question word for word, about the Program, and Dan says yes, from savings. Left alone: "it's only…", any "total value", and the gadget money.

**Minute 21. The ask, and the sort.**

> **Cole:** "Do you want to start on [March 2]?"
>
> **Dan:** "I need to think about it."
>
> **Cole:** "Fair. What would you be weighing?"
>
> **Dan:** "Whether I'll stick with it. I always drop things around week three."
>
> **Cole:** "What happened the last time you started something like this?"
>
> **Dan:** "I couldn't tell if it was doing anything, so I stopped."

The first probe sorted "I need to think" to Self, and the second confirmed it. The repair comes from his own record: "You logged [six] of [seven] days this week with nobody reading them. On the Program someone reads every week, and your week-3 review shows you what the log says before week 3 can decide for you." Then back to the recommendation: "Given that, does starting on [March 2] still fit what you want?" He says yes. Left alone: a third probe, "most people drop out", and any discount.

**Minute 25. Day one.** Cole sends the checkout with the Card and the Expectation Document above the pay button. Dan attests, answers the question again, clears the Fit Check, and pays, and baseline day is booked for [March 2, 7 pm] before they hang up. Next morning the recap carries his goal and stake in his words, the recommendation and its reasons, the price once, the [March 2] start with its last day to join, and baseline day. The [$300] stays out, and there's no credit to leave out, since he came through a free conversation. Cole's time was about an hour all-in. One yes says nothing about his close, which he reads across his last [30] held conversations: [~35%] enrolled, inside the 25–45% planning range with proof.

**The same words from Theo.** Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, sells for a living and bought [a device and a course] that promised structural change, so his door answers set the burned route before he booked. Cole gave him the terms before anything vivid and asked one forward stake question. "Another year of not knowing," Theo said, and Cole took it as given, recommended the Program, and asked. Theo said he needed to think.

## Scripts: The Call Outline and the Fourteen Answers

### The call outline

About 20–30 minutes, or 45 in the founding months. Quoted lines are said word for word; brackets hold his details.

1. **The contract.**

> **You:** "Here's how this goes, about [30] minutes. I'll ask how you've gone about this so far, then tell you straight what can and can't change for an adult. If something I sell fits, I'll recommend one thing and give you the price. A clear yes and a clear no are both good outcomes, and either way you get a written recap within 24 hours. Can I record this for my notes? It's deleted after [30] days, and no is fine."

2. **Excavate.** "Walk me through the last [eight] months: what did you do, and when?" · "How did you check whether it was working?" · "What happened around month 3?" · "What made you look for help now?" · Then "What would that give you?", twice at most, and "Where would you notice it first?"

3. **The stake and the summary.**

> **You:** "If the next 12 months look like the last [eight], what does that cost you?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*
>
> **You:** "So waiting costs you [his words]. Let me check I've got it. You want [his destination]. You've put [eight] months into [his routine] with nothing measured, and what's been missing is a baseline and someone reading your record. Did I get that right?"

4. **Recommendation, the Card, the tiers, the price.** If Range came up, the Honest Answer's spoken version comes first, word for word.

> **You:** "Then I'd recommend [the Program] from [March 2], because [his reasons]. Here's the whole path: [what 12 weeks deliver, when change tends to show, and that every later step is priced on your card before you pay]. There are two real options, top first. Private is $[5,000], with [its deliverables]. The Program is $[2,400], and it's the one I'd recommend for you."
> *[Stop. Say nothing until he speaks.]*

5. **The question, about his pick, then the ask.**

> **You:** "I ask everyone this before any payment. Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"
>
> **You:** "Do you want to start on [March 2]?"

Add seat status only when it could change his start: "This start has [8] seats, and [5] are taken."

6. **Sort, date, day one.** "Fair. What would you be weighing?" → the check question for that link → his evidence → "Given that, does [the Program from March 2] still fit what you want?" If he needs time: "The [March] start's last day to join is [March 13]. What date will you decide by?" On a yes: "Let's book baseline day now: [date, time]. Your recap reaches you within 24 hours."

7. **A stop, at any step.** The lines, word for word:

> **You:** "Understood. That's a clear no, and I won't ask again today. Your recap still comes within 24 hours."
>
> **You:** "Understood, and thanks for saying it straight. Here's the Starter Path, free: logs and self-checks, without the review. The door opens whenever you write."
>
> **You:** "I'm going to stop talking about the program, because what you've just told me matters more. That one's not mine, so let's talk about who to see and what to tell them."
>
> **You:** "Thanks for telling me. I work only with adults, so I'm ending the call here and deleting what you've shared, and anything you paid comes back. For questions about a face that's still growing, an orthodontist is the right person."

Every line does one job in Close by Contract's order, and the order is what keeps each line safe. The price follows a summary he confirmed, so it lands as the price of his plan. The question follows the tiers, so a no can't meet a cheaper one, and the ask follows the question, so you only ask a man who has told you the money is comfortable. The two marked silences are where calls leak, because after the stake and after the price he has to speak first.

The pushback variant comes right after the price:

> **Dan:** "That's a lot."
>
> **You:** "It's a real amount. Is it that it isn't comfortable, or that you're not sure it's worth it?"

If it isn't comfortable, that's a no to the question, and the second stop line follows. If it's the worth, the link is Vehicle: the deliverables, the year he doesn't spend guessing, and the ask again. The line you never say is "If price is the issue, I can do something for you today", a discount that teaches every later buyer to wait. Then return to the recommendation: "Given that, does [the Program from March 2] still fit what you want?"

### The fourteen answers, by link

Each answer ends the same way: back to the recommendation and the ask, or a stop line if one has fired.

1. **"Adults can't change anyway."** *Range.*
> **You:** "You're right to push on that. There's no good evidence that habits change the shape of an adult's bone, and I don't sell that. Some things are debated, and I'll tell you where the evidence is thin. A lot does change and can be measured: your habits, your body composition, how you carry yourself, your grooming, how you're photographed. If what you want is to be at ease in photos and to stop guessing, that's where the work is. Which of those are you working on, and how would you know?"

2. **"It's all free on YouTube."** *Vehicle.*
> **You:** "A lot of it is, and some of it's good. What it can't do is read your week. A record doesn't read itself; review turns it into a decision."

3. **"Why not just see an orthodontist?"** *Range, Guide.*
> **You:** "If your bite or the shape of your jaw is the question, an orthodontist is who to see, and I'll tell you what to ask. My work runs alongside theirs and replaces nothing they do. Jaw pain, bite trouble, or loud snoring goes to them first, and nothing I sell goes ahead of that note."

4. **"I've seen faked before/afters."** *Guide.*
> **You:** "So have I. Ask six things of any result, mine included: Matched? Dated? What else changed? Which rung? Out of how many? Where are the rest? My face is not evidence for the method. The record is, published on the dates I committed to."

5. **"What if nothing changes?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Then you'll know, and you'll stop paying for guessing. By week 6 your record shows what's moving, and by week 12 it can tell you a lever doesn't move for you. If you log at least [80%] of days and none of your markers, the two or three measures we set on baseline day, reaches its threshold by week 12, [a fixed share inside 25–50%] of what you paid comes back in cash within 7 days. The first [14–21] days carry a full refund, and if you've done the work you can stop at week 6 and get back what you paid for the weeks left. What I won't guarantee is how your face looks."

6. **"I need to think about it."** *Now, or a hidden link.*
> **You:** "Fair. What would you be weighing?"

To a burned buyer instead: "Of course. Here's what I'd check: a sample written plan, a sample weekly review, and the guarantee terms. Which start do you want to decide for, and by when?"

7. **"How much of your time do I get for that?"** *Vehicle.*
> **You:** "You get 12 written reviews, each within [48] hours of your check-in, 12 live group calls, a written read at week 6, a re-assessment at week 12, and three captures to one standard. You're paying for judgment on your record every week, and for a year you don't spend guessing."

8. **"Who sees my photos?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Only me. Your captures are stored at [where], never shown in the group, and deleted when you ask. You can join under a handle with your camera off, and the charge shows as [a discreet name]. Nothing is published without your separate consent, and you can take that back."

9. **"How many minutes a day?"** *Self.*
> **You:** "[Minutes a day] on [habit blocks], about 10 minutes for the weekly check-in, and one [60]-minute call a week, for 12 weeks. Walk me through a normal weekday, and let's find where they'd go."

10. **"I always quit."** *Self.*
> **You:** "What happened the last time?" *[Let him answer.]* "You didn't quit because you're weak. You quit because nothing showed you it was working. This week you logged [six] of [seven] days with nobody reading them. On the Program someone reads every week."

11. **"How long, and what's the total?"** *Range, Guide.*
> **You:** "Your log shows within two weeks whether the work is happening. Body composition and posture habits tend to show over months and vary by person, and visible jaw change from habits is debated, so I don't promise it. Over your first [9] months the likely total runs from $[2,400] for the Program alone to $[3,447] with Round Two, a lighter follow-on block, and a few months of the Hold, our measurement subscription. It's all on your card before you pay."

12. **"Can I stop paying?"** *Guide.*
> **You:** "Yes. In the first [14–21] days, one line gets you a full refund. If you've done the work, you can stop at week 6, your remaining payments stop, and what you paid for the weeks left comes back. If a payment fails, the program pauses with no fees and no collections until you pay and carry on, or stop there."

13. **"I need to ask my parents" or "my partner."** *Eligibility, then Now.*
> **You:** "Whose money would it be?"

If it's his parents': "Then I'd hold off, because the question I ask everyone is about your own income or savings. Here's the Starter Path, free, and the door opens whenever you write back." If he adds that he couldn't pay it himself, that's a no, and the pause route is set. If it's shared with a partner: "Makes sense. Everything goes in your recap, price and terms included, so you can go through it together. What date do you want to decide by?" No probes.

14. **"I'm 17. I'd need to ask my parents."** *A stop rule.* No answer, only the minor's stop line, then deletion and a refund of anything paid, with no offer and no "later".

>

On the Dignity Route the stake line holds only time, money from here on, and guessing. After a protective stop there's no recap, only the Starter Path or the referral note.

**The check-in, on his Decision Date.**

> "Today's the date you set for [the Program from March 2], and the last day to join is [March 13]. Yes, no, or a question? Any of the three is a good answer."

**The close-the-loop, if he hasn't replied [two days] later.**

> "I'll close this out so it isn't sitting in your inbox. If you want to pick it up later, reply any time, and we'll start from your recap."

After that, regular email only, and nothing at all while the pause route holds.

## Checklist: The Call Scorecard

Score one recorded call a week, and your last 30 held conversations once a month. A call a stop rule ended is never scored.

1. Did the contract come before any question about him, naming a clear yes and a clear no as good outcomes? → No: open with the contract line, word for word.
2. Did he name the stake in his own words before you mentioned anything you sell? → No: ask the Implication Question and hold the silence.
3. Did he confirm your summary before you recommended? → No: summarize and ask before the next recommendation.
4. Was the price said once, with nothing he'd already spent near it, and silence until he spoke? → No: cut whatever you said after the number.
5. Was the affordability question asked word for word, after the tiers, about his pick? → No: fix it before your next call, because it's a line, not a style.
6. Did each real objection get two probes at most, then a return to the recommendation and the ask? → No: mark where the third probe or the missing return came.
7. Did the call end with day one booked, a clear no, or a Decision Date on or before the next point's last day to join? → No: the floor failed; find the step where it stalled.
8. Across your last 30 held conversations, did 15–35% enroll before proof, or 25–45% with it? → Below: replay three no's against items 1–7 before you touch the offer.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Conversations bind, and every call is also research, so the founding call runs about 45 minutes and discloses both jobs before he books (Module 9). Run the full arc on every call, record with consent, and score weekly, because your first 30 held conversations teach you more about your close than any script. The trap is protecting the research by skipping the ask, which leaves a good-fit adult who helped you learn without a recommendation or a price.

**Growing.** Selling hours bind. Free calls go to uncertain and high-intent buyers under the Call Cap, the weekly limit on free conversations, and the overflow gets a Decision Assessment, your paid, credited written plan, which leads its own call. Run the same arc from the plan's verdict. The trap is letting the assessment credit into the close or the recap, where it becomes a deadline built on money he already paid.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind and 50–70% of enrollments come without a call, so live calls go to Private and to high-uncertainty buyers (Module 20). Keep those calls and their scorecard yourself. The trap is carrying the arc's words onto async surfaces without its stops, when every surface has to end at the same stop lines.

## Standard Check

- **The question after the tiers.** Asking the affordability question about his pick, after every real tier is shown, means a no meets the Starter Path and the pause route, never a cheaper tier: the line on vulnerability, held where a downsell is easiest.
- **Two probes, then a real date.** *Close* runs at full strength: every good-fit buyer is asked, each real objection gets up to two probes, and he leaves with day one or a Decision Date on a real point (*Use real dates*). A burned buyer's "I need to think" gets the kit and his own date instead.
- **Forward money only.** *Name the stakes* reflects money from here on; what he already spent stays in the notes, out of the summary, the price, and the recap (*Present the price*).
- **Stop lines, word for word.** Nothing after one carries a price, an offer, or a date, and the record reads "stopped: stop rule".

## Quick Reference

**In one line.** Agree the ending first, let him name the stake, recommend once, price once, ask, and leave every call with day one, a clear no, or a Decision Date.

**Takeaways**
- The contract comes first because a buyer who knows the structure has nothing left to uncover.
- The affordability question follows the tiers and asks about his pick, so a no meets the free Starter Path and the pause route.
- Every objection names a link: sort it, probe twice at most, repair it with his record, and return to the ask.
- A burned buyer verifies and picks his date; the Dignity Route changes the aim and keeps the recommendation.

**The arc.** Contract → excavate → summary → one recommendation → tiers top first, price once, silence → the affordability line → ask → sort → Decision Date → day one → recap.

**The objection map.** Range: adults can't change, the orthodontist, the timeline. Vehicle: free content, time for money. Guide: faked proof, nothing changes, privacy, stopping payment.
