# 25 · Instagram and X: Replies, DMs and Routes
*Part VI — The Ecosystem*

**The shift:** from *"Every platform is a stage"* to *"Platforms are where engagement starts, and replies, DMs and routes carry it to the door."*

On Thursday night a Reel about month eight lands: same photos, same guessing, no idea what's working. By Saturday your inbox holds [180] messages. [Fourteen] are adults with real questions, like whether it's too late at 26 and what your 12-week Program costs. About [90] are a photo and two words: "rate me". [Six] mention a school timetable or a parent's phone. One, sent at 2 a.m., says he can't stand seeing himself in any photo.

Run that inbox as a stage and every message becomes an audience: a kind rating for each photo, a pitch for the adults, a long reply for everyone, and your weekend gone. Run it as a router and each message already has a destination. The adults get the idea and the door. The photos get no rating. The boys get the education lane, your free content and an orthodontist referral, with nothing kept. The 2 a.m. message gets you, personally, with a referral and no sale.

Instagram and X are where engagement starts, and neither is where it should stay. This module builds the routes to your door, and counts how many platforms your week can carry.

## 1. Roles and the Platform Count Rule

Instagram and X each earn one job here, routing private intent on Instagram and testing your arguments on X, and you run only as many platforms as your editing budget can serve.

### Neither platform is a stage

A stage is judged by its crowd, and both platforms gather small crowds that scatter fast. Median organic engagement per post runs at a fraction of a percent on Instagram, and a smaller fraction still on X. A post fades within days, while a long-form piece keeps working through search for months. Each does something else well: Instagram's reach and intent travel privately, in sends and messages, and X is where adults argue with your claims in public, for free. Instagram reaches about half of US adults and X about a fifth, but reach isn't their job here.

The stage habit costs more in this category than in most. The formats that gather crowds pull the youngest viewers and aim at faces: ratings, comparisons, edited "ideal jaw" posts. In self-selected samples most of the category's keenest audience is under 18, so a crowd like that yields almost no eligible adults, the legal adults who could pay from their own income or savings without new credit. Each platform's job is measured at your door.

### The editing budget sets the count

Every platform charges a native tax, the cost of making a piece belong there: its own first seconds, text, captions, and ask (Module 24). Your editing budget is what you can spend on that tax each week, in your hours and your editor's, once long-form is served. Stretch it and each extra platform gets cross-posted leftovers that underperform. The hours also come out of long-form first, because its cost arrives last: a skipped video shows up a month later as thinner leads.

A hurried adaptation costs twice here. A clip cut away from its caveat is a new claim you never approved. A caption written at 11 p.m. is where "results in 90 days" creeps in, in a category whose skeptics screenshot exactly that line. So the count protects your claims as well as your hours:

**Serve long-form first, then add a platform only while the editing budget still covers its native version.**

That's the **Platform Count Rule**: run only as many platforms as your editing budget serves, and drop one before long-form degrades. It governs X and native Instagram, the posts made only for Instagram: carousels, Stories, a broadcast channel. Reels, your second short-form platform after Shorts, belong to your short-form batch, and the keyword replies that route messages to your door cost reading, not editing.

### Reid adds two platforms and thins the one that mattered

Reid (composite operator) is Early, in month [3], with long-form every other week and [five] native shorts a week cut for Shorts and Reels, each Reel carrying a keyword. Reach feels slow, so he adds a daily X post and [three] carousels a week on the same hours and the same editor. Within [three] weeks the shorts shrink to [three] cross-posted cuts sharing one caption. The carousels eat the design hours, his X posts are mostly links, and his next long-form slips a week. Followers climb on both new surfaces, while eligible leads drift from [about 20] a month toward [about 12], because the pieces adults found him through are the ones that thinned.

The read is a count, with no verdict on either platform. His budget serves long-form, one native short-form batch cut two ways, and the keyword routing. So X and the carousels go in the order the De-Scoping Order, your fixed sequence of cuts for an overloaded week, would take them: X first, then native Instagram, routing kept (Module 11). He keeps X as replies inside his replies line, where it costs no editing. By month [5] long-form is back on cadence and eligible leads are back near [18–22] a month.

### Add and drop by trigger

Read the count monthly at your Operator Review, the standing review of your numbers, from hours logged by line. Add a platform only when reach binds and a logged month shows its native version fits the budget. Then judge it on about 30 eligible leads from its own source tag. Drop one the week long-form slips or a week runs past 25 hours: X first, then native Instagram, never the routing.

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram as a router: Reels, keyword replies, DMs | From month 0, once Reels run as your second short-form platform | A daily DM slot and a keyword per topic |
| X as replies only | Early, while reach binds | Part of about 2 hours a week of replies |
| X as a full lab for your arguments | Growing, with long-form on cadence and room in your replies line | Daily posts and quote-posts from that line |
| Native Instagram beyond Reels | Growing, once keyword yield is proven and the budget serves it | Design hours and a second native tax |

**Default:** Instagram as a router from month 0, and X as replies only until Growing. The router turns Reels you already make into door completions, while a lab needs a log of your calls' objections before it knows what to test. Switch X to the full lab once you're Growing and a logged month shows long-form on cadence with room in your replies line.

## 2. Permission-First Replies

A public reply answers the idea for everyone reading, asks before anything goes private, and routes to the door, because here a comment is a disclosure and an uninvited DM is the closer's opening move.

### A comment is a disclosure with silent readers behind it

Most men working on how they look keep it to themselves, so the one who comments has told everyone reading. Behind him sit many more who read and never write, and your public answer is really to them. It joins the archive a skeptic scrolls before he trusts you, while the same answer sent privately serves one man.

The private side carries the category's history. Men here have been worked by DM closers who answer a comment with "check your inbox" and open with a pitch, so an uninvited message reads as that pattern however kind it is. And the commenter may be 15. A message you start to someone whose age you can't see is contact you never want.

### Answer, ask once, route

A reply runs in three steps. First, answer the idea in full, in public, so it stands even if he never writes again. Holding half back to force a DM is a curiosity device that doesn't pay off where it was raised, which the Payoff Test rules out (Intro). Second, if he's placed himself as an adult, with a job or his age, ask once whether he'd like the free self-assessment by DM. No is a fine answer. Third, a yes gets the door link and nothing else, since the door asks his age before it keeps anything, and a no or silence ends it.

**Answer the idea in public, ask before anything private, and route to the door.**

That's **Permission-First Replies**: answer the idea in public, ask permission before any private message, and route to the door. When you can't tell his age, the public answer and the public door link are the whole reply, because a link is routing and a DM is contact. A minor gets the education lane in public and nothing more (Module 5). A comment that reads as distress gets neither the idea nor the link: a kind line, where to find help, and an open invitation to message you. Replies on other people's threads stay yours, since each is a claim, while routing help, a freelancer who moderates your comments from Growing, can post the templated link under your own pieces.

### Theo asks in public and stays there

> **Theo** *(composite, Burned Struggler)*: "I'm 26 and every coach in this space says 'measured'. The last one I paid measured nothing. Why is yours different?"
>
> **You:** "Fair, and after that you should check rather than trust. What I don't sell: any change to adult bone, because the evidence isn't there. What I do: a written review of each client's logged week, and a dated public log of what I committed to publish and when. Want the sample plan and a sample weekly review by DM? No is a fine answer."
>
> **Theo:** "Post them here."
>
> **You:** "Here: [sample plan] · [sample weekly review] · [the terms]."

His "post them here" was a no to the DM, so the kit went public and nothing private followed. The public answer served every silent reader who shares his doubt, and it repaired Guide, his belief that you're worth trusting with this, with evidence anyone can open.

If he pushes, "So no results, then?", answer from the log: "Not as outcome ranges yet. Those start at 10 graduates, labeled as a small sample, on the date in the log." Never write "DM me and I'll show you results privately", because private results can't be checked and the offer is the closer's opening.

### What replies never do

A reply never closes, never rates, and never comments on features. A price question gets the public price range and one link, and the sale waits for the door. A face gets no rating, even a kind one, since a flattering read invites the hostile one after it. And nobody's features come up, whether his, a bystander's, or a seller's.

With faces out of bounds, all the force goes to practices and business models. Here a man asks under someone else's post whether a [chewing device] will bring his jaw forward:

> **You:** "Fair question, and the appeal makes sense: you want a change you can see, and the device promises one. But there's no good evidence that habits change the shape of an adult's bone, and a device you chew on is a habit with a price tag. Selling it on forward growth sells the one result the evidence doesn't support. Ask any seller, me included, for matched captures and how many people the result came from."

The reply names no seller, touches no face, and ends in a test he can apply to you. That's *Fight ideas, not people* at its sharper setting: a grift claim doesn't get to stand in a thread you've answered. If he answers "I've seen the before-and-afters", ask whether the photos match in light, angle, and distance, and how many people tried it. Never write "anyone selling that is a scammer", which aims at a person and hands the seller's buyers a reason to defend him.

## 3. Instagram as a Private Router

Instagram's value here happens in private, in sends and messages, so you run it as a router that carries intent to the door and keeps minors and faces out of everything it collects.

### Intent arrives by send and by message

The man you most want rarely comments. He watches a Reel about month eight, sends it to a friend with "this is you", and writes to you late at night if he writes at all. On a platform where private sends carry pieces to non-followers, intent collects in the inbox. So does the highest share of minors in your business, since teens use Instagram heavily and "rate me" culture lives there. The router has two jobs: carry the adult to the door fast, and keep the boy from giving you anything to keep.

Recognition travels by send, so the Reels worth making name a situation he's in and never judge a face. Map the surfaces onto the Warmth Ladder, the rule that you never ask for a rung a buyer isn't standing on. A Reel sits on the Stranger rung, and a keyword message is a stranger asking for the door. A message from an adult your door has verified sits on the Deciding rung (Module 18).

### A keyword by DM returns one link

The Reel pays off in full, then ends with one line: send me a keyword by DM for the free self-assessment. His message opens a conversation he started, and the reply returns one link, to your door. The door's first question is whether he's a legal adult where he lives. Nothing is asked in the thread, nothing is sold there, and nothing he types goes into a list. His result goes to his email, and your personal reply follows within hours.

The keyword goes by DM, never as a public comment. A comment keyword marks him in front of everyone as a man working on his face, the disclosure he avoids. It puts minors' self-identification on display, and it fills your comments with one word that reads as bait. The word also sorts who sends it: [RECORD] or [LOG] draws men who want to measure, while [JAW] invites a rating of the jaw.

**A keyword by DM, one link back, and the door does the rest.**

That's the **Keyword Route**: a keyword sent by DM, never as a public comment, that returns a link to your self-assessment. Use one keyword per topic, each returning a tagged link, so your door counts eligible adults by the Reel that sent them. The keyword buys the next step, never the answer, and the Reel carries no price, date, or offer.

### Automation sends the link and nothing else

Platforms let an automated reply answer only a conversation he started, and only for a short window, while a person can reply later. That suits routing and nothing more:

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| You send each link by hand in your daily DM slot | Your slot clears keyword messages the same day | Hours of waiting while his interest cools |
| A labeled automatic reply that sends only the link | From your first keyword Reel | One fixed line, a keyword nobody types by accident, and reading every thread anyway |
| A scripted DM flow that asks, qualifies, or offers | Never | It collects answers outside the door, can't see a minor or distress, and speaks as you |

**Default:** the labeled automatic reply, because the link is routing, the same link your profile carries, and the door asks his age before it keeps anything. Its one line reads: "Automatic reply: here's the free self-assessment, [link]. I read every message myself." Labeled, it reads as a signpost, and your first real contact is the personal reply after his result. Run it on the platform's own keyword setting or a tool that keeps no contact list, since a list of everyone who wrote would hold minors' details. Switch to sending by hand if your setup can't label the reply or insists on the list.

### Carousels and a broadcast channel are optional, and one-way

A carousel can work as a decision tool men save and send, like six things to check before buying another device. Write its caption and alt text as the question an adult types, since search reads captions. It shows no face and ends with the keyword.

A broadcast channel runs on one-way broadcast: broadcast channels announce; they never argue, close, or collect data. Announce a new long-form piece, a dated log entry, or an offer piece with its public price. Never a checkout link, and never a start date, because the channel can't skip a man on the pause route, the tag that holds sales sends and dates back for 60–90 days after a stop rule. Keep member replies off and run no polls or prompts, though the platform offers all three, including prompts that collect photos. Replies would make it a free community under another name, and a photo prompt is a queue of faces with your name on it.

### Minors-exposure controls sit where his message lands

Instagram's teen protections limit who can message a teen first and what he's shown. None of them stops a 16-year-old from sending your keyword, because the message is his. So the controls are yours, and they route rather than engage:

- **The Reel.** Packaged for adults, with an adult situation up front and a calm cover, never a rating, a comparison, or an edited jaw. Those are the Age-Up Dial's choices, the packaging that lowers minors' share (Module 23).
- **The comments.** No keyword asks. Rating requests and minors' personal details get hidden under your comment policy, with no reply.
- **The inbox.** The education-lane line for any message that reveals a minor, with no door link and nothing kept, and never a conversation you start. Keep an anonymous count of minors' messages by Reel.

The count is the control you steer by. When one Reel's messages skew young, the fix goes into the next Reel's packaging, since the reply was already right.

## 4. DM Templates by Case

Every DM is read by you in one daily slot and answered from the template for its case, in an order that puts his safety before his age, his age before his request, and his request before any sale.

### You work the inbox, in one slot

DMs are where minors disclose, distress arrives, and buyers ask about price, so they stay on your Keep-List, the work that never leaves your hands. Routing help never works the inbox, and no tool does beyond the Keyword Route's one line. The Unpriced-Minute Rule, every per-person minute priced, batched, or templated, sets the rest (Module 11): one slot a day, a template per case, and distress answered the day it arrives.

The four templates are written once, approved once, and fitted to each man with a line in his own words. They answer X's messages too, in the same slot. When a verified adult's question is the last thing between him and a decision, answer by voice note: about 1–3 minutes, recorded by you, one question, at most one recommendation.

### Four cases, checked in order

| Order | The case | What he gets | What never happens | What's kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distress: "I can't stand any photo of myself" | A referral to a doctor or mental-health professional, crisis resources if it's acute, and an offer to read what he sends | A sale, a door link, an offer, a date | Nothing new; if he's in your records, the pause route and "stopped: stop rule" |
| 2 | A minor: his age, his school, a parent's say-so | The education-lane line: adults only, the videos free, an orthodontist for a growing face | A door link, "later", "first", or any follow-up | Nothing; if he's in your records, deleted, and anything paid refunded |
| 3 | "Rate me", with or without a photo | No rating, the idea behind the question, and the door link | A word about his photo or any feature, kind or not | Nothing; the photo is never saved or forwarded |
| 4 | An adult with a question | The idea in a line or two, then the door; once he's verified, one recommendation and a checkout link | A close before verification; Private without a call | Only what the door records |

The order matters because messages mix cases. A man who wants a rating and hates every photo of himself is case 1 first, since help comes before any redirect. A 16-year-old asking the price is case 2 and gets no price. A side profile attached to "is this worth it at 27?" is case 3 before case 4, so his question gets an answer and his photo doesn't. A protective stop, a stop rule that protects him, leaves only "stopped: stop rule" on record. Nothing from the thread feeds your objection log or your content plan.

### The DM Handoff decides what an adult can buy here

Unverified contacts go to the door, a verified adult may get one recommendation and a checkout link, and Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables, needs a call first (Module 20). He's verified once he confirms through a link sent to the address his result went to, and your records show a completed door with no pause tag.

> **Sam** *(composite, Ambivalent)*: "Did the assessment and confirmed the email. When's the next start? Probably after the busy season though."
>
> **You:** "The next Program start is Monday [date], and you can join through [date]. It's $[public price] for 12 weeks, with a written review of your week within [turnaround]. What changes for you after the busy season?"
>
> **Sam:** "Honestly? Nothing. There's always another one."
>
> **You:** "Then waiting costs you another season of guessing. My recommendation is the [Month] start: [checkout link]. It asks a few short questions before payment. If you want a day to decide, pick it now, before the last day to join, and I'll check in that morning."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

That's *Sell directly* at full strength: one recommendation, the price once, and a checkout that shows the tiers premium first. It then asks "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?" about his pick, before any pay button. The one question repaired Now, his belief that this is the time to act, with a stake he named himself.

If he asks "Can you hold this price till spring?", keep it plain: "Same price for everyone in [Month]. If [Month] doesn't fit, [Month + 1] starts [date], and it's just as real. Which will you decide by?" Never send "Spots are going fast, lock yours in tonight": hype about seats crosses the line on fake scarcity even in a week they're filling.

If he writes "I can't afford it", the stop rule ends it, and he gets the Starter Path, your free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now: "Understood, and thanks for saying it straight. Here's the Starter Path, free: [link]. The door opens whenever you write." Set the pause route on his record. Nothing after it carries a price, offer, or date.

## 5. X as an Argument Lab

X is where adults argue about ideas in public, so you use it to test the arguments behind your claims, move the ones that hold to long-form, and capture the adults who engage through your door.

### A peer network rewards replies and punishes contempt

X has published how its feed ranks posts, and the principle holds even as the weights shift with experiments. Predicted replies and shares count heavily, and conversation between accounts that follow each other gets extra lift. A predicted report, mute, or block counts against a post far more than a like counts for it, and posts fade within days. So a post earns reach by starting an argument peers want to join, and loses it by making people want to leave.

X's users skew adult and male, and far fewer teens use it than use Instagram, so a claim argued there meets men who could one day buy. But appearance talk on X often runs ironic or hostile, with rating threads and mockery as house style in some corners, and those are the replies that draw mutes. The ranking and the House Standard agree: argue the idea hard and leave people out of it.

### Test the argument, never the claim's words

Your Canon, the 5–7 calibrated claims you repeat in the same words on every surface, changes only when the evidence does, as a dated correction in your public log (Module 14). So X never tests rewordings for likes. It tests the argument around a claim: which reason, example, or framing survives the strongest objection an adult can raise. Post the claim verbatim, name the strongest objection from your calls or replies, give your answer, and let the replies argue.

| Outcome | What it looks like | What you do next |
|---|---|---|
| Held | The objector concedes, narrows his point, or runs out of evidence, and people who follow you back argue it forward | Run it on another thread; once it holds in more than one week, move it to long-form |
| Lost | A counter you can't answer at the evidence's tier | A better answer next week, or a dated correction if the evidence moved |
| No contest | Replies cheer and nobody objects | Nothing learned: take the objection from your calls and post it yourself |

A loss is the lab working. Either next week's answer is better, or the claim gets a dated public correction, which a skeptic reads as a standard nobody fakes for long. So the log keeps every outcome, losses included:

**Quote the claim verbatim, argue it against the best objection, log the outcome, and move what holds to long-form.**

That's the **Argument Lab**: X as the place to test canon claims and framings in public argument, captured to email. Start where your calls point, with the objection your last 30 held conversations raised most, then work the Canon's rotation. Replies on other people's threads cost least and meet the objection where it already lives. A quote-post brings a peer's argument to your followers, and a post of your own starts the thread nobody else has. The log has five columns: the claim, the objection, the argument, the outcome, and that week's door completions tagged X.

### What holds moves to long-form, and captures go through the door

An argument has won when it holds against the strongest objection on more than one thread, in more than one week, with people arguing back rather than cheering. Then it moves to long-form, as an objection piece or a segment of your next decision-stage video, because long-form works for months while a thread fades in days. The argument you like best and the one that holds often differ, which is why you run a lab.

X keeps nothing for you: the follow graph is the platform's, and posts fade. The capture that lasts is an adult's email, and your list has one entrance, the door, age fork first. So each post stands on its own, with the door link in the first reply or your profile. Post under your own name with [Brand] in the bio, since X ranks conversation between people.

### Tone runs under the House Standard

The lab licenses force, all of it aimed at ideas. Go after device grift, manufactured before-and-afters, the endless-tips model that sells the stall back to its buyers, and the "it's all genetics" shrug, pointedly, after steelmanning each. When a thread asks what qualifies you, post your Qualifications Answer in the same words every time: the fact, the scope, the check, the referral (Module 15).

The limits are about people. Quote-post an argument made in public by someone arguing in public, and answer it. Crop a seller's claim to its words, with the account and any face out. Never quote-post a private person's worry, and stay out of rating threads. Clinicians get your concessions on their ground, and your referrals. Spaces and co-hosted threads stay out with podcasts and collaborations, since live talk can't pass your claims approval or hold a stop rule while it runs.

**When the signals disagree.** A large account quote-posts your Honest Answer, your standing answer to the bone question, with "coach admits nothing works lol". By evening [several hundred] replies have piled on, some rating your face. Reach says ride it: reply to everyone, quote him back, post a thread while the eyes are there. The lab says a joke isn't an objection, so there's nothing to test. Your door says [five] completions tagged X, [one] of them eligible.

Go by the door and the Standard. Answer the idea once, in a post of your own, with the claim verbatim beside the strongest honest version of the objection: "if bone won't change, what's left to coach?" Leave the account and the joke out. Hide replies that rate faces, yours included, and meet appearance attacks with the first line of your face statement, the pinned note that your face is evidence neither way. If a real counter-argument surfaces in the noise, answer it on its own thread and log it. Judge the week by tagged completions, never follows.

## 6. Measuring Each Platform

Each platform is judged by the number its job produces, eligible adults through the Keyword Route on Instagram and door completions plus argument outcomes on X, read on fixed denominators and dropped by a rule set in advance.

### Instagram is judged by eligible adults per keyword

Instagram's number is eligible-adult yield, eligible leads per unit of reach by source, counted through each keyword's tagged link. Read it per 1,000 engaged Reel views and per keyword message. Beside it sit two anonymous counts per Reel, minors' messages and rating requests, which steer packaging. Likes and follows stay off the scoreboard, since at a fraction of a percent per post they measure almost nothing.

One ratio in between shows where a Reel leaks: keyword messages to door completions. Many messages and few completions mean the Reel drew people the door turns away, usually minors or men who wanted a rating, so its packaging is what changes.

Until your own ratios exist, plan with the ledger's. Short-form views yield about 0.1–1 lead per 1,000, and about 30–70% of raw leads are eligible, the low end when packaging pulls young. Replace each with your own ratio after about 30 events, because a few messages can make any Reel look like a winner.

### X is judged by captures and by arguments moved

X's numbers are its tagged door completions, the email captures, and its argument outcomes: arguments tested, held, and moved to long-form each month. Tagged completions arrive slowly, so read them quarterly and let the outcomes carry the verdict until about 30 have come in. The lab's real product shows up in long-form. Set each piece built on a held argument beside your other long-form, per 1,000 engaged views, and the lab earns its hours while those pieces yield at least as well as the rest.

| Platform | Its number | Denominator | Read it at | Drop it when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram routing | Eligible leads through keyword links | Per 1,000 engaged Reel views; per keyword message | ~30 eligible leads from its tag | Never: routing stays at every stage |
| Native Instagram | Eligible leads through its tagged links | Per post | ~30 eligible leads | The second cut of a week past 25 h, or yield trailing your Reels' |
| X | Tagged door completions; arguments held and moved | Per month | The quarterly Operator Review | The first cut of a week past 25 h, or a quarter with no argument moved to long-form |

### The drop rule is set before the numbers arrive

Keep each denominator fixed and named in the metric, the habit Denominator Discipline makes a rule, since platforms redefine what counts as a view (Module 12). Drop X first, at a week past 25 hours or at a quarter's review with nothing moved to long-form, then native Instagram. Keep the Keyword Route and your DM slot through every cut, because routing costs minutes rather than editing, and it's where adults arrive. A dropped platform keeps one pinned line pointing to your long-form and door. It returns only on its trigger: reach binds, and the budget serves it natively.

## Worked Example: Cole's Objection, from an X Thread to a Keyword Reel

Cole (composite operator) is Growing in month [11], with the Program at $[2.4k], X running as a full lab, and his Reels routing through the Keyword Route. The last 30 held conversations in his objection log show one doubt more than any other: "Why pay when it's free on YouTube?" It's a Vehicle objection, a doubt that a paid program is the way to get there.

**Week 1. The claim, verbatim.** He posts the Canon's fourth claim word for word, "A record doesn't read itself; review turns it into a decision", beside the objection and his usual answer: free videos give you the method but can't read your record. [Forty] replies agree. Then a strength coach with a larger following objects that most people just need consistency, and that paid review is a crutch. That's the strongest version, so Cole answers with a second argument. Consistency is the half a man can do alone; review is the other half, noticing when eight consistent weeks are aimed at a lever that doesn't move for him.

The coach narrows to "fair, for people already consistent for months". Log: the first argument, no contest; the second, held. [Three] door completions come in tagged X.

**Weeks 2–3. The second argument, twice more.** He uses it in Permission-First Replies on [two] threads where adults ask whether coaching is worth paying for, and it holds on both. A larger account invites him to co-host a Space on "mewing myths", and he declines, since live audio can't pass his claims approval. Tagged completions for the fortnight: [five], [three] of them eligible.

**Week 4. Long-form.** The held argument becomes "Why pay when it's free on YouTube?", an objection piece with a pitch segment and the Program's public price. It opens on the coach's objection at full strength, credited to no one.

**Weeks 5–6. The Reel and the route.** His editor cuts a Reel from the piece's strongest minute, eight consistent weeks and still guessing. It pays off in full, then asks for [RECORD] by DM. Over [two] weeks the automatic reply sends [150] links. [Twelve] threads carry more than the keyword, and Cole reads each in his [8 p.m.] slot:

- [Five] adults ask questions and get the idea and the door link.
- [Three] "rate me" messages with photos get the rating template. One pushes for a number, gets one line, and hears nothing more.

## Templates: The Keyword Route and DM Replies by Case

**The Keyword Route, set up once.**

```
On the Reel:      "Send me [RECORD] by DM for the free self-assessment." Never "comment".
The keyword:      One per topic, about the record ([RECORD], [LOG]); never a feature.
Automatic reply:  "Automatic reply: here's the free self-assessment, [tagged link].
                   I read every message myself."
Runs on:          The platform's own keyword setting, or a tool that keeps no contact list.
Never in it:      A question, a price, a date, an offer, a follow-up.
```

**DM replies by case.** Open with his words where you can, and fit each reply to him with one line.

*Case 1, distress.*

> **You:** "Thank you for telling me. That sounds really hard, and it matters more than anything I sell, so I won't sell you anything. Please talk to [a doctor] or [a mental-health service near you], and if you ever feel unsafe, [a crisis line] is there right now. If you want to write more, I'll read it myself."

It offers a referral conversation and nothing else. If he's in your records, set the pause route and log "stopped: stop rule". Never send "The program could really help with that", which sells into distress.

*Case 2, a minor.* Jordan *(composite, minor)*, 16, writes "rate me, im 16, be honest":

> **You:** "Thanks for asking. What I offer is only for adults, so I can't work with you, and I won't keep any of your details. The videos are free to watch. For questions about a growing face, an orthodontist is the right person to ask."

His age decides the case, so the rating request gets no answer at all. Delete the conversation on your side and add one to the anonymous count. Never send "Come back when you're 18", which markets adulthood to a minor.

*Case 3, "rate me".*

> **You:** "I don't rate faces, yours or anyone's, because a rating can't tell you what to do next. Most of how people read you was never about your jaw. If you want to know what's worth your effort, the free self-assessment starts there: [link]. It never asks for a photo."

The Canon's claim moves his question from his face to how he comes across. If he pushes for "just a number", reply once: "No numbers, for anyone. The self-assessment's there if you want the other kind of answer." Then stop. Never send "Honestly, you look fine", because a kind rating is still a rating.

*Case 4, an adult, not yet verified.* Maya *(composite, welcome, not targeted)*, 28, asks: "Is this only for guys? And roughly what does it cost?"

> **You:** "It's for any adult, and it runs the same way for everyone. The Program is $[public price] for 12 weeks: a written review of your week within [turnaround], a weekly group call with your camera optional, and written reads at weeks 6 and 12. It isn't for anyone who wants a promise about bone. Everything starts with the free self-assessment: [link]. I reply to every result myself."

That's *Sell directly* before verification: the offer, who it's for and isn't, the price, and one step, with the sale left to the door. Once she's verified, she can get the DM Handoff. Never send "Let's hop on a call and I'll walk you through pricing", which hides a public price behind an hour.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Reach binds, and so do your editing hours. Instagram runs as a router on the Reels you already cut, and X stays replies only, first to go when a week runs long. The default is a public answer on every thread your 2 hours of replies can reach, since Permission-First Replies start conversations while your pieces are few. The trap is posting natively everywhere because reach feels slow, which starves the long-form your first skeptics check.

**Growing.** Conversion and selling minutes bind, and your calls have finally filled an objection log worth arguing from. The default is the full Argument Lab, feeding the arguments that hold into objection pieces, while routing help takes comment moderation off your replies line. The trap is letting that helper, or a tool, into the inbox because DMs pile up.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind, and routing help moderates while every DM stays yours, templated by case. The default is to keep only the platforms whose arguments or keyword yield still feed long-form and the door. The trap is a DM flow that qualifies buyers for you, which puts a script where minors and distress arrive.

## Standard Check

- **Replies that never rate.** Permission-First Replies and every DM answer the idea, never a face, a photo, or a feature (the line on shame). On X the force goes to device grift, manufactured proof, and endless tips, and quote-posts carry arguments, never faces or private people (*Fight ideas, not people*).
- **Minors routed, nothing kept.** Keywords arrive by DM only, the automatic reply carries only the door link, and a minor's message gets the education-lane line with no door link, no "later", and nothing stored (the line on vulnerability).
- **DMs sell after the door.** Unverified contacts get the door; a verified adult gets one recommendation and a checkout link whose checks run before the pay button (*Sell directly*).
- **One-way means one-way.** The broadcast channel carries no start date, since only sends that skip paused leads may carry one (*Use real dates*), and no replies, polls, or prompts.
