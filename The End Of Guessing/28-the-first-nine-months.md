# 28 · The First Nine Months
*Part VII — The Build*

**The shift:** from *"I know what to do, but not in what order"* to *"I run a month-by-month build: one number that matters each month, a gate at its end, and my reach band checked at months 3, 6, and 9."*

Month zero, Monday morning. Your notes from 27 modules run to pages. Your calendar is empty, and your channel has no subscribers. The pull is to start filming for the audience you'll need, because content feels like the first brick. In this category it's the slowest one. Rating and comparison formats reach under-18 viewers first, a new channel's long-form pays off over months, and no adult can buy from a page that doesn't exist.

So this week you build the door and put a founding page live with its prices. You publish your straight answer to the bone question. You send one disclosed message to every adult you know. By Friday a stranger can learn what you sell, what it costs, and whether it's for him. A first conversation can sit on your calendar, and month 3 will be decided by conversations.

What your notes lack is a sequence. It runs in three phases, with one number that matters each month and a gate at each month's end. Your reach gets checked at months 3, 6, and 9, so every month has a job you can finish and a test you can fail.

## 1. How to Use the Build

Month zero offers more good moves than a 20-hour week can hold, and in this category the tempting ones arrive early. Rating content wins views fastest while it pulls the youngest viewers. A Scaling operator's tools look like shortcuts to a man with an empty calendar: paid assessments, templated review, ads. Each is a right move in the wrong month, and it spends the hours the right move needed.

The build orders each move by when its inputs arrive, and the Program's 12 weeks and your monthly starts set those dates. The Rung Activation Schedule times the offers: a rung switches on once the rung before it can feed it (Module 4). Each lever's own trigger times the rest. Here is that timing in Band B, the steady one of the four Reach Bands, which plan how many eligible adults a month your channel reaches (Module 1):

| Lever | What it needs first | When, in Band B |
|---|---|---|
| A Price Step, a small scheduled raise | A full start, and the close rate once 30 held exist | Month 4 |
| Round Two and the Hold, the back end | A graduate, 12 weeks after your first client | Month 4 |
| Paid reach | A piece that finds adults, and pages that pass the claims rules | Months 3–4, if week 12 finds under ~15 held |
| Templated review | ~20 clients reviewed, and turnaround creeping | Month 11 |
| The Call Cap, a weekly limit on free calls | Most weeks above ~6–8 held | Beyond month 9 |
| The proof band, $2.4–3.2k | 10 graduates with consented testimonials, and first ranges | Year 2 |

So the calendar sets three phases. Found, months 0–3, builds the door and wins the first clients by hand. Prove, months 3–6, shows whether the price, the product, and your reach hold. Grow, months 6–9, spends what that proof unlocked. Each phase closes on Band Waypoints, what months 3, 6, and 9 should look like in each band.

**Found, Prove, Grow: one number a month, a gate at its end, and your band read at months 3, 6, and 9.**

That's the **Nine-Month Build**. Its months follow your calendar rather than your mood, so a slow week can't talk you into the wrong lever. Every band finishes it with a real, measured business and a clear next decision. At the low end of Bands A–B, though, month 9's profit still sits near zero.

The gates are **Monthly Gates**, the pass-or-fix condition you read from your records on each month's last day. Each is written before its month starts, threshold included. That way a hot week can't pass it and a thin one can't fail it.

When a gate fails, the calendar keeps its dates and next month's list changes. Starts open on schedule, the founding price ends as stated, and announced steps land, because a date that moves teaches buyers to wait. The gate's fix goes first on the list. Every move that depends on the failed condition waits for it to pass. Run all of it at the monthly Operator Review, your fixed look at the numbers that ends in one decision (Module 12).

## 2. Found: The First 30 Days

Your first 30 days build one door and start conversations by hand. Month 3 is decided by conversations in every band, and you have no audience yet to start them.

### Week 1 puts the door, the offer, and the net live before anything links to them

Door v0, the first working version of your door, goes live before any piece points at it (Module 5). It opens on the age fork, "legal adult where you live". Anyone younger gets the education lane: public content and an orthodontist referral, with nothing kept. The Fit Check, a plain-language check for signs that buying could hurt him, runs at every checkout, for the Private seat, the founding group, and the priority tier. Each booking page and checkout asks the affordability question: "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

Wire in the pause route the same day. It's a content-free tag that holds every marketing send and blocks checkout for 60–90 days, then asks permission once. Beside it goes the Starter Path, the free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now (Module 4), so every no leaves with a next step. The first people any link reaches include minors, men whose distress about their appearance is the real story, and men who could pay only on credit. A door built after the first link has already sold to some of them blind. So Stop Rules, the fixed points where selling ends, bind your automation from its first send.

The founding page goes live the same week, because your first messages and replies need a page with a price and one next step. It says who the program is for and who it isn't for. It lists the founding Private seat first, a premium seat with fixed deliverables. The founding group comes next, one standing group for every early client, at a founding price with its end and the next price beside it. The priority tier, the faster paid written plan, comes last.

Your first video is the Honest Answer, your straight public answer to the bone question. Beside it goes the pre-commitment that opens the Dated Record, your dated public log that no rival can back-fill. Write it now, while you have nothing to protect.

### The terms exist before the first checkout

Every term that can decide a refund goes in writing before anyone pays. A term written after the first sale reads to a burned buyer as a term written for that sale. So the Layered Guarantee is on the page from day one (Module 7). Its fit window gives a full refund in the first 14–21 days, and its service guarantee covers your review. The week-6 exit right refunds a client who did the work pro rata. The non-response clause pays part in cash at week 12 when he did the work and no marker reached its threshold.

The Expectation Document, the fuller written terms of the work, goes out before payment. So does the Path and Timeline Card, the whole path and its likely cost on one page (Module 6), with Round Two and the Hold already priced on it. By the end of month 1, two pages join the door. The offer page shows the tiers premium-first, and the Verify Page lets a skeptic check what you claim, charge, and refuse (Module 27). Every line on it can be true before your first graduate.

### Weeks 1–4 start conversations by hand

Your Design Week puts every hour on a line sized to its stage (Module 11). Its Early column protects about 8.5 hours of content. The roughly 7.5 hours no client has claimed yet, for conversations, review, and Private, go to starting conversations. A new channel's long-form pays over months, while a disclosed message can book a call this week.

The Founding Sprint gives those hours their routes: labeled sources for first conversations without an audience (Module 9). The warm-network message goes out once, and Permission-First Replies answer in public before any DM. Native shorts run on Shorts and Reels, and every adult who finishes the door hears from you within hours. Held conversations are counted weekly by booking source, one of your five Stage Metrics, the early numbers that show whether the business works (Module 12). At week 3, if warm network and replies give under 2 held a week, about 2 hours move from long-form into short-form and replies.

Each first call is a Dual-Purpose Conversation, a disclosed fit-and-research call of about 45 minutes. Each follows Close by Contract, the close that agrees its structure first (Module 19). He hears the tiers premium-first, one recommendation, and the price once, then the affordability question about his pick and an ask. Plan on 0–2 clients in month 1, since first cash can slip past it when every route runs slow.

## 4. Prove: Months 3–6

From month 3 the business proves three things: buyers take a rising price, the product holds up in its own reads, and your reach can carry it. Delivery changes shape under all three.

### Price Steps begin once a start fills

The Price Steps are small raises on a published schedule that always happen while starts fill and close rates hold (Module 7). Their gate is read on a start day. The last start must be full, and the close rate over the 30 held conversations since the last announcement must sit inside 15–35%. Before those 30 exist, a filling start can carry a step by itself, sized at the bottom of the range. Each step goes out at least 30 days ahead and names what it adds. A raise that names nothing reads as a markup to a buyer this category taught to look for one.

**Month 4.** On the [March] start day, [~20] held conversations were too few to read, and neither founding start had filled, so no step went out. By the [April] start day, [32] held since day one stand behind a close rate of [~17%], inside the range but in its lower half. March has filled. So the May announcement, sent that day, carries a [June] step to [$1,730] at the bottom of the range, adding [a second group-call time]. His January client graduates in [April], and Round Two and the Hold switch on the same week.

### The product proves itself in reads, graduations, and a log

The week-6 read, your written mid-program read, puts the verdict question first: is the lever moving, given his adherence? It lands on one written outcome: adjust a lever, hold the plan, refer him out, or the exit right. The week-12 re-assessment sets matched captures beside his baseline and gives his verdict before graduation. Your Capture Standard freezes at version 1 once clients match baseline conditions without a retake, because every comparison you'll ever publish rests on it.

The first graduation switches on the back end, the schedule's first new rung after month 0. Round Two, a maintenance block, is offered only on measured momentum: adherence at or above his threshold, and a marker still improving across its last two readings. The Hold, a measurement subscription with a quarterly re-capture, fits the man who moved and holds (Module 10). The Canon Lane, a monthly email repeating your core claims, opens the same month.

Proof in this category is slow and confounded, since weight change, growth into the early 20s, and camera distance all move how a face reads. So Prove publishes process first. Your proof page leads with the dated log: check-in completion and turnaround kept, across every client. Consented process messages sit beneath it, each asked at a measured peak and never rewarded. Outcome ranges wait for the proof milestone, 10 graduates with consented process testimonials, and go up then as a labeled small sample.

### Delivery changes shape as the group grows

Founding depth, about 30–35 care minutes a client-week, is research you can afford only while the group is small. Once the group passes about four members, a client-week shrinks toward the Program's 13–20 care minutes all-in, and the weekly call takes on more of the teaching. The stall patterns the harvest named let a review say more in less time. The Expectation Document doesn't change, because it promises who writes each review and when it lands, never a count of minutes.

Seat Math then grows each start's cap from measured minutes, recomputed monthly and never from demand, so every cap stays a fact you can publish. A Private seat is a fixed line, counted before any group seat. Near 12–15 concurrent clients the combined call splits by program stage, since a man in week 2 and a man in week 10 need different conversations. On Cole's Band B path that split comes around month 7, as his caps grow from [2] seats toward [8].

These months also bring the first claims on the guarantee: a fit-window refund, a week-6 exit, a non-response payment at week 12. Each is paid within 7 days, with no counter-offer, and enters the Dated Record's claim rate beside turnaround kept. A claim rate with real claims in it tells a skeptic the guarantee can be collected. A log with none leaves him unable to tell whether nobody used it or nobody could.

## 5. Grow: Months 6–9

Months 6 to 9 spend what the proof unlocked, and your one number moves from conversations toward enrollments as Growing arrives.

In Band B the volume signal usually arrives first. Steps inside the opening band lift revenue slowly, while older long-form keeps finding adults. When the two disagree, the binding constraint decides which moves apply.

Short-form's line moves on yield, never on revenue (Module 24). Three signs move it. Eligible leads hold at about 50 a month for two months, and long-form beats short-form on leads per hour over two windows of about 30 door completions. Older pieces also bring a growing share. When all three hold, drop short-form to about 1.25 hours. The freed hours buy long-form's third piece a month. It re-films the weakest piece in your Start Here Series and adds the Offer Pieces, answers to a warm buyer's questions about your offer.

## Checklists: The First 30 Days and the Monthly Gates

### The first 30 days, week by week

Answer each item from your own records on its week's last day. A "no" goes first on next week's list. Items 1–4 come before any link or message goes out, because each protects a buyer who hasn't met you yet.

1. Week 1: Did door v0 go live with the age fork, the Fit Check and the affordability question at every checkout, and the pause route wired into your email tool, before any piece linked to it? → If not, unlink every piece until it does.
2. Week 1: Did the founding page go live with the Private seat first, who it's for and who it isn't for, the group's price beside its next price and stated end, and the Starter Path beside the pause route? → If not, hold the warm-network message until they are.
3. Week 1: Did the Honest Answer publish before any other long-form piece, with the pre-commitment beside it? → If not, no other piece ships ahead of them.
4. Before the first checkout: Are the guarantee terms, the Expectation Document, and the Path and Timeline Card written, with Round Two and the Hold priced? → If not, no checkout opens until they are.
5. Weeks 1–2: Did every adult you know get the warm-network message once, disclosed in its first line, with nobody under 18 on the list? → If not, send the rest once each and strike anyone under 18; nobody gets a second message.
6. Every week: Did every adult door completion get a personal reply within hours and a booking inside 24–48 hours? → If not, move your reply batch before adding any source.
7. Week 3: Did warm network and replies give 2 or more held conversations a week? → If not, move about 2 hours a week from long-form into short-form and replies before adding anything else.
8. Week 4: Is the Verify Page live, and did every held conversation carry its booking-source label and end in one recommendation and an ask? → If not, finish the page this month, label from today, and replay any call that ended without an ask.

### The Monthly Gates

Write each gate before its month starts, as one question your records can answer yes or no. Name the record it reads, the threshold from the build, and the day you'll read it. "Calls feel slow" can't fail, while "on pace for about 15 held by week 12, from the booking-source log" can. On that day a pass keeps next month's list as its phase table writes it. A fail puts the fix first and holds whatever depends on it.

1. Months 1–2: Are your held conversations on pace for about 15 by week 12, each carrying its label? → If not, rerun the week-3 shift, and give replies and short-form any hours your week can add, up to 25 in all; 3–6 a week stays the target.
2. Month 3: Did you hold about 15 or more conversations by week 12, and 3 or more clients once 25 or more were held? → Under 15, sources plus the Band A test once pages pass; under 3 from 25 or more, replay the calls, then position, the Card, and price, one change at a time.
3. Month 4: Did the last start to close fill, with the close rate over the 30 held since the last announcement inside 15–35%, or still unreadable? → If yes, announce the step 30 or more days out, at the bottom of the range unless the rate reads in the upper half with every start since the last announcement full; if not, hold the announcement.
4. Month 5: Did every weekly review, week-6 read, and week-12 re-assessment land inside its stated turnaround? → If not, credit or refund each miss within 7 days, and rerun Seat Math on measured minutes before the next start.
5. Month 6: Do your eligible leads sit inside your band's month-6 range? → If not, work sources and packaging first, then your band's reach lever, and leave price where it is.
6. Month 7: Have eligible leads held at about 50 a month for two months, with long-form out-yielding short-form per hour over two windows and older pieces' share climbing? → If yes, move short-form to about 1.25 hours; if not, keep the Early line whatever revenue says.
7. Month 8: Did most weeks hold above about 6–8 conversations, with selling eating the content minimum? → If yes, set the Call Cap and route the overflow to the Decision Assessment. If not, and shows slid toward about 60% or over half ended no-fit, fix screening instead.
8. Month 9: Did both readings, trailing revenue and the volume signal, move you to a new stage? → If yes, swap to that stage's numbers; either way, name your band from its month-9 range and write its next decision before the review closes.

Line 3 is whatever the month-4 row lists that doesn't need volume, such as switching on the back end at your first graduation. The step waits for a start that fills, and the founding price has already ended on its date.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Reach binds and then conversations, so the build puts sources, the door, and the founding group ahead of anything that needs volume. The default is each month's list, with its gate read on the last day whatever the week felt like. The trap is borrowing a later month's lever because the calendar feels full. So is chasing the views rating content brings, which fill your age fork with minors and your calendar with nobody.

**Growing.** Conversion and selling minutes bind, and in Band B you usually arrive on volume before revenue. The default is one change per window at the monthly review, with short-form's line moving only on yield. The trap is a Price Step that lifts revenue past Growing's line while reach still binds, followed by a cut to the reach engine that produced it.

**Scaling.** For most bands it lies beyond month 9. There care minutes bind, and the build hands over to three owners: Seat Math (Module 6), your Scaling Triggers (Module 12), and the $50k configuration (Module 13). The trap is thinning review to make room, since a checking habit or a distress line in a log is the first thing thinner review misses.

## Standard Check

- **Selling from week 1.** The founding page and door v0 go live together, with the offer, who it's for and who it isn't for, the public price, and one next step, so no month waits for an audience before it sells (*Sell directly*).
- **Dates from the calendar, never a launch.** Every date in nine months is a monthly start, the founding price's stated end, or a step announced 30 or more days out. A thin start is published, never rescued with a discount or a moved date (*Use real dates*; the line on fake scarcity).
- **Stop Rules from door v0.** The age fork, the Fit Check, the affordability question, and the pause route go live before the first link, with the Starter Path beside them. No automation in any month sells past a stop rule, and every no has a free next step (the line on vulnerability).
