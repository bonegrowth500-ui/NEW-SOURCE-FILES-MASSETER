# 25 · Instagram and X: Replies, DMs and Routes
*Part VI — The Ecosystem*

**The shift:** from *"Every platform is a stage"* to *"Platforms are where engagement starts, and replies, DMs and routes carry it to the door."*

On Thursday night a Reel about month eight lands: same photos, same guessing, no idea what's working. By Saturday your inbox holds [120] messages. [Fourteen] are adults with real questions, like whether it's too late at 26 and what your 12-week Program costs. [Ninety] are a photo and two words: "rate me". [Six] mention a school timetable or a parent's phone, [nine] are the keyword alone, and one, sent at 2 a.m., says he can't stand seeing himself in any photo.

Run that inbox as a stage and every message becomes an audience: a kind rating for each photo, a pitch for the adults, a long reply for everyone, and your weekend gone. Run it as a router and each message already has a destination. The keywords get their link, the adults get the idea and the door, and the photos get no rating. The boys get the education lane, your free content and an orthodontist referral, with nothing kept. The 2 a.m. message gets you, personally, with a referral and no sale.

Instagram and X are where engagement starts, and neither is where it should stay. This module builds the routes to your door and counts how many platforms your week can carry.

## 1. Roles and the Platform Count Rule

Instagram and X each earn one job here, routing private intent on Instagram and testing your arguments on X, and you run only as many platforms as your editing budget can serve.

### Neither platform is a stage

A stage is judged by its crowd, and both platforms gather small crowds that scatter fast. Median organic engagement per post runs at a fraction of a percent on Instagram and a smaller fraction on X, and most of a post's reach arrives within a day or two. A long-form piece keeps working through search for months. Instagram reaches about half of US adults and X about a fifth, but Reels carry Instagram's reach inside your short-form batch, so the platform adds routing and X adds argument.

The stage habit costs more here than in most categories. The formats that gather crowds, ratings, comparisons, and edited "ideal jaw" posts, pull the youngest viewers. In self-selected samples most of the category's keenest audience is under 18, so such a crowd brings few eligible adults per view, the legal adults who could pay from their own income or savings without new credit.

### The editing budget sets the count

Every platform charges the Native Tax, the cost of making a piece belong there: its own first line, on-screen text, cover, and caption (Module 24). Your editing budget is what you can spend on that tax each week, in your hours and your editor's, once long-form is served. Stretch it and each extra platform gets copies with no native pass, while the hours come out of long-form first, because its cost arrives last. In this category a rushed pass also makes claims, like a clip cut from its caveat or "results in 90 days" in a late caption.

**Serve long-form first, then add a platform only while the editing budget still covers its native version.**

That's the **Platform Count Rule**: run only as many platforms as your editing budget serves, and drop one before long-form degrades. It governs every platform beyond long-form and email: Shorts, Reels, TikTok, X, and native Instagram, meaning carousels, Stories, and a broadcast channel. TikTok joins only on short-form's trial, matching Reels on eligible adults per editing hour over about 30 door completions. Keyword replies cost reading, not editing, so the rule never touches routing.

### Reid adds two platforms and thins the one that mattered

Reid *(composite operator)* is Early, in month [3], with long-form every other week and [five] native shorts a week for Shorts and Reels, each Reel carrying a keyword. Reach feels slow, so he adds a daily X post and [three] carousels a week on the same hours. Within [three] weeks the shorts shrink to [three] copies with no native pass, his X posts are mostly links, and his long-form slips a week. Followers climb, while eligible leads drift from [about 20] a month toward [about 12], because the pieces adults found him through thinned.

At his monthly review the read is a count. His short-form line covers one native batch cut two ways, and his replies line covers his DMs and replies, leaving nothing for carousels or daily posts. Both go in the De-Scoping Order's sequence, your fixed order of cuts for an overloaded week: X first, since testing arguments sits furthest from your door, then native Instagram, with routing kept (Module 11). By month [5] long-form is back on cadence and eligible leads are near [18–22] a month.

### The count runs on your Design Week's lines

"Fits the budget" is arithmetic on your Design Week, where every hour has a line. Reels, carousels, and any TikTok pass come from the short-form line. The replies line covers public replies, Permission-First Replies on both platforms, and your DM slot, which takes its minutes first because routing is never cut. A platform fits when its native version, at its cadence, fits what's left of its line every week:

| Stage | Short-form line | Replies line | DM slot, first | The rest of the replies line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early | 3.5 h | 2.0 h | ~1.0 h, about 10 min a day | ~1.0 h of replies on both platforms; X as replies only |
| Growing | 1.25 h | 1.0 h | ~0.5 h | ~0.5 h for X's full lab; routing help moderates your comments |
| Scaling, $25k | 0.5 h | 0.75 h | ~0.5 h | ~0.25 h for X, while its arguments still reach long-form |
| Scaling, $50k | 0.5 h | 0.5 h | ~0.5 h | None: X goes, or waits for templates to free minutes |

### Add by the binding constraint, drop at the monthly review

An add answers the constraint that binds, the one stopping the next dollar this month. When reach binds, add a reach surface, a native Instagram format or TikTok after its trial, if the short-form line covers its pass. When conversion binds, in Growing, add X's full lab, since arguments that hold become objection pieces. When care minutes bind, add nothing.

Drops wait for the monthly review, since a week past 25 hours only pauses a platform under the De-Scoping Order's step 2, X first and native Instagram second. The review makes a pause a drop when the count stops fitting, or when a platform's yield trails the others' over about 30 eligible leads from its tag. By default, Instagram routes from month 0 and X stays replies only until Growing, because a lab needs your calls' objections before it knows what to test.

## 2. Permission-First Replies

A public reply answers the idea and routes to the door in front of everyone, and it asks before anything goes private, because here a comment is a disclosure and an uninvited DM is the closer's opening move.

### A comment is a disclosure with silent readers behind it

Most men working on how they look keep it to themselves, so the one who comments has told everyone reading. Behind him sit many more who never write, and your public answer is really to them: it joins the archive a skeptic scrolls before he trusts you. The private side carries the category's history. DM closers answer a comment with "check your inbox" and open with a pitch, so an uninvited message reads as that pattern, and the commenter may be 15.

### Answer and link in public; ask only for what he won't say there

A reply runs in three steps. First, answer the idea in full, in public, so it stands if he never writes again; holding half back to force a DM is a curiosity device that doesn't pay off where it was raised, which the Payoff Test rules out (Intro). Second, route in public: the door link under your answer serves him and every silent reader with his question. Third, offer a DM only when his comment shows a question he won't ask in public, like his own history or cost, and only once he's placed himself as an adult. No is a fine answer.

**Answer the idea in public, ask before anything private, and route to the door.**

That's **Permission-First Replies**. A DM's only job is the question he'd rather not ask in public, so everything else stays where the next man can read it. When you can't tell his age, the public answer and link are the whole reply, because a link is routing and a DM is contact. A minor gets the education lane in public and nothing more (Module 5). Distress gets neither idea nor link: a kind line, where to find help, and an open invitation to message you.

Replies on other people's threads stay yours, since each is a claim. From Growing, routing help, a freelancer who moderates your comments, can post the templated link under your own pieces.

### Theo asks in public and gets his step there

> **Theo** *(composite, Burned Struggler)*: "I'm 26 and every coach in this space says 'measured'. The last one I paid measured nothing. Why is yours different?"
>
> **You:** "Fair, and after that you should check rather than trust. 'There's no good evidence that habits change the shape of an adult's bone, and I don't sell that. Some things are debated, and I'll tell you where the evidence is thin. A lot does change and can be measured: your habits, your body composition, how you carry yourself, your grooming, how you're photographed.' I review each client's logged week in writing, and my log is public: [sample plan] · [sample weekly review] · [the log]. Your own read starts here: [door link]."

Nothing in his comment needed privacy, so no DM was offered, and the kit went where every reader with his doubt can open it. The reply quoted the first claim of your Canon, the 5–7 claims you repeat in the same words everywhere, whole (Module 14). The kit repaired Guide, his belief that you're worth trusting, and the link gave him a step on his clock.

If he pushes, "So no results, then?", answer from the log: "Not as outcome ranges yet. Those start at 10 graduates, labeled as a small sample, on the date in the log." Never write "DM me and I'll show you results privately", because private results can't be checked.

### What replies never do

A reply never closes, never rates, and never comments on features. A price question gets the public price range and one link. A face gets no rating, even a kind one, since a flattering read invites the hostile one after it, and nobody's features come up, whether his, a bystander's, or a seller's. That puts the force on practices, as when a man asks under someone else's post whether a [chewing device] will bring his jaw forward:

> **You:** "Fair question, and the appeal makes sense. 'There's no good evidence that habits change the shape of an adult's bone, and I don't sell that. Some things are debated, and I'll tell you where the evidence is thin. A lot does change and can be measured: your habits, your body composition, how you carry yourself, your grooming, how you're photographed.' A device you chew on is a habit with a price tag, so selling it on forward growth sells the one result the evidence doesn't support. Ask any seller, me included, for matched captures and how many people the result came from."

The reply names no seller, touches no face, and ends in a test he can apply to you: *Fight ideas, not people* at its sharper setting. Claim 1 goes out whole again, because the Canon holds by repetition. If he answers "I've seen the before/afters", ask whether the light, angle, and distance match, and how many people tried it. Never write "anyone selling that is a scammer", which aims at a person and gives his buyers a reason to defend him.

## 3. Instagram as a Private Router

Beyond the reach Reels bring, Instagram's value happens in private, in sends and messages, so you run it as a router that carries intent to the door and keeps minors and faces out of everything it collects.

### Intent arrives by send and by message

The man you most want rarely comments. He sends a Reel about month eight to a friend with "this is you", and writes to you late at night if he writes at all. So intent collects in the inbox, along with the highest share of minors in your business, since teens use Instagram heavily and "rate me" culture lives there. The router's jobs are to carry the adult to the door fast and to keep the boy from giving you anything to keep.

On the Warmth Ladder, the rule that you never ask for a rung the buyer isn't standing on, a Reel sits on the Stranger rung and a keyword message is a stranger asking for the door. A follower in your broadcast channel stands on the Returning rung, and a message from an adult your door has verified sits on the Deciding rung (Module 18).

### A keyword by DM returns one link

The Reel pays off in full, then ends with one line: send me a keyword by DM for the free self-assessment. His message opens a conversation he started, and the reply returns one link, to your door, whose first question is whether he's a legal adult where he lives. Nothing is asked in the thread, nothing is sold there, and nothing he types goes into a list.

The keyword goes by DM, never as a public comment. A comment keyword marks him in front of everyone as a man working on his face, puts minors' self-identification on display, and fills your comments with one word that reads as bait. The word also sorts who sends it: [RECORD] or [LOG] draws men who want to measure, while [JAW] invites a rating of the jaw.

**A keyword by DM, one link back, and the door does the rest.**

That's the **Keyword Route**: a keyword sent by DM, never as a public comment, that returns a link to your self-assessment. Use one keyword per topic, each returning a tagged link, so your door counts eligible adults by topic. The keyword buys the next step, never the answer, and the Reel carries no price, date, or offer.

### The automatic reply answers the keyword alone

Platforms let an automated reply answer only a conversation he started, and only within a short window. That suits a bare keyword, which needs no reading first:

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| A labeled automatic reply on the keyword alone, once per thread | Your setting can restrict it that way | One fixed line, a keyword nobody types by accident, and reading every thread anyway |
| You send each link by hand in your DM slot | Your setting can't restrict it | Up to a day's wait for the link |
| A scripted DM flow that asks, qualifies, or offers | Never | It collects answers outside the door, can't see a minor or distress, and speaks as you |

**Default:** the labeled reply, fired only when the whole message is the keyword, and once per thread. Anything longer waits for your slot, because "[RECORD] im 15", or the keyword inside a hard message, needs a person before a link. Run it on the platform's own keyword setting or a tool that keeps no contact list, since such a list would hold minors' details. Where the setting can't restrict itself, send the link by hand.

### Carousels and a broadcast channel are optional, and one-way

A carousel works as a decision tool men save and send, like six things to check before buying another device, captioned as the question an adult types. It shows no face and no before/after, since matched pairs live only in long-form and your site's library, and it ends with the keyword.

Run a broadcast channel as one-way broadcast: it announces, and never argues, closes, or collects data. It's a send you can't filter, reaching men on the pause route, the content-free tag a stop rule sets that holds every marketing send and blocks checkout for 60–90 days, then asks permission once. So it carries only what your public profile already shows anyone, minus anything that sells: pieces without a pitch, entries from your public log, Canon claims quoted verbatim, and the door link. Offers, prices, dates, and checkout links go by your offer page and unpaused email.

Keep member replies, polls, and prompts off, though the platform offers all three, including prompts that collect photos. Replies would make a free community under another name, and a photo prompt is a queue of faces. Instagram Live and Collab posts stay out too, since live talk can't pass your claims approval or hold a stop rule, and a Collab sets another account's claims beside yours.

### Minors-exposure controls sit where his message lands

Instagram's teen protections limit who can message a teen first and what he's shown, but none stops a 16-year-old from sending your keyword, because the message is his. So the controls are yours, and they route rather than engage:

- **The Reel.** Packaged for adults, with an adult situation up front and a calm cover, never a rating, a comparison, an edited jaw, or a before/after pair. Those are the Age-Up Dial's choices, the packaging that lowers minors' share (Module 23).
- **The comments.** No keyword asks. Rating requests and minors' personal details are hidden under your comment policy, with no reply.
- **The inbox.** No link reaches anything longer than the keyword before you've read it, a minor gets the education-lane line and nothing else, and you never start a conversation.

Keep an anonymous count of minors' messages by keyword, because it's the control you steer by. When one keyword's messages skew young, the fix goes into the packaging of its Reels, since the reply was already right.

## 4. DM Templates by Case

Every DM is read by you in a sized daily slot and answered from the template for its case, in an order that puts his safety before his age, his age before his request, and his request before any sale.

### The slot is sized from your replies line

DMs are where minors disclose, distress arrives, and buyers ask about price, so they stay on your Keep-List, the work that never leaves your hands, and routing help never works the inbox. The Unpriced-Minute Rule, every per-person minute priced, batched, or templated, sets the shape (Module 11): templates by case, in a slot on the replies line. It halves from Growing on, when the automatic link and templates carry more.

When the slot runs past its share two weeks running, work the overrun in order: template the reply you keep typing, turn the question you answer most into a Reel or pinned post, then take X's minutes, the first cut. The slot shrinks last, because routing is never cut. Selling inside a thread, a verified adult's recommendation or a voice note of about 1–3 minutes recorded by you, belongs to your selling hours.

Distress can't wait for the slot. Once each evening, slot day or not, read your message previews, a couple of minutes' work, and answer any thread that reads as distress that day. The automatic reply never touches it, since it fires only on the keyword alone.

### Four cases, checked in order

| Order | The case | What he gets | What never happens | What's kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distress: "I can't stand any photo of myself" | A referral to a doctor or mental-health professional, crisis resources if it's acute, and an offer to read what he sends | A sale, a door link, an offer, a date | Nothing new; if he's in your records, the pause route and "stopped: stop rule" |
| 2 | A minor: his age, his school, a parent's say-so | The education-lane line: adults only, the videos free, an orthodontist for a growing face | A door link, "later", "first", or any follow-up | Nothing; if he's in your records, deleted, and anything paid refunded |
| 3 | "Rate me", with or without a photo | No rating, the idea behind the question, and the door link | A word about his photo or any feature, kind or not | Nothing; the photo is never saved or forwarded |
| 4 | An adult with a question | The idea in a line or two, then the door; once he's verified, one recommendation and a checkout link | A close before verification; Private without a call | Only what the door records |

The order matters because messages mix cases. A man who wants a rating and hates every photo of himself is case 1, since help comes before any redirect. A 16-year-old asking the price is case 2 and gets no price. A side profile attached to "is this worth it at 27?" is case 3 before case 4, so his question gets an answer and his photo doesn't. A protective stop, a stop rule that protects him, leaves at most "stopped: stop rule" on record, and nothing from the thread feeds your objection log or content plan.

### A thread meets the door through a code

The DM Handoff sets what an adult can buy here: unverified contacts go to the door, and a verified adult may get one recommendation and a checkout link, while Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables, needs a call first. He's verified once your records show a completed door with no pause tag and he has confirmed from the address his result went to (Module 20).

In a thread, a code finds his record without asking. His result page carries a short one, like [R-4827], beside "Prefer Instagram? Send me this code." You look it up in your own records and email the address on his record to ask whether the thread is his, and his reply confirms him. Nobody asks in a thread for his age, his email, or a photo. A code that shows a pause tag gets only what the pause route allows, with no price, offer, or date. A verified adult who asks about the next start gets one line:

> **You:** "Found your result, thanks. The next Program start is Monday [date], and you can join through [date]. It's $[public price] for 12 weeks: [checkout link]. It asks a few short questions before payment."

## 5. X as an Argument Lab

X is where adults argue about ideas in public, so you use it to test the arguments behind your claims, move the ones that hold to long-form, and capture the adults who engage through your door.

### A peer network rewards replies and punishes contempt

As X published it, predicted replies and shares count heavily, and conversation between accounts that follow each other gets extra lift. A predicted report, mute, or block counts against a post far more than a like counts for it, and the sizes change with X's experiments. So a post earns reach by starting an argument peers want to join, and loses it by making people want to leave.

X's users skew adult and male, and far fewer teens use it than use Instagram, so a claim argued there meets men who could one day buy. But appearance talk on X often runs ironic or hostile, and the replies that draw mutes are the ones aimed at people. So the ranking and the House Standard agree: argue the idea hard and leave people out of it. On the rungs, a reply meets strangers, a follow makes him Returning, and his door completion makes him Assessed.

### Argue on peers' threads, half an hour a week

Argue where your peers already argue: threads by adjacent educators in training, posture, grooming, and photography, and clinicians' threads when they argue ideas. On a clinician's thread you argue the idea and concede the clinic, with no clinical opinion and no word implying standing you don't hold, since the line on credentials holds on every thread. Follow the peers you've disagreed with well, because X lifts conversation between accounts that follow each other. The lab runs on about half an hour a week of Growing's replies line: one claim, one post of your own, and replies on the threads where its objection lives.

### Test the argument, never the claim's words

Your Canon changes only when the evidence does, as a dated correction in your public log, so X never tests rewordings for likes. It tests the argument around a claim: which reason, example, or framing survives the strongest objection an adult can raise. Post the claim verbatim, name the strongest objection from your calls or replies, and give your answer.

| Outcome | What it looks like | What you do next |
|---|---|---|
| Held | The objector's own words concede or narrow his point, or he drops it without counter-evidence | Run it on another thread; once it holds in more than one week, move it to long-form |
| Lost | A counter you can't answer at the evidence's tier | A better answer next week, or a dated correction if the evidence moved |
| No contest | Replies cheer and nobody objects | Nothing learned: take the objection from your calls and post it yourself |

A loss is the lab working: next week's answer is better, or the claim gets a dated public correction, which a skeptic reads as a standard nobody fakes for long. Reach never enters the verdict, so a held argument in a quiet thread outranks a cheered one in a busy thread:

**Quote the claim verbatim, argue it against the best objection, log the outcome, and move what holds to long-form.**

That's the **Argument Lab**: X as the place to test Canon claims and framings in public argument, captured to email. Start with the objection your last 30 held conversations raised most, then work the Canon's rotation, replying on peers' threads first and quote-posting a peer's argument when it earns your followers' eyes. Log the claim, the objection, the argument, the outcome in the objector's words, and the week's tagged door completions.

### What holds moves to long-form, and captures go through the door

An argument has won when it holds against the strongest objection on more than one thread, in more than one week, each time against a real objector. Then it moves to long-form, as an objection piece or a segment of a decision-stage video, because long-form works for months while most of an X post's reach arrives within a day or two. The argument you like best and the one that holds often differ, which is why you run a lab.

X keeps nothing for you, since the follow graph is the platform's. The capture that lasts is an adult's email, and your list has one entrance, the door, age fork first. So each post stands on its own, with the door link in the first reply or your profile, under your own name with [Brand] in the bio, since X ranks conversation between people.

### Tone runs under the House Standard

The lab licenses force, all of it aimed at ideas: device grift, manufactured before/afters, the endless-tips model that sells the stall back to its buyers, and the "it's all genetics" shrug, pointedly, after steelmanning each. When a thread asks what qualifies you, post your Qualifications Answer in the same words every time: the fact, the scope, the check, the referral (Module 15).

The limits are about people. Quote-post an argument made in public by someone arguing in public, and crop a seller's claim to its words, account and face out. Never quote-post a private person's worry, and stay out of rating threads. Spaces and co-hosted threads stay out with podcasts and collaborations, for the reasons that keep Instagram Live out.

**When the signals disagree.** A large account quote-posts your Honest Answer, your standing answer to the bone question, with "coach admits nothing works lol", and by evening [several hundred] replies have piled on, some rating your face. Reach says ride it: reply to everyone, quote him back, post a thread while the eyes are there. The lab says a joke isn't an objection. Your door says [five] completions tagged X, [one] of them eligible.

Go by the door and the Standard. Answer the idea once, in a post of your own, with the claim verbatim beside the strongest honest version of the objection: "if bone won't change, what's left to coach?" Leave the account and the joke out. A real counter-argument in the noise gets its own thread and a line in the log. Hide the replies under your own posts that rate faces, yours included. Meet appearance attacks with your face statement's first sentence, word for word: "My face isn't evidence that this works, for you or anyone, and it isn't evidence that it doesn't."

## 6. Measuring Each Platform

Each platform is judged by the number its job produces: eligible adults through the Keyword Route on Instagram, and arguments moved on X, on denominators that hold still when a platform recounts.

### Instagram is judged by eligible adults per keyword

Instagram's number is eligible-adult yield, eligible leads per unit of reach by source, counted through each keyword's tagged link. Read it per keyword message and per 1,000 views on Instagram's own fixed count, dated whenever the platform changes what it counts, the habit Denominator Discipline makes a rule (Module 12). Beside it sit anonymous counts per keyword of minors' messages and rating requests, which steer packaging. Likes and follows stay off the scoreboard, since they count minors and raters as readily as buyers.

Many keyword messages with few completions mean a Reel drew people the door turns away, so its packaging changes, never the reply. Until your own ratios exist, plan on about 0.1–1 raw lead per 1,000 short-form views, with 30–70% of raw leads eligible.

### X is judged by arguments moved, read each quarter

X's product arrives late and somewhere else. A held argument earns nothing on X; it pays when a long-form piece built on it answers the objection for months, so a monthly glance at X's own numbers reads the wrong place. Keep the weekly log you already write, and once a quarter count arguments tested against a real objector, arguments held in more than one week, and arguments moved into a long-form piece that shipped. Tagged door completions from X ride beside them as a count, read once about 30 have arrived.

| The quarter shows | The read | The move |
|---|---|---|
| Held and moved, and the moved piece earns at least as well as its peers | The lab is paying | Keep its minutes; take the next objection from your calls |
| Held, but nothing moved | Your long-form calendar is the bottleneck | Put the held argument in the next piece whose topic fits; start no new tests until it ships |
| Tested, and nothing held | Your answers lose to the best objection | Sharpen the answer, or log a dated correction if the evidence moved; both are the lab working |
| Nothing tested against a real objector | You posted claims nobody contested | Post your calls' top objection yourself; a second quarter like this argues for giving X's minutes back |

Compare a moved piece only with its peers: long-form on the same rung, published in the same quarter, on long-form's fixed count. Once the pooled pieces reach about 30 door completions, read two lines: eligible leads per 1,000 engaged views from its tagged link, and how often its objection comes up on your calls in the month after it ships against the month before. The first line is reach, and the second is the belief it was built to repair.

Cole *(composite operator)* meets three kinds of quarter in his first year of the lab. One quarter he tests [five] arguments, [two] hold, and [one] moves into an objection piece that draws [~2.2] eligible leads per 1,000 engaged views against his Returning pieces' [~1.8], so the lab keeps its half hour. Another quarter moves nothing: [four] tested, none held, two lost to a clinician's point about [bite changes]. That's the lab working, so he sharpens one answer and logs a dated correction to an example he'd been using, with the claim's words untouched.

The hard quarter is the one where a held argument's piece yields [~1.2] per 1,000, under his Returning pieces, while its objection falls from [six] mentions a month on his calls to [one]. Reach says the piece underperforms, and his calls say it repaired the belief it was built for. He goes by its rung: a Returning objection piece earns on belief and offer demand, so it joins the objection pieces on his offer page, and the lab keeps its minutes.

## Worked Example: Cole's Objection, from an X Thread to a Keyword Reel

Cole *(composite operator)* is Growing in month [11], with the Program at [$1,910], X running as a full lab, and his Reels routing through the Keyword Route. His objection log's last 30 held conversations show one doubt most: "Why pay when it's free on YouTube?" It's a Vehicle objection, a doubt that a paid program is the way to get there.

**Week 1. The claim, verbatim.** He posts the Canon's fourth claim word for word, "A record doesn't read itself; review turns it into a decision", beside the objection and his usual answer: free videos give you the method but can't read your record. [Forty] replies agree. Then a strength coach with a larger following objects that most people just need consistency, and paid review is a crutch.

The first argument has no answer to that, so Cole tries a second: consistency is the half a man can do alone, and review is the other half, noticing when eight consistent weeks are aimed at a lever that doesn't move for him. The coach narrows, in his own words: "fair, for people already consistent for months". Log: the first argument lost, the second held, [three] door completions tagged X.

**Weeks 2–3. Twice more.** He uses the second argument in Permission-First Replies on [two] threads where adults ask whether coaching is worth paying for, the door link under each answer, and both objectors narrow the same way. He declines an invitation to co-host a Space on "mewing myths", since live audio can't pass his claims approval. Tagged completions: [five], [three] eligible.

## Templates: The Keyword Route and DM Replies by Case

**The Keyword Route, set up once.**

```
On the Reel:      "Send me [RECORD] by DM for the free self-assessment." Never "comment".
The keyword:      One per topic, about the record ([RECORD], [LOG]); never a feature.
Automatic reply:  "Automatic reply: here's the free self-assessment, [tagged link].
                   I read every message myself."
Fires on:         The keyword alone, once per thread; if it can't, send by hand in your slot.
```

**DM replies by case.**

*Case 1, distress.*

> **You:** "Thank you for telling me. That sounds really hard. Please talk to [a doctor] or [a mental-health service near you], and if you ever feel unsafe, [a crisis line] is there right now. If you want to write more, I'll read it myself."

The reply offers a person and a path, since a door link would read as a sale. If he asks "so you won't help me?", answer: "I'll read whatever you send. I'm not selling you anything." Set the pause route if he's in your records, and never send "The program could help with that."

*Case 2, a minor.* Jordan *(composite, minor)*, 16, writes "rate me, im 16, be honest":

> **You:** "Thanks for asking. What I offer is only for adults, so I can't work with you, and I won't keep any of your details. The videos are free to watch. For questions about a growing face, an orthodontist is the right person to ask."

A minor can't be sold to, so the reply closes the thread kindly with nothing to come back for. If he says his parents would pay, nothing changes. Delete the thread, add one to the count, and never send "Come back when you're 18."

*Case 3, "rate me".*

> **You:** "I don't rate faces, yours or anyone's, because a rating can't tell you what to do next. Most of how people read you was never about your jaw. If you want to know what's worth your effort, the free self-assessment starts there: [link]. It never asks for a photo."

The Canon's claim moves his question from his face to how he comes across. If he pushes for a number, reply once, "No numbers, for anyone," then stop. Never send "Honestly, you look fine": it's still a rating.

*Case 4, an adult, not yet verified.* Maya *(composite, welcome, not targeted)*, 28: "Is this only for guys? And roughly what does it cost?"

> **You:** "It's for any adult, and it runs the same way for everyone. The Program is $[public price] for 12 weeks, with a written review of your week and a weekly group call. It isn't for anyone who wants a promise about bone. Everything starts with the free self-assessment: [link]."

That's *Sell directly* before verification: the offer, who it's for and isn't, the price, and one step. If she wants to pay today, the door is still the way in, and it ends in your recommendation. Never send "Let's hop on a call about pricing."

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Reach binds, and so do your editing hours. Instagram routes the Reels you already cut through a DM slot of about 10 minutes a day, and X stays replies only, the first cut when a week runs long. The default is a public answer and link on every thread your remaining replies time reaches, since those replies start conversations while your pieces are few. The trap is posting natively everywhere because reach feels slow, which starves the long-form your first skeptics check.

**Growing.** Conversion binds, and your calls have filled an objection log worth arguing from. The default is the full lab on about half an hour a week, feeding held arguments into objection pieces, while routing help moderates your comments. The trap is letting that helper, or a tool, into the inbox because DMs pile up.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind, and the replies line shrinks to 0.75 hours, then 0.5 at $50k, most of it the slot. The default is to keep only the platforms whose arguments or keyword yield still feed long-form and the door. The trap is a DM flow that qualifies buyers for you, a script where minors and distress arrive.

## Standard Check

- **Replies that never rate.** Permission-First Replies and every DM answer the idea, never a face, a photo, or a feature (the line on shame). On X the force goes to practices, and quote-posts carry arguments, never faces or private people (*Fight ideas, not people*).
- **The keyword alone.** Keywords arrive by DM only, and the automatic link fires only on the keyword by itself, so a message that could reveal a minor or distress reaches you first (the line on vulnerability).
- **The case order.** Every DM is read for distress, then a minor, then a rating request, and only then as a sale: an unverified contact gets the door, a verified adult one recommendation and a link (*Sell directly*).
- **One-way means one-way.** The broadcast channel carries only what your public profile shows anyone, never an offer, price, date, or checkout link. Each start's dates still reach engaged adults through your page and unpaused email (*Use real dates*), never a man on the pause route (the line on vulnerability).
