# 11 · The Operating Week
*Part III — Start, Extend, Run, Grow*

**The shift:** from *"Work harder when it's busy"* to *"My week is designed line by line, overload triggers a fixed order of cuts, and the leverage gets built on a protected line."*

Reid *(composite operator)* logs [31] hours in week [23] of his second year. He answers every DM, including [a dozen] from men asking him to look at their jaw. He spends [an evening] arguing with a rival's fans in his comments and holds a free call with a man who wanted a promise about bone. By Friday night his inbox is empty, and his channel has nothing new on it. It feels like the most productive week of his year.

Six weeks later his eligible leads, the adults who could pay, fall by [about a third]. He blames the season and a rival's viral clip, and he never connects the two weeks. Nothing on his dashboard links a full inbox in one month to a thin calendar in the next.

A solo business has to run in about 20 hours with slack, or it doesn't run. So the week gets designed before it starts, line by line, with its cuts decided before the heavy week arrives. Then a full inbox can't quietly spend next month's leads, and a protected hour of building each week is what makes the $25k week lighter than the Growing one.

## 1. The Design Week

Your week needs a line for every kind of work before the work arrives, because in this niche per-person demand outbids anything that pays off later.

### Per-person demand outbids the work that pays later

One-to-one demand runs unusually high in this category. A young audience raised on rating culture asks for private verdicts on its faces by DM. Skeptics want to argue in public, and burned buyers want a free call to test you before they'll trust a page. Each request is small, and most feel like service. Together they grow with your audience rather than your revenue, and because each one arrives with a person attached, they win every contest against work whose payoff comes weeks later.

A solo week has a hard edge at 20–25 hours, and work without a budget expands into it in the order it shouts. Content shouts least, since a skipped video sends no message. Review shouts late: a slipping turnaround surfaces a week after the damage, as a credit you owe. So a week nobody designed drifts toward replies and calls, starves content and building, and runs hottest on the lines that compound least.

The answer is to decide where every hour goes before the week starts. Each kind of work gets its own line, sized to your stage, so a request without a line has to take its hours from one you can see. The total sits near 20, and the gap to 25 absorbs the weeks that lump. That's the **Design Week**, and it fits in a sentence:

**Every hour has a line, every line has a size, and the gap to 25 is slack.**

### Each stage's week is priced line by line

Every line in the four stage weeks carries hours, including the four that operators leave off: milestones, the measurement subscription called the Hold, the premium Private seat, and building. ### The forgotten lines are promises with dates

Milestones cost about 0.75–1.25 hours per enrollment beyond weekly review: the Commit Ritual, the review and decision on baseline day; the written week-6 read; and the week-12 re-assessment, the closing capture and verdict. So that line grows with monthly enrollments rather than with clients, and it lumps, because a start's week-6 reads all land in the same week. The Hold's line climbs as graduates join, since every member is owed a re-capture and a written review each quarter, and from about 30 alumni it also carries the moderation of the alumni room, which stays yours because room posts are client content.

Private gets a line only while a seat earns its place: a founding seat while minutes are spare, and about one seat at $25k. At $50k it has none until a seat passes the Parity Rule by earning at least what a seat in your 12-week Program earns per hour of care (Module 13). One or two async-first seats at parity add about 0.6 hours a week each, taking that week from about 20.5 hours to 21–22. The build line is where leverage gets made: the first working version of your door and the check-in form early, then the templates that make Scaling lighter. It's protected because every later week is only as light as what it built.

Slack is the fifth line, and it has no row. Your range is 20–25 hours, and the design sits near the bottom on purpose, leaving the slack the table's last column shows. It absorbs what the Measurement Calendar, your fixed dates for captures, makes lumpy: baseline days at the start of a cohort and re-assessments at its end, plus a dispute, a sick week, or a piece that lands. Growing's gap is the thinnest because selling and untemplated review peak there together, while the leverage that lightens Scaling is still on the build line.

Cole *(composite operator)* runs the Early column in month [2]: a long-form piece every other week, [five] native short pieces batch-filmed on [Monday], and [four] Dual-Purpose Conversations, his disclosed fit-and-research calls. The baseline days come out of slack. The seat, sold after the checks every paid step runs, fits the half-hour Private line while minutes are spare, and none of its time comes from short-form, the line producing his month-3 conversations.

Design your week once per stage from the column that fits, and give each line a place as well as a size: content in batches, review in fixed blocks, live work in windows, all on the calendar before any client slot exists. Whenever the stage changes or a week runs past 25, log hours by line for two weeks, because an overrun hides in whichever line nobody logged. At the monthly Operator Review, your standing review of the numbers, compare each line with its design, and let your own measured line replace a planning one only after about 30 events stand behind it.

## 2. The Protected Content Minimum and the De-Scoping Order

Two rules keep a heavy week from eating the business: content has a minimum that client work can never take, and past 25 hours the cuts come in an order fixed in advance.

### Content is the first line an overloaded week cuts

Content is the line an overloaded week cuts first, because its cost arrives weeks later, when nobody is looking. Leads come from pieces published weeks before, so a skipped video costs nothing this week and shows up a month on as a thinner calendar, with its cause forgotten. In this category the lag runs long. A burned buyer watches for weeks before he trusts you enough to book, and long-form carries that trust, so every gap is paid for by a skeptic you were halfway to convincing.

So each stage's four content lines form a minimum client work can't take: about 8.5 hours early, 6.75 in Growing, 5.25 at $25k, and 5 at $50k. That's the **Protected Content Minimum**, and no stage's version drops below about 5 hours a week. It holds line by line, because each line feeds a different part of the path to your door: long-form carries trust, short-form reach, email the private room, and replies the first conversations.

**Content goes on the calendar first, seat caps keep clients off it, and no week drops below about 5 hours.**

Three things hold it. Filming happens in batches every two weeks, blocked before any client slot is offered. Seat Math, the monthly seat caps set from your measured care minutes, keeps client work from ever needing those hours (Module 6). And your weekly review flags any week under the minimum, so a slide gets caught in days.

### Past 25 hours, the cuts come in a fixed order

Decide the cuts before the week arrives, because inside an overloaded week everything looks urgent and the loudest line wins. When a week runs past 25 hours, cut in this order, cheapest now first:

1. **Building beyond the protected line, and experiments.** Their payoff is the longest and least certain, so a pause costs nothing this month. The build line itself stays, and if the wrong project sits on it, you swap the project and keep the hours.
2. **Extra platforms: X first, then native Instagram, keeping the routing.** X goes first because its job, testing arguments in public, sits furthest from your door. Instagram's own content goes next, while the Keyword Route, the DM keyword that returns your door link, keeps running, because adult leads arrive there. That's the Platform Count Rule's logic: run only the platforms your editing budget can serve (Module 25).
3. **Short-form above its weekly minimum.** Past your stage's line, short-form is mostly hook testing, and a short view yields far fewer leads than a long-form view.
4. **Long-form above its minimum cadence.** That's every other week early, three a month in Growing, and two to three at scale. Above it, a cut costs trust slowly.
5. **New conversations.** Pause booking and tighten the Call Cap, your weekly limit on free conversations. This step comes last because it costs revenue this month.

That's the **De-Scoping Order**. Five things sit outside it and are never cut: review turnaround, milestones, Hold and Private deliverables, the content minimum, and the Operator Review. The first three are the product, and they're guaranteed, so a cut there returns as a service credit and a client who stops trusting the review. The minimum protects the leads a month out, and the review is the hour that tells you whether the order worked.

The order protects selling on purpose. Building, platforms, and extra content all go before a single conversation does, and even at step 5 the offer stays on sale. Warm pages keep their public prices and checkouts, and a verified, good-fit buyer who writes in gets one recommendation and a checkout link, minutes of work where a call takes an hour.

### Two weeks at step 5 means capacity binds

One heavy week is noise. Two weeks running at step 5 means capacity binds, and the pause stops being a rescue and becomes a cap you set on purpose. Seat Math recomputes the next start's cap now, from this month's measured minutes, and that usually takes seats off. The build line moves to whichever template cuts care minutes next, and the next Price Step, a small raise on your published schedule, keeps its date (Module 7). Booking reopens once the lower cap holds the week under 25. More hours never join that list, because a week past 25 is what the order exists to end.

**When the signals disagree.** A piece breaks out, and inquiries run at [three times] normal for [two] weeks. The hours say step 5; the revenue says take every call. Run step 5 as written: tighten the Call Cap until free conversations fit their line, about four a week in Growing, and cap paid overflow assessments at what your slack can buy, since each costs 0.5–1.5 hours. Warm pages keep selling, and everyone else hears your next real start. Keep the content minimum; the next piece converts the new viewers. Still at step 5 after two weeks, spike or not, you're at capacity. The Launch Line, the test that keeps a date honest, holds as written (Module 8).

## 3. The Unpriced-Minute Rule

Overruns come from minutes spent on one person that nothing governs, so the rule for them starts in your log, and every such minute leaves it with a fee, a window, or a template.

### A leak is a minute with no fee, no window, and no template

Start from the log. When a line runs past its design, list what filled it, and mark every minute spent on one named person that nothing controls: no fee pays for it, no window holds it, and no template shapes it. The untimed fit conversation is the classic leak. Booked free and never told when to end, it drifts past an hour, so the four a Growing line budgets at 45 minutes each, all-in, take a third more than their line. The rest hide the same way inside lines that look healthy: the DM answered whenever it arrives, the no-show rebooked a third time, the client question answered at 11 at night.

Each minute's answer depends on what it's for. When he's buying judgment, charge for it, the way the paid assessment turns a free overflow hour into paid work. When it needs you live, give it a window: a fixed slot, a fixed length, and no booking anywhere else. When its words repeat, template them once, approve them, and reuse them. Many minutes take two answers, and none takes zero. That's the **Unpriced-Minute Rule**:

**Every per-person minute is priced, batched, or templated.**

| Per-person minute | The answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Rate me" or "look at my jaw" | Comments: hidden under your policy, no reply. DMs: your "rate me" template (Module 25), which rates nothing and points to the door | A free read is a face rating, and the asker may be a minor |
| Fit conversations | Batched: a 20–30-minute slot inside a live window; past the Call Cap, priced as the paid assessment | An untimed call is the costliest leak in a Growing week |
| DMs | Batched into a fixed daily slot; templated by case | DMs are where minors and distress show up most, so you work them yourself |
| Speed-to-lead replies | Templated, then batched into reply windows as leads grow | Speed lifts booking, and a template keeps it fast |
| Fit-check conversations, the talk a signal on your pre-payment check opens | Batched into a live window; templated questions; never skipped | Skipping one sells into a signal |
| Follow-up after a held conversation | Templated: a recap within 24 hours, one check-in on his date, one close-the-loop | A fixed count can't turn into chasing |
| No-shows and reschedules | The no-show rule: templated, and rebooked only into a window | It ends the chase before one starts |
| Client questions between check-ins | Batched into his next review; distress the same day | The weekly review is the product, and on-call help isn't |

### Calls and live replies get 2–3 fixed windows a week

Give calls and live replies 2–3 fixed windows a week, and let your booking page show only those slots. Live work means fit conversations, live assessments, group calls, Private calls, and fit-check conversations. The reason is review. The reviewer's job includes catching quiet signals, such as a rising urge to check his face, a distress line in a log, or a capture shot under different light, and a day chopped up by calls misses them. A missed signal is exactly what your service guarantee pays for with free corrective weeks. Put at least one window outside working hours, since the core buyer works full-time and many keep this pursuit private at work.

### One rule covers every missed call

The no-show rule is one templated rebooking offer, and a second miss releases the slot. Its time sits on the half-hour line for no-shows, recaps, and follow-up. Bookings land within 24–48 hours with one reminder, because show rates fall the further out a slot sits. A reschedule request gets the same offer. If he misses the rebooked slot, it goes back on the calendar, and the written path his self-assessment gave him carries him until he books again.

Nobody chases him with a second message, a "still interested?" sequence, or a fee after the fact, and the reason is who misses. A man who books on a hopeful night and loses his nerve by morning is common in a category this close to shame, and a reproachful chase confirms what he was afraid of. If no-shows still eat your selling hours once bookings land fast with a reminder, switch the fit conversation to a small credited booking fee, the door's own option for exactly this (Module 5).

## 4. The Keep-List, Routing Help, and the Build Queue

Four kinds of work never leave your hands, production goes to freelancers billed by the task, public routing goes to a helper with hard limits, and the build line works through a queue ranked by hours saved.

### Four kinds of work stay with you

The Keep-List is the work that never leaves your hands: claims approval, selling, review, and client communication. Each one carries a line of the House Standard, the playbook's rules for persuasion and proof, or the product itself, so handing one off hands away what buyers came to trust you with.

- **Claims approval.** Every title, thumbnail, caption, and clip is a claim, and in a category built on structural promises one careless caption can undo a year of your Honest Answer, your straight public answer to the bone question. Public replies beyond a templated link are claims too.
- **Selling.** The Fit Check, your plain-language check before any paid step, the affordability question about paying without new credit, and the Stop Rules that end a sale all take judgment in the moment. So does asking a good-fit buyer and naming what waiting costs him.
- **Review.** It's the product, and your written terms promise that every line of judgment in it is yours.
- **Client communication.** His check-ins, captures, and messages hold what he tells nobody else, and distress shows up in them first.

### Freelancers take production, billed by the task

Production goes out first: long-form editing, then clips cut from it, thumbnails, design, and scheduling. Start with editing, because it's the largest block of hours in which your judgment isn't the product, and the Early week's long-form line already assumes an editor. So the first freelancer arrives with your first long-form pieces, paid from founding revenue. Billing per piece keeps the business solo, and any handoff can end the week it stops paying for itself. Every derivative comes back to you before it ships, since a clip cut away from its caveat is a new claim you never made. Freelancers never see a capture, a check-in, or a client's message.

### Routing help moderates, links, and stops there

From Growing, public comments outgrow your reply line, and the Keep-List still holds. Routing help, a helper who works only your public comments, does two jobs and nothing else:

- It's task-billed per batch, never paid by the hour, and never staff.
- It moderates public comments against your published comment policy. Face-rating requests ("rate me") get hidden under that policy, with no reply.
- It sends one templated door link to anyone asking where to start.
- Anything revealing distress, a minor, a purchase question, or client content goes to you at once, and it never answers any of them.
- It never sees check-ins or fit-check answers, and it never works DMs, email, or any reply to a client.

Each limit follows a line. A comment that reveals a minor needs the education lane, a public answer and an orthodontist referral with nothing collected, and only you give it. Distress needs a referral conversation the day it appears, so "at once" means when the helper sees it, never at the end of a batch. Purchase questions need the checks every paid step runs, and DMs and email stay yours because minors disclose, distress arrives, and buyers ask about price there.

A rating request gets no reply because even a polite refusal keeps the ask on display and invites the next one. The templated link counts as routing, never contact, since the door asks his age before it keeps anything. Honest criticism is never removed; the helper flags the best of it for you to answer.

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| You do all of it | Early, while replies start conversations | Content hours as your comments grow |
| Routing help, billed per batch | From Growing, once moderation and door-link replies keep overrunning your reply line | A per-batch fee, a written brief, and your weekly spot-check |
| An auto-reply tool on comments | Never, for routing | It can't tell distress from a question, so it answers a man in distress with a link, in public |

**Default: you through Early, then routing help from Growing.** Early replies start conversations and need your judgment, while later ones are mostly moderation a brief can hold. Switch once moderation and door-link replies keep overrunning your replies line, about an hour a week in Growing, the trigger you list among your Scaling Triggers, the signals set in advance for each move (Module 12).

### The Build Queue runs in order of hours saved

The build line keeps its size whatever the week does, and hours freed elsewhere go back to slack. Its own hours go to the Build Queue, your leverage builds ranked by hours saved each week per hour spent building. Take the first item whose trigger has fired, one at a time, since a build started early freezes your weakest version into the product:

1. **Templated review.** Review is the biggest recurring care line: 12–20 minutes per client-week for your first 20 or so clients, 6–10 once templated. Build it once about 20 clients have taught you what a good review says, and disclose how reviews are made before any tool helps.
2. **The Decision Assessment template.** The paid written plan takes 1–1.5 hours all-in early and 0.5–0.75 once templated, and the saving starts the week the Call Cap overflows.
3. **The Async Arc.** Carrying the call's arc through pages, emails, and a recorded walkthrough moves half or more of Scaling enrollments off your calendar (Module 20).
4. **Round Two and Hold terms.** Written once, they end the per-person negotiation at every graduation.
5. **Private deliverables.** Fixed deliverables make a seat priceable, and checkable against the Parity Rule.
6. **The Self-Serve System.** These tools for DIY buyers wait for about 20 graduates, and they're the biggest build with the smallest weekly saving.

## 5. Systems, AI, and Privacy Operations

Keep the stack short, give AI a written scope, and run privacy as two records you could show any client, because every system you add is one more place his face or words can leak and one more voice that could speak for you.

### One tool per job, and a tool defined in writing

Delivery needs two systems named in his written terms: the check-in form he fills in each week, and the one secure store that holds his records and captures. In those terms a tool is anything beyond those two, and any tool that would touch his check-ins, from a spreadsheet that tallies his logs to an AI summary, is a separate opt-in. The rest stays short: an owned site with the self-assessment and checkout, one email tool, one booking tool showing only your windows, one processor with buy-now-pay-later and third-party financing off, and a shared folder of raw footage for freelancers.

Add a tool only when it takes minutes off a line or removes a risk. Before it touches anything a client gave you, answer four questions in writing: what it touches, who can see it, where it stores, and how it deletes. A tool that can't answer the fourth stays away from check-ins.

### AI drafts your words, never his review

The AI rule, "AI assists behind the scenes, never speaks as you, and clients are told where it helps," sets three limits. Behind the scenes means drafting first versions of your own words: scripts, outlines, captions, email copy, and the templates you rewrite and approve, plus transcribing your own footage and suggesting clips. It never means a review or a reply to a client or a lead, because your terms promise every line of judgment is yours and that no tool reads his written answers. You write every review and every reply, or approve each line from your own template.

Never speaking as you means no AI avatar, no cloned voice, and no AI-written reply sent in your name. The line on fabricated proof covers the rest: no AI-made or AI-edited face or result, ever. No tool reads a capture either, because software that measures a face is face analysis, whatever the tool calls it.

**AI drafts your words, tools touch his numbers only with consent, and nothing reads his face.**

He's told where it helps through the review disclosure in his written terms, which names each opted-in tool and what it touches, numbers only. A client who declines gets the same review, with his numbers tallied by hand. Record each consent with its date and scope, because the disclosure is only as good as the record behind it.

### Privacy runs on a retention map and a consent log

What the client experiences belongs to delivery: captures seen only by you, pseudonymous and camera-optional cohorts, deletion on request (Module 6). Operations make those promises true, and they come down to one map and one log.

| Record | Who can see it | How long it's kept |
|---|---|---|
| Self-assessment answers | You; your email tool gets tags, never answers | [Your stated period], or until he asks |
| Fit-check answers | You only; never a marketing tool or a helper | [A short stated period], then deleted |
| Check-ins | You, in the record store; an opted-in tool sees numbers only | While he's a client or in the Hold, then [period] |
| Captures | You only, in the record store; no tool reads them | As check-ins; published only with separate consent |
| Client messages | You only; no helper or tool answers them | As check-ins |
| Call recordings | You, recorded only with his consent | [A short stated period] for your own review, then deleted |
| Consent records | You | As long as what he agreed to is in use |
| Payment records | You and your processor | As the law requires |

The consent log holds every yes with its scope and date: an opted-in tool on his check-ins, a capture or testimonial in marketing, his record in a consented dataset. Each is separate and revocable, and you check the log before anything publishes. A deletion request is closed in every place on the map, then confirmed to him in writing. No hub page carries an ad pixel, and health-adjacent answers stay out of event names and URLs, where an ad platform would read them.

### A minor found late is deleted the same day

A minor found after the age fork, your door's legal-adult question, leaves for the education lane with a full refund (Module 5). Operationally, that means deleting his data the same day from every place on the map, email tags and recordings included, and keeping no list of minors, hashed or otherwise. A list of who was underage is a minor's data kept after the minor is gone, and the fork asks again if he ever returns.

## 6. The Risk Register

Six legal and platform risks are big enough to write down, and each arrives here by a route you can learn to spot.

### Each flag arrives as an ordinary week's event

Legal risk here sits where the category's history does: young audiences, health-adjacent claims, money taken over months, and private data about faces. It shows up as a dispute notice, a rejected ad, or a parent's email, and the Risk Register, your short list of the biggest legal and platform risks, exists so you read that event as a flag. Six are few enough to read at every quarterly Operator Review, and again whenever you add an offer, a tool, a country, or a paid channel. The register says when to buy advice and never stands in for it.

| Flag | How it arrives here | Your default | Get advice when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payment processor and disputes | A charge he doesn't recognize under your discreet name; at solo volume one dispute can breach a tolerance near one in a hundred transactions | A descriptor in your brand's name with no face or jaw words; refunds within 7 days, before disputes; at most one dispute per rolling 90 days early | A processor warns you, holds funds, or questions your business model |
| Installment plans as consumer credit | A buyer where local law counts even your own installments as credit | Your own installments only, at most three, inside delivery; no lender or buy-now-pay-later | Before you offer a plan, and again for each new country |
| Health-adjacent ads, claims, and endorsements | An ad rejected for a condition word, a clip stripped of its range, or a product name that sounds structural, then a health label on your site | Process language, no medical or dental opinion; outcome claims only from your records, with range and denominator; testimonials consented and typical, any reward disclosed | Before your first paid ad, published outcome range, testimonial, or product name |
| Minors and platform policy | A 16-year-old who ticked "adult", a parent's email, or an appearance short age-restricted; adulthood starts at 19 in two US states and 21 in one state and one territory | The age fork; the education lane; same-day deletion and a refund when a minor surfaces | A platform strikes or age-restricts your account, or a parent writes to you |
| Data privacy | A deletion request, an add-on quietly reading check-ins, or a first client where face data has special protection | The retention map and consent log; captures coach-only; no tool that measures faces | Before any new tool touches client data, and before your first client in a new region |
| Statutory cancellation | A UK or EU client cancelling in his first 14 days, or a Hold renewal he says he didn't expect | Those rights stated ahead of your fit window; a reminder before each Hold charge, with a one-click exit | Before your terms go live, and before selling a subscription in a new country |

### A dispute gets read before it's argued

In month [7], Cole gets a dispute notice on a client's second installment, marked "unrecognized". The signs: the client's check-ins arrived on time through week [5], no refund request sits in the inbox, and the charge ran under [BRAND CO], the discreet name his privacy terms promise, which the pre-charge reminder never named. Among [20] charges that month, one dispute is [5%] of transactions, five times the line processors watch. The read is recognition, not quality: the name that protects the client's privacy is the one his statement couldn't place.

So Cole answers the processor with the accepted terms and the delivery record, sends the client one plain message naming the charge, and puts the exact descriptor in every pre-charge reminder. The dispute is withdrawn [that week]. He leaves alone the discreet name, a second message, any fee, and paid advice, since the flag's trigger hasn't fired.

## Worked Example: Reid's [29]-Hour Growing Week, Redesigned

Reid is Growing in month [19], six weeks after the [31]-hour week that opened this module. He has about [22] concurrent clients across three monthly starts, the Program at [$2.4k], and eligible leads down from [about 90] a month to [about 60]. ## Checklists: The De-Scoping Card and the Risk Register Review

### The de-scoping card

Run it the Monday after any week past 25 hours, from last week's log.

1. Which lines ran over their design, and which per-person minutes filled them? → Give each a fee, a window, or a template this week.
2. Anything beyond your build line, or any experiment, on this week's list? → Pause it; the build line keeps its hours.
3. Publishing natively beyond long-form, short-form, email, and the Keyword Route? → Drop X, then native Instagram.
4. Short-form above its weekly minimum (3.5 h Early, 1.25 Growing, 0.5 Scaling)? → Cut it to the line.
5. Long-form above its minimum cadence (every other week Early, three a month Growing, two to three Scaling)? → Cut it to the cadence.
6. Still past 25? → Tighten the Call Cap and pause booking; verified, good-fit buyers still get a recommendation and a checkout link.
7. Did turnaround, a milestone, a Hold or Private deliverable, the content minimum, or the Operator Review slip? → Put it back first.
8. Second week at item 6? → Capacity binds: recompute Seat Math now and put the next care-minute template on the build line.

### The Risk Register review

Run it quarterly at the Operator Review, from your own records.

1. More than one dispute in any rolling 90 days, or a descriptor that isn't your brand's discreet name? → Investigate each dispute; fix the descriptor before the next charge.
2. Any plan sold where you haven't checked whether installments count as credit? → Stop plans there until you're advised.
3. Any title, page, ad, product name, or testimonial that implies structural change, names a condition, or hides a reward? → Pull it this week.
4. Did any minor surface after the fork this quarter, by your tally (a count, no names), without same-day deletion and a refund? → Fix the step that failed.
5. Any tool beyond your check-in form and record store processing check-ins without a recorded opt-in? → Switch it off until consent is on file.
6. Any deletion request open past [your stated period]? → Close it everywhere on the map and confirm in writing.
7. Do your terms state statutory cancellation rights, and does the Hold remind before each charge with a one-click exit? → No: fix it before the next sale.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Reach binds, so content takes the biggest share of the week and conversations the next. The default move is to block the short-form batches and your live windows first, then spend the build line on the door and the founding assets. The trap is long personal answers to every comment and DM while volume is low, habits that become a Growing week's unpriced minutes.

**Growing.** Selling minutes and untemplated review bind together, which makes this the heaviest design, about 22 hours with the thinnest slack. The default is to hold the build line through bad months and bring in routing help once its trigger fires. The trap is skipping the build line to survive a heavy month, which keeps every later month heavy.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind, yet the week falls back to about 20 hours at both configurations because templates do the work. The default is to hand derivatives out, approve every claim yourself, and read two weeks at step 5 as a seat and template decision. The trap is letting routing help drift into DMs because it's quicker, which puts minors, distress, and purchase questions in front of someone who can't hold those lines.

## Standard Check

- **Claims never leave your hands.** Every title, caption, thumbnail, and clip a freelancer makes passes your approval before it ships, AI drafts only your own words, and no tool makes or edits a face or a result (the line on structural claims; the line on fabricated proof).
- **Routing help routes, and that's all.** It hides rating requests without a reply and posts one templated link. It never sells, makes a claim, works a DM or an email, or sees a check-in or fit-check answer, and anything touching a minor or distress reaches you at once (the line on vulnerability).
- **Consent before any tool.** Any tool beyond the check-in form and record store needs his recorded opt-in, and captures stay coach-only (the house rules on AI and privacy).
- **Overload cuts selling last.** Building, platforms, and extra content go before any conversation, and at step 5 warm pages keep their prices while verified, good-fit buyers get one recommendation and a checkout link (*Sell directly*).
