# 21 · Onboarding, Adherence and the Plateau
*Part V — Selling and Transformation*

**The shift:** from *"Results depend on the client's discipline."* to *"Adherence is designed. Every plateau renews the decision, and some end in an honest exit."*

Sunday night, week 5. The check-in from Dan *(composite, Struggler)*, 24, arrives at [9:40 pm]: [2] of [7] days logged, after four weeks at six or seven. His plan-fit rating reads [1], and where his question usually goes there's one line: "I don't think this is working." He has been here before. His eight months of solo effort ended in a week like this one, with a flat stretch nobody could read and a quiet decision to stop. The category has taught him to hear any stall as a verdict, on the method, on his genes, or on him.

What happens in the next 48 hours decides three things you care about. It decides whether he asks for a refund at week 6, whether his week-12 record argues for more coaching, and whether he ever sends a friend your way. Very little of it turns on his discipline. It turns on what you built before his first bad week: terms he signed on day one, a first win he could check, and a review that reaches him before he goes quiet. It also turns on a plan, written in advance, for exactly this Sunday. This module builds that design, and the honest exit for the plateaus that should end.

## 1. Why Adherence Is Designed

Whether a client follows through depends more on what you build than on the discipline he brings. The tools with the best evidence behind them are things you design, so that's where your hours pay. Effort still matters. It's the part you can't install.

### A record helps most when someone reads it

Tracking progress toward a goal has a moderate effect on reaching it. The effect grows when progress is written down and reported to someone who reads it, and that last condition is what his eight months lacked. He checked constantly, in the mirror and in comparison photos. Nothing was recorded the same way twice, and nobody turned any of it into a decision. That's how a man in this category spends a year both busy and uninformed.

Unguided products show the same thing from the other side. Self-paced courses and apps keep a few percent of their users past the first weeks, and paying lifts completion partly because paying selects the committed. Most men at your door have already run that experiment with a device, a course, or a feed of tips. So the Program, your 12-week flagship, asks about 10 minutes a week for his check-in. Your review turns that log into his next decision, the part no course can ship.

### Contact you start keeps him from going quiet

Encouragement that reaches out on a schedule gives better results and roughly half the dropout of self-help. Support he has to ask for adds little, and more contacts a week matter more than the total number of sessions. The reason bites harder here than in most coaching, because shame sits close to this work. A man who has missed four days on a program about his face doesn't write to his coach that night. He waits for a better week, and silence feels safer than a report.

"Message me anytime" hands the burden of contact to the man least able to carry it. Contact you start can't be dodged, because the check-in is due whether his week went well or not. Your review then arrives on schedule, before he feels ready to be reached. Format matters less than rhythm: at equal intensity, one-to-one, group, and guided formats do about equally well.

### Small tools stack, and none of them is the engine

If-then plans, a pre-set "when this happens, I do that", help less than their headlines suggest once bias is corrected. The effect is real and small. Give them five minutes on his first day and keep them off your sales page. No single tactic doubles adherence, and a page that claims one is selling a hero the evidence can't support.

Habits get the same honesty. Automaticity takes a median of about two months and ranges from days to most of a year. So many of his habits won't run on their own by week 12, which sets his expectations and explains why some records need the months after the Program. It never sets a date for an outcome, least of all one on his face.

**Adherence is built from a record he keeps, a reader who answers on a clock, and contact you start before he goes quiet.**

## 2. The Commit Ritual and the First-14

The first fortnight decides whether he judges the Program on evidence or on doubt, so it runs on two designed moments. One makes the terms clear on his first day. The other hands him a result he can check before his fit window, a full refund in his first 14–21 days of delivery, closes.

Doubt peaks after a costly purchase: unease, second-guessing, a worry that he overpaid. Here it cuts deeper, because every earlier purchase felt like this for a week before it turned out to be a course, a device, or a feed of tips. Reassurance can't answer that. Preparation can, since knowing what a process involves tends to reduce dropout, and so can real work that registers on a record he can read.

### Baseline day makes the terms clear and asks for nothing else

Baseline day, the Program's first review-and-decision day, produces his first capture, his first log week, and his markers, the two or three pre-agreed measures that decide his verdict (Module 6). Inside that day, in about [25] minutes, three things happen. First, you read his week-zero record, the days he logged before day one, and decide the first change from it: [his habit block] moves to [before his shift], and nothing else changes for [two] weeks.

Second, he writes two or three lines, in his own words, on what he wants these twelve weeks to settle. Third, he signs off the Expectation Document, the written terms he read before paying: the time, what can and can't move, how plateaus are handled, his adherence threshold, and his markers. You close by naming his exits plainly, with the fit window's last date written in. That's the **Commit Ritual**, and its one job is clarity:

**Decide the first change from his record, let him write his reasons for you alone, and sign the terms with every exit in view.**

The ritual stays small on purpose. Pledges do less for follow-through than their reputation suggests, and an identity announced to an audience can feel like progress already made. A pledge would also sit beside a refund right: a man who declares himself all in on day 0 has to break his word to use a fit window that runs to day [16]. So nothing in the ritual is public or promised. His reasons are his to reread whenever he likes. You read them only to check that the work still fits his goal, and you never quote them back to keep him, at a plateau or in any exit.

### The first fortnight answers doubt with his record

Two moves carry the fortnight. Within 72 hours of baseline day, a short note reads his first [two or three] days back to him and names one fact from them, such as the new slot holding twice. It proves the rhythm is proactive before he needs it, while doubt runs highest and no habit exists yet. A templated welcome doesn't count, because it shows nothing about whether anyone reads.

The second move is a win chosen on baseline day: a result about his practice that his record will show by day 14. The default is his first change read against week zero, such as shift-day misses falling from [3] to [1]. Size it so most clients reach it. Keep his appearance out of it, since captures wait for their dates, and state it as a fact from his record. Earned Labels, praise built on a count from his record, wait until the fit window closes, so no label sits beside a refund decision (Module 17).

Together the two moves are the **First-14**: a reviewer's touch inside 72 hours and a win he can verify inside the first fortnight. Succeeding at something he can check is how a man comes to believe he can do the steps, and it's what his eight months never gave him.

### Doubt gets evidence, and a decision to leave gets a refund

Remorse inside the fit window takes two forms. "I'm second-guessing this" is doubt, so answer it with the plan and the day his record will settle it. By day 14 he'll see whether [the new slot] survives his shifts. Then say once, plainly, that the window runs to [date] and one line claims a full refund. "I want out" is a decision. The refund goes out within 7 days, any conversation stays optional and feedback-only, and nothing in it re-pitches or mentions his reasons.

The First-14 exists so his fit decision rests on delivered review, which is why the window closes just after his [second] weekly review. It's never built to carry him past that date. A man who leaves on day [12] takes a full refund and his fortnight's record, and that clean exit costs far less than the dispute a harder one invites.

## 3. Running Proactive Review

A weekly review keeps him logging only while he trusts the person writing it, so every review reads the relationship as closely as the record. The Review Rhythm, proactive review every week on a stated clock plus one live call, is what he bought (Module 6). Its effect runs through the reviewer.

### He follows through for a reviewer he trusts

People keep going for a reviewer they judge competent, well-meaning, and trustworthy, and they judge it inside the loop, whatever titles you hold. The working alliance, agreement on goals and tasks plus a bond, tracks outcomes at a steady correlation of about .3 across many studies. Your client reads each review for signs he's being handled, because the category taught him the signs. Cheer from a template, a new tip every week, praise that doesn't match his log: each one tells him he bought another course.

So check the relationship as often as the log, with two questions: does he feel heard, and does the work fit his goal? After every live call he answers both with one tap each on a [1–5] scale. In every review you read them again in his check-in: his plan-fit rating, the length of his answers, a blank blocker, a question he stopped asking.

That's the Alliance Check, the two-question read on heard and fit. A drop of [2] points, or any answer at [2] or below, opens the next review: name it, ask one question, change one thing. Polite ratings run high, so watch the change more than the level. Read goal fit against the reasons he wrote on baseline day, the one use you make of them.

### Feedback counts what the record shows

Reviews speak in action and data words, such as record, block, plan, and decision, with the warmth kept in. "That's frustrating, and it makes sense" is validation he needs before he can hear a change. That's the Plain-Language Rule, action and data vocabulary with the clinic's words and framing left out (Module 15). A clinic's framing tells him he's receiving care for a condition, when the work he bought is work he directs.

Praise follows the same discipline. Earned Labels lead with a count and name the practice it shows: "[22] of [24] days logged is what doing this properly looks like." Praise his record doesn't show is out, even when it would be kind. At the low end the count stays as plain as anyone's, because flattery about a [3] of [7] week is what he'll later remember as a lie. Labels also pause for the 7 days after his week-6 read, the written mid-program read, for the reason they wait out the fit window.

### The comeback earns the credit

One missed day doesn't undo a forming habit. The story the miss gets can. Blaming a lapse on a lasting flaw, "I always quit", predicts the slide, and one broken rule tends to become "the week's gone anyway". In a very large study of gym members, rewarding the return after a missed workout beat dozens of other tactics. So the rule is Never Miss Twice: reward the comeback, not the streak.

A Struggler's history is a string of broken streaks, and a streak counter turns one miss into a loss he knows too well. So nothing you run counts streaks. Reviews count returns: "[three] misses this month, each followed by a logged day." A missed check-in gets a one-line note the same day, and the day after a miss runs his miss plan, [a minimum version] agreed in review. No message about logging mentions his refund terms, because his log decides his refund eligibility and a reminder would aim his own record at his rights.

Two structural rules protect all of this. Routing help, the task-billed helper who moderates your public comments (Module 11), never sees a check-in. No one but you reads or answers his messages, since check-ins are where distress, checking, and jaw pain first surface. Boards stay opt-in, open only after the fit window, and rank only timestamps, such as check-ins sent on time. No status he holds depends on leaving a refund right unused, and no room or board mentions who left.

**Read the relationship with the record, count only what's true, and give the comeback the credit.**

## 4. The Plateau Plan and the Renewed Decision

Most clients hit a plateau, and whether it becomes a stall, a refund, or a comeback depends on what you set up before it and do in the 48 hours after it shows. Measures like his markers rarely climb in a straight line. Motivation sags in the middle of a goal, when the start is behind him and the finish far off. Here the middle weeks also hold the week-6 read and the exit right that opens with it, a pro-rata refund if he did the work and stops.

### A flat stretch reads as a verdict unless you read it first

A plateau lands on a man the category trained to hear stalls as verdicts. On the method it says "another grift", on his genes "it was always genetics", and on himself "I always quit". Every reading ends in a stop, a refund request, or silence. A flat stretch turns into a verdict when it arrives unexpected, unexplained, and unanswered, so the design removes all three. Announced in advance, it lands as the plan behaving as described. Re-read from his record, it gets a cause he controls. Answered within 48 hours with one change, it can't become a second lost week. That's the **Plateau Plan**, in one line:

**Announce the plateau before it comes, re-plan within 48 hours, reward the comeback, and at week 6 ask the verdict question first.**

| Step | When | What it does | What stays out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Announce | The Expectation Document, read at the Commit Ritual | Names the flat stretch and what you'll do | A promise it will be short |
| Re-attribute | The first review that shows it | Reads when the misses fell, what plan fit did, and how each marker was read, for a cause he controls | "You weren't consistent"; "it's genetics" |
| Re-plan | Within 48 hours of the check-in that shows it | One change in writing; the marker sheet stays as signed | A new plan, a second change, a fresh tip |
| Reward the comeback | Every review after it | Counts the return | Streaks; any mention of refunds |
| Read week 6 in order | The week-6 read | Verdict question, markers, then the honest stall reasons | A search through his photos |

The 48-hour clock starts on any of four signs. They are [two] or more fewer logged days than his average, a plan-fit rating at [1–2], an Alliance Check dip, or any line saying it isn't working. Reid *(composite operator)* answers each of those signs with a pep talk and a new tip. By week 6 he has changed three things in three weeks, and no marker can say which one did anything. One change per plateau keeps the record readable, and the week-6 read can only judge a readable record.

| Option | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Written re-plan (a same-day note or the next review) | Every plateau, by default | [10–15] minutes; it joins his record |
| Voice note | He asked for voice | Harder to reread next week |
| [15]-minute call | The Alliance Check dipped, or a second plateau follows within [three] weeks | Call time your seat caps must carry |

**Default:** written, because he bought written review on a clock, and a re-plan he can reread is one he can follow. Switch to a short call when the Alliance Check dips or a second plateau follows within [three] weeks.

### Week 6 asks the verdict question first

At week 6 the order of the read decides its honesty. The verdict question comes first: given your adherence, is the lever moving? Below his adherence threshold, the record can't judge the lever yet. The decision then turns on fitting the plan to his week, and the exit right isn't his, because it belongs to a client who did the work. At or above the threshold, the markers come next, each read against its baseline wobble. If they're flat, Stall Verdicts decide among the three honest reasons a stall happens: unmeasured, misdirected, or "the lever doesn't move for this person" (Module 14).

"Misdirected" has to earn its label with a named lever, a documented correction, and early movement at the next reading. Without all three, "the lever doesn't move for this person" stands, and nobody relabels it to sell more weeks. If the miss was your own review's, a signal you didn't catch or a step you set wrong, the weeks it cost come back free as corrective weeks, capped at 6 (Module 7). Putting adherence first blocks two classic errors: judging a lever he never ran, and blaming effort he did put in. It also keeps the mirror out of the verdict.

### Every plateau renews the decision

A plateau re-read with his own data reopens the question he answered when he paid. So put it to him plainly: continue as planned, change one thing, or stop. That's **Every Plateau Renews the Decision**. It comes with your one recommendation from the record, because a client left to drift through week 6 is as poorly served as one who's pushed. Between the fit window and the week-6 read, the real choices are continue and adjust, and the exit right waits at the read. After its window closes, stopping is still his, and you tell him plainly what the terms pay.

Then say the hard true thing, with force aimed at the situation. To a man at [26] of [42] days: "[Twenty-six] days can't judge this lever, and I won't pretend they can. The plan hasn't had a fair test yet, and that's the problem to fix, not you." Softening it into "plateaus are normal, hang in there" leaves him alone with the mirror. On the Dignity Route, the way of running a sale or review that keeps force off his worth, the force aims at the guessing, and no missed moment he named comes back to him.

## 5. The Honest Exit and Vulnerability During Delivery

Some plateaus should end, and an exit handled cleanly is part of the product. The programs your burned clients left before ended in arguments about money. A clean exit is also cheaper: refunds and disputes plan at about 2–8% of revenue, and a refund paid on time rarely becomes a dispute.

### A clean exit is part of what he bought

Sometimes he asks to stop under a right he holds, and sometimes his markers say the lever doesn't move. Either way the move is the same: stop, pay per the clause, and refer where his question belongs. That's the **Honest Exit**, run the same way every time:

**Confirm it in writing, pay within 7 days, refer where a trigger applies, and ask for nothing.**

The exit right runs from the week-6 read until 7 days after it's delivered, for a client at his adherence threshold. A written request is enough. The undelivered weeks, about half the fee, come back within 7 days, and any remaining installments stop. The read states the right in the same letter as the plan, just as plainly, with no label or status line beside it.

At week 12 the non-response clause pays a fixed cash share inside 25–50% within 7 days of the verdict, and no renewal offer follows (Module 7). You raise it before he asks. "Haven't moved" means no marker reached its threshold, so a single crossing means the lever moves for him and nothing is owed. A client below the adherence threshold isn't covered. He gets an honest verdict, and the week-6 exit was his route out.

Three things stay out of every exit: his written reasons, a re-pitch, and a testimonial ask. The ask never comes in a conversation where a refund right is decided, and never goes to a client who claimed one. He keeps his record, a closing count of what he did, and his standing among the adults who measure, a membership the practice earned. Nobody in the room hears about it from you. Where his remaining question belongs to someone else, the Scope Boundary applies: "I can't help with this, and here's who can" (Module 15). Its triggers are sleep or snoring signs, jaw pain, bite concerns, and distress or fixation.

### Rising checking or distress pauses every ask

Weekly review puts you closest to him in the weeks a checking habit can grow, so read for it in every check-in. The signs are a climb in how often he checks his face, interim photos sent to you, a request to rate a capture, distress in his answers, or "everything is ruined". Any one of them pauses persuasion: no renewal offer, no referral or testimonial ask, no hard true thing, no sharing, no board. The scheduled captures stop. The pause route, a content-free tag that stops automated sales sends for 60–90 days, goes on too, so your tools stop with you.

Then the Scope Boundary runs as a referral conversation. It names a doctor or a mental-health professional, with crisis resources if it's acute, and it names no condition. His behavior review continues if he wants it, with the same warmth, and every refund layer holds on its terms. His markers are read without photos, so his verdict loses nothing. Record only what the referral needs, and for any stopped sale, "stopped: stop rule".

**When the signals disagree.** Say a client logs [42] of [42] days and sends every check-in on time. In week [6] he mentions he has started photographing his side profile every morning "to see if it's working". The log says model client, maybe one due a label. The photos say a checking habit is growing around the work. The checking outranks the log, because the log counts his habit blocks and says nothing about his relationship with the mirror.

So the rising-checking rules run, even with every other number perfect. Name it once in the review, calmly. Explain why captures run only on fixed dates: a phone at arm's length distorts a face, and checking tends to feed the dissatisfaction it's meant to settle. Ask how often he checks and how he feels afterward, then have the referral conversation plainly. Credit his days as the count they are, with no label that makes the photos part of the achievement, and hold every ask while the checking lasts. If he asks what he's losing, the answer is nothing that decides his verdict.

## 6. Adherence Data as the Renewal Case

His renewal case at week 12 is written in weeks 1 to 11 or not at all, from data the review collects anyway. Measured momentum, the qualifier for Round Two, the maintenance block after graduation, has two halves (Module 10). His adherence sits at or above his threshold, and at least one marker is still improving across its last two readings. Both halves must be on the record before anyone asks, so capture five things now:

1. Days logged each week, with comebacks counted, since the threshold reads all twelve weeks.
2. Every marker reading on schedule through weeks 10–12, since "its last two readings" needs two readings near the end.
3. His effort and plan-fit ratings by [habit block], which show which habits still take effort. Automaticity takes a median of about two months and up to most of a year, so many won't run alone by week 12. That's the honest case for maintenance, and never a timeline for his face.
4. Every re-plan, with its named lever, its correction, and the next reading, so a corrected misdirection has its evidence and a review error shows its free weeks.
5. The Alliance Check's trend, which says whether the relationship can carry a recommendation.

The same data tells some men they don't need more, and "you don't need Round Two" belongs to the case as much as a renewal does. Nothing is asked at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit. The ask belongs to the Renewal Case, argued from his record at the week-12 re-assessment, the closing capture and verdict (Module 22). Everything captured stays in the record he takes with him.

## Scripts: The Commit Ritual, the Plateau Conversation, and the Honest Exit

### The Commit Ritual, on baseline day

Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, travels most weeks for work and joined under a handle, camera off.

> **You:** "Your week zero: [five] of [seven] days logged, both misses on travel days. So the first decision comes from that: [your routine] moves to [the slot before your first meeting], wherever you wake up, and nothing else changes for [two] weeks. Does that fit?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*
>
> **Adrian:** "It fits. What will my markers read at week 12?"
>
> **You:** "I can't give you that number, and anyone who does is guessing. I can give you the precision I control: two markers with thresholds you sign today, and a written read at week 6 on whether they're moving. Expect a flat stretch somewhere in the middle; page [two] says what we'll do. Last, two lines in your own words on what you want these twelve weeks to settle. They're for me, so every review aims at them."

Notice the move. The ritual opened on his record and turned his demand for a forecast into precision you control: signed thresholds, a dated read, a plateau announced in advance. That's the Commit Ritual doing its one job, clarity. His reasons reach you as the reference for goal fit, and they stay out of everything else.

The pushback comes as doubt:

> **Adrian:** "Honestly, I'm half wondering whether I should've just done this myself."
>
> **You:** "Fair, and common in the first days after paying. By day 14 your record shows whether [the pre-meeting slot] survives your travel, which beats a feeling either way. Your fit window runs to [date], and one line gets a full refund, no call needed."

What you never say: "You just wrote that you're done guessing, so let's not back out now." Spending his reasons on his refund right turns the ritual into the device it exists to avoid. Then return to the decision: "So: [the pre-meeting slot] from tomorrow, with the terms as written. Sign if they read right."

### The plateau conversation, at week 8

Sam *(composite, Ambivalent)*, 22, an apprentice electrician, hits his busy season [three] weeks early. His check-in shows [3] of [7] days and a note: he might "pause till after the busy season". He takes a [15]-minute call inside the 48 hours.

> **You:** "Here's your record. Seven weeks at six or seven days, then three, and every miss on a [twelve-hour site day]. Your [posture tally] went from [2] to [5] and has read [5] twice. That's the flat stretch page [two] of your terms described, landing in your busiest [three] weeks. I'd keep the record running with one change: on site days, [the minimum version] after your shift, and the full plan back on [date]."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*
>
> **Sam:** "Can't I just pause and pick it up after?"
>
> **You:** "The terms don't have a pause, so it's continue, adjust, or stop. Here's the hard part, straight: 'after the busy season' is how the last [two] years went. A plan that only runs in quiet months stops every busy season. Let's build the version that survives this one."

Notice the move. The record came first, so the cause he heard was timing on site days, which he controls, and the flat reading arrived as the stretch his terms predicted. That's the Plateau Plan's re-read and one-change re-plan. The hard true thing aimed at the plan and the waiting, and left his worth alone.

If he pushes on money:

> **Sam:** "If I stop now, do I get anything back?"
>
> **You:** "Not at week 8. Your terms put the exit at the week-6 read and the clause at week 12. I'd rather that didn't decide it. Decide on whether [the minimum version] fits a site day."

What you never say: "Stop now and you lose your week-12 refund, so you might as well keep going." That turns a refund right into a consistency device. Then return to the decision: "[The minimum version] after site days, check-in Sunday as usual, full plan back on [date]. Yes?"

### The honest exit, at week 12

Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, logged [77] of [84] days, every reading clean, and neither marker reached its threshold. He has asked twice about [his bite]. You raise the clause before he asks.

> **You:** "Here's your verdict. [77] of [84] days logged, every marker read the same way each time, and neither reached its threshold. Your effort isn't the question, and I won't make it one. This lever doesn't move for you on this plan. So [a fixed share inside 25–50%] of what you paid comes back in cash by [date], inside 7 days. Your record is yours: every log, every capture with its conditions, the marker sheet."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*
>
> **Theo:** "Could we call it misdirected and try another lever? I'd pay for another round."
>
> **You:** "No. Misdirected needs a lever I can name as wrong for your goal, a correction on paper, and early movement after it, and your record has none of the three. Another round would be the [device] again with better paperwork. Your question about [your bite] belongs with an orthodontist, and I'll send a note today with what to ask."

Notice the move. The verdict arrived with its evidence and its payment in one breath, and his request for more time met the standard instead of a sale. That's the Honest Exit: stop, pay per the clause, refer. For a burned buyer, a coach who turns down his money sends the signal his last two purchases never did.

If he offers something back:

> **Theo:** "You're the first one who's been straight with me. Want me to write something up?"
>
> **You:** "Thank you. Not in this conversation. Today is your verdict and your refund, and nothing else."

What you never say: "On day one you wrote that you wanted to know for sure, and now you do." His reasons stay out of every exit, even dressed as a compliment. Then close on the facts and stop: the refund lands by [date], the note goes out today, and no Round Two offer follows.

**The Alliance Check.** Run it on every client before you write this week's reviews.

1. Did he answer both pulse items after this week's call? → A blank pulse counts as a dip.
2. Did either item fall [2] points, or land at [2] or below? → The next review opens on it: name it, ask one question, change one thing.

5. Did your last review answer his question in writing and name one fact from his log? → If not, the next one does.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** What binds is knowledge: you don't yet know where plateaus land or what a good re-plan says, and your founding clients will show you. The default move is to log every plateau with its week, pattern, change, and next reading, writing each re-plan from scratch at about 30–35 care minutes, your review and call time, per client-week. The trap is answering the founding group's closeness with "message me anytime", which feels generous and teaches your first clients to wait.

**Growing.** Care minutes start to bind, and a whole start's week-6 reads land in the same week. The default move is to template the plateau response's structure from what the founding plateaus taught you, then batch the reads. The trap is a template that answers without reading, a stock "plateaus are normal" a burned client spots at once.

**Scaling.** At about 13–15 care minutes per client-week, a thin review can miss a plateau until week 6. The default move is numeric flags, used with his consent: a drop of [two] logged days, plan fit at [1–2], or an Alliance Check dip moves his check-in to the top of your reading. You still handle every plateau conversation and every exit yourself. The trap is letting flags decide which check-ins you read at all.

## Standard Check

- **Force at the plateau.** The hard true thing aims at the pattern and the plan, never at his face or worth, and on the Dignity Route it aims at the guessing (*Say the true thing*; the line on shame).
- **Reasons and refunds kept apart.** The Commit Ritual's reasons are never quoted back in any exit, no label sits inside the fit or exit window, and no Never Miss Twice message ties logging to a refund (*Build identity on evidence*).
- **The renewed decision.** Every plateau puts continue, adjust, or stop to him in plain words, and "misdirected" is used only with its three conditions, never to sell more weeks (*Close*).
- **Exits and vulnerability.** The Honest Exit pays within 7 days with no re-pitch and no testimonial ask, and rising checking or distress pauses every ask and offer, with the pause route binding automation (the line on vulnerability).
