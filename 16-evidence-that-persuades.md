# 16 · Evidence That Persuades
*Part IV — The Psychology of Belief*

**The shift:** from *"proof means before/afters and testimonials"* to *"proof is a format a skeptic can audit, plus a test of honest evidence he can apply to anyone."*

Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, checks everything before he buys, because the last device he bought came with a promise about his jaw. Tonight two posts sit one above the other in his feed. The first is a transformation pair: the after shot lit from above, the chin dipped, "[90] days" in bold, a discount code underneath, and no dates anywhere. The second is plainer. It shows two captures of a stranger, taken at a marked distance under the same lamp and dated 12 weeks apart. The caption names what was measured, says he also lost [a few] kilograms over those weeks, and shows how [n] of [N] graduates did on the same measure, the flat ones included.

Theo has seen that overhead light before, so he scrolls past the first. He reads the second twice, opens the dated log behind it, and books a fit conversation, the free first call that ends in one recommendation.

The dramatic pair lost because he knew how it was made. The modest one won because he could check how it was made, what else changed, and who else was counted. In this category, that's what proof is: a format a skeptic can audit, plus a test of honest evidence he can use on anyone, you included. This module builds both, from the first sentence you publish to the log you keep for years.

## 1. Why Proof Fails Here

In this category, more proof persuades less, because your buyer arrives knowing how proof gets made and audits yours the same way.

### He knows how the photos were made

Once a buyer recognizes a persuasion tactic, he discounts the whole message and starts asking what the seller wants, which is among the better-established findings about persuasion. Your buyer has had years of practice at recognizing them: manufactured before/afters, devices sold with a jaw promise attached, and tips that never end. The camera facts behind the first are established and easy to show. A closer phone widens a face, overhead light carves a jawline, a dipped chin redraws a profile, and a few kilograms lost show along the jaw. He has concluded that any photo can be staged, and he's right.

So the dramatic pair works against you. The bigger the change it shows, the harder he hunts for the light, and a page of them reads as the same tell repeated. The Burned Struggler, the buyer who already paid for a structural promise, goes further: he assumes any proof he can't check was made the way his last purchase was sold.

### Volume reads as selection

A pile of best cases raises the one question a pile can't answer: out of how many? Selective publication is how the category's proof got made, so a skeptic reads any result without a count as the top of a hidden stack. Testimonials carry the same flaw. A testimonial that names a result implies the result is typical, regulators read it that way too, and small print saying results vary doesn't undo that. It only turns his question into "by how much?", and a vague hedge costs you more trust than a plain range with its count beside it.

### Formats that survive an audit

Every format you might publish meets the same audit, and most fail it on the first question:

| Format | His first question | It survives when |
|---|---|---|
| A dramatic before/after, uncaptioned | "Same camera, same light, same weight?" | Never, as posted |
| A testimonial that names a result | "Is that typical?" | It sits beside the range everyone got |
| A screenshot of a delighted message | "Who didn't write?" | As a process moment, with consent; never as outcome proof |
| "Results may vary" | "By how much?" | A range with its count replaces it |
| A day-count promise | "Measured how, and on whom?" | Never |
| Matched captures with their context | "What else changed?" | The caption answers before he asks |
| A dated log of process and outcomes | "Did they publish the bad months?" | The flat results are in it |

The survivors share three properties: he can check how the proof was made, what else could explain it, and who else was counted. Build every piece to answer all three before he asks, because to a buyer who audits, an answer he had to request reads as a concession, and one you volunteered reads as a standard.

## 2. The Claim Ladder and the Context Stack

Every claim you publish sits at one of five heights, and the higher it sits, the more context it must carry before it ships.

### Claims climb from what you do to what happens to everyone

The category's captions collapse those heights into one sentence. "Weekly check-ins, and your jaw will sharpen" sets a fact you control beside a forecast about his face, in one breath and with one confidence, and the certainty of the first leaks onto the second. The same collapse turns one client's result into a promise to the next man who reads it.

What separates the heights is how he could check the claim. He can check a process claim himself within a week, because the review arrives on time or it doesn't. He checks an educational claim against how well supported it is. A claim about one man means little without that man's context, and a claim about a group means little without its size and the men it didn't work for. A claim about everyone, a certain result, or his health can't be checked by anyone, which is why the grift lives there. Put in order, every claim has a rung:

| Rung | What it says | In this niche | What it must carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process | What you do and what he receives | "Every check-in gets a written review within [72] hours" | The truth, stated as a point |
| Educational | How something works in general | "Camera distance changes how wide a face reads" | Its evidence tier |
| Observational | What one man's record showed | "His [body-composition marker] crossed its threshold by week 12, with matched captures beside it" | His full context, stated as observation |
| Outcome | What a group's records showed | "[n] of [N] graduates crossed a marker threshold by week 12" | Full context, the range, the denominator, the flat results |
| Universal, guaranteed, or medical | What happens to everyone, for certain, or to his health | "Anyone can build a sharper jaw"; "fixes your bite" | Nothing can carry it |

That's the **Claim Ladder**, and it fits on one line you can say aloud:

**Process → educational → observational → outcome → universal, guaranteed, or medical (never).**

Educational claims carry their evidence tier, how well supported a claim is: established, plausible, or debated. For the bone question, the Outcome Map has already sorted the tiers, your straight answer to what adults can and can't change, by age and evidence (Module 3). So an educational claim about jaws takes its tier from the map, never from how confident you feel that day.

### Sam's sentence comes down the ladder

Cole *(composite operator)* meets the ladder in a check-in. In week [9], Sam *(composite, Ambivalent)*, 22, a founding client, ends his log with a line he wants public: "My jaw is way sharper. This method works. Post it." Every word is sincere, and the sentence sits on the top two rungs. "This method works" is an outcome claim about everyone with no count behind it, and "my jaw is sharper" is a bathroom-mirror reading of the debated column. Cole replies the same day: "Good to hear it. Keep logging, and at week 12 we'll compare matched captures and your markers. That's the only version I'd put in public, and only with your say-so."

At week 12 the record gives Cole what the sentence couldn't. One of Sam's markers, the two or three measures agreed at baseline that decide his verdict, crossed its threshold. His log also shows [a beard shaved off in week 8], a grooming change that redraws a jawline the same afternoon, and at 22 he may still be maturing. With his consent, the published line reads: "Sam, 22: [body-composition marker] crossed its threshold by week 12; matched captures from weeks 0 and 12 below. He also shaved off a beard in [week 8], and at 22 may still be maturing. Observed among clients, not caused." The sentence came down two rungs and gained what the mirror couldn't give it: a reader who can check it.

### Every observation carries its context

An observation about one man, or a range across many, means something only when six questions already have answers beside it. Who is he, and where did he start? What else changed over the same weeks? Over what dates? Under what capture conditions? How does he compare with everyone else measured? Who's counted? That's the **Context Stack**: every observational or outcome claim carries who, what else changed, timeline, capture conditions, range, and denominator, and says it was observed, not caused.

| Item | The question it answers | In the caption |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Is he like me? | "24, eight months of solo effort, nothing measured" |
| What else changed | What else could explain it? | "Lost [~4] kg; new haircut in [week 9]" |
| Timeline | Over what dates? | "Baseline [date]; week 12 [date]" |
| Capture conditions | Same camera, same light? | "Matched on distance, lens, angle, light, expression, and hour; [one] deviation noted" |
| Range | Is that typical? | "[A–B] across [N] graduates; he sits in the [upper third]" |
| Denominator | Who's counted? | "[N] started, [n] graduated, [k] flat on every marker" |

The closing phrase is a rule of its own. Observed, not caused is the phrasing rule for published ranges: a range says it was observed among clients, lists its confounders, sits beside a comparison where an honest one exists, and never uses a causal verb. "Built", "gave him", "fixed", and "transformed" each claim a cause the record can't show, and a change to his face that your method caused is a structural claim however softly it's worded.

Three confounders turn up so often here that every stack names them. Weight change comes first, because body composition shows in the face, so a few kilograms move the jawline in any photo. Maturation comes next: men in their late teens and early twenties may still be maturing, so their captures can change with no help from anyone. Then lighting and lens, checked against the Capture Standard, matched distance, lens, angle, light, expression, and time of day for every capture (Module 6), because a capture that drifted shows you the camera instead of the man.

The operating rule follows. Every observational or outcome claim ships with all six items and the phrase, and a claim missing an item drops a rung or waits for the record. The top rung stays empty in every format, including a client's own words you'd republish, a title, and a thumbnail.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a founding graduate's week-12 pair is the most striking you've taken: matched on every condition, the lower face visibly leaner, and he wants it everywhere. His log also shows he lost [~7] kg over the same 12 weeks. The pair says result and the stack says weight, and both are true. Don't bury it, because a real change in the changeable column is what *Name the destination boldly* exists to show, and hiding it under-sells what can move. Don't caption it as jaw change either, because the weight explains what the pair shows. Publish it as a body-composition case: the kilograms first, the pair as what that change looks like in a photo, observed, not caused, and his place in your range once one exists. Told that way, the pair teaches a true sentence this category rarely hears: body composition shows in the face, and it can be measured.

## 3. Points for Process, Ranges for Results

The shape of a number is evidence to this buyer, so state what you control as a point and what varies as a tight range with its denominator.

### A point for what you control, a range for what varies

At first glance most buyers prefer a point. "[X] in [90] days" sounds certain, and certainty sells until someone checks. Your buyer checks, and once he's weighing how a claim was made, the preference turns around: side by side, a range reads as transparency and a point reads as a pitch. The evidence for that reversal is moderate and conditional, and the conditions are the rule. It holds when he can compare the two formats, it collapses when the range is wide, and it runs backwards on anything fixed, where a range sounds like nobody's counting. A review promised "within one to five days" sounds like a business that doesn't know its own week.

So the rule is Points for Process, Ranges for Results: fixed facts as points, outcomes as tight ranges.

| The claim | Its format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Review turnaround | A point: "within [72] hours" | You control it, so a range sounds like slack |
| Reviews and captures | Points: "a written review every week; captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12" | Fixed by design |
| Price | One number, stated once | A range reads as a negotiation |
| Check-in completion across clients | A range, with how many clients | It varies from man to man |
| Marker change | A tight range, its denominator, and the flat results | It varies, and some men don't move |
| Visible jaw or profile change | No number at all | It sits in the debated column, so it's said at its tier and never measured into a promise |

### Tight ranges, with a named denominator

A range wide enough to cover everything is a hedge in number form. If your honest range is wide, split it by what moves it, usually adherence, and publish the parts, because a wide range tends to hide two groups: men who did the work and men who didn't. Then name who's counted, since the same results read three ways depending on the denominator:

| Denominator | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone who started | Always, as the count above every range | Early exits pull the picture down, which a skeptic respects |
| Every graduate | For the range itself | It hides exits unless the first count sits above it |
| Graduates at or above the adherence threshold | Beside the main range, to answer "what if I do the work?" | Alone, it's a filtered sample that flatters you |

**Default: every graduate, with the other two beside it.** He needs to know what happened to everyone and what happens to a man who does the work, so the starting count sits above the range and the at-threshold range sits beside it. The third row never runs alone.

### Show the comparison

The range only wins when he can see both formats, so put them side by side yourself:

| How the category phrases it | How you phrase it |
|---|---|
| "Results in [30] days" | "A written review every week; captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12" |
| "Guaranteed jaw gains" | "[n] of [N] graduates crossed a marker threshold by week 12; [k] didn't" |
| "Results may vary" | "[A–B] across [N] graduates, [k] of them flat on every marker" |
| "Clients love it" | "Check-ins completed: [X–Y]% across [N] clients this quarter" |

Before your records hold any range, the honest version has no outcome row at all. Publish the points about your process and the date your first range will appear, which is a claim he can check the day it arrives. A buyer who watched you name that date and then keep it has seen the format work once before you ever show him a result.

## 4. The Four Stories

Four stories carry this brand, and each persuades only at the rung your records can support that month.

### Stories move him, and imply what's typical

A story works by carrying him into one man's weeks. Narrative persuades with a small to moderate effect, moving intentions a little more while statistics move beliefs a little more, so the pair beats either alone, and every story here travels with its numbers or with a plain statement that it has none. The same power carries the risk. A story about one man implies he's typical, which is why each story below carries its own context, and why any story built from several clients is labeled a composite in the asset itself, with numbers taken only from your published range.

Four stories do most of this brand's work, because each answers a different question he brings: why you built this, why he stalled, what changes when a man starts measuring, and what happened to one measured man among everyone measured. That's the **Four Stories**: Origin, the Stall Told Back, the Transition, and the Case, each with its context.

| Story | What it proves | Rung | Usable from | It never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Origin | Why the standard exists | Process and educational | Day one | Implies a credential, or offers your face as proof |
| The Stall Told Back | His stall had a cause he can act on | Educational | Day one; sharper once founding records exist | Turns the cause into his worth or his face |
| The Transition | A man can stop guessing and start measuring | Process, told as a story | Your first consenting founding client | Makes an appearance claim |
| The Case | What happened to one measured man, among everyone measured | Observational, placed in the outcome range | Your first range, at 10 graduates | Ships without its Context Stack |

### Origin and the Stall Told Back work from day one

Origin tells why the standard exists: what you watched fail, what you couldn't find, and what you built instead, with dates and what you measure now. It never implies a qualification you don't hold, and it never includes your own before/after. Your appearance is never evidence for the method, which you say publicly in What My Face Does and Doesn't Prove, the statement of what your face can't show (Module 15).

The Stall Told Back is his story told better than he tells it, with a better cause. Build it from the stall patterns in your founding records, stripped of names, and aim its force at the situation:

> "Eight months. You did the work most nights and took a comparison photo most weeks, and no two were taken the same way, so the photos could never tell you anything. That's not laziness, and it isn't your genes closing a door. You stalled because nobody gave you a way to know what was moving."

Dan *(composite, Struggler)* hears himself in it, and that's the point. The cause it names is missing measurement, the one part of his stall he can change this week, so he leaves more capable rather than more defective, which is the Dignity Check every version of this story has to pass (Intro).

### The Transition works before any outcome exists

The Transition is a founding client's change of belief, in his words and with his consent: from asking the mirror whether it's working to reading his record for what's moving. It needs no outcome, because what changed is how he works and what he knows, so it's usable in your first months, long before any range exists. It also carries the destination, said the way a client would say it. Maya *(composite, welcome, not targeted)*, 28, gave one like this:

> "I used to check the mirror every night and still not know. Now I log, read my review on Sunday, and walk into the week knowing what's moving and what isn't. The funny part is that the knowing ended up mattering more than the photo."

Reid *(composite operator)* made the common misread here. He waited for outcome proof before publishing any proof at all, so his first [eight] months carried no story but his own. Cole published his first Transition in month [3], and it did a job no outcome could do yet: it showed a skeptic what the work feels like from inside.

### The Case waits for its range

The Case is one man's measured arc: his starting point and markers, the written read at week 6, the capture and verdict at week 12, what else changed, and where he sits in the range, with the men who didn't move in view. It's the only story that makes an outcome claim, so it's the only one that waits, until your first range exists at 10 graduates. Choose among the four by one rule: lead with the story your records can carry this month, which means three stories from month 1 and the fourth once you can place it.

## 5. Testimonials and Integrity Levels

A testimonial is a claim you publish in someone else's voice, so it carries every rule your own claims carry, plus consent, typicality, and a timing rule that keeps it away from his refund rights.

### Consent, typicality, and no reward

Consent comes separately from enrollment, in writing, and scoped: which words, whether his voice or face appears, under what name, on which surfaces, for how long, and how he withdraws it. It's revocable at any time and never tied to a refund, a discount, or anything else in his terms, because a testimonial he couldn't safely refuse is evidence of nothing. Typicality comes next. A testimonial that names a result sits beside the range everyone got, and until a range exists, testimonials stay on the process: what the weekly review was like, what he did, and what he knows now.

The default is no incentive, because anything of value changes what the words are evidence of, and in a category known for paid endorsements a reward reads as payment even when it's disclosed. If anything of value ever goes with one, disclose it on the testimonial itself, give it to everyone asked, and never make it depend on what he says. Ask every client whose record shows progress, not only the ones you expect to glow, and never suppress a critical review. The men whose markers stayed flat appear in your log instead, where the count keeps the selection honest.

### The ask waits for a measured peak after the exit decision

Timing is fixed, because an ask near a refund right turns a testimonial into a trade. Never ask before the fit window, his full refund in the first 14–21 days, has closed. Never ask in the week-6 conversation where his exit right, a pro-rata refund for a client who did the work, is on the table. The first ask comes at the first measured peak after he has settled whether he's staying, from about week 7, and only if his record shows progress. That's the testimonial case of Measured-Peak Asks: every ask happens at a measurement moment that shows progress on his record, never at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit (Module 22).

> **You:** "Adrian, before next week's plan: [52 of 56] days logged, and your [posture-habit marker] crossed its threshold in this review. Would you write a few lines about what the weekly reviews have been like? Only the process, nothing about your face. It's separate from everything else: nothing changes either way, and you can pull it whenever you like."
>
> **Adrian** *(composite, Optimizer)*: "Where would it show up? I don't want anyone at work connecting me to this."
>
> **You:** "Only where you say. Initials or no name, text only, on the site and in email. It never goes in an ad."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

It's week [8]. His fit window closed long ago, he chose at his week-6 read to stay, and his week to claim the exit right has passed. The ask lands on a Measured-Peak Ask's moment, a marker crossing, names one process-only request, and makes refusing free before he has to look for a way out. His question was about privacy, so the answer was scope, and consent scoped to his terms is what makes his words usable as evidence later.

If he pushes on timing instead, answer with the rung:

> **Adrian:** "Can I wait until week 12, when there's an actual result?"
>
> **You:** "You can. This one's only about the process, which you've already lived. Anything about results waits until there's a range to put it beside."

The line you never say is the ask moved into the exit conversation: "Before you decide whether to stay, would you write something for the site?" It makes his refund right a bargaining chip, and any words it produced would be evidence of the bargain. A no ends the ask, and his next review reads exactly like the last one.

### Integrity Levels grade every before/after

Before/afters get their own discipline, because a pair of photos is where this category's proof has been faked longest. Integrity Levels are three levels of before/after discipline, and every pair you hold sits on one:

| Level | What it requires | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Matched | Unedited, unfiltered originals, dated, with conditions logged; the pair matches the Capture Standard, or the deviation is written down | Any comparison at all, including the private one at his week-12 re-assessment |
| 2. In context | Level 1, plus the full Context Stack, the phrase observed, not caused, and his separate written consent to public use | Publication in long-form, email, and your site |
| 3. Placed | Level 2, plus his place in your published range, with its denominator and the flat results in view | A Case, or any pair that stands for more than one man |

A pair that fails Level 1 isn't a before/after; it's two photos, and it never appears as a comparison. Captures stay context at every level: his markers decide the verdict, and the pair shows what that verdict looks like in a photo.

### Clips travel without their captions

Where each kind of proof may travel is the Proof Portability Gradient: process proof goes everywhere, outcome stories live in long-form, email, and your site, and no outcome proof goes in an ad (Module 27). Organic short-form is where a result travels furthest, so use it. A testimonial clip that names a result runs there with its range and denominator on screen for as long as the claim is, because a clip gets cut, shared, and watched without the page it came from, and the numbers have to travel inside the frame.

## 6. The Honest-Evidence Test and the Proof Stack

Teach buyers the test your proof was built to pass, because a skeptic who holds the standard stops discounting all proof equally and starts trusting the proof that meets it.

### He can't tell yours from theirs

A man fooled by manufactured proof usually can't say how it was made. He only knows he was fooled, so he discounts every pair and every testimonial by the same amount, yours included. Some of that discount ends in the genetics shrug, and some ends with the loudest photo winning again, because a vague distrust can't tell a lit jawline from a matched one.

Forewarning works here. Showing people a weakened version of a manipulation and naming how it works gives moderate protection against the next attempt, and the protection fades without repeats. It also has a known failure: taught as general distrust, it breeds a cynicism that lands on every claim, including yours. The protection that lasts comes from naming specific tells and pairing each one with what honest evidence looks like, and that pairing binds the teacher. Every viewer you train will run your lesson on you, so your Capture Standard goes public before your first teardown.

Six tells cover most of the category's manufactured proof, and each has an honest counterpart:

| The tell | What it does | The honest standard |
|---|---|---|
| The camera moved | A closer phone or wider lens widens the face | Matched distance and lens, stated |
| The light moved | Overhead light carves a shadow under the jaw | The same light, facing him, both times |
| The pose moved | A dipped chin, a turned head, or a clench reads as a new jawline | Head level, neutral expression, the same views |
| The body moved | A few kilograms, water, or the hour changes the lower face | What else changed, in the caption |
| The dates vanished | "[90] days" with no dates, or photos years apart | Dated captures on a fixed calendar |
| The rest vanished | Best cases only, retouched, or borrowed | Unedited originals, consent on file, the count, and the flat results |

That's the **Honest-Evidence Test**: the tells of manufactured proof and the standard of honest evidence, taught so buyers can judge anyone, you included. He can carry it in five questions:

**Matched? Dated? What else changed? Out of how many? Where are the rest?**

### Cole's first teardown aimed at a practice

Cole wrote his Capture Standard in month 1 and published his first teardown in month [3]. The target was a sponsored post circulating among adults, a transformation pair sold with a [chewing-device] code. He cropped out the face, removed the handle and watermark, and opened with the steelman: the product answers a real wish, and the man in the post may well have done real work. Then he ran the five questions on the photograph alone. Matched? No: the after shot's light sits overhead, the chin drops, and the background shows the camera closer. Dated? Only "[60] days". What else changed? The post doesn't say, and weight, water, and the hour all move a lower face. The count? One man, posted by the seller of the lever. The rest? Nowhere. His sharpest line went at the practice: "[That's a lighting choice with a discount code attached.]" Then he ran the same questions on his own record, on screen: his standard, his dated captures, his check-in completion, and the date his first range would appear. The comments asked for his capture sheet, and [three] adults booked fit conversations that week.

The default is one teardown the month your Capture Standard goes public, then another whenever a published claim draws attention among adults, each ending on your own record, because the protection fades without repeats and the ending is what turns his new skepticism into trust in you. Targets are businesses' published claims: ads, sales pages, sponsored posts. Never a private person's post, anyone who could be under 18, or a clinician. Crop or blur every face, strip names and handles, and analyze the photograph and the claim, never the person in it, because the features of someone who hasn't consented are never yours to discuss.

### The Proof Stack matches proof to the doubt

Proof persuades when it answers the doubt he has right now, and each doubt is a link in the Belief Chain, the beliefs a purchase has to complete (Module 14). The Proof Stack is proof matched to the doubt, published on a pre-committed schedule:

| His doubt | The proof that answers it | It exists from |
|---|---|---|
| "Is this another grift?" | The dated process log: reviews on time, check-ins kept, guarantee claims, fit declines | Month 1 |
| "Can anything change at my age?" | Educational claims at their tier | Day one |
| "Will I stick with it?" | Transition stories beside the log's check-in completion | Your first consenting founding client |
| "Does it work for men like me?" | Cases placed in the range | 10 graduates |
| "What if it doesn't work for me?" | The flat results, then the non-responder share | 10 graduates, then 30 |

### Publication runs on the schedule you committed to

The Dated Record is the moat: a published pre-commitment plus a dated log of process and outcome metrics, started in month 1, that no rival can back-fill. Its null-result stance means a result that didn't move is published beside the ones that did, never buried (Module 3).

The mechanics are yours to run. Process metrics go up every month from month 1, on a fixed day. The first outcome ranges join at the proof milestone, 10 or more graduates who've given consented process testimonials, labeled as a small sample, observed among clients, with denominators and the flat results included. From 30 graduates they become the standing published log, with the non-responder share beside them, the share whose markers didn't move despite the work. The recommendation mix joins after 30 Decision Assessments, the paid written plans that end in one recommendation. Corrections arrive as new dated entries, and a disappointing number goes out on the same date, in the same format, over the same denominator:

```
[Month, year] · [Brand] dated log · next entry [date]
Process: check-ins completed [X–Y]% · reviews inside [72] h: [n of n] · guarantee claims [n] · fit declines [n]
Outcomes (a small sample: [10] graduates): at least one marker crossed [n of 10] · flat on every marker [k of 10] · range [A–B]
Observed among clients, not caused. Confounders logged: weight change [n] · still maturing [n] · capture deviations [n]
```

## Worked Example: Cole, Four Assets from Dan's 12 Weeks

Dan, 24, joins Cole's founding group, the first cohort every early client joins, in month [5], after about eight months of solo effort, nothing measured, and a nightly comparison-photo habit. Cole has [nine] graduates and no published range yet. Here are the 12 weeks and the four assets they became, each at its rung, with its consent and context.

**Week 0. Baseline day.** Cole takes Dan's captures to the standard and keeps the originals unedited, dated, and with their conditions logged, so the pair sits at Level 1 from its first frame. They agree two markers, [a body-composition measure] and [a posture-habit measure], and Dan retires the nightly mirror photos, since the calendar captures him at weeks 6 and 12. Testimonials don't come up, because his fit window has weeks to run.

**Week 6. The read, and no ask.** One marker is improving and one is flat. The written read asks the verdict question first, and Dan chooses to continue. Cole asks for nothing in that conversation, because a man deciding whether to stay can't give a free testimonial.

**Week 8. Asset 1: a process testimonial.** Dan's exit-right window closed a week earlier. His log shows [54 of 56] days, and the [posture-habit measure] crossed its threshold in this review, his first measured peak after the exit decision. Cole asks once, with a consent note scoped to text, his initial, the site, and email, revocable. Dan writes: "[Eight months of mirror photos told me nothing. ## Templates: Context Stack, Testimonial Consent, Story Skeletons

### The Context Stack

Fill it in for every observational or outcome claim before it ships. A blank line means the claim drops a rung or waits for the record.

```
Claim: [one sentence, no causal verb] · Rung: [observational / outcome]
Who: [age] · [starting point] · [months of solo effort]
What else changed: weight [±~X kg] · grooming [change, week] · other care [none / named] · maturation [age]
Timeline: baseline [date] · captures [dates] · [weeks]
Capture conditions: matched to the standard · deviations [none / listed]
Range: [A–B] across [N] graduates · his place [lower / middle / upper third]
Denominator: [N] started · [n] graduated · [n] at or above the adherence threshold · [k] flat
Consent: [scope] · [surfaces] · [date signed] · revocable
Observed among clients, not caused.
```

### Testimonial consent outline

Send it after he says yes to the ask, as its own document and never inside enrollment terms.

1. **What we'd use.** Your words as written, plus [your voice] or [your captures] only if you tick them.
2. **Where, and under what name.** [The site, email, organic short-form with the range on screen], under [your initials / first name / no name]. Never in an ad.
3. **How long.** Until you withdraw it. Reply [remove] at any time, and it comes down everywhere we control within [a stated number of days].
4. **What it's tied to.** Nothing. Saying no, or withdrawing later, changes nothing in your reviews, refunds, or terms, and nothing of value goes with a yes.
5. **What sits beside it.** Any result you mention appears only beside the range everyone got, with its count.
6. **Your final look.** You see the exact version, caption included, before it goes up.

## Checklist: Teardown Safety

Run it before filming and again before publishing. One "no" holds the piece.

1. Is the target a business's published claim, such as an ad, a sales page, or a sponsored post, and not a private person's post or anyone who could be under 18? → If not, drop it.
2. Is every face cropped or blurred, with names, handles, watermarks, and identifying backgrounds removed? → If not, don't publish.
3. Does every line describe the capture or the claim (distance, lens, light, pose, dates, count), with none describing a person's features? → Rewrite any line about a face.
4. Did you steelman first, saying what the claim gets right or why a reasonable man believed it? → Add it before the first tell.
5. Is your Capture Standard public, and does the piece end by running the same five questions on your own record? → If not, wait.
6. Would a man who bought that product leave more capable, and would the piece survive the Hostile-Screenshot Test, the check of whether a line survives the category's harshest critic (Intro)? → Cut any line that fails either.
7. Is your own comparison free of causal verbs and of anything from the debated or never-claimed columns? → If not, you're showing the tell you came to teach.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Evidence is the scarcity that binds this topic: you have no outcome data, and every skeptic is meeting you for the first time. Build on process proof, Origin, the Stall Told Back, and your first consented Transition, and publish your first teardown the month your Capture Standard goes public. The trap is filling the gap with borrowed proof, such as a client's delighted message posted as a result before any range exists.

**Growing.** Your first range arrives at the proof milestone, and the work shifts to Cases placed in it, a testimonial ask at every measured peak, and the claim rate in the log. The trap is letting the first flattering range become a headline and dropping its small-sample label, or asking only the clients you expect to glow.

**Scaling.** Volume makes the standing log the proof skeptics check first: ranges over 30 or more graduates, the non-responder share, and a comparison where an honest one exists. The trap is denominator drift, quietly swapping in the at-threshold range or a friendlier window as the numbers grow, and letting templated asks slide toward a refund window. Check both at every quarterly review.

## Standard Check

- **Proof built to be audited.** Integrity Levels and the Context Stack keep every pair matched, dated, and in context, and composites are labeled in the asset itself, so nothing edited, unmatched, or invented reaches a caption (the line on fabricated proof). A striking but confounded result still ships as what it is, because hiding a real result fails *Name the destination boldly*.
- **Numbers only from your records.** Every range names its denominator and its flat results, small samples say so, and observed, not caused keeps a caused change to his face out of every sentence (the line on structural claims).
- **Teardowns aim at the claim.** Faces cropped or blurred, no names, no features described: *Fight ideas, not people* at full force, with the line on shame intact.
- **Asks stay clear of refunds.** No testimonial ask before the fit window closes or in the week-6 exit conversation; the first comes at a measured peak after the exit decision, unrewarded and revocable, so nothing is timed to his refund rights.
