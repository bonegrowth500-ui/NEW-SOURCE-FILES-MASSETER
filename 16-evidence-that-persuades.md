# 16 · Evidence That Persuades
*Part IV — The Psychology of Belief*

**The shift:** from *"proof means before/afters and testimonials"* to *"proof is a format a skeptic can audit, plus a test of honest evidence he can apply to anyone."*

Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, checks everything before he buys, because the last device he bought came with a promise about his jaw. Tonight two posts sit in his feed. The first is a transformation pair: the after shot lit from above, the chin tilted, "[90] days" in bold, a discount code underneath, and no dates anywhere. The second is plainer. It shows two captures of a stranger, taken at a marked distance under the same lamp and dated 12 weeks apart. The caption names what was measured, says he also lost [a few] kilograms over those weeks, and shows how [n] of [N] graduates did on the same measure, including the men whose measures didn't move.

Theo has seen that overhead light before, so he scrolls past the first. He reads the second twice, opens the dated log behind it, and books a fit conversation, the free first call that ends in one recommendation.

The dramatic pair lost because he knew how it was made. The modest one won because he could check how it was made, what else changed, and who else was counted. In this category, that's what proof is: a format a skeptic can audit, plus a test of honest evidence he can use on anyone, you included.

## 1. Why Proof Fails Here

In this category, more proof persuades less, because your buyer arrives knowing how proof gets made and audits yours the same way.

### He knows how the photos were made

Once a buyer recognizes a persuasion tactic, he discounts the whole message and starts asking what the seller wants, which is among the better-established findings about persuasion. Your buyer has had years of practice: manufactured before/afters, devices sold with a jaw promise attached, and tips that never end. The camera facts behind the first are well documented and easy to show. Distance and lens change a face's proportions, and a phone held close enlarges the nose most visibly. Overhead light carves shadows along the jaw, a chin tilted up or down changes how the jaw reads, and a few kilograms lost show in the lower face. He has concluded that any photo can be staged, and he's right.

So the dramatic pair works against you. The bigger the change it shows, the harder he hunts for the light, and a page of them reads as the same tell repeated. The Burned Struggler, the buyer who already paid for a structural promise, goes further: he assumes any proof he can't check was made the way his last purchase was sold.

### Volume reads as selection

A pile of best cases raises the one question a pile can't answer: out of how many? Selective publication is how the category's proof got made, so a skeptic reads any result without a count as the top of a hidden stack. Testimonials carry the same flaw. A testimonial that names a result implies the result is typical, regulators read it that way too, and small print saying results vary doesn't undo that. It only turns his question into "by how much?", and a vague hedge costs you more trust than a plain range with its count beside it.

### Formats that survive an audit

Every format you might publish meets the same audit. Most fail on the first question he asks:

| Format | His first question | It survives when |
|---|---|---|
| A dramatic before/after, uncaptioned | "Same camera, same light, same weight?" | Not as posted |
| A testimonial that names a result | "Is that typical?" | It sits beside the range everyone got |
| A screenshot of a delighted message | "Who didn't write?" | As a process moment, with consent; never as outcome proof |
| "Results may vary" | "By how much?" | A range with its count replaces it |
| A day-count promise | "Measured how, and on whom?" | It can't |
| Matched captures with their context | "What else changed?" | The caption answers before he asks |
| A dated log of process and outcomes | "Did they publish the bad months?" | The men it didn't work for are in it |

The survivors share three properties: he can check how the proof was made, what else could explain it, and who else was counted. Build every piece to answer all three before he asks, because to a buyer who audits, an answer he had to request reads as a concession, and one you volunteered reads as a standard.

## 2. The Claim Ladder and the Context Stack

Every claim you publish sits at one of five heights, and the higher it sits, the more context it must carry before it ships.

### Claims climb from what you do to what happens to everyone

The category's captions collapse those heights into one sentence. "Weekly check-ins, and your jaw will sharpen" sets a fact you control beside a forecast about his face, in one breath and with one confidence, and the certainty of the first leaks onto the second. The same collapse turns one client's result into a promise to the next man who reads it.

What separates the heights is how he could check the claim. A process claim he checks himself within a week, because the review arrives on time or it doesn't, and an educational claim he checks against how well supported it is. A claim about one man means little without his context, and a claim about a group means little without its size and the men it didn't work for. A claim about everyone, a certain result, or his health can't be checked by anyone, which is why the grift lives there. In order, every claim has a rung:

| Rung | What it says | In this niche | What it must carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process | What you do and what he receives | "Every check-in gets a written review within [72] hours" | The truth, stated as a point |
| Educational | How something works in general | "Camera distance changes how wide a face reads" | How well supported it is |
| Observational | What one man's record showed | "His [body-composition marker] reached its threshold by week 12, with matched captures beside it" | His full context, stated as observation |
| Outcome | What a group's records showed | "[n] of [N] graduates reached a marker threshold by week 12" | Full context, the range, the denominator, the men it didn't work for |
| Universal, guaranteed, or medical | What happens to everyone, for certain, or to his health | "Anyone can build a sharper jaw"; "fixes your bite" | Nothing can carry it |

That's the **Claim Ladder**. It fits on one line you can say aloud:

**Process → educational → observational → outcome → universal, guaranteed, or medical (never).**

Educational claims carry their evidence tier, how well supported a claim is: established, plausible, or debated. For the bone question the tiers are set in the Outcome Map, your straight answer to what adults can and can't change, sorted into changeable and measurable, debated, never claimed, and during growth (Module 3). A claim about jaws takes its tier from the map, not from how sure you feel that day.

### Sam's sentence comes down the ladder

Cole *(composite operator)* meets the ladder in a check-in. In week [9], Sam *(composite, Ambivalent)*, 22, a founding client, ends his log with a line he wants public: "My jaw is way sharper. This method works. Post it." The sentence is sincere, and it sits on the top two rungs. "This method works" is an outcome claim about everyone with no count behind it, and "my jaw is sharper" is a bathroom-mirror reading of the debated column. Cole replies the same day: "Good to hear it. Keep logging. At week 12 we'll look at matched captures and your markers, and that's the only version I'd ever put in public, with your say-so."

At week 12, one of Sam's markers, the two or three measures agreed at baseline that decide his verdict, reaches its threshold. Once the verdict is settled, Sam agrees to a results testimonial, which waits for Cole's first range. When it runs, the caption answers everything a skeptic will check:

> "Sam, 22, eight months of solo effort before he joined. [Body-composition marker]: [baseline reading] on [date], [week-12 reading] on [date], past his threshold. Matched captures below: same distance, lens, light, and hour, [no] deviations. Also changed: he shaved off a beard in [week 8], and at 22 he may still be maturing. Among all clients so far, [N] started, [n] left at week 6, and [n] graduated; [n] graduates reached a threshold on at least one marker, and [k] reached none. On this marker type the range was [A–B] across [n] graduates. Observed, not caused."

The sentence came down two rungs and gained what the mirror couldn't give it: a reader who can check it.

### Every observation carries its context

Sam's caption answers six questions, and a skeptic asks them of every result: who he is, what else changed, over what dates, under what capture conditions, how he compares with everyone measured, and who's counted. That's the **Context Stack**: every observational or outcome claim carries who, what else changed, timeline, capture conditions, range, and denominator, and says it was observed, not caused.

Observed, not caused is the phrasing rule for published ranges: a range says it was observed among clients, lists its confounders, sits beside a comparison where an honest one exists, and never uses a causal verb. "Built", "gave him", "fixed", and "transformed" each claim a cause the record can't isolate, and beside a photo of a face, any of them reads as the structural promise you never make.

Three confounders turn up so often here that every stack names them. Weight change comes first, because body composition shows in the face, so a few kilograms move the jawline in any photo. Maturation comes next: men in their late teens and early twenties may still be maturing, so their captures can change with no help from anyone. Then lighting and lens, checked against the Capture Standard, matched distance, lens, angle, light, expression, and time of day for every capture (Module 6), because a capture that drifted shows you the camera instead of the man.

The operating rule covers both tools. Sort every claim by rung before it ships, and build by default on process and educational claims. An observational or outcome claim ships with all six items and the phrase, or it drops a rung, or it waits for the record, and the top rung stays empty everywhere, including a client's words you'd republish, a title, and a thumbnail.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a graduate's week-12 pair is the most striking you've taken, fully matched, the lower face visibly leaner. His log also shows [~7] kg lost over the same 12 weeks. The pair says result, the stack says weight, and both are true. Don't bury it: hiding a real change under-sells what can move, the failure *Name the destination boldly* exists to prevent. Don't caption it as jaw change either, because the weight explains the pair. Publish it as a body-composition case, kilograms first, the pair as what that change looks like, observed, not caused, and placed in your range, so it waits if you have none yet. It then teaches a true sentence: body composition shows in the face, and it can be measured.

## 3. Points for Process, Ranges for Results

The shape of a number is evidence to this buyer, so state what you control as a point and what varies as a tight range with its denominator.

### A point for what you control, a range for what varies

At first glance most buyers prefer a point. "[X] in [90] days" sounds certain, and certainty sells until someone checks. Your buyer checks, and once he's weighing how a claim was made, the preference can turn around: side by side, a range reads as transparency and a point reads as a pitch. The evidence for that reversal is weak to moderate and conditional, and the conditions are the rule. It holds when he can compare the two formats, it collapses when the range is wide, and it runs backwards on anything fixed, where a range sounds like nobody's counting. A review promised "within one to five days" sounds like a business that doesn't know its own week.

So the rule is Points for Process, Ranges for Results: fixed facts as points, outcomes as tight ranges. Here's how it sorts the claims you make most often:

| The claim | Its format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Review turnaround | A point: "within [72] hours" | You control it, so a range sounds like slack |
| Reviews and captures | Points: "a written review every week; captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12" | Fixed by design |
| Check-in completion | Counts while the sample is small, then a range | A percentage of three clients is a point in disguise |
| Marker change | A tight range per marker type, its denominator, and the men who reached no threshold | It varies, and some men don't move |
| Visible jaw or profile change | No number at all | It sits in the debated column, so it's said at its tier, with no number to promise |

### Tight ranges, with a named denominator

A range wide enough to cover everything is a hedge in number form. If your honest range is wide, split it by what moves it, usually adherence, and publish the parts, because a wide range tends to hide two groups: men who did the work and men who didn't. Keep ranges per marker type, since each client's markers differ, and name who's counted, because the same results read three ways by denominator. The third uses the adherence threshold, the logging level agreed at baseline that counts as doing the work:

| Denominator | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone who started | Always, as the count above every range | Early exits pull the picture down, which a skeptic respects |
| Every graduate | For the range itself | It hides exits unless the first count sits above it |
| Graduates at or above the adherence threshold | Beside the main range, to answer "what if I do the work?" | Alone, it's a filtered sample that flatters you |

**Default: every graduate, with the other two beside it.** He needs to know what happened to everyone and what happens to a man who does the work, so the starting count sits above the range and the at-threshold range sits beside it. Don't publish the third row alone.

### A comparison only where one is honest

Observed, not caused puts a comparison beside a range only where an honest one exists, and usually none does. Your graduates chose to buy and differ from any group you could set beside them, so a comparison misleads as easily as it informs:

| Comparison | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| None | By default | Readers compare you with the category's claims instead |
| At vs below the adherence threshold | A reader asks whether the work matters | It compares effort, not the program, and must say so |
| Men waiting for a later start, over the same weeks | Monthly entry leaves paid clients waiting, and enough of them consent to share their logs | They chose to wait, and their weeks differ from a client's |

**Default: none.** The at-threshold split already sits beside every range, and a weak comparison teaches a skeptic to distrust the range beside it. Switch to the waiting-start comparison once enough waiting men share their logs to form a range of their own, and say on the page what it can't show.

### Show him how the category phrases it

The range only wins when he can see both formats. So put them side by side yourself:

| How the category phrases it | How you phrase it |
|---|---|
| "Results in [30] days" | "A written review every week; captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12" |
| "Guaranteed jaw gains" | "[n] of [N] graduates reached a marker threshold by week 12; [k] didn't" |
| "Results may vary" | "[A–B] across [N] graduates, [k] of them with no marker at its threshold" |

Before your records hold any range, the honest version has no outcome row at all. Publish points about your process and the trigger your first range waits for, such as the first monthly entry after your tenth graduate with a consented process testimonial, because a promised date can slip whenever a client exits. A buyer who watched you name that trigger and then keep it has seen the format work once before you ever show him a result.

## 4. The Four Stories

Four stories carry this brand, and each persuades only at the rung your records can support that month.

### Stories move him, and imply what's typical

A story works by carrying him into one man's weeks. Narrative persuades with a small to moderate effect, moving intentions a little more while statistics move beliefs a little more, so the pair beats either alone, and every story here travels with its numbers or a plain statement that it has none. The same power carries the risk, because a story about one man implies he's typical. So each story carries its context, and the four split by whose voice they're in.

Each answers a question he brings: why you built this, why he stalled, what changes when a man starts measuring, and what happened to one measured man among everyone measured. That's the **Four Stories**: Origin, the Stall Told Back, the Transition, and the Case, each with its context. Two are yours and run from month 1. Two are his and wait for consent, asked no earlier than his first measured peak after his week-6 decision, so no client story exists in month 1.

| Story | Whose voice | What it proves | Rung | Usable from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Origin | Yours | Why the standard exists | Process and educational | Month 1 |
| The Stall Told Back | Yours, from anonymized patterns | His stall has a cause that measuring can find | Educational | Month 1 |
| The Transition | His words; your edits are cuts he approves | A man can stop guessing and start measuring | Process, told as a story | His first measured peak after his week-6 decision, from about week 7 |
| The Case | His record, placed in your range | What happened to one measured man, among everyone measured | Observational, placed in the outcome range | Your first range, at 10 graduates |

Composites belong only in the Stall Told Back, labeled in the asset as a composite of several men's patterns. A composite Transition or Case would be an invented testimonial, which the line on fabricated proof rules out.

### Your two stories run from month 1

Origin tells why the standard exists: what you watched fail, what you couldn't find, and what you built instead, with dates and what you measure now. It implies no qualification you don't hold and includes no before/after of your own, because your appearance is never evidence for the method. You say so publicly in What My Face Does and Doesn't Prove, the statement of what your face can't show (Module 15).

The Stall Told Back is his story told better than he tells it, with a better cause, built from anonymized patterns in your conversations and, later, your records. Its force goes at the situation, and it claims no verdict it can't check:

> "Eight months. You did the work most nights and took a comparison photo most weeks, and no two were taken the same way, so the photos couldn't tell you anything. Most stalls I see come from direction: real effort, no map, and nothing measured. Yours might be one of those, or it might be a lever that doesn't move for you, and right now nobody can say which, you included. Measure it, and by week 6 your record shows what's moving. By week 12 it can tell you if a lever doesn't."

Dan *(composite, Struggler)* hears himself in it and gets nothing he can't check. It names the part of his stall he can change this week, the missing measurement, and it says plainly that a third answer exists, which keeps it inside Stall Verdicts: a stall is unmeasured, misdirected, or "the lever doesn't move for this person" (Module 14). It also passes the Dignity Check, whether he leaves more capable or more defective (Intro), because it grades his setup and never his face or his discipline.

### His two stories wait for his consent

The Transition is a client's change of belief in his own words: from asking the mirror whether it's working to reading his record for what's moving. It needs no outcome, because what changed is how he works and what he knows, yet it's still a testimonial, so it follows testimonial timing and can't exist before about week 7 of your first client. You never supply the words. You send him a few questions, and your only edits are cuts he approves, because a Transition you drafted reads as coached to the skeptic it's meant for. Maya *(composite, welcome, not targeted)*, 28, wrote hers like this:

> "I kept swapping routines every few weeks because nothing seemed to work. My second review said to hold the plan and log it, and for the first time I could see what was moving. I'm back in the team photos, and I've stopped thinking about my face in design reviews. Knowing did more for that than anything I changed."

Reid *(composite operator)* made the common misread here: he waited for outcome proof before publishing any, so his first [eight] months carried no story but his own. Cole ran his first Transition in month [3], at his first client's first measured peak after the exit decision, and it showed skeptics what the work feels like from inside, a job no outcome could do yet.

### The Case runs in an order set before anyone qualifies

The Case is one man's measured arc: his starting point and markers, the written read at week 6, the capture and verdict at week 12, what else changed, and his place in your range, with the men who reached no threshold in view. It's the only story that makes an outcome claim, so it waits for the proof milestone, 10 or more graduates who've given consented process testimonials, when your first range appears. The category picks its showcases from the top, so set your order before anyone qualifies: Cases rotate through the thirds of your range, middle first, each time with the next graduate in that third who consents. The men whose markers reached no threshold stand beside every Case as a count and are never asked for a story, because a results ask needs a marker at its threshold and can't come near a refund.

## 5. Testimonials and Integrity Levels

A testimonial is evidence only of what a client would say freely, so every rule here protects that freedom, and any result in it carries its range.

### Consent he shapes, in words he wrote

Consent comes separately from enrollment, in writing, and scoped: which words, whether his voice or face appears, under what name, on which surfaces, for how long, and how he withdraws it. The default is the narrowest version, text only under his initials, because a face can't be recalled once it's been screenshotted, and in a category this close to shame that cost lands on him. He widens the scope if he wants to, and you never widen it for him. A client who came in through the signal pause, the slower path a fit-check signal sets, or who came in checking his photos, gives words only, never captures, whatever he offers.

Typicality comes with it. A testimonial that names a result sits beside the range everyone got, and a process line such as "[52 of 56] days logged" sits beside the log's check-in completion across all clients. An experience testimonial names the destination, being taken seriously, at ease in photos, or knowing instead of guessing, and credits what his record showed him, never his face as the cause of what he wanted. On review sites, invite every graduate or none, because inviting only the happy ones is gating under another name.

The default is no incentive, because anything of value changes what the words are evidence of, and in a category known for paid endorsements a reward reads as payment even when disclosed. If anything of value ever goes with one, disclose it on the testimonial itself and give it to everyone asked, whatever he says.

### Asks stay away from every refund decision

Timing is fixed, because an ask near a refund right turns a testimonial into a trade. Never ask before the fit window, his full refund in the first 14–21 days, has closed. Never ask in a conversation that decides a refund right: the week-6 exit, his pro-rata refund for doing the work, or the week-12 verdict on the non-response clause, his partial refund when no marker reached its threshold despite the work. Never ask a client who claimed a refund. That leaves two asks at most. The process ask comes at his first measured peak after the week-6 decision is settled, from about week 7, if his record shows progress. The results ask comes after the week-12 verdict is settled, and only if a marker reached its threshold. A no to either is final. Both are Measured-Peak Asks, made only at a measurement moment that shows progress on his record (Module 22).

The ask itself is its own skill. What belongs here is the scope conversation after a yes:

> **You:** "Thanks for writing this. Before anything goes up, you decide where it lives. By default it's text only, under your initials, on the site, in email, and in long-form, and it stays out of ads. You can narrow any of that now or pull it later."
>
> **Adrian** *(composite, Optimizer)*: "Could anyone at work see it?"
>
> **You:** "Only on the surfaces you tick. If you'd rather keep it off video entirely, untick it, and nothing about your reviews changes."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

Notice the move: scope comes before publication, the narrowest version is the default, and Adrian is the only one who widens it. The ask came at his measured peak, and this exchange decides where its words may travel, because consent he shapes is consent he keeps. If he pushes the other way, hold the same boundary:

> **Adrian:** "Can you just write it up for me? You know what to say better than I do."
>
> **You:** "I won't write your words. Write it rough, a few lines, and I'll only ever suggest cuts, which you approve."

The line you never say is "I've drafted something for you; just reply 'approved'." A testimonial you wrote is your claim in his name, which is the fabricated proof a skeptic is hunting for. If he declines, or pulls it later, that's final: nothing is asked again, and his next review reads exactly like the last one.

### Integrity Levels: match to compare, add context to show, place to claim

A pair of photos is where this category's proof has been faked longest, so before/afters get their own discipline. Integrity Levels are three levels of before/after discipline, and each level unlocks one use:

| Level | What it requires | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Matched | Unedited, unfiltered originals, dated, conditions logged; the pair matches the Capture Standard, or the deviation is written down | Making the comparison at all |
| 2. In context | Level 1, plus his own context beside it: who, what else changed, timeline, capture conditions, and observed, not caused | Showing it: to him at his reads, or publicly as a capture illustration that claims no result, with his separate consent |
| 3. Placed | Level 2, plus his place in your published range, with its denominator and the men who reached no threshold in view | Claiming a result: a Case, or any pair offered as one |

A pair below Level 1 isn't a before/after; it's two photos, and it never appears as a comparison. Captures stay context at every level, since his markers decide the verdict and the pair only shows what that verdict looks like.

### Clips carry their numbers inside the frame

Where each kind of proof may travel is the Proof Portability Gradient: process proof everywhere, outcome stories in long-form, email, and your site, contextualized clips in organic short-form with range and denominator on screen, and no outcome proof in ads (Module 27). Short-form carries a result further than any page, so use it, on four conditions. The range, denominator, and "observed, not caused" stay on screen for as long as the claim does. No pair sits in the first frame, since the first frame travels alone as a thumbnail. The clip opens on an adult situation, such as his work week, because rating-style openings pull the youngest viewers fastest, and the category's hardcore audience is majority under 18. And each clip passes the Clip Context Check, the rule that an extracted clip is an original claim: restore the caveat or drop it (Module 24).

## 6. The Honest-Evidence Test and the Proof Stack

Teach buyers the test your proof was built to pass, because a skeptic who holds the standard stops discounting all proof equally and starts trusting the proof that meets it.

### He can't tell yours from theirs

A man fooled by manufactured proof usually can't say how it was made, so he discounts every pair and every testimonial by the same amount, yours included. Forewarning helps him. Showing people a weakened version of a manipulation and naming how it works gives moderate protection against the next attempt, though the protection fades without repeats. Taught as general distrust, it can breed a cynicism that lands on every claim, including yours. The protection that lasts comes from naming specific tells and pairing each with what honest evidence looks like, and that pairing binds the teacher: every viewer you train will run your lesson on you, so your Capture Standard goes public before your first teardown.

The tells sit in the photo and in the words. Each has an honest counterpart:

| The tell | What it does | The honest standard |
|---|---|---|
| The camera moved | Distance and lens change facial proportions, most visibly the nose | Matched distance and lens, stated |
| The light moved | Overhead light carves shadows along the jaw | The same light, facing him, both times |
| The pose moved | A chin tilted up or down, a turned head, or a clench changes how the jaw reads | Head level, neutral expression, the same views |
| Something else changed | Weight, water, the hour, a beard or haircut; braces, fillers, or surgery in the gap | Every change named, or the pair isn't used |
| The file changed | Filters, retouching, AI edits | Unedited originals, kept with their dates |
| The dates vanished | "[90] days" with no dates, or photos years apart | Dated captures on a fixed calendar |
| The rest vanished | Best cases only, with no count | The denominator, and the men it didn't work for |
| The words climbed | "Anyone", "rebuild", "fixed", anything about bone | Each claim on its rung |
| The money hid | An affiliate code, or a paid endorsement not disclosed | Conflicts removed, rewards disclosed |

That's the **Honest-Evidence Test**: the tells of manufactured proof and the standard of honest evidence, taught so buyers can judge anyone, you included. The honest side also states what the seller refuses, a scope and published refusals, since no grift pattern can afford either. He can carry it in six questions:

**Matched? Dated? What else changed? Which rung? Out of how many? Where are the rest?**

Run them on the genetics shrug too. "It's all genetics" is a claim about everyone, the top rung, with no count, no dates, and no sign of the men whose changeable column moved, so it fails the test the transformation post fails, from the other side.

### Cole's first teardown aims at the practice

Cole wrote his Capture Standard in month 1 and published his first teardown in month [3]. The target was a sponsored post circulating among adults, a transformation pair sold with a [chewing-device] code. He blurred the face, removed the handle and watermark, paraphrased the caption so a search couldn't trace it, and opened with the steelman: the product answers a real wish, and the man in the post may well have done real work.

Then he ran the six questions on the photograph and its words. Matched? No: in the after shot the light sits overhead, the chin is tilted, and the background shows the camera moved in, so he recreated each change on a consenting volunteer and let viewers watch the tell happen. Dated? Only "[60] days". What else changed? The post doesn't say. Which rung? "Rebuild your jawline" claims a cause no photo can show. The count? One man, posted by the seller of the lever, with the rest nowhere. His sharpest line went at the practice: "That's a lighting choice with a discount code attached." Then he ran the same questions on his own record, on screen: his capture log, his check-in completion, and the trigger for his first range. The comments asked for his capture sheet, and [three] adults booked fit conversations that week.

The default is a standing teardown every quarter, plus one whenever the same claim pattern starts showing up in your door answers, where buyers say what they've already tried. Each ends on your own record, because the protection fades without repeats and the ending turns his new skepticism into trust in you. Targets are businesses' published claims: ads, sales pages, sponsored posts. A private person's post, anyone who could be under 18, and clinicians are never targets. Blur every face and analyze the photograph and the claim, never the person, because the features of someone who hasn't consented aren't yours to discuss.

### The Proof Stack matches proof to the doubt

Proof persuades when it answers the doubt he has right now, and each doubt belongs to a link in the Belief Chain, the beliefs a purchase has to complete (Module 14). The Proof Stack is proof matched to the doubt, published on a pre-committed schedule:

| Link | His doubt | The proof that answers it | It exists from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | "Why did I stall?" | The Stall Told Back, its verdicts named | Month 1 |
| Range | "Can anything change at my age?" | Educational claims at their tier | Month 1 |
| Guide | "Is this another grift?" | The process log and the Capture Standard, both public | Month 1 |
| Self | "Will I stick with it?" | Transitions beside the log's check-in completion | About week 7 of your first client |
| Self | "Does it work for men like me?" | Cases placed in the range | The proof milestone |
| Range | "What if it doesn't work for me?" | The men who reached no threshold, then the non-responder share | The proof milestone, then 30 graduates |

### Publication runs on the schedule you committed to

The Dated Record is the moat: a published pre-commitment plus a dated log of process and outcome metrics, started in month 1, that no rival can back-fill. Its null-result stance means a result that didn't move is published beside the ones that did, never buried (Module 3).

The mechanics are yours. Process metrics go up monthly from month 1, on a fixed day, as counts while the sample is small. The first outcome ranges join at the proof milestone, labeled as a small sample, observed among clients, with every count in the chain. From 30 graduates they become the standing published log, with the non-responder share beside them: graduates at or above the adherence threshold whose markers reached none. The recommendation mix, how often your one recommendation was each option, "don't buy" included, joins after 30 Decision Assessments, the paid written plans. A client who asks for deletion leaves the counts on a dated line of his own, and a disappointing number goes out on the same date, in the same format, over the same denominator:

### Testimonial consent outline

Send it after he says yes, as its own document, outside your enrollment terms.

2. **Where, and under what name.** [The site, email, long-form, organic short-form with the range on screen], under [your initials / first name / no name]. Not in ads.

## Checklist: Teardown Safety

Run it before filming and again before publishing. One "no" holds the piece.

1. Is the target a business's published claim, such as an ad, a sales page, or a sponsored post, and not a private person's post or anyone who could be under 18? → If not, drop it.
2. Is every face blurred, with names, handles, watermarks, and identifying backgrounds removed, and the claim's words paraphrased so a search can't trace them? → If not, don't publish.
3. Does every line describe the capture or the claim (distance, lens, light, pose, dates, count), with none describing a person's features? → Rewrite any line about a face.
4. Did you steelman first, saying what the claim gets right or why a reasonable man believed it? → Add it before the first tell.
5. Is each tell shown on a consenting volunteer, and does any client material carry consent scoped to this piece? → If not, cut that material.
6. Is your Capture Standard public, and does the piece end by running the same six questions on your own record? → If not, wait.
7. Would a man who bought that product leave more capable, and would the piece survive the Hostile-Screenshot Test, the check of whether a line survives the category's harshest critic (Intro)? → Cut any line that fails either.
8. Is your own comparison free of causal verbs and of anything from the debated or never-claimed columns? → If not, you're showing the tell you came to teach.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Evidence binds: skeptics arrive long before your first graduate, and nothing you own can be placed in a range yet. The default move is to write your rules while nobody can tempt you, the milestone trigger, the Case order, and the consent template, and to let the public process log carry the proof. The trap is borrowing proof to fill the gap, such as a client's delighted message posted as a result.

**Growing.** Your first range arrives at the proof milestone, and the work shifts to Cases in their set order, the two asks at each client's measured peaks, and guarantee claim counts published beside them. The trap is letting the first flattering range become a headline and dropping its small-sample label.

**Scaling.** Volume makes the standing log the proof skeptics check first: ranges over 30 or more graduates, the non-responder share, and the waiting-start comparison once it's honest. The trap is drift, quietly swapping in the at-threshold range or a friendlier window, or letting a templated ask slide toward a refund conversation. Check both at every quarterly review.

## Standard Check

- **Proof built to be audited.** Integrity Levels and the Context Stack keep every pair matched, dated, and in context, and only the Stall Told Back may be a composite, labeled, so nothing edited, unmatched, or invented reaches a caption (the line on fabricated proof). A striking, confounded result still ships as what it is, since hiding it fails *Name the destination boldly*.
- **Numbers only from your records.** Every range names its denominator, its exits, and the men who reached no threshold, and observed, not caused keeps a caused change to his face out of every sentence (the line on structural claims).
- **Teardowns aim at the claim.** Faces blurred, captions paraphrased, tells shown on a consenting volunteer, no features described: *Fight ideas, not people* at full force, with the line on shame intact.
- **Testimonials he chose freely.** Scope set by him, his own words, no reward, and no ask near a refund decision, so each testimonial is evidence of his view rather than a trade (the line on fabricated proof).
