# 16 · Evidence That Persuades
*Part IV — The Psychology of Belief*

**The shift:** from *"proof means before/afters and testimonials"* to *"proof is a format a skeptic can audit, plus a test of honest evidence he can apply to anyone."*

Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, checks everything before he buys, since his last device came with a promise about his jaw. Tonight two pairs of photos reach him. The first sits in his feed, another seller's transformation pair: the after shot lit from above, the chin tilted, "[90] days" in bold, a discount code underneath, and no dates anywhere. The second is plainer, in a long-form video he found by searching. It shows two captures of a stranger, taken at a marked distance under the same lamp and dated 12 weeks apart. The caption names what was measured, says he also lost [a few] kilograms over those weeks, and shows how [n] of [N] graduates did on the same measure, including the men whose measures didn't move.

Theo has seen that overhead light before, so he scrolls past the first. He pauses the second on its caption, opens the dated log linked below it, and books a fit conversation, the free first call that ends in one recommendation.

The dramatic pair lost because he knew how it was made. The modest one won because he could check how it was made, what else changed, and who else was counted. In this category, that's what proof is: a format a skeptic can audit, plus a test of honest evidence he can use on anyone, you included.

## 1. Why Proof Fails Here

In this category, more proof persuades less, because your buyer arrives knowing how proof gets made and audits yours the same way.

### He knows how the photos were made

Once a buyer recognizes a persuasion tactic, he discounts the whole message and starts asking what the seller wants, which is among the better-established findings about persuasion. Your buyer has had years of practice: manufactured before/afters, devices sold with a jaw promise attached, and tips that never end. The camera facts behind the first are well documented and easy to show: distance, lens, overhead light, and a tilted chin each change how a jaw reads, and a few kilograms lost show in the lower face. He has concluded that any photo can be staged, and he's right.

So the dramatic pair works against you: the bigger the change it shows, the harder he hunts for the light. The Burned Struggler, the buyer who already paid for a structural promise, goes further and assumes any proof he can't check was made the way his last purchase was sold.

### Volume reads as selection

A pile of best cases raises the one question a pile can't answer: out of how many? Selective publication is how the category's proof got made, so a skeptic reads any result without a count as the top of a hidden stack. Testimonials carry the same flaw. A testimonial that names a result implies the result is typical, and regulators read it that way too. Small print saying results vary only turns his question into "by how much?", and a vague hedge costs more trust than a plain range with its count.

### Formats that survive an audit

Every format you might publish meets the same audit. Most fail on the first question he asks:

| Format | His first question | It survives when |
|---|---|---|
| A dramatic before/after, uncaptioned | "Same camera, same light, same weight?" | Not as posted |
| A testimonial that names a result | "Is that typical?" | It sits beside the range everyone got |
| A screenshot of a delighted message | "Who didn't write?" | As a process moment, with consent; never as outcome proof |
| A day-count promise | "Measured how, and on whom?" | It can't |
| Matched captures with their context | "What else changed?" | The caption answers before he asks |
| A dated log of process and outcomes | "Did they publish the bad months?" | The men it didn't work for are in it |

The survivors share three properties: he can check how the proof was made, what else could explain it, and who else was counted. Build every piece to answer all three before he asks, because to a buyer who audits, an answer he had to request reads as a concession, and one you volunteered reads as a standard.

## 2. The Claim Ladder and the Context Stack

Every claim you publish sits at one of five heights, and the higher it sits, the more context it must carry before it ships.

### Claims climb from what you do to what happens to everyone

The category's captions collapse those heights into one sentence. "Weekly check-ins, and your jaw will sharpen" sets a fact you control beside a forecast about his face, in one breath and with one confidence, and the certainty of the first leaks onto the second. The same collapse turns one client's result into a promise to the next man who reads it.

What separates the heights is how he could check the claim. He checks a process claim himself within a week, since the review arrives on time or it doesn't, and an educational claim against how well supported it is. A claim about one man means little without his context, and one about a group means little without its size and the men it didn't work for. Nobody can check a claim about everyone, a certain result, or his health, which is why the grift lives there:

| Rung | What it says | In this niche | What it must carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process | What you do and what he receives | "A written review of every check-in within [72] hours" | The truth, as a point |
| Educational | How something works in general | "Camera distance changes how wide a face reads" | How well supported it is |
| Observational | What one man's record showed | "His [body-composition marker] reached its threshold by week 12" | His full context |
| Outcome | What a group's records showed | "[n] of [N] graduates reached a marker threshold" | Full context, range, denominator, the men it didn't work for |
| Universal, guaranteed, or medical | Everyone, for certain, or his health | "Anyone can build a sharper jaw"; "fixes your bite" | Nothing can carry it |

That's the **Claim Ladder**. It fits on one line you can say aloud:

**Process → educational → observational → outcome → universal, guaranteed, or medical (never).**

Educational claims carry their evidence tier, how well supported a claim is: established, plausible, or debated. For the bone question the tiers are set in the Outcome Map, your straight answer to what adults can and can't change, sorted into changeable and measurable, debated, never claimed, and during growth (Module 3). A claim about jaws takes its tier from the map, not from how sure you feel that day.

### Sam's sentence comes down the ladder

Cole *(composite operator)* meets the ladder in a check-in. In week [9], Sam *(composite, Ambivalent)*, 22, a founding client, ends his log with a sincere line he wants public: "My jaw is way sharper. This method works. Post it." "This method works" is an outcome claim about everyone with no count behind it, and "my jaw is sharper" is a bathroom-mirror reading of the debated column. Cole replies the same day: "Good to hear it. Keep logging. At week 12 we'll look at matched captures and your markers, and that's the only version I'd put in public, with your say-so."

At week 12, one of Sam's markers, the two or three measures agreed at baseline that decide his verdict, reaches its threshold. Once the verdict is settled, Sam agrees to a results testimonial and offers his captures, and it waits for Cole's first range. When it runs in his site library, the caption reads:

> "Sam, 22, [eight] months of solo effort before he joined. [Body-composition marker]: [baseline reading] on [date], [week-12 reading] on [date], past his threshold. Matched captures below: same distance, lens, light, and hour, [no] deviations. Also changed: he shaved off a beard in [week 8], and at 22 he may still be maturing. Among all clients so far, [N] started, [n] left at week 6, and [n] graduated; [n] graduates reached a threshold on at least one marker, and [k] reached none. On this marker type the range was [A–B] across [n] graduates. Observed, not caused."

### Every observation carries its context

The sentence came down two rungs and gained a reader who can check it. The caption answers the six questions a skeptic asks of any result: who he is, what else changed, over what dates, under what conditions, how he compares with everyone measured, and who's counted. That's the **Context Stack**: every observational or outcome claim carries who, what else changed, timeline, capture conditions, range, and denominator, and says it was observed, not caused.

Observed, not caused is the phrasing rule for published ranges: a range says it was observed among clients, lists its confounders, sits beside a comparison where an honest one exists, and never uses a causal verb. "Built", "gave him", "fixed", and "transformed" each claim a cause the record can't isolate, and beside a photo of a face, any of them reads as the structural promise you never make.

Three confounders turn up so often that every stack names them. Weight change comes first, because body composition shows in the face. Maturation comes next, because men in their late teens and early twenties may still be maturing. Then lighting and lens, checked against the Capture Standard, matched distance, lens, angle, light, expression, and time of day for every capture (Module 6), because a drifted capture shows you the camera instead of the man.

The operating rule covers both tools. Sort every claim by rung before it ships, and build by default on process and educational claims. An observational or outcome claim ships with all six items and the phrase, or it drops a rung, or it waits for the record. The top rung stays empty everywhere, including in a client's words, a title, or a thumbnail.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a graduate's week-12 pair is the most striking you've taken, fully matched, the lower face visibly leaner. His log also shows [~7] kg lost over the same 12 weeks. The pair says result, the stack says weight, and both are true. Don't bury it: hiding a real change under-sells what can move, the failure *Name the destination boldly* exists to prevent. Don't caption it as jaw change either, because the weight explains the pair. Publish it as a body-composition case, kilograms first, the pair as what that change looks like, observed, not caused, and placed in your range, so it waits if you have none yet. It then teaches a true sentence: body composition shows in the face, and it can be measured.

## 3. Points for Process, Ranges for Results

The shape of a number is evidence to this buyer, so state what you control as a point and what varies as a tight range with its denominator.

### A point for what you control, a range for what varies

At first glance most buyers prefer a point, because "[X] in [90] days" sounds certain, and certainty sells until someone checks. Your buyer checks, and once he's weighing how a claim was made, the preference can turn: side by side, a range reads as transparency and a point as a pitch. The evidence for that reversal is weak to moderate and conditional, and the conditions are the rule. It holds when he can compare formats, collapses when the range is wide, and runs backwards on anything fixed: a review promised "within one to five days" sounds like a business that doesn't know its own week.

So the rule is Points for Process, Ranges for Results: fixed facts as points, outcomes as tight ranges. Here's how it sorts the claims you make most often:

| The claim | Its format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Review turnaround | A point: "within [72] hours" | You control it, so a range sounds like slack |
| Reviews and captures | Points: "a written review every week; captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12" | Fixed by design |
| Check-in completion | Counts while the sample is small, then a range | A percentage of three clients is a point in disguise |
| Marker change | A tight range per marker type, its denominator, the men who reached no threshold | It varies, and some men don't move |
| Visible jaw or profile change | No number at all | It sits in the debated column, said at its tier |

### Tight ranges, with a named denominator

A range wide enough to cover everything is a hedge in number form, and it usually hides two groups: men who did the work and men who didn't. So split a wide range by adherence, keep ranges per marker type, since each client's markers differ, and name who's counted. The third option uses the adherence threshold, the logging level agreed at baseline that counts as doing the work:

| Denominator | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone who started | Always, as the count above every range | Early exits pull the picture down, which a skeptic respects |
| Every graduate | For the range itself | It hides exits unless the first count sits above it |
| Graduates at or above the adherence threshold | Beside the main range, to answer "what if I do the work?" | Alone, it's a filtered sample that flatters you |

**Default: every graduate, with the other two beside it.** He needs what happened to everyone and what happens to a man who does the work. Don't publish the third row alone.

### A comparison only where one is honest

A published range sits beside a comparison only where an honest one exists, and usually none does. Your graduates chose to buy, and they differ from any group you could set beside them:

| Comparison | Pick it when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| None | By default | Readers compare you with the category's claims instead |
| At vs below the adherence threshold | A reader asks whether the work matters | It compares effort, not the program, and must say so |
| Men waiting for a later start, over the same weeks | Paid clients wait for the next start, and enough consent to share their logs | They chose to wait, and their weeks differ |

**Default: none.** A weak comparison teaches a skeptic to distrust the range beside it. Switch to the waiting-start comparison once enough waiting men share their logs to form a range of their own, and state what it can't show.

### Show the contrast, and a trigger instead of a date

The range only wins when he sees both formats, so show him the contrast yourself. Set "results in [30] days" beside "a written review every week, captures at weeks 0, 6, and 12", and "guaranteed jaw gains" beside "[n] of [N] graduates reached a marker threshold; [k] didn't". Before your records hold any range, publish your process points and the trigger your first range waits for, such as the first monthly entry after your tenth graduate with a consented process testimonial. A promised date can slip whenever a client exits, and a trigger kept in public shows him the format working before you ever show him a result.

## 4. The Four Stories

Four stories carry this brand, and each persuades only at the rung your records can support that month.

### Stories move him, and imply what's typical

A story carries him into one man's weeks. Narrative persuades with a small to moderate effect, moving intentions a little more while statistics move beliefs a little more, so every story here travels with its numbers or a plain statement that it has none. The risk comes with the power: a story about one man implies he's typical, so each carries its context, and the four split by whose voice they're in.

Each answers a question he brings: why you built this, why he stalled, what changes when a man starts measuring, and what happened to one measured man among everyone measured. That's the **Four Stories**: Origin, the Stall Told Back, the Transition, and the Case, each with its context. Two are yours and run from month 1. Two are his and wait for consent asked at his first measured peak after his week-6 decision, a reading with his adherence and one marker at threshold, so no client story exists in month 1.

| Story | Whose voice | What it proves | Rung | Usable from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Origin | Yours | Why the standard exists | Process, educational | Month 1 |
| The Stall Told Back | Yours, from anonymized patterns | His stall has a cause measuring can find | Educational | Month 1 |
| The Transition | His words, cut only with his approval | A man can stop guessing and start measuring | Process, as a story | About week 7 of your first client |
| The Case | His record, placed in your range | One measured man, among everyone measured | Observational, placed | Your first range, at 10 graduates |

Composites belong only in the Stall Told Back, labeled in the asset as a composite of several men's patterns. A composite Transition or Case would be an invented testimonial, which the line on fabricated proof rules out.

### Your two stories run from month 1

Origin tells why the standard exists: what you watched fail, what you couldn't find, and what you built, with dates and what you measure now. It implies no qualification you don't hold and includes no before/after of your own, because your appearance is never evidence for the method. You say so publicly in What My Face Does and Doesn't Prove, the statement of what your face can't show (Module 15).

The Stall Told Back is his story told better than he tells it, built from anonymized patterns in your conversations and, later, your records. Its force goes at the situation, and it claims no verdict it can't check:

> "Eight months. You did the work most nights and took a comparison photo most weeks, and no two were taken the same way, so the photos couldn't tell you anything. Most stalls we see are direction problems: months of real effort with no map and nothing measured. Measuring is how you'd know if yours is. Right now nobody can say, you included. By week 6 your record shows what's moving, and by week 12 it can tell you a lever doesn't move for you."

Dan *(composite, Struggler)* hears himself in it and gets nothing he can't check. It names the part of his stall he can change this week, the missing measurement, and admits the third answer, which keeps it inside Stall Verdicts: a stall is unmeasured, misdirected, or "the lever doesn't move for this person" (Module 14). It grades his setup, not his face or his discipline, so it passes the Dignity Check, whether he leaves more capable or more defective (Intro).

### His two stories wait for his consent

The Transition is a client's change of belief in his own words, from asking the mirror whether it's working to reading his record for what's moving. It needs no outcome, but it's still a testimonial, so it follows testimonial timing. You never supply the words: you send him a few questions, and your only edits are cuts he approves, because a Transition you drafted reads as coached to the skeptic it's meant for. Maya *(composite, welcome, not targeted)*, 28, wrote hers like this:

> "I kept swapping routines every few weeks because nothing seemed to work. My second review said to hold the plan and log it, and for the first time I could see what was moving. I'm back in the team photos, and I've stopped thinking about my face in design reviews. Knowing did more for that than anything I changed."

Reid *(composite operator)* made the common misread and waited for outcome proof before publishing any, so his first [eight] months carried no story but his own. Cole's first Transition ran in month [3] and showed skeptics the work from inside, which no outcome could do yet.

### The Case runs in an order set before anyone qualifies

The Case is one man's measured arc: his starting point and markers, the written read at week 6, the capture and verdict at week 12, what else changed, and his place in your range, with the men who reached no threshold in view. It's the only story that makes an outcome claim, so it waits for the proof milestone, 10 or more graduates who've given consented process testimonials, when your first range appears.

The category picks its showcases from the top, so set your order before anyone qualifies. Cases rotate through the thirds of your range, middle first, each time with the next graduate in that third who consents. The men whose markers reached no threshold stand beside every Case as a count and are never asked for a story, because a results ask needs a marker at its threshold and can't come near a refund.

## 5. Testimonials and Integrity Levels

A testimonial is evidence only of what a client would say freely, so every rule here protects that freedom, and any result in it carries its range.

### Consent he shapes, in words he wrote

Consent comes separately from enrollment, in writing, and scoped: which words, whether his voice or face appears, under what name, where, for how long, and how he withdraws it. The default is the narrowest version, text only under his initials, because a face can't be recalled once it's been screenshotted, and in a category this close to shame that cost lands on him. He can widen the scope; you never widen it for him. A client who came through the signal pause, the slower path a fit-check signal sets, or who arrived checking his photos, gives words only, whatever he offers.

Typicality comes with it. A testimonial that names a result sits beside the range everyone got, and a process line such as "[52 of 56] days logged" sits beside the log's check-in completion across all clients. An experience testimonial names the destination, being taken seriously, at ease in photos, or knowing instead of guessing, and credits what his record showed him, never his face. On review sites, invite every graduate or none, because inviting only the happy ones is gating under another name.

The default is no incentive, because anything of value changes what the words are evidence of, and in a category known for paid endorsements, a reward reads as payment even when disclosed. If anything of value ever goes with one, disclose it on the testimonial and give it to everyone asked, whatever he says.

### Asks stay away from every refund decision

Timing is fixed, because an ask near a refund right turns a testimonial into a trade. Never ask before the fit window, his full refund in the first 14–21 days, has closed. Never ask in a conversation that decides a refund: the week-6 exit, his pro-rata refund for doing the work, or the week-12 verdict on the non-response clause, his partial refund when no marker reached its threshold despite the work. And never ask a client who claimed a refund.

That leaves two asks at most. The process ask comes at his first measured peak after the week-6 decision is settled, from about week 7, if his record shows progress. The results ask comes after the week-12 verdict is settled, and only if a marker reached its threshold with his adherence at or above its threshold. A no to either is final. Both are Measured-Peak Asks, made only at a measurement moment that shows progress on his record (Module 22). The ask is its own skill; this module's part is the scope conversation after a yes:

> **You:** "Thanks for writing this. Before anything goes up, you decide where it lives. By default it's text only, under your initials, on the site, in email, and in long-form, and it stays out of ads. You can narrow any of that now or pull it later."
>
> **Adrian** *(composite, Optimizer)*: "Could anyone at work see it?"
>
> **You:** "Only on the surfaces you tick. If you'd rather keep it off video entirely, untick it, and nothing about your reviews changes."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

Notice the move: scope before publication, the narrowest version as the default, and Adrian as the only one who widens it, because consent he shapes is consent he keeps. If he pushes the other way, hold the same boundary:

> **Adrian:** "Can you just write it up for me? You know what to say better than I do."
>
> **You:** "I won't write your words. Write it rough, a few lines, and I'll only ever suggest cuts, which you approve."

The line you never say is "I've drafted something for you; just reply 'approved'." A testimonial you wrote is your claim in his name, the fabricated proof a skeptic is hunting for. If he declines, or pulls it later, that's final: nothing is asked again, and his next review reads like the last one.

### Integrity Levels: match to compare, add context to show, place to claim

A pair of photos is where this category's proof has been faked longest, so before/afters get their own discipline. Integrity Levels are three levels of before/after discipline, each unlocking one use:

| Level | What it requires | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Matched | Unedited originals, dated, conditions logged; matched to the Capture Standard, or the deviation written down | Making the comparison at all |
| 2. In context | Level 1, plus his own context: who, what else changed, timeline, conditions, observed, not caused | Showing it: at his reads, or publicly as an illustration claiming no result, with his separate consent |
| 3. Placed | Level 2, plus his place in your published range, with its denominator and the men who reached no threshold | Claiming a result: a Case, or any pair offered as one |

A pair below Level 1 isn't a before/after; it's two photos, and it never appears as a comparison. Captures stay context at every level, since his markers decide the verdict.

### Clips carry their numbers inside the frame

Where each kind of proof may travel is the Proof Portability Gradient: process proof everywhere, outcome stories in long-form, email, and your site, contextualized clips in organic short-form with range and denominator on screen, and no outcome proof in ads (Module 27). Short-form carries a result further than any page, so use it, on four conditions.

The range, denominator, and "observed, not caused" stay on screen as long as the claim does. No before/after pair appears in the clip at all, since pairs live in long-form and your site library, where their context travels with them. The clip opens on an adult situation, because rating-style openings pull the youngest viewers fastest in a category whose hardcore audience is majority under 18. And each clip passes the Clip Context Check: an extracted clip is an original claim, so put the caveat back or drop the clip (Module 24).

## 6. The Honest-Evidence Test and the Proof Stack

Teach buyers the test your proof was built to pass, because a skeptic who holds the standard stops discounting all proof equally and starts trusting the proof that meets it.

### He can't tell yours from theirs

A man fooled by manufactured proof usually can't say how it was made, so he discounts every pair and testimonial by the same amount, yours included. Forewarning helps: showing people a weakened version of a manipulation and naming how it works gives moderate protection against the next attempt, though it fades without repeats. Taught as general distrust, it can breed a cynicism that lands on every claim, yours too. What lasts is naming specific tells and pairing each with what honest evidence looks like, and that pairing binds the teacher, so your Capture Standard goes public before your first teardown.

The tells sit in the photo and in the words. Each has an honest counterpart:

| The tell | What it does | The honest standard |
|---|---|---|
| The camera moved | Distance and lens change facial proportions, most visibly the nose | Matched distance and lens, stated |
| The light moved | Overhead light carves shadows along the jaw | The same light, facing him |
| The pose moved | Chin up or down, a turned head, or a clench changes how the jaw reads | Head level, neutral expression |
| Something else changed | Weight, water, the hour, a beard or haircut; braces, fillers, or surgery in the gap | Every change named, or no pair |
| The file changed | Filters, retouching, AI edits | Unedited originals, dated |
| The dates vanished | "[90] days" with no dates | Dated captures on a fixed calendar |
| The rest vanished | Best cases only, no count | The denominator, and the men it didn't work for |
| The words climbed | "Anyone", "rebuild", "fixed", anything about bone | Each claim on its rung |
| The money hid | An affiliate code, or an undisclosed paid endorsement | Conflicts removed, rewards disclosed |

That's the **Honest-Evidence Test**: the tells of manufactured proof and the standard of honest evidence, taught so buyers can judge anyone, you included. The honest side also publishes its scope and its refusals, which no grift can afford. He can carry it in six questions:

**Matched? Dated? What else changed? Which rung? Out of how many? Where are the rest?**

Run them on the genetics shrug too. "It's all genetics" is a claim about everyone, the top rung, with no count, no dates, and no sign of the men whose changeable column moved, so it fails the same test from the other side.

### Cole's first teardown aims at the practice

Cole wrote his Capture Standard in month 1 and published his first teardown in month [3], on a sponsored post circulating among adults: a transformation pair sold with a [chewing-device] code. He blurred the face, removed the handle and watermark, paraphrased the caption so a search couldn't trace it, and opened with the steelman: the product answers a real wish, and the man in the post may well have done real work.

Then he ran the six questions. Matched? No: in the after shot the light sits overhead, the chin is tilted, and the background shows the camera moved in, so he recreated each change on a consenting volunteer. Dated? Only "[60] days". What else changed? The post doesn't say. Which rung? "Rebuild your jawline" claims a cause no photo can show. The count? One man, posted by the seller of the lever, and the rest nowhere. His sharpest line went at the practice: "That's a lighting choice with a discount code attached." Then he ran the same questions on his own record: his capture log, his check-in completion, and the trigger for his first range. [Three] adults booked fit conversations that week.

The default is a standing teardown every quarter, plus one whenever a claim pattern starts showing up in your door answers, where buyers say what they've already tried. Each ends on your own record, because the protection fades without repeats and the ending turns his skepticism into trust in you. Targets are businesses' published claims, such as ads, sales pages, and sponsored posts, and never a private person, anyone who could be under 18, or a clinician. Blur every face and analyze the photograph and the claim, never the person, because the features of someone who hasn't consented aren't yours to discuss.

### The Proof Stack matches proof to the doubt

Proof persuades when it answers the doubt he has now, and each doubt belongs to a link in the Belief Chain, the beliefs a purchase has to complete (Module 14). The Proof Stack is proof matched to the doubt, published on a pre-committed schedule:

| Link | His doubt | The proof that answers it | It exists from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | "Why did I stall?" | The Stall Told Back, verdicts named | Month 1 |
| Range | "Can anything change at my age?" | Educational claims at their tier | Month 1 |
| Guide | "Is this another grift?" | The public process log and Capture Standard | Month 1 |
| Self | "Will I stick with it?" | Transitions beside check-in completion | About week 7 of your first client |
| Self | "Does it work for men like me?" | Cases placed in the range | The proof milestone |
| Range | "What if it doesn't work for me?" | The men who reached no threshold, then the non-responder share | The milestone, then 30 graduates |

### Publication runs on the schedule you committed to

The Dated Record is the moat: a published pre-commitment plus a dated log of process and outcome metrics, started in month 1, that no rival can back-fill. Its null-result stance means a result that didn't move is published beside the ones that did, never buried (Module 3).

The mechanics are yours. Process metrics go up monthly from month 1, on a fixed day, as counts while the sample is small. The first outcome ranges join at the proof milestone, labeled a small sample, with every count in the chain. From 30 graduates they become the standing log, beside the non-responder share: graduates at or above the adherence threshold whose markers reached none. The recommendation mix, how often your one recommendation was each option, "don't buy" included, joins after 30 Decision Assessments, the paid written plans. A deletion request becomes a dated line, and a disappointing number goes out on the same date, in the same format, over the same denominator:

## Worked Example: Cole, Choosing the First Case

Cole reaches his proof milestone in month [10], and his first real proof decision comes with it: which graduate's Case goes up first. He made most of that decision in month 1.

### Testimonial consent outline

Send it after he says yes, as its own document, outside your enrollment terms.

1. **What we'd use.** Your words as written. Your voice or captures only if you offer them and tick them here.
2. **Where, and under what name.** [The site, email, long-form, organic short-form with the range on screen], under [your initials / first name / no name]. Not in ads.
3. **How long.** Until you withdraw it. Reply [remove] at any time, and it comes down everywhere we control within [a stated number of days].
4. **What it's tied to.** Nothing. Saying no, or withdrawing later, changes nothing in your reviews, refunds, or terms, and nothing of value goes with a yes.
5. **What sits beside it.** Any result you mention appears only beside the range everyone got, with its count.
6. **Your words, your final look.** We suggest cuts only, and you see the exact version, caption included, before it goes up.

## Checklist: Teardown Safety

Run it before filming and again before publishing. One "no" holds the piece.

1. Is the target a business's published claim, such as an ad, a sales page, or a sponsored post, and not a private person's post or anyone who could be under 18? → If not, drop it.
2. Is every face blurred, with names, handles, watermarks, and identifying backgrounds removed, and the claim's words paraphrased so a search can't trace them? → If not, don't publish.
3. Does every line describe the capture or the claim, such as distance, light, pose, dates, or count, and none a person's features? → Rewrite any line about a face.
4. Did you steelman first, saying what the claim gets right or why a reasonable man believed it? → Add it before the first tell.
5. Is each tell shown on a consenting volunteer, and does any client material carry consent scoped to this piece? → If not, cut that material.
6. Is your Capture Standard public, and does the piece end with the same six questions run on your own record? → If not, wait.
7. Would a man who bought that product leave more capable, and would the piece survive the Hostile-Screenshot Test, the check of whether a line survives the category's harshest critic (Intro)? → Cut any line that fails either.
8. Is your own comparison free of causal verbs and of anything from the debated or never-claimed columns? → If not, you're showing the tell you came to teach.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Evidence binds: skeptics arrive long before your first graduate, and nothing you own can be placed in a range yet. The default move is to write your rules while nobody can tempt you, the milestone trigger, the Case order, and the consent template, and to let the public process log carry the proof. The trap is borrowing proof to fill the gap, such as a client's delighted message posted as a result.

**Growing.** Your first range arrives at the proof milestone, and the work shifts to Cases in their set order, the two asks at each client's measured peaks, and guarantee claim counts published beside them. The trap is letting the first flattering range become a headline and dropping its small-sample label.

**Scaling.** Volume makes the standing log the proof skeptics check first: ranges over 30 or more graduates, the non-responder share, and the waiting-start comparison once it's honest. The trap is drift, quietly swapping in the at-threshold range or a friendlier window, or letting a templated ask slide toward a refund conversation. Check both at every quarterly review.

## Standard Check

- **Proof built to be audited.** Integrity Levels and the Context Stack keep every pair matched, dated, and in context, and only the Stall Told Back may be a labeled composite, so nothing edited or invented reaches a caption (the line on fabricated proof). A striking, confounded result still ships as what it is, since hiding it fails *Name the destination boldly*.
- **Numbers only from your records.** Every range names its denominator, exits, and the men who reached no threshold, and observed, not caused keeps a caused change to his face out of every sentence (the line on structural claims).
- **Teardowns aim at the claim.** Faces blurred, captions paraphrased, tells shown on a volunteer, no features described: *Fight ideas, not people* at full force, with the line on shame intact.
- **Testimonials he chose freely.** His scope, his words, no reward, and no ask near a refund decision, so each is his view, not a trade (the line on fabricated proof).
