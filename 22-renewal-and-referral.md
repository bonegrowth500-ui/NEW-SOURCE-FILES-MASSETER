# 22 · Renewal and Referral
*Part V — Selling and Transformation*

**The shift:** from *"Renewals and referrals happen if clients are happy"* to *"Asks are timed to measured peaks, renewal is argued from his record, and every client gets a private referral ask."*

Two men finish the Program, your 12-week flagship, in the same week under two different operators. Both logged most of their days. Both have a marker, one of the measures agreed on their first day, past its threshold. The first gets a last weekly review like the eleven before it, then an email with thanks and a review link. Within a month he's checking the mirror again, and when a friend asks, he says the program was fine.

The second opens his week-12 re-assessment, the end-of-program capture and verdict, beside his baseline. It shows [70] of [84] days logged, each marker scored against its threshold, and captures matched to one standard. His reviewer sets that record against the card he read before paying and recommends one next step plainly. Then comes one private question: has anyone asked you what you've been doing?

Their records were much alike. What differed was when each thing was asked, and what the asking stood on. This module times every ask to a read that shows progress. It argues the renewal from his record and gives every client at a peak one private referral ask.

## 1. Peaks and Measured Moments

Every ask the back end makes, for a renewal, a referral, a testimonial, or an upgrade, belongs to a moment when his record shows progress, and to no other.

### Asks on your calendar land on his worst weeks

Most operators ask on their own calendar. A review request goes out in week 4, a pitch for another block in the last week, a referral email to everyone at once. That calendar can't see his mid-program plateau, the 7-day pro-rata exit right his week-6 read opens, or a week-12 verdict that pays a refund. So some asks land at exactly those points, where this category sold men one more device when nothing had moved. A man who remembers that reads the ask as the same pattern.

### The honest peak here is a measured one

Memory weighs an experience's best moment and its ending more heavily than its average week, a finding with moderate support. So the same ask can get a different answer depending on when it lands. In this niche the best moment has to be a measured one, because appearance evidence is slow and confounded. A good week feels like progress, a capture under kind light looks like it, and neither is. A read of his markers against their thresholds is progress he can check himself.

So an ask waits for two things. First, a read showing progress: adherence at or above the threshold printed before he paid, and at least one marker at its threshold. Second, no refund decision left open. His fit window, the full refund in his first 14–21 days, has closed, his week-6 exit decision is settled, and at week 12 the verdict comes first. A marker still climbing isn't enough: until one reaches its threshold, the non-response clause, his partial cash refund if none does despite his effort, is still his to collect.

**Progress on his record, every refund decision settled, then the ask.**

That's **Measured-Peak Asks**: renewal, referral, testimonial, and upgrade asks made only at a measurement moment that shows progress on his record, never at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit. A read that meets both parts is a measured peak. A flat read has no marker at its threshold and none improving. A capture that looks better to him never makes a peak, since captures are observations and markers decide.

### Dan's twelve weeks held two peaks

Dan *(composite, Struggler)*, 24, started in a group of ten with about eight unmeasured months behind him. Nothing was asked when his fit window closed in week 3 or at his plateau in week 5. His week-6 read, the written mid-program read, stated his exit right for 7 days with no label or ask beside it.

In week [9], [his posture-habit marker] reached its threshold with [52] of [63] days logged, his first measured peak. It carried one process-testimonial ask, and his no ended those for good. At week 12 the verdict came first: moved, with momentum. Only then did his graduation call bring one renewal recommendation and one private referral ask.

### Each ask has one home

The operating rule: every ask waits for a measured peak, and each has its home. The renewal is argued once, at graduation, for Round Two, the lighter maintenance block, or the Hold, the quarterly measurement subscription. An upgrade to Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables, waits for a record that calls for one.

| Ask | Its measured peak | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Process testimonial | His first, from about week 7 | The fit window; the week-6 exit conversation |
| Results testimonial | Week 12, after the verdict is written | The verdict conversation; after any refund |
| Round Two or the Hold | Graduation, previewed at week 10; for a Hold member who hasn't had Round Two, a quarterly peak with momentum | A plateau, a flat read, an exit; after the clause pays |
| Referral | Graduation, once | His fit or exit window; after "I can't afford it", which ends every ask in that conversation |
| Upgrade to Private | One where his record calls for a full block | A record that calls for maintenance |

Thresholds are set within reach of about 12 weeks' work for most clients who do it. So most first peaks arrive in the Program's last month. Timed that way, every ask meets a man holding evidence he can check, and his yes still stands if his results land at the low end of the range.

## 2. The Peak-End Finish

The last fortnight decides how he remembers the whole Program, so build it around the moment he knows, and let his record carry that moment.

### This niche's default ending is a photo

Memory weighs an ending heavily, so the last two weeks set how the other ten are remembered and what he tells the friend who asks. The usual ending is administrative: a last review like the others, a feedback form, a billing notice. The ending a man in this niche stages for himself is worse, a new selfie beside an old one under bathroom light. His comparison-photo habit gets its biggest moment, and twelve weeks of record get none.

### The page he reads puts the record before the photo

The written verdict goes out before any call, and its order does the designing. It opens on his count, days logged against his threshold, because that's the part he did. Then come his markers against their thresholds, the captures labeled as observations, the verdict, and his next capture date. By the time he reaches the photos, the markers have decided, so a pair that shows little can't overturn a verdict that moved.

**Count, markers, captures, verdict, next date: in that order.**

That's the **Peak-End Finish**: the final fortnight designed as the Program's peak, built from his record and ending on it. Week 10's review names what week 12 decides, with the card line each outcome leads to for a man at a measured peak. Week 11 checks his capture setup and adds no lesson. A reveal or a countdown to "results day" would build feeling around a photo, feed the checking habit, and leave a low-end client feeling set up. The record holds where a staged peak can't, because even the man at the low end leaves knowing what moved.

### When the markers moved and the captures didn't

Theo *(composite, Burned Struggler)*, 26, finishes with [his posture-habit marker] at threshold since week [10] and [his body-composition measure] past its own. His week-12 captures, matched to the standard, show little a stranger would notice. He once paid for a device sold on a before-and-after, and he has waited twelve weeks for this comparison. If the call opened on the photos, he'd file the Program beside the device.

So his page runs in order: [68] of [84] days logged, both markers past their thresholds, the pair labeled as observations, and the verdict, moved. On the call you say it plainly: "Your captures show little change at twelve weeks, which is common. Your markers decide, and they moved." Nobody promises what later photos will show, because visible change from habits is debated. His peak is what he now knows about his own levers, and he can check every line.

### A record without a peak gets the same finish, minus the asks

For a man with no measured peak, the finish runs the same way and asks for nothing. His verdict is written plainly, and the clause pays within 7 days where it applies. A referral to [the professional his question belongs with] goes with it where one fits. He paid to stop guessing, and a clear no is still an answer. The Dignity Check, whether he leaves more capable or more defective, turns here on whether the verdict reads his record or his face.

## 3. The Renewal Case

At graduation his next step is argued from his own record against the timeline he accepted before paying, and one recommendation comes out of it, including, when it's true, that he doesn't need more.

### His card already priced the choice

Before he paid, he read the Path and Timeline Card, the one page that states what 12 weeks deliver and when change tends to show (Module 6). It priced his likely first [9] months down each path he could take next, full intensity included. So graduation brings no new offer. This category's usual renewal is a surprise next phase sold to everyone while he's most hopeful: the endless-tips pattern with an invoice.

Both offers, Round Two and the Hold, are designed elsewhere (Module 10). This module builds the argument for one of them, or for neither, and that argument is the **Renewal Case**. His record makes the case, and your job is to read it out.

### Three facts from his sheet decide it

The case rests on three facts from his marker sheet: adherence against his threshold, each marker against its threshold, and each marker's last two readings. The first two say whether he's at a measured peak. The third says whether a trend is left to protect. Measured momentum, adherence at or above threshold with at least one marker still improving across its last two readings, is Round Two's qualifier.

| His record at week 12 | What it says | Your recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Moved, with measured momentum | A trend review can still protect | Round Two, once |
| Moved and holding | Nothing left for review to protect | "You don't need Round Two"; the Hold by default, his own date when he reads his record himself |
| Moved late, after a week-6 correction from outside your plan | The corrected lever needs full review | Full-intensity Round Two, priced as a Program, as his card showed |
| A signal your reviews missed | He lost weeks to your miss | Corrective weeks at no charge, up to 6, before any paid block |
| No measured peak | Adherence below threshold, or no marker at one | No offer |

Full intensity must pass the verdict's own tests: a named lever, a documented correction, and early movement at the next reading. His intake and logs must also have shown nothing sooner. A miss that was yours earns the weeks it cost, up to 6, free and counted in the claim rate you publish. When you can't tell whose miss it was, count it as yours. Round Two runs once by default, and a second needs its own measured momentum, plus his ask.

### The stake is time and guessing

The true stake is what the next three months do to his record. Habits take a median of about two months to become automatic, with a range from days to most of a year. His logs usually show which of his still lean on the review. So he's choosing between a reviewer on a trend his record shows and three months in which nobody reads the weeks he skips. Money enters only as money from here on, and his face, any structural claim, and his past spending stay out.

"You don't need Round Two" belongs to the same case. When his record says moved and holding, say it plainly, with the reason: nothing is still moving for more review to protect. Default to the Hold for that record, since a reviewer's quarterly read answers "did it hold?" better than a mirror. Switch to his own quarterly date when his check-ins name his misses before your review does. From 30 graduates the card publishes the share told "you don't need Round Two", which makes your yes believable to a man who's been upsold.

### One recommendation, one plain ask, then the question

Run the decision as a short contract, the arc of Close by Contract, from an agreed structure to one recommendation, a price stated once, and a clear ask (Module 19). Show the options his record qualifies him for, premium first at his card's prices, and recommend one with its reasons. Ask plainly whether he wants to start it, so a no is as easy as a yes. When he picks, ask about his pick, word for word:

> "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

A yes goes to the Fit Check, the plain-language check for signals that buying could hurt him, and then to booking his start. A no ends money talk, with no Hold after a Round Two no and no plan or start date. He keeps his record and his quarterly date. The pause route, a content-free tag, stops sales sends for 60–90 days, then asks permission again. Nothing starts by default: Round Two ends at its own week 12, and the Hold begins only on his yes.

"Let me think" is a deferral, a real objection to sort, and it isn't a no. Ask what he's weighing, then agree his Decision Date, a day he picks on or before the last day to join Round Two's next start. The Follow-Up Rule carries it from there: a written recap within 24 hours, one check-in on his date, then one close-the-loop message.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a graduate at a clear measured peak asks for [$200] off Round Two "as a returning client". His record says yes, but his request puts the price in question, so ask the affordability question first: it separates "I can't comfortably pay this" from "I'd like to pay less". A no ends money talk, with no Hold as the cheaper route and no trimmed-down block. A yes means you hold the price on his card and ask once more, because nobody pays less for the same seat. In a category known for "today only" offers, a discount would say the recommendation was a sale.

## 4. Measured-Peak Asks for Testimonials and Upgrades

Testimonial and upgrade asks follow the same peak rule, and each adds a condition of its own. A testimonial goes to everyone who reaches a measured peak, twice at most. An upgrade goes where his record calls for what Private sells.

### A testimonial is evidence of what he'd say freely

The testimonial rules live elsewhere: scoped, revocable consent, text only by default, his own words, no incentive, and everyone's range beside any result (Module 16). No ask comes before his fit window closes, or in a conversation that decides a refund, meaning the week-6 exit or the week-12 non-response verdict. None goes to a client who claimed a refund. Within that, he gets two asks at most, and a no to either is final. A client who came through the signal pause, the slower path a fit-check signal sets, or who arrived checking his photos, gives words only, even if he offers a capture.

The process ask comes at his first measured peak from about week 7, and the results ask once his week-12 verdict is written. Ask every client who reaches one. Asking only the men you expect to glow is selection, and a skeptic assumes it the moment he sees a wall of praise. Accounts written close to an experience also tend to read as more credible than memories of it. Keep the ask small: two or three lines, in his words, about what the record showed him.

### The upgrade is a real recommendation when his record calls for a full block

Private sells speed, privacy, and attention as fixed deliverables, and it promises no better result. At Scaling it sells only at parity, under the Premium Lane, the Optimizer's path from the priority tier to Private (Module 13). The ask is honest when his record calls for a full block and what he has asked for is what Private delivers. In a category where "precision" has meant a closer read of the face, every Private page, call, and recap says "We never read or score your face."

Say a graduate at Scaling moved late. His week-6 read found a misdirection from outside your plan, [a routine he'd never logged]. Since the correction, his logs have held above threshold, that marker has improved at both readings, and [another marker] sits at its own. His check-ins show his rotating shifts never once fit the group call. So Private goes first, beside the same block priced as a Program:

> **You:** "Your record calls for twelve more weeks at full review. Private is [$10,000]: a recorded walkthrough of your plan at weeks 0, 4, 8, and 12, a deeper written review every week, written replies within a day on weekdays, up to [two] a week, and three short calls at times you choose. We never read or score your face. The same block in the group is [$3,900]. My recommendation is Private, because the call has never fit your shifts. Do you want to start it?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

A yes sends the affordability question to Private, word for word, then the Fit Check. A no to that question ends money talk, and the group block isn't offered in Private's place. The seat itself must fit Seat Math, the monthly caps set from measured care minutes, and pass the Parity Rule, earning at least what a Program seat earns per care hour.

On a record that calls for maintenance, no upgrade is asked. Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, a consultant whose client travel kept him off most calls, asks whether Private would get him "further". His sheet shows momentum and nothing more. So you tell him no, since Round Two's review is written and the calls he missed cost him nothing.

## 5. Ask Everyone, Privately

Every client at a measured peak gets one direct, specific, private referral ask, because direct asks succeed far more often than operators expect, and your plan counts on none of what they bring.

### Direct asks work more often than you'd guess

Reid *(composite operator)* never asked. He assumed men in this niche keep the work to themselves and would find the question awkward. His referrals came only from clients who volunteered, [two] in his first year. Half of that was right: many men here do keep it private, since being seen to buy help with how your face photographs can read as insecurity. What Reid got wrong was the answer in private, because people expect roughly half the yeses a direct, specific request actually gets.

Two more findings shape the ask. Intentions to refer run well ahead of referrals made, so an ask must name a person and a small step. And people pass on what makes them look good, so what he forwards should make him look rigorous.

**One private, specific ask of every client at a measured peak, and none of it in the plan.**

That's **Ask Everyone, Privately**: a direct, specific, private referral ask of every client at a peak, never planned on. It repairs the Share link, the belief that recommending you makes him look careful rather than insecure. Asked this way, a referral stops being a favor he's embarrassed to do and becomes a standard he passes on.

### The ask names a person, stays private, and pays nothing

Specific means it names a person, a step, and what happens next. The person is "the one who's asked what you've been doing", and the step is forwarding one link or one card. What happens next is the same self-assessment he took, his friend's own answers, and nobody contacting anyone unless he books.

Keeping it private means one-to-one, in his own conversation, with nothing about either man passed to the other and no public "share your results". Nothing is paid for it. A reward turns his word into a paid endorsement to a friend who still has to pass your Fit Check. In a category known for paid endorsements, it reads as one. After "I can't afford it" nothing is asked at all, since a stop rule ends every ask in that conversation.

Never planned on means your Reverse Funnel, the plan worked back from profit to reach, counts none of it. In a niche where most men keep the work private, referrals ride on a handful of friendships and swing from month to month. Referred clients do tend to stay longer and be worth more, so nobody at a peak is skipped. The plan still works without them.

### Shareables let him pass it on without exposing himself

Identity-safe shareables are referral objects that make the sharer look rigorous and reveal nothing he didn't choose to share. They're public education a skeptic would respect, such as the six conditions that make a capture comparable, or the six questions that test any before-and-after. Each carries one link to your self-assessment. Each also passes those six questions itself, claiming nothing above the educational rung and holding no face, capture, or result of his.

They travel further than you intend, and a large share of this category's most engaged audience is under 18. So the link opens on the age fork, the door's first question, and nothing on any card asks for anything. The capture card sets a cadence of every few months at most. Nothing rewards posting a face, either: no contests, no free month for a before-and-after, no tags, since a posted face can't be recalled once it's screenshotted.

### The partner seat is the most specific ask of all

The Training-Partner Seat is pair enrollment of two adults into one start, each through his own door, at the published price, with no pair discount (Module 10). At graduation it works as a referral: the graduate introduces the man he trains with, and the graduate isn't part of the pair. The man who shares his gym, schedule, or home is often the one person who knows about the work. His check-ins usually name him.

That partner has two routes in. He can enroll alone, as an ordinary buyer, or start with a partner of his own as a pair, two seats from one start's real cap. Either way he answers the age fork, the affordability question, and the Fit Check himself, in private. A graduate who offers to pay for his seat hears a plain no. Money that isn't the buyer's own routes the buyer to the Starter Path, the free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now.

## 6. Alumni Status and Graduation

Graduation confers alumni status, a dated fact in his record that nothing he does next can add to or take away, and it comes before any ask the graduation call makes.

### Alumni status is a dated fact, the same for every graduate

Everyone who reaches week 12 graduates and keeps his whole record. Alumni status is that fact, dated in his record: [Brand] graduate, [date], reached week 12. Beside it go a label that leads with a count from his logs and his next capture date, on his own or in the Hold. When prices step up, alumni keep the rates they were shown. What the status means for who he is belongs to Adults Who Measure, the group of adults who measure instead of guess (Module 17).

Nothing about it rests on his results, his purchases, or a refund right, and nobody presents it as something an exit costs. Take Client 10 in the worked example below, whose clause paid. He carries the same dated line as a man who renewed, a label with his own count, "[71] of [84] days logged", and his own quarterly date. The asks skip him, since his graduation isn't a measured peak, and his status is untouched.

### The call confers the status before it asks for anything

On the graduation call, the status comes first. His record is read in the verdict's order, then he's given the dated line and his label. Only then, at a measured peak, come the Renewal Case, the referral ask, and a results ask unless he has already said no. So no ask can read as the price of the status, and no answer changes it. A man who declines all three leaves with the same line as one who says yes to each.

### The room is opt-in, and nothing a graduate is owed sits inside it

The alumni room is part of the Hold, opening once you have about 30 alumni, and each member chooses to join (Module 10). Standing inside comes from re-captures kept and logs sent, and no room, board, or email mentions who left. A graduate paid under the clause isn't offered the Hold, so the room isn't open to him. He loses nothing a graduate is owed, since his record, label, date, and status all live outside it.

## Scripts: The Graduation Call and Its Asks

**The graduation outline.** About [25] minutes, after the written verdict has gone out. A graduate without a measured peak gets steps 1–3 and step 7, with no recommendation and no asks.

1. **Contract.** "Your verdict's in writing already. Today we read your record together, I'll recommend one next step, and I have two short questions at the end. A no to any of it is fine." Without a peak: "Today we read your record and set your next capture date. Nothing's for sale in this call."
2. **The record, whole.** In the verdict's order. "Here's what your record shows against the thresholds you signed on day one."
3. **Graduation.** "You reached week 12. That's a fact in your record now, whatever comes next." Then the label, count first.
4. **The Renewal Case.** Options premium first at his card's prices, one recommendation, each price once, one plain ask, then the affordability question about his pick and the Fit Check.
5. **The referral ask.** One private, specific question.
6. **The testimonial ask.** Only if he hasn't said no and no refund was paid.
7. **Close on his record.** "Your next capture is [your date]. You came here to stop guessing, and now your record says what's moving and what to do next."

Notice the order. The record comes before any recommendation, so the peak is something he can check. The asks follow his decision, so none reads as the price of the next step. That's the Peak-End Finish at work, and if a stop rule fires at step 4, the call goes straight to step 7.

**The renewal conversation.** Dan's record, read in step 2, sets it up.

> **You:** "Here's where your record puts you. [Seventy] of [eighty-four] days logged, your posture marker at threshold since week [nine], and your second marker improving across its last two readings. That's measured momentum, on the rule we printed on day one. Your card priced what comes next before you paid: Round Two at [$1,000], a written review every second week with re-captures at its weeks 6 and 12, or the Hold at [$55] a month for a quarterly re-capture and review."
>
> **Dan** *(composite, Struggler)*: "So which one?"
>
> **You:** "My recommendation is Round Two, once. Change like yours tends to show over months, your second marker is still moving, and your logs show the evening block still leans on the review. Do you want to start it?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

If he says yes, the question about his pick comes next, word for word:

> **You:** "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

Notice the move. The case came from three facts on his sheet and a card he read before paying, so nothing in it was new to him. Both options were priced before the recommendation, and the ask was one plain question he could answer no to as easily as yes. That repairs the Hold link, the belief that staying on buys something he can check.

If he argues for stopping, reflect it, ask to challenge it, then use his own record:

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Proof binds, because the proof milestone, 10 or more graduates with consented process testimonials plus the first outcome ranges, gates your next price band. So ask each founding client for a process testimonial at his first measured peak, and for one private referral if he graduates at one. The trap is reading gratitude as a peak: asking a warm founding client in week 3, inside his fit window, with nothing on his record.

**Growing.** A start graduates every month, so Round Two and the Hold sell at cohort scale. Asks also drift back onto the calendar through templates. The default move is one outline for every graduation call, with each client's peaks checked against his sheet. The trap is an automated week-12 email asking everyone for a referral, which reaches the flat reads and the clause.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind, and Round Two clears parity only with volume and templated reviews, the Hold only near the top of its price range. Until each does, Seat Math caps it. The default is the written re-assessment with a recorded walkthrough, its asks one line each in the graduation letter. A live call is kept for a close case, full intensity, or Private. The trap is letting a steady flow of alumni referrals into the enrollment plan.

## Standard Check

- **Asks on the record's clock.** Every ask waits for a measured peak, after the fit window, the exit decision, and the written verdict; testimonial consent is scoped and text-only by default, and a refund ends testimonial asks for good (the line on vulnerability).
- **One plain recommendation, and the honest no.** Momentum graduates hear one recommendation and a plain ask, holding graduates hear "you don't need Round Two", and a no to the affordability question ends money talk with no downsell (*Close*; *Present the price*).
- **Private asks that reward nothing.** Every client at a measured peak is asked once, privately; nothing rewards a referral or a posted face, and shareables pass the six questions and open on the age fork (*Sell directly*; the line on shame).
- **Status as a record fact.** Alumni status is the dated fact of reaching week 12, and no exit, purchase, or room decides it (*Build identity on evidence*).
