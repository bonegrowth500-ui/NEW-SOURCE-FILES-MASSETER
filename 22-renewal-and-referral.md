# 22 · Renewal and Referral
*Part V — Selling and Transformation*

**The shift:** from *"Renewals and referrals happen if clients are happy"* to *"Asks are timed to measured peaks, renewal is argued from his record, and every client gets a private referral ask."*

Two men finish the Program, your 12-week flagship, in the same week under two different operators. Both logged most of their days, and both have a marker, one of the measures agreed on their first day, sitting past its threshold. The first gets a last weekly review like the eleven before it, then an email: thanks for being part of it, good luck, and a link to leave a review. He doesn't reply. Within a month he's checking the mirror again, and when a friend asks, he says the program was fine.

The second opens his week-12 re-assessment, the end-of-program capture and verdict, beside his baseline: [70] of [84] days logged, each marker scored against its threshold, and captures matched to one standard. His reviewer sets that record against the card he read before paying and recommends one next step plainly. Then comes one private question: has anyone asked you what you've been doing?

The work was the same. What differed was when each thing was asked, and what the asking stood on. This module times every ask to a read that shows progress, argues the renewal from his record, and gives every client at a peak one private referral ask.

## 1. Peaks and Measured Moments

Every ask the back end makes, for a renewal, a referral, a testimonial, or an upgrade, belongs to a moment when his record shows progress, and to no other.

### Asks on your calendar land on his worst weeks

Most operators ask on their own calendar: a review request in week 4, a pitch for another block in the last week whatever the verdict says, one referral email to every client at once. The calendar can't see where each man is. His middle weeks may hold a plateau, his week-6 read, the written mid-program read, opens a pro-rata exit right for 7 days, and his week-12 verdict may pay a refund, so a calendar ask reliably reaches some men at their lowest point. That's where this category sold them one more device when nothing had moved, and a man who remembers it reads a mistimed ask as the same pattern.

Testimonials take the worst of it. One given in week 3 is evidence of hope, and one given in the conversation that decides his refund is evidence of a trade. A skeptic reading either can't tell which kind he's looking at, so he discounts them all.

### The only honest peak here is a measured one

Memory weighs the best moment and the ending of an experience more heavily than its average week, a finding with moderate support. So the same ask gets a different answer depending on when it lands. In this niche the best moment has to be a measured one, because appearance evidence is slow and confounded: a good week feels like progress, a capture under kind light looks like progress, and neither is. A read of his markers against their thresholds is the one kind of progress he can check himself. A yes given there is informed, so it holds up even for the client whose result sits at the low end of the range.

So an ask waits for two things. The first is a scheduled read that shows progress: at least one marker at or past its threshold, with his adherence at or above the threshold printed before he paid. The second is that no refund decision is left open around it. His fit window, the full refund in his first 14–21 days, has closed, his week-6 exit decision is settled, and at week 12 the verdict is written before anything is asked. A marker still climbing toward its threshold isn't enough, because until one reaches it the non-response clause, a partial cash refund when no marker moves despite his effort, is still his to collect at week 12.

**Progress on his record, every refund decision settled, then the ask.**

That's **Measured-Peak Asks**: renewal, referral, testimonial, and upgrade asks made only at a measurement moment that shows progress on his record, never at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit. A flat read is one where no marker sits at its threshold and none is improving. A capture that looks better to him doesn't make a peak, since captures are observations and the markers decide.

### Dan's twelve weeks held two peaks

Dan *(composite, Struggler)*, 24, started in a group of ten with a comparison-photo habit and about eight months of unmeasured effort behind him. Week 3 closed his fit window, and nothing was asked. Week 5 brought a plateau, [three] flat weeks of readings, which got a re-plan and no request. His week-6 read held the plan and set out his exit right for the next 7 days, with no label, status line, or ask beside it.

In week [9], [his posture-habit marker] reached its threshold at the weekly review, with [52] of [63] days logged: his first measured peak. It carried one ask, for a process testimonial, and his no ended testimonial asks for good. At week 12 the verdict was written first, moved with momentum, and only then came the graduation call with his renewal recommendation and one private referral ask. Those were the only asks he heard in twelve weeks.

### Each ask has one home

The operating rule: each ask has a home among his reads, and moments it never touches. The renewal, into Round Two, the lighter maintenance block, or the Hold, the quarterly measurement subscription, is argued once, at graduation. An upgrade to Private, the premium seat with fixed deliverables, waits for a record that calls for one. The referral is asked once, and testimonials get two asks at most, with a no to either final.

| Ask | Its home | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Process testimonial | His first peak from about week 7 | Inside the fit window; in the week-6 exit conversation |
| Results testimonial | After the week-12 verdict is written, if a marker reached its threshold | In the verdict conversation; after any refund |
| Round Two or the Hold | Graduation, previewed at the week-10 review; a Hold member's quarterly review that shows it holding | At a plateau, a flat read, or an exit; after the clause pays |
| Referral | Graduation, once | Inside his fit or exit window; after "I can't afford it" |
| Upgrade to Private | A peak where his record calls for a full block | On a record that calls for maintenance |

Thresholds are set within reach of about 12 weeks' work for most clients who do it, so most peaks, and most asks, arrive in the last fortnight. That's why the last fortnight gets designed.

## 2. The Peak-End Finish

The last fortnight decides how he remembers the whole Program, so build it around the Program's most informative moment and let nothing smaller come after it.

### Most programs end on their weakest week

The default ending is administrative: a last review like the others, a feedback form, then a billing notice or an "access ends" email. That's what he carries out the door. The ending a man in this niche stages for himself is often worse, a new selfie beside an old one under whatever light the bathroom has. The comparison-photo habit he arrived with gets its biggest moment, and the record he spent twelve weeks building gets none.

Because endings weigh so heavily in memory, those two weeks set how the other ten are remembered, and whether a trend feels worth protecting. The take on Round Two moves with them. So does what he tells the friend who asks, since a man describes a program by how it ended.

### Build the peak from his record, read whole

The peak worth building is the moment he knows: his whole record read at once, against the baseline he started from. Each week of the fortnight has one job and keeps one thing out.

| Week | Its job | Kept out |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | The review names what week 12 decides: each marker against its threshold, his adherence count, his capture date, and, for a man at a peak, the card line each outcome leads to | A new lever, unless a plateau calls for a re-plan |
| 11 | A capture check against the standard: the tripod mark, the lamp, the hour | New lessons; any photo "preview" |
| 12 | The re-capture, the written verdict, then the graduation call: his record side by side, graduation, one recommendation, and asks only at a peak | A survey before the verdict; billing notices; any ending on a form |

**The last things he holds are his record and his next capture date.**

That's the **Peak-End Finish**: the final fortnight designed as the Program's peak, built from his record and ending on it. The peak is information he can check, which is why it holds up where a staged one fails. A reveal, a countdown to "results day", or a hype video would build feeling around a photo, feed the checking habit, and leave a client at the low end of the range feeling set up.

### A record that didn't move gets the same finish, without the asks

For a man whose markers reached no threshold, the finish runs the same way and asks for nothing. His verdict is written plainly, the clause pays within 7 days where it applies, a referral goes with it where one fits, and his record is his to keep. He paid to stop guessing, and a clear no is still an answer he can use. The Dignity Check, whether he leaves more capable or more defective, bites hardest here, and it turns on whether the verdict reads his record or his face.

## 3. The Renewal Case

At graduation his next step is argued from his own record against the timeline he accepted before paying, and one recommendation comes out of it, including, when it's true, that he doesn't need more.

### His card already priced the choice

Before he paid, he read the Path and Timeline Card, the one page that states what 12 weeks deliver, when change tends to show, and his likely cost over the first [9] months (Module 6). It priced the Program alone, the Program with the Hold, the Program with Round Two and the Hold, and the full-intensity line. So graduation brings no new offer, only a line he has already read. This category's usual renewal is a surprise next phase sold to everyone at the moment he's most hopeful, which is the endless-tips pattern with an invoice attached.

Round Two, the maintenance block for graduates with measured momentum, and the Hold, the measurement subscription for graduates, are designed as offers elsewhere (Module 10). What's built here is the argument for one of them, or for neither, and that argument is the **Renewal Case**. His record makes the case, and your job is to read it out.

### Three facts from his sheet decide it

The case rests on three facts from his marker sheet: his adherence against his threshold, each marker against its threshold, and each marker's last two readings. The first two say whether the lever moved for him. The third says whether a trend is left to protect. Measured momentum, adherence at or above his threshold with at least one marker still improving across its last two readings, is Round Two's qualifier.

| His record at week 12 | What it says | Your recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Moved, with measured momentum | A trend review can still protect, on habits not yet running alone | Round Two, once |
| Moved and holding | Nothing for more review to protect; the open question is whether it lasts | "You don't need Round Two"; the Hold, or his own quarterly date |
| Moved late, after a week-6 correction from outside your plan | The corrected lever needs full review | Full-intensity Round Two, priced as a Program, as his card showed |
| A signal your reviews missed | He lost weeks to your miss | Corrective weeks at no charge, up to 6, before any paid block |
| Not moved, or adherence below threshold | No peak | No offer |

Round Two runs once by default. A second block needs measured momentum at Round Two's own week-12 read, and his ask. Full intensity is a separate path with its own condition, and a miss that was yours earns the weeks it cost, up to 6, free and counted in the claim rate you publish. When you can't tell whose miss it was, count it as yours.

### The stake is time and guessing

The true stake is what the next three months do to his record. Habits take a median of about two months to become automatic, with a range from days to most of a year, and a momentum graduate's logs usually show which of his still lean on the review. So the choice is between a reviewer on a trend his record shows, and three months in which nobody reads the weeks he skips, until his next capture finds him guessing again. Money enters only as money from here on, the price on his card. His face, any claim about structure, and what he has spent before stay out of it.

"You don't need Round Two" belongs to the same case. When his record says moved and holding, say it plainly, with the reason from his sheet: nothing is still moving for more review to protect. Sometimes the honest answer covers the Hold too, for a man who reads his own record and will keep his quarterly date, which is what graduation was built to make possible. From 30 graduates the card publishes the share told "you don't need Round Two", and that share is what makes your yes believable to a man who has been upsold before.

### Price once, then the question about his pick

Run the decision as a short contract, the arc of Close by Contract, which moves from an agreed structure to one recommendation, a price stated once, and a clear ask (Module 19). Read his record, recommend one option with its reasons, then show the options his record qualifies for, premium first, at his card's prices. State the price once and hold the silence. When he picks, ask about his pick, word for word: "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?" Then run the Fit Check, the plain-language check for signals that buying could hurt him, and book his next date.

A no to that question ends money talk. No Hold follows a Round Two no, and no plan or date does either. He keeps his record and his quarterly date, and the pause route, a content-free tag, stops sales sends to him for 60–90 days. Nothing renews by itself: Round Two ends at its own week 12, and the Hold starts only on his yes at the review.

**When the signals disagree.** Say a graduate at a clear peak, with measured momentum and Round Two recommended, asks for [$200] off "as a returning client". His record says yes, and his request says the price is in question. Ask the affordability question before anything else, because it separates "I can't comfortably pay this" from "I'd like to pay less". If he answers no, money talk ends, with no Hold offered as the cheaper route and no trimmed-down block. If he answers yes, hold the price: it's the one on the card he read before paying, and nobody pays less for the same seat. Restate the recommendation once and ask. A discount here would tell every graduate the recommendation was a sale, and in a category known for "today only" offers it would read as one.

## 4. Measured-Peak Asks for Testimonials and Upgrades

Testimonial and upgrade asks follow the same peak rule, and each carries a condition of its own: a testimonial is asked of everyone who reaches a peak, twice at most, and an upgrade waits for a record that calls for what Private sells.

### A testimonial is evidence of what he'd say freely

The testimonial rules are set elsewhere (Module 16): consent separate from enrollment, scoped and revocable, text only under his initials by default, never ghostwritten, no incentive, and the range everyone got printed beside any result. What this module sets is the timing. No ask comes before his fit window closes, none in a conversation that decides a refund, meaning the week-6 exit or the week-12 non-response verdict, and none of a client who claimed a refund. Within that, two asks at most: the process ask at his first peak from about week 7, and the results ask after the week-12 verdict is written, only if a marker reached its threshold.

Ask every client who reaches a peak. Asking only the men you expect to glow is selection, and a skeptic in this category assumes selection the moment he sees a wall of praise. Written at a measured moment, a testimonial carries the record he's looking at, and accounts written close to an experience tend to read as more credible than memories of it. Keep the ask small and specific: two or three lines, in his words, about what the record showed him. A client who came through the signal pause, the slower path a fit-check signal sets, or who arrived checking his photos, gives words only, whatever he offers.

### An upgrade follows what his record needs

The upgrade ask moves a client into the Premium Lane, the Optimizer's path from the priority tier, the faster paid written plan, to founding Private seats while minutes are spare and to Private at parity once they bind (Module 13). Private sells speed, privacy, and attention as fixed deliverables, and it promises no better result. In this category "precision" has meant a closer read of the face, so every Private page, call, and recap carries one line: "We never read or score your face."

Two conditions make the ask honest, and both come from his file. His record calls for a full block, such as full intensity after a correction from outside your plan. And what he has asked for is what Private delivers: a rota that never fits the group call, replies inside a day, or no group at all. Then show Private first beside the Program-priced block, recommend whichever fits, and ask the affordability question about his pick. Seat Math, the monthly caps set from measured care minutes, must hold a Private line, and at Scaling the seat must pass the Parity Rule, which sells it only if it earns what a Program seat earns per care hour.

On a record that calls for maintenance, an upgrade sells attention the record doesn't need. Adrian *(composite, Optimizer)*, 31, asks at graduation whether Private would get him "further". His sheet shows momentum and nothing more, so the answer is no. Private buys speed and privacy, his record calls for maintenance, and Round Two's review is written, so the calls his travel ruled out won't cost him anything. He takes Round Two.

## 5. Ask Everyone, Privately

Every client at a peak gets one direct, specific, private referral ask, because direct asks succeed far more often than operators expect, and your plan counts on none of what they bring.

### Direct asks work more often than you'd guess

Reid *(composite operator)* never asked. He assumed men in this niche keep the work to themselves and would find the question awkward, so his referrals came only from clients who volunteered, [two] in his first year. Half of that was right. Many men here keep the work private, and public referral is capped by what sharing says about the sharer, since being seen to buy help with how your face photographs can read as insecurity. What Reid got wrong was the private yes. People expect roughly half the yeses a direct, specific request actually gets, so an operator who predicts a no rarely finds out he was wrong.

Two more findings shape the ask. Intentions to refer run well ahead of referrals made, so an ask has to name a person and a small step to turn one into the other. And people pass on what makes them look good, so the thing he forwards should make him look rigorous. Put together, they give one rule:

**One private, specific ask of every client at a peak, and none of it in the plan.**

That's **Ask Everyone, Privately**: a direct, specific, private referral ask of every client at a peak, never planned on. It repairs the Share link, the belief that recommending you makes him look careful rather than insecure.

### The ask names a person, stays private, and pays nothing

Specific means the ask names a person, a step, and what happens next. The person is "the one who's asked what you've been doing", the step is forwarding one link or one card, and what happens next is the same self-assessment he took, his friend's own answers, and nobody contacting anyone unless he books. Private means one-to-one, in his own conversation, with nothing about either man passed to the other. There's no public "share your results", no board of referrers, and no reward. A reward turns his word into a paid endorsement to a friend who still has to pass your Fit Check and the affordability question, and in a category known for paid endorsements it reads as one.

Never planned on means your Reverse Funnel, the plan worked back from profit to reach, counts none of it. Referred clients tend to stay longer and be worth more than other clients, which is why nobody at a peak is skipped, and why the plan has to work without them. Count what the asks bring as a source, and treat it as upside.

### Shareables let him pass it on without exposing himself

Identity-safe shareables are referral objects that make the sharer look rigorous and reveal nothing he didn't choose to share. They're public education a skeptic would respect, such as the six conditions that make a capture comparable or the questions that expose a faked before-and-after, and each carries one link to your self-assessment. They hold no face, no capture, and no result of his. They also travel further than you intend, and a large share of this category's most engaged audience is under 18, so the link opens on the age fork, the door's first question, and nothing on the card asks for anything.

Nothing rewards posting a face: no contests, no free month for a before-and-after, and no tags. A posted face can't be recalled once it's been screenshotted, it invites the rating culture your rooms ban, and a before-and-after on social media is the format this category has faked longest.

### The partner seat is the most specific ask of all

The Training-Partner Seat, pair enrollment for two adults who train together, is designed as an offer elsewhere: two seats in one start from its real cap, each partner through his own door, each at the published price, with no pair discount (Module 10). Its ask goes to the relationships that already know. Many men here keep the work private, and the men who share his gym, his schedule, or his flat are often the exception. His check-ins usually name them, in a blocker or a shared session.

When two of them would start, the seat puts them in one start. Each is a separate buyer who answers the age fork, the affordability question, and the Fit Check himself, in private, and neither learns the other's answers, verdict, or refund unless he shares it. A client who offers to pay for a seat hears a plain no, because money that isn't the buyer's own routes the buyer to the Starter Path, the free sequenced path for anyone who shouldn't buy now. The ask waits for graduation and stays out of the client's own fit and exit windows, where a friend's new seat would weigh on his decision to stop.

## 6. Alumni Status and Graduation

Graduation confers alumni status as a fact in his record, earned by reaching week 12, and nothing about it rests on his results, his next purchase, or a refund right.

Graduation is a deliverable: everyone who reaches week 12 graduates, keeps his whole record, and leaves with his next capture date (Module 6). Alumni status is the record fact of that graduation, written in the Program's terms before he pays: "Graduation: you reached week 12. It's a fact in your record, whatever your markers did." It sits inside Adults Who Measure, the group of adults who measure instead of guess, where membership is the practice itself (Module 17). Alumni status adds one dated line to that practice: a measured 12 weeks, completed.

So a man paid under the non-response clause graduates as fully as a man who renews, and no status tracks tier or spend. Nobody presents alumni status as something an exit costs. A client who stops early keeps his record, a closing count of what he did, and his standing as one of the adults who measure, and no room, board, or email mentions who left. The graduation label leads with a count, "[70] of [84] days logged, every capture on its date", and bridges to no offer, because the label rests on his logs and the recommendation rests on his markers. In the alumni room, the Hold's paid room once about 30 alumni are in it, standing comes from re-captures kept and logs sent.

## Scripts: The Graduation Call and Its Asks

**The graduation outline.** About [25] minutes, held after the written verdict has gone out. A record that didn't move gets steps 1–3 and step 7, with no asks.

1. **Contract.** "Your verdict's in writing already. Today we read your record together, I'll recommend one next step, and I have two short questions at the end. A no to any of it is fine."
2. **The record, whole.** Logs, the scored marker sheet, and the captures beside it as observations. "Here's what moved against the thresholds you signed on day one."
3. **Graduation.** "You reached week 12. That's a fact in your record now, whatever comes next." Then the label, count first.
4. **The Renewal Case.** One recommendation from his sheet, his options premium first at his card's prices, the price once, the affordability question about his pick, then the Fit Check.
5. **The referral ask.** One private, specific question.
6. **The testimonial ask.** Only if a marker reached its threshold, no refund was paid, and he hasn't already said no.
7. **Close on his record.** "Your next capture is [date]. You know what moved and what to do next, which is what you came for, and much of how you're read was never about your face."

Notice the order. The record comes before any recommendation, so the peak is something he can check, and the asks come after his decision, so none reads as the price of the next step. The label and the recommendation stand on different evidence, his logs for one and his markers for the other. That's the Peak-End Finish doing its job, and if a stop rule fires at step 4, the call goes straight to step 7.

**The renewal conversation.** Dan's record, read in step 2, sets it up.

> **You:** "Here's where your record puts you. [Seventy] of [eighty-four] days logged, your posture marker at threshold since week [nine], and your second marker better at each of its last two readings. That's measured momentum, on the rule we printed on day one. Your card priced this before you paid: Round Two at [$1,000], a written review every second week with re-captures at its weeks 6 and 12, or the Hold at [$55] a month for a quarterly re-capture and review. My recommendation is Round Two, once, because your second marker is still moving and your logs show your evening block still leans on the review."
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*
>
> **Dan** *(composite, Struggler)*: "And if I just stop here?"
>
> **You:** "Then you keep everything: your record, the standard, and a quarterly date. What you'd give up is someone reading the weeks you skip while a trend is still moving. Round Two or the Hold, which fits?"
>
> **Dan:** "Round Two."
>
> **You:** "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"
> *[Stop. Let him answer.]*

Notice the move. The case came from three facts on his sheet and a card he'd read before paying, so nothing in it was new. Both real options were on the table before the question, premium first, so a no to the money couldn't meet a cheaper offer. That repairs the Hold link, the belief that staying on buys something he can check.

If he argues for stopping, reflect it, ask to challenge it, then use his own record:

> **Dan:** "I reckon I can keep it going on my own."
>
> **You:** "You might. Can I show you one thing from your record first?"
>
> **Dan:** "Go on."
>
> **You:** "Your two thinnest weeks, [four] and [eight], both fell in [overtime weeks], and both times the review is what moved the block. Nothing on your sheet says that pattern's gone. So the question is whether you'd rather find out alone."

What you never say: "Stop now and your jaw will slide back." It aims the stake at his face and claims what no record supports. Return to the decision: "Round Two, the Hold, or your own date?" A yes to the question goes to the Fit Check and Round Two's first check-in date. A no ends money talk: "Then we leave money there. Your record and your quarterly date are yours." No Hold follows.

**The private referral ask.** Sam, at his graduation peak, after his no to Round Two was accepted.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Founding clients are few and proof binds, because the proof milestone, 10 or more graduates with consented process testimonials, gates your next price band. So ask every founding client for a process testimonial at his first peak from about week 7, and give each graduation a full Peak-End Finish, since founding graduates are Round Two's first cohort. The trap is reading gratitude as a peak: asking a warm founding client in week 3 because he's enthusiastic, inside his fit window, with nothing yet on his record.

**Growing.** Graduations land every week across stacked starts, so the constraint is drift, with asks sliding back onto the calendar through templates. The default move is one outline for every graduation call, each client's peaks checked against his sheet, and renewal reviews held to their own budgeted line. The trap is an automated week-12 email asking everyone for a review or a referral, which reaches the flat reads and the clause, because stop rules bind automation too.

**Scaling.** Care minutes bind, and Round Two and the Hold sit below parity, so Seat Math caps both and they sell while minutes are spare. The default move is the written re-assessment with a recorded walkthrough and one recommendation, keeping a live call for a close case, full intensity, or Private. The trap is letting a steady flow of alumni referrals into the month's enrollment plan and trimming content hours against it.

## Standard Check

- **Asks on the record's clock.** Renewal, referral, testimonial, and upgrade asks wait for a read with a marker at its threshold, after the fit window, the exit decision, and the written verdict. Testimonial consent is scoped and text-only by default, and a paid refund ends testimonial asks for good (the line on vulnerability).
- **One plain recommendation, and the honest no.** Every momentum graduate hears one recommendation at his card's prices, a holding graduate hears "you don't need Round Two", nothing renews by itself, and a no to the affordability question ends money talk with no downsell (*Close*; *Present the price*).
- **Private asks that reward nothing.** Every client at a peak is asked once, privately; nothing rewards a referral or a posted face, and shareables open on the age fork (*Sell directly*; the line on shame).
- **Status from the record.** Alumni status is the fact of reaching week 12, whatever his markers did, and no exit is presented as costing it (*Build identity on evidence*).
