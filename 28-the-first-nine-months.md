# 28 · The First Nine Months
*Part VII — The Build*

**The shift:** from *"I know what to do, but not in what order"* to *"I run a month-by-month build: one number that matters each month, a gate at its end, and my reach band checked at months 3, 6, and 9."*

Month zero, Monday morning. Your notes from 27 modules run to pages. Your calendar is empty, and your channel has no subscribers. The pull is to start filming for the audience you'll need, because content feels like the first brick. In this category it's the slowest one. Rating and comparison formats reach under-18 viewers first, a new channel's long-form pays off over months, and no adult can buy from a page that doesn't exist.

So this week you build the door and put a founding page live with its prices. You publish your straight answer to the bone question. You send one disclosed message to every adult you know. By Friday a stranger can learn what you sell, what it costs, and whether it's for him, and a first conversation can sit on your calendar. Month 3 will be decided by conversations, and this is the week they start.

The playbook has given you the moves, and this module gives them an order. It sets three phases, one number that matters each month, and a gate at each month's end, with your reach checked at months 3, 6, and 9. Each lever arrives in the month the earlier ones make possible, and no sooner.

---

## 1. How to Use the Build

The playbook gives you the levers, and the build gives you their order, which comes from what each lever needs before it can work.

Month zero offers more good moves than a 20-hour week can hold, and in this category the tempting ones arrive early. Rating content wins views fastest while it pulls the youngest viewers. A Scaling operator's tools look like shortcuts to a man with an empty calendar: paid assessments, templated review, ads. Each is a right move in the wrong month, and it spends the hours the right move needed.

Each lever waits on an input that only earlier months produce. The Program's 12 weeks and your monthly starts set when those inputs arrive. Here is the timing in Band B, the steady one of the four Reach Bands, the planning bands for how many eligible adults a month your channel reaches (Module 1):

| Lever | What it needs first | When, in Band B |
|---|---|---|
| A Price Step, a small scheduled raise | A full start and 30 held conversations | Month 4 |
| Round Two and the Hold, the back end | A graduate, 12 weeks after your first client | Month 4 |
| Paid reach | A piece that finds adults, and pages that pass the claims rules | Months 3–4, if week 12 finds under ~15 held |
| Templated review | ~20 clients reviewed, and turnaround creeping | Month 11 |
| The Call Cap, a weekly limit on free calls | Most weeks above ~6–8 held | Beyond month 9 |
| The proof band, $2.4–3.2k | 10 graduates with consented testimonials, and first ranges | Year 2 |

So the calendar sets three phases. Found, months 0–3, builds the door and wins the first clients by hand. Prove, months 3–6, shows through the first steps, reads, and graduations whether the price, the product, and your reach hold. Grow, months 6–9, spends what that proof unlocked. Each phase closes on Band Waypoints, what months 3, 6, and 9 should look like in each band.

**Found, Prove, Grow: one number a month, a gate at its end, and your band read at months 3, 6, and 9.**

That's the **Nine-Month Build**. Its months follow your calendar rather than your mood, so a slow week can't talk you into the wrong lever. Every band finishes it with a real, measured business and a clear next decision. The business is simply a different size in each.

The gates are **Monthly Gates**, the pass-or-fix condition you read from your records on each month's last day. Each is written before its month starts, with its ledger threshold in it. That way a hot week can't pass it and a thin one can't fail it.

When a gate fails, the calendar keeps its dates and next month's list changes. Starts open on schedule, the founding price ends as stated, and announced steps land, because a date that moves teaches buyers to wait. The gate's fix goes first on the list, and every move that depends on the failed condition waits for it to pass. Run all of it at the monthly Operator Review, your fixed look at the numbers that ends in one decision (Module 12).

## 2. Found: The First 30 Days

Your first 30 days build one door and start conversations by hand, because month 3 is decided by conversations in every band. None of them can come from an audience you don't have yet.

### Week 1 puts the door and the offer live before anything links to them

Door v0, the first working version of your door, goes live before any piece points at it (Module 5). It opens on the age fork, "legal adult where you live", with the education lane behind it: public content and an orthodontist referral for anyone younger, with nothing kept. The Fit Check, a plain-language check for signs that buying could hurt him, runs at both checkouts, the founding group's and the priority tier's, before any pay button. The booking page shows your price range and asks the affordability question: "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?"

Wire in the pause route the same day, a content-free tag that holds every marketing send and blocks checkout for 60–90 days, then asks permission once. The first people any link reaches include minors, men whose distress about their appearance is the real story, and men who could pay only on credit. A door built after the first link has already sold to some of them blind, so Stop Rules, the fixed points where selling ends, bind your automation from its first send.

The founding page goes live the same week, because your first messages and replies need a page with a price and one next step. It lists the founding Private seat first, a premium seat with fixed deliverables. The founding group, one standing group for every early client, comes next at a founding price, with its end and the next price beside it. The priority tier, the faster paid written plan, comes last.

Your first video is the Honest Answer, your straight public answer to the bone question, since every later piece points to it (Module 3). Beside it goes the pre-commitment that opens the Dated Record, your dated public log that no rival can back-fill. Write it now, while you have nothing to protect: what you'll publish, at what sample size, and on what schedule.

**Week 1.** Cole *(composite operator)* starts from zero in [January] and reads what he can count. He has [no] subscribers, [zero] door completions, [about 40] adults he knows by name, and about 7.5 open hours in his week. His founding page shows [one] founding Private seat at [$6,000], async-first, then the founding group at [$1,500], with [4] seats and [$1,650] from the [March] start, then the priority tier at [$450]. He opens his payment processor with a small charge that week. His channel will prove to be Band B, though nothing in his first quarter can show him that.

### Weeks 1–2 start conversations by hand

Your Early week protects about 8.5 hours of content: long-form every other week, 4–7 native shorts on Shorts and Reels, the result email, and replies (Module 11). The lines no client has claimed yet, roughly 7.5 hours for conversations, review, and Private, go to starting conversations. A new channel's long-form pays over months, while a disclosed message can book a call this week.

Three routes open at once, each shaped by a buyer who hides the pursuit. The warm-network message goes once to each adult you know, with "this is a business message" in its first line. It names the offer, who it isn't for, and the founding price, then asks only for a forward, since friends rarely buy but often know the man who would. Permission-First Replies answer in public and ask before anything moves to DMs (Module 25). An uninvited DM reads as a closer's move here, and the commenter may be 15.

Short-form built on adult situations reaches the man who never comments (Module 24). Each Reel routes through the Keyword Route, a DM keyword that returns your door link. Every adult who finishes the door then gets speed to lead: a personal reply within hours and a booking inside 24–48 hours. That's what turns 10–20% of eligible leads into held conversations.

### Weeks 2–4 end every conversation in one recommendation

Each first call is a Dual-Purpose Conversation, a disclosed fit-and-research call of about 45 minutes whose booking page names its purpose, your price, and "one recommendation" (Module 9). It asks what he did, never what he'd pay. Then it runs Close by Contract, the close that agrees its structure first (Module 19): tiers premium-first, one recommendation, the price once, the affordability question about his pick, and an ask.

Plan on 0–2 clients in month 1, since first cash can slip past it when every route runs slow. Before your first client pays, he sees the Path and Timeline Card, the whole path and its likely cost on one page (Module 6). Then baseline day opens his record, a win he can verify lands inside two weeks, and your first review arrives before he asks (Module 21). Label every held conversation by the route that booked it, so week 3 has something to read.

### Week 3 moves hours when the warm routes run thin

The week-3 source check reads two labels, warm network and replies (Module 9). If together they give fewer than 2 held conversations a week, move about 2 hours a week from long-form into short-form batches and Permission-First Replies. Both warm labels front-load, since your network has had its one message and replies fade as first pieces age, while Permission-First Replies find new threads every day. Keep the Honest Answer up, and leave price, paid reach, and new platforms alone, since none adds a conversation this month.

**Week 3.** Over [three] evenings in weeks 1–2, Cole sent the warm-network message to all [40] adults, and his first client came through a forward and took the [January] start's late entry. ## 3. Found: Months 2–3 and the Month-3 Gate

## 4. Prove: Months 3–6

From month 3 the business proves three things: that buyers take a rising price, that the product holds up in its own reads, and that your reach can carry it. The calendar sets when each proof can arrive.

### Price Steps begin once a start fills and 30 conversations exist

The Price Steps are small raises on a published schedule that always happen while starts fill and close rates hold (Module 7). Their gate is read on a start day: the last start full, and the close rate over the 30 held conversations since the last step was announced inside 15–35%. Until those 30 exist, a start that fills carries a step alone, at the bottom of the range. Each step goes out at least 30 days ahead and names what it adds, because a raise that names nothing reads as a markup to a buyer this category taught to look for one.

**Month 4.** On the [March] start day, [~20] held conversations were too few to read and neither founding start had filled, so no step went out. ### The product proves itself in reads, graduations, and a log

The program's milestones start landing on their dates. The week-6 read, the written mid-program read, asks the verdict question first. The exit right sits beside it: a pro-rata refund for a client who did the work and asks to stop (Module 7). The week-12 re-assessment sets matched captures beside his baseline and gives the verdict. Your Capture Standard freezes at version 1 once clients match baseline conditions without a retake, because every comparison you'll ever publish rests on it (Module 6).

The first graduation switches on the back end. Round Two, a maintenance block, is offered only on measured momentum: adherence at its threshold and a marker still improving across its last two readings. The Hold, a measurement subscription with a quarterly re-capture, fits the man who moved and holds (Module 10). The Canon Lane, a monthly email repeating your core claims, opens the same month.

Proof in this category is slow and confounded, since weight change, growth into the early 20s, and camera distance all move how a face reads. So Prove publishes process first. Your proof page leads with the dated log: check-in completion and turnaround kept, across every client. Consented process messages sit beneath it, each asked at a measured peak and never rewarded. Outcome ranges wait for the proof milestone, 10 graduates with consented process testimonials, and go up then as a labeled small sample (Module 16).

## 5. Grow: Months 6–9

Months 6 to 9 spend what the proof unlocked. Your one number moves from conversations toward enrollments as Growing arrives.

### Growing arrives on one signal before the other

The Stage Map places you by trailing three-month revenue and a volume signal (Module 1). Growing means about $8–30k a month, or about 50–150 eligible leads with 15–25 clients running. In Bands A–B the volume signal usually arrives first, because steps inside the opening band lift revenue slowly while older long-form keeps finding adults. When the two disagree, the binding constraint decides which moves apply.

Short-form's line moves on yield, never on revenue (Module 24). Three signs move it: eligible leads at about 50 a month for two months, long-form beating short-form on leads per hour over two windows of about 30 door completions, and older pieces bringing a growing share. When all three hold, drop short-form to about 1.25 hours. The freed hours buy long-form's third piece a month. It re-films the weakest of your Start Here Series at a higher bar and adds the Offer Pieces, the pieces that answer a warm buyer's questions on your offer page (Module 18).

### Below your band, fix reach before price

A month-6 count under your band's range usually means you're in the band below, and the fix runs in order. Sources and packaging come first: the week-3 shift again, Permission-First Replies, and the Age-Up Dial, packaging that lowers the minors' share (Module 23). A piece can gain views while it loses adults, so packaging is reach work. The reach lever comes second.

Two moves feel like fixes and widen the gap. A price cut lowers revenue per eligible lead, and a third of that is the most you can pay for a lead. So the cut shrinks the very paid reach a thin band depends on. A new platform splits an editing budget that can't yet serve the ones you run (Module 25).

Band A's lever comes from three places. The paid test is one, scaled only once revenue per eligible lead makes its cost affordable. A piece that breaks out is another, judged at the age fork by adults found, never by views. The third is referrals from Ask Everyone, Privately, one direct, private ask of every client at a measured peak, which you never plan on (Module 22).

## Checklists: The First 30 Days and the Monthly Gates

### The first 30 days, week by week

Answer each item from your own records on its week's last day. A "no" goes first on next week's list.

6. Every week: Did content hold about 8.5 hours, with 4–7 native shorts on Shorts and Reels? → If not, put the filming block back first.
7. Week 3: Did warm network and replies give 2 or more held conversations a week? → If not, move about 2 hours a week from long-form into short-form batches and Permission-First Replies.
8. Week 4: Did every held conversation carry one booking-source label and end in one recommendation and an ask? → If not, label from today and replay any call that ended without an ask.

### The Monthly Gates

Read each on the month's last day. A pass keeps next month's list as the build writes it; a fail puts the fix first and holds whatever depends on it.

4. Month 5: Did every weekly review, week-6 read, and week-12 re-assessment land inside its stated turnaround? → If not, credit or refund each miss within 7 days, and recompute Seat Math before the next start.

6. Month 7: Have eligible leads held at about 50 a month for two months, with long-form out-yielding short-form per hour over two windows and older pieces' share climbing? → If yes, move short-form to about 1.25 hours; if not, keep the Early line whatever revenue says.
7. Month 8: Did most weeks hold above about 6–8 conversations, with selling eating the content minimum? → If yes, set the Call Cap and route the overflow to the Decision Assessment; if shows slid toward 60% or over half ended no-fit at lower volume, fix screening instead.
8. Month 9: Do trailing revenue and the volume signal place your stage, and do eligible leads name your band at its month-9 range? → Swap to your stage's numbers, and write the next decision for your band.

## Stage Notes

*Stages: Early < ~$8k/month · Growing ~$8–30k · Scaling ~$30k+, on a trailing 3-month average plus a volume signal.*

**Early.** Reach binds and then conversations, so the build puts sources, the door, and the founding group ahead of anything that needs volume. The default is each month's list, with its gate read on the last day whatever the week felt like. The trap is borrowing a later month's lever because the calendar feels full: a paid overflow, templated review, or ads while every conversation still counts.

**Growing.** Conversion and selling minutes bind, and in Bands A–B you usually arrive on volume before revenue. The default is one change per window at the monthly review, with short-form's line moving only on yield. The trap is a Price Step that lifts revenue past Growing's line while reach still binds, followed by a cut to the reach engine that produced it.

**Scaling.** For most bands it lies beyond month 9, when care minutes bind. The build then hands over to your Scaling Triggers (Module 12), and to Seat Math, the Parity Rule for premium seats, and the steady state at $50k (Module 13). The trap is keeping founding habits, every call free and every reply personal, in a week that care minutes already fill.

## Standard Check

- **Selling from week 1.** The founding page and door v0 go live together, with the offer, who it isn't for, the public price, and one next step, so no month waits for an audience before it sells (*Sell directly*). The warm-network message says it's a business message in its first line.
- **Dates from the calendar, never a launch.** Every date in nine months is a monthly start, the founding price's stated end, or a step announced 30 or more days out. A thin start is published, never rescued with a discount or a moved date (*Use real dates*; the line on fake scarcity).
- **Stop Rules from door v0.** The age fork, the Fit Check, the affordability question, and the pause route go live before the first link points at the door, so no automation in any month sells past a stop rule (the line on vulnerability).
