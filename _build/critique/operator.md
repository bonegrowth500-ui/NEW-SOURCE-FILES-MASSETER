# Critique — operator

## Verdict
The foundation is rigorous on ethics, claims discipline and steady-state economics. Its default engine (screened door → paid decision assessment → 12-week group → continuity) is a credible design for months 18–24, and the hard lines are well put into practice. As an operating plan for an early operator with no audience, though, it is built backwards. It optimizes the constraint that binds at maturity (synchronous minutes), but reach and qualified conversations are what bind for the first 6–12 months. In practice, demand breaks first. On the ledger's own ratios, the month-12 waypoint needs roughly 75k–550k engaged long-form views a month. There is no scenario for an operator whose channel never breaks out, and the founding 'cash before audience' phase has no way to produce its first conversations. The operator's week breaks second. The base case already sits at ~24.5 h/week before adding work the documents assign to the operator: the Private layer, screen-flag conversations, Down-Path human touches, DMs and public replies, and freelancer claim-checks. By my estimate that adds roughly 6–19 h/week. Content, which gets cut first when client work grows, has no protected minimum. Several choices add friction exactly when leads are scarcest: a paid door from day one, strict research/sales separation, a first quarter framed as instrumentation, and six rungs plus a full trust apparatus before the first cash. Price is framed as a capacity lever while the business is limited by demand, and the back end leaves renewal revenue unclaimed. A real operator would take every disclosed fit conversation and personally reply to every adult lead. He would sell a founding offer near the real price in month 1 and run no more than two or three rungs at once. He would add the paid door, the Private layer and automation only when calls and care minutes actually bind. Nearly all of this can be fixed without loosening any hard line: stage-gate the door, price gates, rungs and north star; add scenarios for different reach levels; and write a concrete founding sprint.

## Findings

### OPE-1 [critical] LEDGER A 'Default scenario profit' and LEDGER C 'Required long-form engaged views'; THESES B14; D30
**Issue:** Every revenue figure is a mature steady state that quietly assumes top-tier reach. On the ledger's own ratios (lead→assessment 2–6%; 2–5 leads per 1,000 engaged views), the ~$15–18k 'early-year waypoint' needs ~370–1,100 leads and ~75k–550k engaged long-form views a month. That waypoint is what the business research calls the month-12 state (~22 paid assessments a month). Maturity (~28 a month) needs ~93k–700k. The ecosystem research itself notes that ~85% of views go to ~3% of channels. In my judgment, an operator whose channel never breaks out is more likely at ~5–40k engaged long-form views a month by month 9. There is no demand-constrained scenario and there are no month-3/6/9 waypoints. The 'early-year' label invites the operator to plan months 6–12 on a breakout that may never come.

**Fix:** Add a scenario table to LEDGER A banded by reach (e.g., <15k / 15–50k / 50–150k / 150k+ engaged long-form views a month). Each row should carry adult leads, conversations or assessments, enrollments, revenue, profit and operator hours. It should also show the levers that don't need reach: price, renewal, response speed and conversion. Relabel the waypoint 'month-12, conditional on ~75k+ engaged views a month'. The build plan should report month-3/6/9 outcomes for each band and say plainly that in the middle bands the $25k floor is a year-2 outcome.

### OPE-2 [critical] THESES B12; D26; D32; D20/D22 routing limits; GAPS B9 (Cash Before Audience)
**Issue:** 'Founding clients from real conversations in month 1' has no source of conversations. There is no audience yet, and podcasts and collabs are excluded. DMs may only route, cold viewers may only be sent to the free door (D20), and research calls may not pitch (D32). Nothing in the foundation generates the first 25–50 conversations. The ecosystem research's own founding math (5–10 clients ≈ 170–2,000 leads, ~100k engaged views in the mid case over months 0–4) is itself a breakout assumption. As written, B12's 'before an audience exists' is impossible; it means 'before a large audience exists'.

**Fix:** Define a Founding Sprint as a named default with these parts. (a) A standing founding offer from day 1 (price, seat count, end condition), presented on the door's result page and in decision-stage pieces as D20 allows; it is a standing offer, not a launch campaign. (b) Native short-form as the main early source of reach, routed to the door. (c) A personal reply to every adult door completion within hours. At ~15–25% lead→conversation versus ~5% automated, five clients need ~50–170 leads instead of ~250–500. (d) Permission-based replies to adults who engage publicly. (e) Disclosed fit conversations. (f) A target of 3–6 held conversations a week, with honest expectations: 0–2 clients in month 1 and 3–6 by month 3. (g) A month-3 gate: fewer than 3 clients from 25+ held conversations means fix the offer or positioning before building the group.

### OPE-3 [critical] LEDGER A 'Default scenario … ~24–25 h/wk'; D30; D34; D29; STANDARD Operational Rules; THESES B3
**Issue:** The base case reaches the profit floor at the hours ceiling (~24.5 h/week) with no slack. It also leaves out work the foundation itself assigns to the operator: (1) the Private layer, which D30 puts in the default engine but whose revenue, hours and sales calls are not modeled; (2) screen-flag conversations; (3) the Down-Path 'scheduled human touch'; (4) assessment follow-ups; (5) all DMs and public replies (D34); (6) freelancer briefs and clip claim-checks; (7) live enrollment sessions (D29); (8) refund, plan-default and escalation handling. By my estimate these add ~6–19 h/week, which puts the default scenario at ~30–43 h/week. Admin at ~2 h/week is also thin for ~28 assessments, ~14 onboardings, ~50 continuity members and freelancers.

**Fix:** Rebuild the default scenario's hours line by line with these rows, and design to ~20 h/week so ~5 h of slack absorbs variance. Convert per-person work into priced, one-to-many or templated forms (see the finding on unpriced per-person minutes). Keep Private out of the base case until care minutes are measured. Record a decision on the spec tension: SPEC §3 allows only project freelancers, but a task-billed, scripted moderation and inbox freelancer who only routes (no selling, no claims, no conversations before age is confirmed) may be needed. Add a de-scoping order that says what gets cut first when a week runs past 25 h.

### OPE-4 [major] THESES B1; §1 Five Scarcities; D31 north star; P13 Call Economics Test
**Issue:** B1 says sales minutes bind first. The business research's own Constraint Clock says attention and trust bind early, conversations bind in the Growing stage, and care binds at scale. At 20–100 leads a month the operator has spare minutes and too few adults. Applying target economics early does damage. P13's hurdle of ~$280–860 revenue per call-hour, and profit per operator hour as the single north star over D31's stage metric sets, both tell an early operator to gate or skip conversations. They also penalize the content hours that create future demand. The Five Scarcities have no row for reach; 'Sellable adults' describes the mix, not the volume.

**Fix:** Rewrite B1 as a Constraint Sequence: reach → conversations → care minutes. Add sellable-adult reach per week to the organizing model, or redefine 'Sellable adults' as volume × share. Stage the north star: Early = held qualified conversations per week and cash collected; Growing = enrollments per month and lead→enrollment; Scaling = profit per operator hour. Set P13's hurdle at the hour's current opportunity cost, not the target average.

### OPE-5 [major] D18 default door; THESES B5; LEDGER A 'Operator hours per enrollment'; LEDGER C 'Paid assessment → program 25–50%'
**Issue:** The paid decision assessment is the default door from day one; D18 keeps fit conversations only as an option. B5's arithmetic compares hours per enrollment and ignores enrollments per lead, which is the scarce input early. Its 0.75 h per assessment, including a written plan, is a steady-state figure. At a realistic 1–1.5 h early, the cost is ~2.5–6 h per enrollment, which overlaps the free-call range (2.7–8.3). A $150–250 fee from an unknown, uncredentialed operator with no proof will cut the few conversations available. The 25–50% conversion figure is a guess that assumes proof exists, and it is the model's most sensitive number: default-scenario profit moves from ~$20k to ~$28k between 25% and 40%. The 'disguised pitch' worry about free calls is a labeling problem, not a price problem. And a written plan that answers everything invites the buyer to do it himself.

**Fix:** Stage-gate the door. Early default: a free or token-fee (credited) 20–30-minute fit conversation, with its purpose, the price and 'one recommendation' disclosed on the booking page. Switch to the paid decision assessment when held calls exceed ~6–8 a week, the show rate falls below ~70%, or more than half of calls are no-fit. Re-estimate assessment time at 1–1.5 h early and 0.5–0.75 h once it is templated or async. Plan conversion before proof exists at ~15–35%. Scope the written plan to the decision, the refer-outs and the first 14 days, and name what needs ongoing review. It should not be a full do-it-yourself protocol.

### OPE-6 [major] THESES B3; D27; D18; D29; LEDGER B 'Down-Path tool'; E14/E16/P11 infrastructure
**Issue:** A 20–25-hour operator with no audience is asked to stand up six rungs: paid assessment, founding 1:1, cohort, Private, continuity and a paid Down-Path tool. On top of that come a quiz door (age fork, screen, result by email, stage tags), a verification page, a published refusal policy, a claims library, a comment policy and live enrollment sessions. Each rung needs a page, terms, onboarding, delivery assets and emails, and building them eats the hours that should be producing the first clients. A paid Down-Path tool 'from day one' also contradicts B3's own rule that self-serve products wait until 30–50 graduates have produced the stall taxonomy.

**Fix:** Add a Rung Activation Schedule to D27. Months 0–3: door v0 (a form, the age fork, five questions and a personal reply), founding 1:1, and a free Down-Path (a resource, a referral, one monthly group Q&A). Months 3–6: a founding/beta group as founding 1:1 sunsets. Months 6–9: continuity at the first graduation, and the paid assessment once call load binds. Add Private only when care minutes are measured and seats are spare. Build the paid Down-Path tool after ~20 clients, and start live enrollment sessions once the adult list reaches ~1–2k. Give each hard line its minimum viable implementation first, and build the rest of the trust apparatus as volume arrives.

### OPE-7 [major] THESES B7; LEDGER A 'Price leverage at 75–85% margin'
**Issue:** 'Price mostly throttles demand to seats — a capacity lever more than a profit lever' is true only when demand exceeds seats. For most of years 1–2 the operator is limited by demand. At fixed volume, nearly all of a price rise reaches profit, and a +30% raise breaks even on profit only if volume falls ~23%. The '+1.2–1.3%' figure measures how profit moves relative to price; it does not say price doesn't matter. Gating raises only on utilization above 85% for two cycles plus outcome milestones will keep an operator who is short of demand at the launch band indefinitely, even when high close rates show he is underpriced.

**Fix:** Rewrite B7's closing sentence: price is a capacity lever when seats bind and the main profit lever when demand binds. Add a conversion gate beside the utilization gate. For example, if assessment→program stays at ≥45% for two cycles, or price is rarely the stated objection, raise on the next announced date and grandfather alumni. Track revenue per sellable lead as the pricing metric.

### OPE-8 [major] THESES B11; LEDGER B/D (continuity as the only back end); B2's 12-week container
**Issue:** Continuity is only ~8–10% of revenue at target. The research's Phase 2 renewal was dropped from THESES and LEDGER; it was priced at 50–70% of Phase 1 and offered at the week-10 measurement to clients with momentum. The 12-week container ends before most visible change: habit automaticity has a median of ~2 months and can take most of a year. So the business earns little in months 4–9, which is when proof, referrals and renewals are born. When demand is the limit, higher revenue per enrollment cuts the reach needed in proportion. A 30–60% continuity take at $39–79 for measurement-only value is also optimistic for young men.

**Fix:** Add a Phase 2 renewal rung and model it in the default scenario: planning uptake ~20–40%, ~$0.8–1.5k, offered only on measured momentum and gated by the Informed-Client Test. Present container length as options with a default pick: (a) 12 weeks plus Phase 2; (b) a 12-week intensive with a lighter Hold phase included, at a higher price; (c) the status quo plus continuity. Choose using founding-client data, and plan continuity take at ~20–40% until measured.

### OPE-9 [major] STANDARD Operational Rules ('a screen flag triggers a conversation'); D18; THESES B6 and B3 (Down-Path 'scheduled human touch'); E16
**Issue:** Two per-person operator tasks sit unpriced at the free layer. First, D18 puts the appearance-concern screen inside the free self-assessment. That means ~12–28% of adult completions (tens to ~200 a month at maturity) each trigger a conversation. A BDD-style screen in a lead magnet also adds friction and false positives (only ~⅓–¾ of flags are true), which turns away real buyers, and it puts mental-health answers into marketing tools. Second, the Down-Path's human touch at $27–97 earns ~$100–520 per operator hour, against the ~$280–860 the target needs.

**Fix:** At the free door, keep the age fork and at most a one- or two-question distress check. Route anyone flagged there to an education track with no offers and a dignified templated referral. Run the brief screen proper at the paid-step booking, which is still 'before any paid step' as STANDARD words it; a flag there gets a human conversation. Store only the routing outcome, not raw answers, and keep it out of the email platform, CRM notes and pixels. Make the Down-Path human touch one-to-many (one monthly group session) or a single scheduled, templated async reply.

### OPE-10 [major] LEDGER A 'Care capacity ~40–65'; 'Review minutes per client-week (6–10)'; THESES B2 stage calls
**Issue:** Six to ten minutes per client-week assumes a mature check-in instrument. The first ~20 clients will realistically cost 12–20+ minutes each while the stall taxonomy and templates are being built. At the top of the range, 65 clients × 10 minutes plus three stage calls is ~14 h/week of delivery, leaving ~6–11 h for content, selling and admin. Three weekly stage calls are also poor value while there are only a handful of clients.

**Fix:** Plan 12–20 minutes per client-week for the first ~20 clients, and 6–10 only after the feedback is templated. Templated feedback and summaries the operator reviews are the minimal-AI lever. State realistic concurrent capacity inside 20–25 h as ~25–45 clients. Set seat caps from measured minutes each month. Run one combined weekly group call until there are ~12–15 concurrent clients, then split by stage.

### OPE-11 [major] LEDGER B 'Private layer'; THESES P4 (Show the work); D30; D49 (Optimizer)
**Issue:** Private adds $2.5–4k for ~1–1.5 h/month of extra one-to-one time (~3–4.5 h over 12 weeks). That prices the added time at ~$560–1,330 an hour, against a market rate of ~$230–300. P4 says to publish review minutes and where the fee goes, which invites buyers to do exactly that division and risks failing the Informed-Client Test. If Private really delivers the speed and privacy it is sold on (24-hour async turnaround and a call every two weeks, ~2–3 h/month), its yield falls to ~$280–670 an hour. That breaks the case for building it as a layer, which rested on keeping cohort-level yield per hour. Private also needs sales calls and targets a second segment (D49), yet it appears in neither the default scenario's revenue nor its hours.

**Fix:** Specify Private's deliverables as fixed points (response time, review frequency, call cadence) and price to them. Model it in LEDGER A with its delivery and sales minutes. Remove it from the base engine until month 9+, and let it grow from demand among cohort buyers before targeting the Optimizer. Amend P4 to publish capacity (seats, cadence, response times), not cost per minute or 'where the fee goes' at ~80% margins.

### OPE-12 [major] D22; D23; D25; E6
**Issue:** For an operator with no audience, short-form is the only surface that can produce reach within weeks. Making it 'native cuts' of 3–4 long-form videos a month ties early reach to long-form output. It also frames short-form's main job as information (a testing ground for hooks). That starves the founding phase of the adult leads it needs, because long-form search and suggested traffic usually take months to build.

**Fix:** For months 0–4, make batches of native short-form (~5–7 a week, framed around adult situations, profile linking to the door) the main source of reach, with one long-form video a week for trust and conversion. Keep analyzing short-form as the hook-testing ground. Shift back toward long-form as the spine once long-form reaches the second reach band in the reach-banded scenarios.

### OPE-13 [major] THESES B14; §7 Instrumentation list; E12 ('ads wait until organic ratios exist')
**Issue:** 'One clean test of the front door' in the first 90 days is statistically impossible at early volume. Telling 25% from 40% assessment→program at 80% power needs ~150 assessments per arm. Telling 2% from 6% lead→assessment needs ~375 leads per arm. Calling the first 90 days 'an instrumentation project' also sets a slow tempo, and the 14-item instrumentation list is a dashboard for a mature business. Waiting for organic ratios that won't stabilize for 6–12 months can delay any paid learning by a year.

**Fix:** Make the first 90 days a 'first five clients' project with minimal tracking: UTMs, a 'where did you hear about us' field, and five weekly numbers (adult leads, held conversations, enrollments and cash, pieces published, client check-in completion). Replace tests with decision thresholds. Let a ratio from the operator's own data replace a ledger range only after ~30 events. Offer (not as the default) a small adult-only reach budget of ~$300–1,000 a month from months 3–6 to speed up data, once the door and landing pages are compliant (E10, E16).

### OPE-14 [major] D25; E5; LEDGER A default-scenario content hours (~7.5 h/week)
**Issue:** At maturity, 7.5 h/week has to cover a lot: 3–4 long-form videos (≈4–6 operator hours each, even with an editor), native short-form, a weekly email, Instagram routing, pinned replies to skeptics and clip claim-checks. Realistically that is 9–12 h. More important, the steady-state model has no feedback loop. As client load grows, content gets cut first, and leads fall a month or two later — the classic feast-and-famine cycle in coaching.

**Fix:** Make content hours a protected minimum (a batch-filming block every two weeks), not whatever is left after delivery. Set the scaling-stage cadence at 2–3 long-form videos a month. Set seat caps so client work cannot eat into the content minimum, and add a line to the Operator Review that flags any week below it.

### OPE-15 [major] Step 2 module allocation (research/business SYNTHESIS §2 candidate map) vs SPEC §3 and GAPS B9
**Issue:** SPEC §3 says pricing and unit economics, systems, freelancers and time design are 'in scope but NOT in-depth'. Yet the candidate map gives whole modules to business math, pricing, payments/guarantees/disputes, the operating model, metrics and the premium layer. Meanwhile the cash-before-audience engine (GAPS B9, a P1 gap) and the demand equation get no module. Depth sits where the spec asked for less and is missing where early revenue is decided.

**Fix:** Merge payments, plans, guarantees and disputes into the pricing module, and metrics into the operating-model module. Fold Private into offer architecture. Use the freed slots for 'The Founding Phase' (the sprint, the founding offer, R&D capture, the move into the first group) and 'The Demand Equation' (reach → conversation → enrollment by stage and reach band).

### OPE-16 [major] LEDGER B 'Founding 1:1 (R&D phase) — honest founding price'; missing beta-group row
**Issue:** The only offer live in months 0–4 has no price, seat cap or duration. The beta group that the business research places in months 3–5 appears in neither THESES nor LEDGER. A founding 1:1 priced well below the future group also tests willingness to pay at the wrong price. And it leaves a public price history in which one-to-one was cheaper than the group.

**Fix:** Give ranges. Founding 1:1 should sit at or near the cohort launch band (~$1.0–1.5k for 8–12 weeks), with 4–6 concurrent seats. Disclose it as founding, give extra access rather than a steep discount, and state the next price. Add a beta-group row (~$1.2–1.5k, 4–8 seats) with its trigger: at least 4 founding clients active or completed.

### OPE-17 [major] D32 (research and sales conversations kept separate)
**Issue:** Strict separation roughly doubles the conversation minutes per founding client. It delays the first cash in exactly the phase where conversations are scarcest. The conflicts file (C22) already offered a disclosed dual-purpose option, which passes the Informed-Client Test.

**Fix:** Make the founding-phase default a disclosed fit-and-research conversation. State the purpose, the founding price and 'I'll make one recommendation or point you elsewhere' on the booking page and in the opening line, and ask permission before presenting the offer. Keep pure research calls labeled and free of any pitch.

### OPE-18 [major] SPEC §2 track order and §8 'read in order' (a fix inside the Intro)
**Issue:** The reach engine (Ecosystem) comes last, and the build plan follows ~27 modules (~190k words). An early operator needs to act on reach, the door and the founding offer in week 1. Reading in order delays the actions that produce the first cash by weeks.

**Fix:** Put a one-page 'Early-Stage Fast Path' in the Intro; this is allowed because it is neither a diagnostic index nor an integration map. List the exact sections to act on in weeks 1–4: door v0, the founding offer, content cadence, the conversation talk track and the minimum version of each hard line. Have the Early stage notes in business modules point to the specific ecosystem sections.

### OPE-19 [minor] THESES P17; D37 (proof order); H4
**Issue:** The rule that every story ships with its range, context and denominator is right for appearance outcomes. As written, though, it also blocks consented testimonials about experience and process, such as responsiveness, clarity or 'I logged 80 of 84 days'. These make no appearance claim and are available from founding clients by weeks 2–4. Apart from process content, they are the early operator's only social proof.

**Fix:** Explicitly allow consented experience and process testimonials from the founding phase, with incentives disclosed and no appearance outcome implied. Keep the range + context + denominator rule for outcome and appearance claims.

### OPE-20 [minor] LEDGER C 'Lead → paid assessment 2–6%'; LEDGER A margin basis and waypoint label
**Issue:** The 2–6% row doesn't say whether its denominator is raw or sellable leads, and the demand row doesn't show the gross-up to sellable leads that the business research asked for. If 2–6% is per sellable lead, the raw leads needed rise 1.4–3.3×, to roughly 133k–2.3M engaged views a month. Leaving it unclear breaks H4's own denominator rule. LEDGER A also mixes margin bases: 'cash needed' uses 65–85%, while 'revenue per delivery hour ~$700–2,600' implies 75–85% (the 65–85% basis gives ~$2,900).

**Fix:** Name the denominator (raw leads, 60 days) and show the gross-up explicitly (age-eligible × passes the screen × able to pay). Use one margin basis across LEDGER A, and relabel 'early-year waypoint' as 'month-12 waypoint'.

### OPE-21 [minor] THESES B3 (rolling monthly starts); D36; LEDGER A stage-call format
**Issue:** No minimum viable cohort is defined. Early monthly starts will often have 1–4 enrollments, so peer effects and cohort social proof are thin. A start date with a single entrant is also awkward to honor as a real deadline.

**Fix:** Run a standing group with monthly entry dates until there are ~12–15 concurrent clients. Don't sell peer dynamics until a stage has ~6 people. State a merge rule: start dates still hold, and a small intake joins the standing group.

### OPE-22 [minor] THESES B9 and B10; §6 verification debt (processor tolerance); build-plan cash timing
**Issue:** Cash-flow timing isn't modeled. A new processor account taking $1.5–6k coaching charges can trigger reviews, holds or rolling reserves. Installments spread cash over 2–3 months, and the 14–21-day fit window puts the first cash at risk. The foundation covers billing descriptors and dispute ratios, but not reserves or a cash curve for months 0–9.

**Fix:** Open the processor early and build history with small charges (founding deposits, assessments). Ask about reserve policy before the first group, and hold ~2–3 months of costs in reserve. Add a month-by-month cash curve for each reach band to the build plan. Keep other income until trailing three-month profit covers personal costs.

### OPE-23 [minor] D8 Stage Notes; LEDGER (no stage definitions)
**Issue:** Every module carries Early / Growing / Scaling notes, but the foundation never defines the stages, so notes written across 28 modules will drift. The business research proposed revenue bands: under $8k, $8–30k and $30–75k a month.

**Fix:** Define the stages in LEDGER by trailing monthly revenue plus one volume signal (adult leads per week or concurrent clients). Name each stage's binding constraint and north-star metric, as in the constraint-sequence finding.


## Missing
- A set of scenarios banded by reach, including a base case where the channel never breaks out, with month-3/6/9 waypoints (leads, conversations, enrollments, cash, hours) and an honest statement of when the $25k floor is likely in each band.
- A founding-phase acquisition design: where the first 25–50 conversations come from, weekly conversation targets, founding-offer terms (price, seats, duration, end condition) and a month-3 gate that checks whether the offer works.
- A lead-handling rule for low volume (a personal reply to every adult door completion within hours) and the volume at which automation takes over.
- How founding 1:1 clients move into the first group, including a beta-group rung, its price and its trigger.
- A Phase 2 renewal rung and container-length options (12 weeks plus renewal, or a longer container with an included Hold phase).
- Stage definitions (Early / Growing / Scaling) by trailing revenue plus one volume signal, each naming its binding constraint and north-star metric.
- A month-by-month cash curve for months 0–9 covering installment timing, fit-window refunds and processor holds or reserves, plus a rule for keeping other income until trailing profit covers personal costs.
- Decision thresholds to replace A/B tests at low volume: a minimum event count before the operator's own data replaces a ledger range, and triggers for switching the door, raising price and activating each rung.
- A de-scoping order for weeks that run past 25 hours, and a protected content minimum, so overload cuts the right work first.
- A live-calendar design for a part-time operator: assessments, group calls, Private calls and enrollment sessions batched into two or three fixed windows that work across US/UK/EU time zones.
- An explicit decision on whether a task-billed, scripted moderation/inbox freelancer is acceptable, given SPEC §3 allows only project freelancers and D34's operator-only list does not fit the hours.
- A check that the niche still has demand after its main authorities collapsed (search trends for adult decision queries, growth of comparable channels) before committing to the default engine's reach assumptions.
- Seasonality in enrollment planning (e.g., New Year and pre-summer peaks) for cohort dates and announced price-raise dates.
- The minimal template/AI lever for written plans and check-in summaries (reviewed by the operator), which is the realistic route to 6–10 review minutes per client-week.
- A no-show, reschedule and capped follow-up protocol, with its time cost built into the hours ledger.


## Proposed theses
- The binding constraint moves in a fixed order — reach, then conversations, then care minutes — so the door, the price gates, the call policy and the north star are chosen for the constraint that binds this month, not the one that binds at maturity (replaces B1).
- Reach is the first scarcity: before minutes, trust or evidence bind, too few sellable adults know the brand exists, so the organizing model names sellable-adult reach per week as its own scarcity (amends the Five Scarcities).
- While leads are scarce, revenue per sellable lead matters more than revenue per operator hour; price, renewal and response speed are the early profit levers, and friction is added only when calls exceed what the week can hold (amends B5, B7 and P13).
- The door starts as a disclosed fit conversation and becomes a paid decision assessment once held calls exceed roughly six to eight a week; charging for the conversation is a capacity tool, not a starting position (amends B5 and D18).
- Plan on the audience you will probably have: every scenario states the engaged views it needs, and the build plan includes a case where the channel never breaks out that still reaches first profit through price, renewal, conversion and personal lead handling rather than through reach (new, beside B14).
- At low volume, a personal reply to every adult lead within hours is the highest-yield hour in the business and cuts the reach needed for the first clients several-fold; automate only when replies take more than about an hour a day (new).
- Every task that costs operator minutes per person is priced, batched into one-to-many, or templated; an unpriced per-person minute is a leak that grows with the audience (new; governs screen flags, the Down-Path touch, DMs and follow-ups).
- Price is a capacity lever when seats bind and the main profit lever when demand binds; a raise that loses a smaller share of buyers than its percentage flows almost entirely to profit, so raises are triggered by conversion signals as well as utilization (corrects B7).
- The back end must reach the months when outcomes appear: a renewal or a longer container that runs into months four to nine earns from the period when visible progress, proof and referrals are born, which a 12-week flagship backed only by low-priced continuity leaves unclaimed (amends B11).
- Content hours are a protected minimum, not whatever is left after delivery; seat caps are set so client work can never eat into that minimum, because every content hour cut today removes leads a month or two from now (amends B15 and E5).
- At low volume you decide by thresholds and qualitative signal, not by tests; a ratio remains a ledger planning range until roughly thirty of your own events have produced it (replaces B14's 'clean test').
- Founding clients pay close to the flagship's future price and get more access rather than a discount, so the offer is proven at the price you will actually charge and the public price history never shows one-to-one cheaper than the group (sharpens B12).
- The age fork belongs at the free door and the appearance-concern screen at the paid gate; a flag first receives a dignified templated referral, and gets a human conversation when the person asks for one or tries to buy (amends B6 and the screen-before-charge rule).
