# Critique — evidence

## Verdict
The foundation is unusually self-aware. It has evidence tags, a verification-debt list and hard lines that actually bind, and most of its operating prescriptions point the right way. I web-checked the riskiest dated platform claims and they held up: YouTube's 24 Aug 2026 first-frame view count (https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/08/17/youtube-changes-how-it-counts-views-handing-marketers-two-numbers-instead-of-one/) and Instagram's 30 Apr 2026 originality update (https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/). The evidence layer is still weaker than its tags suggest, in three structural ways. (1) The proof engine has no validity model. The brand's core reframe (P9) has no evidence tag on its substantive claim. Outcome ranges are to be licensed by uncontrolled client data, which is confounded by regression to the mean, late growth in young men, weight change and camera geometry. The monitoring design also invites frequent face-checking, which is a known harm pathway for the BDD-enriched buyer this niche attracts. (2) Evidence is carried over too confidently from depression psychotherapy, free MOOCs and apps, e-commerce and B2B sales. In several places the synthesis dropped contrary evidence its own dossiers held (paying MOOC learners complete at ~46%; defaults have d≈0.7). Elsewhere it reads the carried-over evidence backwards: firms named after their owner become an argument for a separate brand, guided-iCBT supporters become 'sell judgment', and loss aversion becomes loss framing. (3) The derived economics lean toward the chosen defaults. The paid-assessment arithmetic leaves out the written-plan time and the drop in volume that any fee on the first step causes. The capacity model only closes at the minimum review time: inside 25 h the care ceiling is ~29–48 clients, not 40–65. Price is mislabelled a 'capacity lever'. A 10–15% plan premium implies roughly 80–190% APR, paid mostly by weak-credit buyers. None of this needs a rewrite. It needs about ten theses re-tagged as hypotheses, about fifteen ledger rows corrected, decoys and loss framing struck from the Standard, four new house rules (measure behaviour often and appearance rarely; outcome ranges shown beside a comparison group; the scored screen at the paid step; small numbers lie) and three one-line legal flags.

## Findings

### EVI-1 [critical] P9, B11, P17, D37, D39; Five Scarcities 'Evidence' row
**Issue:** The proof engine has no validity model. P9 is the brand's core reframe, but it tags only its belief-change mechanics (S). Its substantive claim ('genetics set a range; habits... decide where you land in it') has no tag. For adults, habit-driven facial change beyond body composition, posture and presentation is not established. B11, P17 and D39 then let an uncontrolled client dataset license outcome ranges. Before/after data here is confounded by: regression to the mean (buyers enrol at peak dissatisfaction); late growth in many 18–22-year-old men; weight change; grooming; capture drift (camera distance alone changes apparent nasal width by ~30% between 12 in and 5 ft); and demand effects when clients self-report to their own coach. Ranges built this way will be read as 'typical results' the program caused. That is an FTC-typicality exposure and a hard-line-1 (misleading numbers) exposure.

**Fix:** Tag P9's substantive half 'unestablished, scope-limited'. Restrict 'real' to process, adherence, body composition, posture, presentation and 'clarity within N weeks'. Ban 'bone growth' as the brand's self-description; use it only as a search keyword. Add a house rule for appearance ranges: publish them as 'observed among clients', beside a comparison group (waitlisted applicants measured over the same interval; rolling starts make this nearly free). Attach confounder fields (weight change, age under ~23, capture deviations), collect self-reports on neutral forms, and use no causal verbs.

### EVI-2 [critical] P6; B11 (quarterly photo sets); E3 ('this is you at month 8'); D37/D38 capture standard
**Issue:** P6's evidence (progress monitoring d≈0.40) is about monitoring goal-directed behaviour. In this niche the tempting progress metric is the face. Frequent appearance checking (mirror checks, repeated selfies) keeps BDD going in cognitive-behavioural models, and it raised appearance anxiety in selfie experiments. That matters in a buyer pool the theses themselves call BDD-enriched. It also destroys signal: lighting, water retention, posture and lens distance vary more day to day than realistic monthly change. The business synthesis listed 'checking faces often instead of logging behaviour' as failure mode 16; THESES dropped it.

**Fix:** Add a thesis: 'Measure behaviour often, appearance rarely'. Log and review behaviour weekly. Capture standardized appearance only at fixed milestones: baseline, ~week 6, week 12, then quarterly in continuity. Ask clients not to take interim comparison photos. Add checking frequency to the screen and to the stop signals. Recognition content shows situations, not faces.

### EVI-3 [critical] STANDARD operational rule 'Screen before charge'; B6; LEDGER C 'Screen-positive share' row; D18/E16 door
**Issue:** The screen sits in the free self-assessment (D18/E16), and every flag 'triggers a conversation'. Ledger maturity volumes are ~470–1,400 leads a month, perhaps 40–80% of them adults, with 12–28% flagged. That is ~25–300 flags a month: roughly 4–100 unbudgeted hours of mental-health-adjacent conversation for an uncredentialed coach, which also strains the 'never diagnose' line. Validity is unknown. BDDQ accuracy comes from clinician-interview validation in dermatology and cosmetic-surgery clinics. Self-administered as a gate to something the respondent wants, it will miss more cases. With PPV of ~1/3–3/4, many flagged adults are false positives who get declined. In a looksmaxxing-adjacent audience, flags may exceed 28%.

**Fix:** Use two tiers. At the free door: one or two unscored distress/fixation items that route automatically to non-labelling resources (never 'you screened positive'). At the paid step (~30–60 applicants a month): the scored brief screen, with a short scripted human decline-and-refer whose minutes are budgeted in LEDGER A. Retag the 12–28% row 'unknown in this setting; measure'. Store screen answers minimally, with separate consent.

### EVI-4 [major] Master thesis; P5; B4
**Issue:** Evidence misapplied. The 'guide's qualification barely moderates outcomes' finding comes from guided internet CBT, comparing clinician and technician guides. There, non-experts deliver supportive accountability on top of expert-built, manualized content; they do not supply expert judgment. So the evidence supports selling proactive accountability and presence. It does not support selling 'judgment he can trust about his own situation', which is exactly where missing credentials hurt and where 'judgment' drifts toward the diagnosing hard line. B4's 'commodity-proof' also ignores that AI apps now produce generic 'why you stalled / what to change first' analysis cheaply.

**Fix:** Restate the master thesis around structured, proactive accountability plus scoped decisions. Turn 'judgment' into published decision rules a buyer can audit (refer-out triggers, stall checklist, fit criteria), plus visible reasoning. Soften B4 to 'commodity-resistant while tied to a human who reviews you every week'.

### EVI-5 [major] LEDGER A (Care capacity; Default scenario profit; Revenue needed per delivery hour; Per-delivery-hour yield); B1; B15; D25
**Issue:** The capacity model doesn't close inside 25 h/week. 'Care capacity ~40–65' implies ~6.5 h/week of review plus ~3 h of stage calls. Add the default scenario's other monthly hours (assessments 21, content 32.5, admin 8.7, onboarding 7, continuity 5) and the total is ~115 h/month, about 26.7 h/week. Inside 25 h the ceiling is ~29–48 concurrent clients (about 10–16 enrollments a month). The ~$25–30k base case works only at the ledger's minimum of 6 review minutes per client-week. It budgets no hours for written assessment plans, Down-Path human touches, screen conversations, instrumentation or the Private layer, and the planning-fallacy literature says estimates like these run short. The cohort's own yield ($575–1,450 per delivery hour) sits mostly below the required $700–2,600. No modelled scenario reaches $50k; the high case is about $45k.

**Fix:** Re-run the default at mid inputs (8 review minutes per client-week; ~1.25 h per assessment including the written plan) with a 15–20% time buffer. Restate care capacity as '~29–48 inside 25 h; 40–65 only at ≤6 min or >25 h'. Add a ledger line saying the $50k end needs one of: post-proof pricing above the current band, a scaled Private layer, or the self-serve layer.

### EVI-6 [major] B5; P13; LEDGER A 'Operator hours per enrollment' and 'Free-call sales ceiling'; D18
**Issue:** The arithmetic favours the chosen door. (a) It prices the paid assessment as a bare 0.75 h slot, but the deliverable includes pre-review and a written plan. At ~1.5 h and 25–50% conversion, that is 3–6 h per enrollment, overlapping the free call's 2.7–8.3 h. (b) It ignores volume. Moving the first step from $0 to $150–250 crosses the zero-price boundary, where take-up falls sharply: in field experiments in other domains, small fees cut take-up by roughly 60–80%. Whether price screens for commitment is contested (Ashraf et al. found screening; Cohen & Dupas did not). (c) Hours per enrollment, conditional on booking, is the wrong metric. The right comparison is profit per operator hour per 1,000 screened leads. (d) A free fit call that is honestly labelled, with the price stated up front, is not a 'disguised pitch'.

**Fix:** Retag B5 'D; hypothesis to test'. Change the ledger row to ~2.5–6 h per enrollment including prep and write-up (E). Keep D18 as a provisional default. Run the paid-vs-labelled-free comparison the business synthesis already recommended, with decision thresholds set in advance.

### EVI-7 [major] LEDGER B 'Payment plans' row (premium ≤10–15%, RULE (M evidence)); B9
**Issue:** Evidence: B1 tags the premium size WEAK (course-platform blogs), but the ledger upgrades it to M. Ethics: a 10–15% premium on 3–4 instalments inside ~90 days implies roughly 80–190% APR. It is charged mostly to the weak-credit buyers the ledger says produce the plans' +20% sales lift. A buyer shown that APR would not endorse it (Informed-Client Test; the spirit of hard line 5). Law, as a one-line flag: under Reg Z the gap between cash price and plan price is a finance charge (time-price differential). A seller who extends such credit more than 25 times a year becomes a TILA creditor with APR-disclosure duties, and state retail-instalment rules may also apply.

**Fix:** Cap the premium at 0–5%, set by cost (processing plus expected leakage) and stated as a total. Alternatively, route plans through a regulated BNPL provider. Retag the row 'RULE (W evidence)'. Add the TILA line to the legal flags.

### EVI-8 [major] E16 door; B6 screen; B11/P17 capture protocol and client library; H4; SPEC 'legal minimal'
**Issue:** Three biggest-risk legal flags are missing; THESES cover only ad-pixel policy (E10/E16). (1) The door collects health-adjacent and psychological data (snoring, jaw pain, distress items). That is 'consumer health data' under Washington's My Health My Data Act (separate consent; private right of action), and special-category data under GDPR for UK/EU buyers. (2) Standardized face photos are fine, but using software to extract facial geometry (landmarks, angles) is a biometric scan under Illinois BIPA, with statutory damages per person. (3) Testimonial-led content (notch 5) falls under the FTC's 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, which carries civil penalties.

**Fix:** Add one H-rule line for each. Data: collect the minimum, get separate consent for health and screen answers, keep a deletion schedule. Biometrics: no software face-geometry measurement without BIPA-grade written consent (better: none at all). Testimonials: handle under the 2024 FTC rule (no incentives conditioned on sentiment, no suppression of negatives). Flags only; no legal module.

### EVI-9 [major] P1; LEDGER E 'Self-efficacy change' row; Five Scarcities 'Efficacy' row
**Issue:** P1 treats efficacy as one quantity. Fear-appeal research (Witte & Allen's meta-analysis; EPPM) separates self-efficacy (I can do the steps) from response efficacy (the steps work). The d≈0.47 figure is for induced self-efficacy change on health behaviours. 'Largest tested belief lever' holds only among the three constructs that meta-analysis compared. The stalled buyer's real doubt is usually response efficacy ('does any of this work for adults?'). That is the one lever the hard lines and the thin adult evidence cap. As written, P1 invites raising response efficacy through the back door.

**Fix:** Split P1. Raise self-efficacy hard: small verifiable steps and stories of similar people (Bandura's mastery and vicarious-experience sources). Raise response efficacy only as far as calibrated evidence allows, and make the honest promise 'clarity': you will know within N weeks, with measurements you can trust. Edit the ledger note to 'largest of attitude, norm and self-efficacy change in health-behaviour experiments'.

### EVI-10 [major] STANDARD notches 3, 7, 8; LEDGER E 'Loss aversion' row; P16
**Issue:** Three loosened notches rest on weak or negative evidence. Notch 3 ('loss-aversion framing'): loss aversion in risky choice (λ) does not make loss-framed messages more persuasive. Meta-analyses find negligible gain/loss differences: r≈.03 across 93 prevention studies and r≈−.04 across 53 detection studies. Notch 7 ('public commitments' and identity labels): announcing identity-relevant intentions publicly reduced follow-through (Gollwitzer et al. 2009). Identity-noun framing ('be a voter' rather than 'vote') failed large field replications (Gerber et al. 2016). Public disclosure also contradicts P18's stigma premise. Notch 8 ('decoys'): the attraction effect is fragile, reliable in ~11 of 91 attempts. An option built not to be chosen fails the Informed-Client Test, and the persuasion synthesis already said 'no decoys'. P16 ('mostly filter') overreaches: deposit contracts showed higher efficacy among acceptors (Halpern et al. 2015, instrumental-variable estimates), and commitment savings had real intention-to-treat effects (Giné, Karlan & Zinman 2010).

**Fix:** Notch 3: 'true, specific stakes; frame chosen for accuracy, not loss'; add a ledger note that λ does not license loss framing. Notch 7: 'commitments reported to the reviewer, not the public; identity labels as flavour, not a lever'. Notch 8: strike decoys ('real tiers, one recommendation'). P16: 'low uptake, modest real effects among takers, strong filtering; publish denominators'. Sources: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1460-2466.2009.01417.x ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10810730701615198

### EVI-11 [major] LEDGER E 'Nudges/defaults' row; LEDGER D 'Unguided completion' row; P6; B3; P5
**Issue:** The synthesis dropped contrary evidence its own dossiers held. Defaults: Maier et al. 2022 found evidence against information and assistance nudges, but called the evidence 'undecided' for structural nudges such as defaults. The dedicated default meta-analysis (Jachimowicz et al. 2019, 58 studies, d≈0.68, cited in P2) and auto-enrollment field data show defaults work. 'Don't build continuity on defaults' is right, but for the opposite reason: defaults work, so revenue from non-users comes back as disputes and regulatory exposure. MOOCs: 3.13% is completion among all (free) participants. The same paper reports ~46% among paying verified learners; P2 had it as '~46%†', and the synthesis and ledger omitted it. The 3.9% app figure is for free mental-health apps.

**Fix:** Ledger E: 'information nudges: no reliable effect after bias correction; defaults: robust (d≈0.7); never used for renewal because they work'. Drop defaults from P6's adherence list. Ledger D: 'free unguided ≈3–4%; paid, self-selected ≈ half; guidance still helps'. Re-ground B3's 30–50-graduate gate on proof and stall-taxonomy readiness, not on 'nobody finishes'. Sources: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2200300119 ; https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aav7958

### EVI-12 [major] B7; LEDGER A 'Price leverage at 75–85% margin' row
**Issue:** Logic error. '+1% price → ~+1.2–1.3% profit' is just 1/margin: it measures proportional leverage, not importance. Once seats are full, price and minutes per client are the only levers that raise profit per operator hour, because volume can't rise. A price rise that keeps seats full goes to profit dollar for dollar. Calling price 'a capacity lever more than a profit lever' will push the playbook to underprice. Separately, gating raises on 'utilization stays high' collides with the dual-entitlement finding (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1986): increases justified by demand read as unfair, while increases justified by cost or added value read as fair.

**Fix:** Replace B7's last sentence with: 'Once capacity binds, price and minutes per client are the main levers on profit per hour.' Keep the proof gate and grandfathering. Announce each raise by what was added (proof, review speed, outcome data), never by demand.

### EVI-13 [major] B2; LEDGER D 'Format effects', 'Contact frequency', 'Proactive human encouragement' rows
**Issue:** 'S in domain' covers one domain, depression psychotherapy, via the synthesizer's own between-trial re-analysis (confounded, not peer-reviewed), which THESES relabel 'behavior-change trials'. Closer analogues exist and partly disagree. Smoking-cessation group vs individual counselling: similar at matched intensity, and both beat self-help. Proactive vs reactive quitline counselling: proactive calls raise quit rates, which supports 'proactive beats available' better than the re-analysis does. Group vs individual weight loss, and health-coaching trials, also apply. In lifestyle programs, total contact and extended contact do predict outcomes and maintenance: more contact brings more weight loss, and extended care keeps ~3 kg more off. That contradicts 'total sessions ≈ no effect'.

**Fix:** Re-anchor B2 on lifestyle-behaviour evidence. Scope 'total sessions ≈ no effect' to depression psychotherapy, or drop it. Retag the self-guided re-analysis row W–M. Add maintenance evidence to B11, so continuity is justified by keeping outcomes, not only by the dataset.

### EVI-14 [major] B14; THESES §7 instrumentation list; E12 holdout; D31 guardrails
**Issue:** The instrumentation-first plan assumes ratios can be learned in ~90 days. At founding volumes (the ecosystem mid-case is ~500 leads over months 0–4), a two-arm door test of 250 leads per arm yields ~4 enrollments per arm. The 95% Poisson intervals run roughly 1–10, so even a 2× difference is invisible. Refund and dispute rates 'by sales promise' rest on single-digit counts. A holdout on a $1–3k/month ad budget is just as underpowered.

**Fix:** Add a house rule, 'Small numbers lie'. Set decision thresholds before any test. Pool over longer windows. Update the ledger's planning ranges gradually (Bayesian-style) instead of replacing them with one month's ratio. Change course only on large differences. Treat call transcripts, refund reasons and stall notes as the main early evidence.

### EVI-15 [major] B12; D26; E12; B14; D22
**Issue:** 'Cash before audience, via real conversations' has no named source that complies with the Standard. The ecosystem synthesis's own math puts 5–10 founding clients at ~170–2,000 leads, or ~17k–2M engaged views. Views concentrate heavily (≈85% go to ≈3% of channels, on dated data). DMs route and never close (D22). Cold outreach is out. Ads wait until organic ratios exist (E12/B14), which at early volumes takes months. As sequenced, the plan blocks the spec's 'faster path to profit'.

**Fix:** Name the founding path: warm network, disclosed replies in existing public threads, and a founding-offer page behind the door. Once destination compliance passes, allow a small adult-targeted paid test to the door as instrumentation, not scale. Say plainly that first cash may slip if none of these works.

### EVI-16 [major] P4; 'Seat Math' framework seed; LEDGER E 'Cost transparency' row
**Issue:** The cost-transparency evidence concerns disclosing the production costs of goods; P4 applies it to disclosing a service's labour minutes. Publishing '6–10 review minutes a week' lets a buyer work out roughly $750–1,800 per hour of individual review on a $1.5–2.2k cohort. In a grift-fatigued market that may read as markup, not transparency. The persuasion synthesis listed exactly this as open question 4, yet P4 states its three wins as settled (M).

**Fix:** Retag P4 'W–M; hypothesis'. Lead with the system and the visible effort: turnaround, every log personally reviewed, what gets checked, total touchpoints including stage calls. Operational-transparency and labour-illusion evidence supports showing effort, not quantifying scarce minutes. A/B test seat-math copy before it becomes standard on the offer page.

### EVI-17 [major] B8; LEDGER B 'Coaching market rate (hourly)' row
**Issue:** The ~$230–300/h figure comes from ICF coach surveys, which are dominated by executive and business coaching, often paid by employers. For a 20–32-year-old buying appearance coaching, the relevant comparison (online fitness or body-composition coaching, personal training) is unverified (business synthesis open question 6). It is probably far below the cohort's ~$500–730 a month. Using the ICF rate as an 'honest anchor' fails B8's own test and the Informed-Client Test.

**Fix:** Drop 'market coaching rates' from B8's anchor list unless replaced by verified consumer-coaching price bands. Relabel the ledger row 'ICF (mostly executive) coaches; not a buyer-facing anchor'. Anchor on the buyer's own sunk spend and the delivery math.

### EVI-18 [major] P3; LEDGER E 'Range vs point claims' row
**Issue:** P3 is tagged M on one in-press multi-study paper about products, and that paper's baseline result runs the other way. Buyers prefer point estimates unless four conditions hold: they are already auditing how claims are made, formats sit side by side, ranges are tight, and the attribute is believed to vary. The field evidence is a single sponsored-ad CTR (1.49% vs 1.13%). The robust nearby evidence (van der Bles et al. 2020, PNAS) shows numeric ranges cost little trust relative to points, while verbal hedges cost more. That supports 'ranges over hedges', not 'ranges beat points'.

**Fix:** Retag P3 W–M and restate it: 'numeric ranges cost little credibility and beat vague hedges; they beat point promises only when buyers compare formats'. Update the ledger row to match. Keep ranges for variable outcomes on ethical grounds regardless.

### EVI-19 [major] E3; E15 ('S X weights'); E2 ('master profit variable')
**Issue:** E3 claims shame bait 'loses every downstream signal', with no evidence. Rating, comparison and 'mogging' content has spread widely in this category, and YouTube's teen limits only curb repeat recommendations to logged-in teens. The published X weights are production defaults; the E1 dossier itself says experiments shift them and learned features can override them. So 'S' overstates what they reveal about live distribution. E2 calls age mix 'the master profit variable' on weak (W) minor-share inputs and evidence borrowed from a study of political content.

**Fix:** Reframe E3: 'Dignity keeps the audience adult and sellable and the channel in good standing; it is not proven to win raw reach.' Judge it on sellable-adult yield, not views. Retag the X weights M and teach only the principle. Change E2 to 'a major, testable lever'.

### EVI-20 [major] E7 vs P12; D19
**Issue:** Internal inconsistency. P12 makes email the belief-maintenance channel over months ('a subscription, not an event'). E7 says the list's value is flow, makes revenue within ~60 days the primary KPI, and sunsets non-clickers after 60–120 days. The 60-day window is an unverified assumption (ecosystem open question 11). It rests on e-commerce welcome-flow data and a 2011 B2B lead-response audit, and is applied to a $1.5–2.2k considered purchase that young buyers may save toward for months.

**Fix:** Make E7 a hypothesis. Report revenue by lead-age cohort (0–60, 61–180, 181–365 days) before adopting any sunset rule. Re-permission instead of deleting. Keep a low-frequency track that repeats the core claims for silent readers.

### EVI-21 [minor] E13; E6; E5; E12
**Issue:** Platform mechanics are overstated. E13: the rule texts (TikTok For You eligibility; Instagram's 30 Apr 2026 originality update) target content you didn't create and third-party watermarks. A clean re-upload of your own footage isn't ruled ineligible, and 'it underperforms' is a practitioner claim (W). Yet E13 sets the size of the Platform Count Rule. E6: short-form results are noisy, heavy-tailed and drawn from a younger, colder audience, so a 'winning framing' weakly predicts adult long-form performance. E5's 'only surface that compounds' ignores TikTok search and Google's indexing of Instagram posts. E12: paid targeting uses declared age, not verified age.

**Fix:** E13: 'no watermarks, no one else's content; clean cross-posts of your own footage are allowed; budget light adaptation and test whether full re-cuts pay'. E6: a framing must win repeatedly, then be confirmed with long-form Test & Compare. E5: 'compounds most reliably'. E12: 'reduces minor exposure'. Sources: https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/ ; https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards

### EVI-22 [minor] B12; B13; LEDGER B 'Just-below price endings' and 'Price–quality signal'; LEDGER D 'Habit automaticity'
**Issue:** Evidence tags are inflated or misapplied. B12's S (hybrid entrepreneurs exit less) is about keeping a day job, not about founding clients as R&D. B13's S (firms named after their owner earn more because the owner's reputation is at stake) doesn't support separating brand assets from the person; if anything it favours visibly tying the operator's name to the brand. The S-rated 'no effect' for just-below price endings conflicts with robust field evidence of left-digit bias (car mileage thresholds; retail pricing). Habit automaticity rests on one small study (median ~66 days) plus a small review. That is M, and it is not an appearance timeline, though the business synthesis used it as one. 'Weaker for services' is doubtful: when quality is hard to judge before purchase, buyers usually lean on price more.

**Fix:** B12: D. B13: D, noting that the owner-name evidence favours a 'Name | Brand' link. Price endings: C ('left-digit effects exist; round prices chosen for brand fit'). Habit automaticity: M, never used for outcome timing. Verify or drop 'weaker for services'.

### EVI-23 [minor] LEDGER C 'Landing page conversion'; LEDGER F 'Clicks when an AI summary appears'; LEDGER B 'Dispute-rate target'; LEDGER G 'Buyer income'
**Issue:** Four ledger rows are mislabelled or misleading. The ~6–7% landing-page median covers all traffic (B1 says so), not 'cold' traffic. The AI-summary row merges Pew's all-query result (8% vs 15% of visits) with a vendor's branded/non-branded split. '<0.5% of transactions' is meaningless at 30–60 transactions a month, where one dispute is 1.7–3.3%. '≈4–6% of gross annual pay' understates affordability, for four reasons: ~$2.5k is roughly a month's take-home pay for a full-time 20–24-year-old; many 18–24s aren't full-time; about a third of US adults couldn't cover a $400 emergency in cash; and the income figures are US-only while the audience is international.

**Fix:** Relabel the landing-page figure 'all-traffic median'. Split the AI row into its two sources. State the dispute target as a rolling count (e.g., at most one per quarter, and <0.5% over the trailing 12 months). Express affordability in months of take-home pay. Add geography to the sellable-adult definition and the instrumentation list.

### EVI-24 [minor] P8 lexicon; H6/STANDARD vocabulary; B1 vs D31; E2 vs E10/H6
**Issue:** Small inconsistencies that will leak into copy. P8's control lexicon includes 'protocol'. That word is not on the H6/STANDARD vocabulary list, carries a treatment connotation, and is the spec's own word for the excluded method content ('techniques/protocols'). B1's master metric is 'revenue (and profit) per operator hour', while D31's north star is profit per operator hour. E2 proposes 'breathing habits at work' as an adult topic, but breathing and sleep signs are refer-out triggers (H6) and a health-classification risk for the pages content links to (E10).

**Fix:** Swap 'protocol' for 'system', 'standard' or 'block'. Align B1 to D31 (profit per operator hour). Keep breathing content habit-only, with the refer-out on the page, and never on pages ads link to.

### EVI-25 [minor] P11; B6 ('declines... costly, therefore credible')
**Issue:** Several listed 'costly signals' are cheap talk. Publishing a refusal policy or stating limits costs a grifter as little as it costs an honest operator, so it doesn't tell them apart. A signal is costly only when it means visible forgone revenue or builds a record that can be checked. Research on two-sided messages adds a wrinkle: outside advertising, acknowledging a limitation without answering it tends to lower persuasion, while acknowledging it and then answering it raises persuasion. Credibility rises in both cases.

**Fix:** Split P11 into hygiene signals (policy pages, stated limits) and costly ones: decline counts published over time, guarantees visibly honoured, ranges that include poor outcomes, and a long consistent record. Pair every stated limit with why it exists and what to do instead.


## Missing
- Message-framing meta-analyses (O'Keefe & Jensen 2007, 2009): gain- and loss-framed messages differ negligibly (r≈.03 prevention; r≈−.04 detection). This corrects the rationale behind notch 3's 'loss-aversion framing' and the ledger's λ row.
- Two-sided messages (O'Keefe 1999; Eisend 2006): answering the opposing point outperforms one-sided messages, which outperform merely acknowledging it; two-sidedness raises credibility either way. This is the mechanism under P2's 'raise the objection yourself' and under P11.
- Inoculation/prebunking as a positive tool (Banas & Rains 2010 meta-analysis; Roozenbeek et al. 2022, a field test run in YouTube ads). Teaching viewers the techniques behind fake before/afters (lens distance, lighting, posture, water weight) builds resistance and signals competence. THESES treat prebunking only as a constraint.
- Communicating uncertainty (van der Bles et al. 2020, PNAS): numeric ranges cost little trust compared with points; verbal hedges cost more. This is the robust base for P3.
- Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy (mastery, vicarious experience, persuasion, affect) and EPPM's split between response efficacy and self-efficacy (Witte & Allen 2000). Together they organize P1, P7 and P17: the 14-day milestone as mastery, similar-other stories with ranges as vicarious experience.
- Default effects (Jachimowicz et al. 2019, 58 studies, d≈0.68): the correct reason continuity must never auto-renew by default.
- Zero-price effect and fee-driven drops in take-up (Shampanier, Mazar & Ariely 2007; Kremer & Miguel 2007; Cohen & Dupas 2010), set against price screening (Ashraf, Berry & Shapiro 2010). This is the missing volume term in B5's arithmetic.
- Price fairness / dual entitlement (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1986): raises justified by cost or added value are accepted, raises justified by demand are resented. This matters for B7's utilization-gated raises.
- Relapse prevention and maintenance evidence: extended-care meta-analysis (~3 kg better weight maintenance); lapse vs relapse and the abstinence-violation effect. This is the outcome case for the Hold phase and continuity, plus a lapse/plateau script for client transformation.
- Closer comparisons for delivery format than depression psychotherapy: Cochrane group vs individual smoking-cessation counselling; proactive vs reactive quitline counselling; group vs individual weight-loss programs; health-coaching and workplace-coaching meta-analyses (e.g., Theeboom et al. 2014).
- Threats to validity in uncontrolled before/after data: regression to the mean, maturation (facial growth into the early 20s in many men), reactivity and demand effects, and capture geometry (selfie distance changes apparent nasal width by ~30%, Ward et al. 2018). Also the remedy: a waitlist/stepped-wedge comparison drawn from rolling cohort starts.
- Body checking as a maintaining factor in BDD and appearance anxiety (cognitive-behavioural models; selfie-taking experiments), and exposure to idealized or transformation imagery raising men's body dissatisfaction (media meta-analyses). This is the evidence base for 'measure behaviour often, appearance rarely' and for the Dignity Check.
- Credence-goods economics (Darby & Karni 1973; Dulleck & Kerschbamer 2006). It formally models the Trust scarcity: when buyers can't verify quality even after purchase, only verification, liability (guarantees) and reputation discipline sellers. It sharpens the master thesis.
- Expectation assimilation (Szymanski & Henard 2001: expectations correlate positively with satisfaction; disconfirmation is the stronger driver) and outcome expectancies fuelling effort. Calibrate expectations accurately, not low (B10).
- Peak-end effects, including a randomized field trial (Redelmeier, Katz & Kahneman 2003). Design the week-12 graduation review as the program's best moment, because it shapes recall, continuity take-up and testimonials.
- Value of referred customers (Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte 2011): referred customers are more loyal and more valuable. This supports investing in private, identity-safe referral mechanics despite P18's cap on referral volume.
- Choice overload (Chernev et al. 2015 meta-analysis): the mechanism for 'real tiers, one recommendation'. It is in the persuasion synthesis but absent from THESES.
- Planning fallacy (Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994): time budgets need an explicit buffer. This bears directly on the 24–25 h base case.
- Announcing identity goals publicly can reduce follow-through (Gollwitzer et al. 2009), and identity-noun framing ('be a voter' rather than 'vote') failed large field replications (Gerber et al. 2016). Both calibrate notch 7.
- Completion among paying learners vs everyone (Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente 2019): 46% of paying verified learners completed, against 3.13% of all participants.
- Inference from small samples: at solo volumes, random variation dominates monthly ratios. Decision thresholds and pooling over longer windows are prerequisites for the §7 instrumentation list.


## Proposed theses
- Master thesis (revised): Sell structured, proactive accountability plus scoped decisions made by published rules. Deliver it in scarce minutes, and verify it with measurement that separates what the program did from what time, weight and camera distance did. Aim force at the situation, never at the person.
- P9 (revised): Bounded agency is the brand's core reframe, but its 'real' half is limited to what adult evidence supports: habits, adherence, body composition, posture, presentation, and the certainty of finding out within a set window. It never covers adult skeletal change. 'Bone growth' is a search term the brand explains, not a promise it makes.
- New: Measure behaviour often, appearance rarely. Log and review behaviour every week, and capture appearance only at fixed milestones under one standard. Frequent face-checking maintains appearance anxiety in the buyers most at risk, and it adds noise that hides real change.
- New: An outcome range describes; it doesn't prove. Every published appearance range sits beside a comparison group (waitlisted applicants measured over the same interval), carries its confounders (weight change, age under ~23, capture deviations), and never uses causal verbs.
- P1 (revised): Split efficacy in two. Raise self-efficacy hard with small, verifiable steps and stories of similar people. Raise belief that the method works only as far as calibrated evidence allows, promising clarity ('you will know within N weeks') rather than change.
- P3 (revised): Numeric ranges cost little credibility and beat vague hedges. They beat point promises only when buyers are comparing formats, so show the contrast, keep ranges tight, and state fixed process facts as points.
- P4 (revised, hypothesis): Showing the system (cadence, turnaround, what gets reviewed and the effort behind it) probably raises perceived value. Publishing raw review minutes may invite a per-minute price calculation, so test seat-math copy before making it standard.
- B5 (revised): Whether a paid assessment beats an honestly labelled free fit call is an empirical question. The fee pushes the first step off $0, which cuts take-up, and the written plan adds hours. Compare both on profit per operator hour per 1,000 screened leads, and keep the paid door as the default only if it wins or screening requires it.
- B7 (revised): Once seats are full, price and minutes per client are the main levers on profit per operator hour. Raise prices on announced dates when proof and delivered value have grown, and justify each raise by what was added, never by demand.
- B9 (revised): Payment plans help mainly the buyers least able to absorb risk. Price them at cost or route them through a regulated lender, keep every instalment inside delivery, and state the total cost plainly.
- B2 (revised): In lifestyle behaviour change, frequent proactive contact beats on-demand access, and well-run groups match individual delivery at equal intensity. Extended low-intensity contact protects maintenance. So the cohort is the flagship because of its structure, and continuity earns its place by keeping outcomes as well as by building the dataset.
- B10 (revised): Calibration means accuracy, not modesty. Expectations colour perceived results and fuel effort, so set expectations the program reliably meets and state them with confidence.
- E3 (revised): Dignity keeps the audience adult and sellable and the channel in good standing. It is not proven to win raw reach in this category, so judge it by sellable-adult yield, not views.
- E7 (revised): Flow probably dominates, but a considered purchase can take months to ripen. Measure revenue by lead-age cohort before adopting any sunset rule, and re-permission rather than delete.
- E6 (revised): Short-form is a noisy signal from a younger audience. A framing that wins there is a hypothesis to confirm with long-form title and thumbnail tests, not a result.
- New house rule: Small numbers lie. At solo volumes most monthly ratios are noise, so set decision thresholds before testing, pool across longer windows, and change the business only on differences large enough to survive the uncertainty.
- Screen placement (revised): The free door carries only unscored distress items, with automatic, non-labelling routing. The scored screen sits at the paid step, where each flag gets a brief scripted human decline-and-refer that is budgeted in the time model.
- STANDARD notches (revised): Stakes must be true and specific, with the frame chosen for accuracy rather than loss. Commitments are reported to the reviewer, not announced publicly. Tiers are real, one is recommended, and there are no decoys.
- P16 (revised): Commitment devices have low uptake, real but modest effects among those who take them, and a strong filtering effect, so publish every range with its denominator.
