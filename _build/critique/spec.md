# Critique — spec

## Verdict
The foundation is rigorous, well cross-referenced and mostly on-spec on the big exclusions. Faceless strategy, operator mindset and podcasts stay out at the thesis level, and a free community does too, apart from a few edge leaks. The business-led direction and the hybrid engine hold together, and the Standard Card itself is correctly written as looser than the strict trust-first standard. A strict spec audit still finds three critical failures that would spread into all 28 modules. (1) The evergreen-urgency machinery adds up to a rolling launch with no bright-line test between an offer attribute and a launch campaign: recurring live enrollment sessions, enrollment windows on monthly cohorts, announced price dates, per-subscriber deadlines, a 'launch band' and a 'beta cohort'. (2) The brand's core reframe (P9) implies adult structural change, and the foundation never defines what 'bone-growth coaching' honestly offers a 20–32-year-old. Modules will either cross hard line 2 or quietly re-niche into generic presentation coaching. (3) THESES/DECISIONS re-tighten notches 2, 3, 4, 5 and 9 and add constraints the strict standard never had: a no-sale screen, a ban on outside numbers, a ban on analyzing any real face, and DMs that never close. The net posture is about half to one notch looser, not one to two. Every audit question tests only for crossing hard lines, so this drift will pass every module audit. There are also secondary problems. The Standard Card and E1 are written relative to the prior material, so the mandatory Standard Check will reference the sources. Paid community and standalone 1:1 are shut off instead of offered as options. Legal coverage is broad but misses minors' data at the door. Operations and ecosystem get more weight than 'not in-depth' and 'least focus' allow. The default engine reaches only the bottom of the profit band, using the full hour budget. And 'cash before audience' has no lead source that fits the exclusions. Before any drafting, fix the three criticals, publish a native House Standard with a Notch Map, and add a Launch Line.

## Findings

### SPE-1 [critical] THESES P13, B7; DECISIONS D29, D36; STANDARD notch 4; LEDGER B 'Cohort (flagship), launch band' and C 'Live enrollment session attendance / attendee → buyer'; REGISTRY 'Real-vs-Fake Urgency Test'
**Issue:** Launch campaigns come back in without a label. The foundation includes every part of a rolling/evergreen launch: monthly rolling cohorts with 'enrollment windows' (STANDARD notch 4), 'genuinely live recurring enrollment sessions' as the one-to-many close (P13, D29), and a LEDGER row built on B2B webinar data. It adds announced price-rise dates (B7, D36) and per-subscriber deadlines 'if truly enforced' (D36). That is the core machinery of evergreen launch funnels, and it contradicts P14's rule that deadlines belong after the diagnosis. It also counts an 'expiring bonus' as legitimate urgency (REGISTRY), uses a flagship 'launch band' (B7, LEDGER B), and plans a 'beta cohort' in months 3–5 (business research T16). Nothing draws the line GAPS B6 asked for between an offer attribute and a launch campaign; D36 only asserts 'without launch machinery'. Recurring free live group sessions with the list are also the closest thing in the plan to a free community gathering.

**Fix:** Add a 'Launch Line' test to STANDARD and THESES. A promotion is an excluded launch if: the offer can't be bought outside a window; a window gets its own lead-in content, bonuses, prices or extra email; or the cart 'closes' with no next date. Offer attributes: the next start date is always purchasable; seats come from the published capacity math; the price schedule is public; weekly content and email cadence never change as a date approaches (cap: one announcement plus one reminder per start date, engaged segments only). Take live enrollment sessions out of the default path in D29/P13. If a live one-to-many path is wanted, make it a paid, credited group decision session, not a free webinar. D36: no per-subscriber deadline except the assessment credit window. Strike 'expiring bonus' from REGISTRY. Rename 'launch band' to 'opening band'. State that the first cohort is filled from founding clients, assessment buyers and the waitlist through the standing door, not a beta launch.

### SPE-2 [critical] THESES P9 (core reframe); DECISIONS D42; THESES H1; SPEC §6 niche
**Issue:** P9 is the brand's core reframe, repeated in every module and in public content. It says 'genetics set a range; habits, consistency, measurement, and honest assessment decide where you land in it'. To an adult buyer, 'where you land' in a facial range is a structural-change claim (hard line 2), even though D42 says never claim adult bone remodeling. D42 is itself imprecise: bone remodels throughout life; the forbidden claim is that habits produce visible adult skeletal change. More fundamentally, nothing in the foundation says what 'facial-development & bone-growth coaching' honestly offers a 20–32-year-old. Minors, the only group where growth is in play, get no paid offers, and H1 bans even 'younger bodies respond better' framing. Modules will either cross hard line 2 to stay niche-specific or quietly re-niche into generic presentation coaching, which is off-spec under §6.

**Fix:** Rewrite P9 to name what can change. Structure is set by genetics and growth; for adults, habits and measurement move presentation and function (posture, breathing habits, body composition, how he is photographed) and how fast he learns what's worth pursuing. Replace D42's last clause with: 'never claim or imply that habits produce visible adult skeletal change; present that question at its evidence tier'. Add a 'Bone Claim Map' thesis by age × evidence tier: growth modification during growth → orthodontist referral; adult skeletal change from habits → unproven/debated; adult presentation and function → measurable. Every module can then be bone-growth-specific without crossing the line. Limit H1's 'younger bodies' ban to selling and marketing, so the education lane can state the true age dependence with a referral.

### SPE-3 [critical] THESES P1, P2, P4, P14, H2; DECISIONS D35, D36, D37, D40; STANDARD 'Quick audit questions' and 'Operational rules (added in 1.4)'
**Issue:** The posture has drifted back toward strict. STANDARD loosens nine notches, but the thesis and decision layer takes several back:
- Notch 3 permits loss-aversion framing and future-pacing without a count; P1/H2/D35 allow 'one true stake'.
- Notch 2 permits firmly surfacing the objection and challenging the buyer's stories; H2/D35 allow surfacing it 'once', and P2 says to stop persuading 'when defensiveness rises'.
- Hard line 3 bans only fake timers; P14/D36 put deadlines only 'after the diagnosis' and ban countdowns even to true dates.
- Notch 5 permits 'testimonial-led content'; D37 puts 'process proof first everywhere' and keeps outcome stories out of organic short-form, a restriction platforms impose only on ads. This recreates the prior proof-order inversion.
- D40's 'stakes about appearance need softening' is the prior softener rule, against notch 9.
- P4 disparages value stacks, against notch 8.
Add the new constraints the strict standard never had (screen, numbers, faces, DMs) and the net is about ½–1 notch looser, not 1–2. Every audit question tests only for crossing a hard line, so a module that under-uses the notches passes: the drift is invisible to the audit. The 1.4 operational rules tightened the card without a logged decision.

**Fix:** Append a Notch Map to STANDARD. For each notch, state the governing rule as a default plus a licensed stronger variant:
- Stakes: few, true and specific, always outweighed in minutes by the step.
- Objections: up to two probes, then stop on a clear no.
- Defensiveness: challenge the story once with his own evidence (logs, months, spend), then reflect; stop only if sustain talk persists.
- Countdowns: true countdowns to real dates are allowed on decision-stage assets.
- Testimonials: contextualized clips are allowed in organic short-form with range and denominator on screen.
- Appearance stakes: stated plainly as conditional situations, never blurred into vagueness.
Add an audit question: 'Where would a permitted notch have served the buyer, and does at least one worked script use it at full strength?' Log the 1.4 operational rules in DECISIONS as deliberate tightenings, with reasons.

### SPE-4 [major] STANDARD (preamble, 'NEWLY PERMITTED', notch 1, claim ladder); THESES E1; DECISIONS D45; STYLE module template item 7 (Standard Check)
**Issue:** These files will force references to the sources. STANDARD is written relative to prior material: 'The prior standard was trust-first… loosens it one to two notches', 'NEWLY PERMITTED', '(there is no free-community buffer anymore)', outcome claims 'usable more freely than before'. D45 says 'the older tests are absorbed'. E1 is written as a removal story ('A free community did five jobs… Removing it removes none of the jobs'). STYLE makes a Standard Check mandatory in all 28 modules, but no playbook-facing, source-free version of the card exists. All 28 checks will inherit comparative language ('newly', 'looser', 'anymore', 'no longer'). That breaks SPEC §1 ('never mentions… the program you read before') and leaves a standalone reader wondering what was removed.

**Fix:** Write a native 'House Standard' under its own name, registered in REGISTRY. It states permissions, hard lines and operational rules in absolute terms, with no notches or comparisons, and STYLE's Standard Check references only it. Rewrite E1 natively ('Nurture is five jobs; each needs an owning asset') with no free-community framing. Add a banned-phrase list to STYLE's hard rules: 'anymore', 'no longer', 'newly', 'than before', 'looser', 'unlike before', 'this time'.

### SPE-5 [major] STANDARD op rule 'Screen before charge'; DECISIONS D50; THESES B6, H1; LEDGER C 'Screen-positive share of adult applicants'
**Issue:** The screen over-reaches hard line 5 and moves toward clinical practice. Hard line 5 bans exploiting acute distress. The operational rule turns a brief appearance-concern screen (a validated body-dysmorphia screener, per the business research) into 'never a label or a sale' on any flag. The LEDGER's own figures say it flags ~12–28% of adult applicants, with positive predictive value only ~⅓–¾. So a large share of the refused adults are false positives: an unmodeled hit to the profit target, and a stance the strict standard never took. An uncredentialed coach administering a psychiatric screener also conflicts with the non-clinical vocabulary rule (D14, H6) and creates health-data exposure that 'legal minimal' doesn't cover.

**Fix:** Replace the scored screen with a check based on signals from the Standard's own stop list: acute distress, fixation or 'everything is ruined' language, surgery-seeking, compulsive checking, functional impairment. A signal pauses the sale for a human conversation, which ends in one of three outcomes: enroll with adjusted expectations, the Down-Path, or referral. Only acute signals end the sale. Split the LEDGER row into 'signal rate' and 'decline rate' (PL, to be replaced by own data). Never name a clinical instrument in intake copy; collect the minimum data needed.

### SPE-6 [major] DECISIONS D28, D18, D30; THESES E1, B2, B3, B11, B12; LEDGER B 'Continuity (alumni measurement + review)'
**Issue:** In-scope offer types are shut off instead of offered as 'options + default pick' (SPEC §3). Paid community is explicitly in scope, but it survives as a single clause: 'A paid space exists only for clients and alumni' (E1/D28). D28 lists belonging, but B11 and the LEDGER define continuity as measurement plus review only. No thesis, option set, price/seat/moderation row, or persuasion mechanism (belonging, peer proof, group identity) supports it. Standalone 1:1 is likewise reduced to a sunsetting R&D phase (B12) plus a Private layer that requires cohort membership (D30). That contradicts B2's claim that premium is sold on privacy: a privacy-seeking Optimizer must join a group.

**Fix:** Present paid community as options plus a default.
- Default: the continuity layer, explicitly named and designed as the paid alumni community (measurement, review, belonging).
- Option: an in-cohort peer space.
- Option at Growing/Scaling: an open paid membership for age-verified, screen-passing adults, once moderation minutes are budgeted.
Add LEDGER rows for community price, members per moderation hour and churn by design, plus a persuasion thesis on belonging. For 1:1, add a capped 'Private-only' option at a price that yields the same revenue per hour, or drop 'privacy' from B2's premium rationale.

### SPE-7 [major] THESES E16, E15; DECISIONS D18, D20, D22; REGISTRY 'DM Conversation Framework'
**Issue:** The foundation contradicts notch 1 ('selling paid offers straight from content'). E16 says 'every call to action routes to one owned self-assessment', yet D18 lets warm buyers 'enroll directly via a recorded walkthrough or page', and D20 lets decision-stage and offer videos 'carry offers with public prices'. D22/E15 say DMs 'route, never close' for everyone. But REGISTRY's DM Conversation Framework adds 'DM-to-checkout', and the Standard forbids DM selling only with minors. As written, E16 plus D22 bring back the strict 'send everyone to the next free step' posture.

**Fix:** Amend E16: 'Every discovery CTA routes to the door; decision-stage and offer assets may link straight to an offer page that has its own age attestation and one signal question before checkout.' Amend D22/E15: 'DMs never close an unverified contact; once the door has verified age and checked signals, a DM may carry one recommendation and a cohort checkout link; Private requires a call; stop rules apply.' Align the REGISTRY entry.

### SPE-8 [major] THESES B7; DECISIONS D36; STANDARD hard line 3
**Issue:** B7 builds in a hard-line-3 trap: 'raise on announced dates only when utilization stays high and outcome milestones are met.' Suppose a date is announced before the gate is met, and the gate then fails. The operator must either raise anyway, breaking B7, or not raise, which makes it a deadline that didn't hold: fake urgency under hard line 3.

**Fix:** Gate the announcement, not the raise: 'Once utilization and milestones have held for two cycles, announce a date at least 30 days out; an announced raise always happens.' Mirror this in D36 and in the pricing module's checklist.

### SPE-9 [major] THESES B4, B5; DECISIONS D14, D18; STANDARD 'Vocabulary'
**Issue:** The default paid front door sits on hard line 2 with no operating rule. B5 moves the assessment async-first ('recorded review + written plan'), and the research design has it assess 'habits, history and standardized photos'. A recorded review of a stranger's face photos is the category's face-analysis product under non-clinical labels, and nothing says what the operator may say about the photos. Separately, at 25–50% assessment → program conversion, 50–75% of payers never enroll. Nothing requires the written plan to be usable without buying, which is both the Informed-Client Test for those buyers and the line between a paid assessment and a paid pitch.

**Fix:** Add a 'Baseline, Not Reading' rule. Photos are captured and filed under the capture standard for later matched comparison. Recorded reviews comment only on history, habits, logging, capture quality and fit, never on structure or features. Add to B4: 'The written plan must be worth its fee to a buyer who never enrolls, and the recommendation may be not to buy.'

### SPE-10 [major] LEDGER A 'Default scenario profit', 'Operator hours', C 'Required long-form engaged views at maturity'; THESES B1, B15; SPEC §3 profit target and §1 build plan
**Issue:** The target is $25–50k/month profit in 20–25 h/week. The default engine reaches ~$25–30k only at ~24–25 h/week, the top of the hour band. Its high case is ~$45k and never reaches $50k. It also depends on ~90–280k engaged long-form views a month, which the ecosystem research itself calls a top-tier outcome. The upper half of the band has no modeled path. No row shows care, sales, content, email/DM and admin fitting inside 20–25 h. The only waypoint ('early-year ~$15–18k') is ambiguous and not tied to month 9, where the build plan ends.

**Fix:** Add an 'Upper-Band Levers' thesis and LEDGER rows. For each lever (proof-gated cohort price, async-first assessment, capped Private seats, continuity programming, post-graduate digital system, adult reach buy), give its profit delta and hour cost. Add a default weekly hour-allocation row that sums to 20–25 h at Early, Growing and Scaling, a scenario at 20 h/week, and an explicit month-9 profit and hours waypoint for the build plan.

### SPE-11 [major] THESES B12; DECISIONS D26, D32, D22; REGISTRY 'Cash Before Audience'; ecosystem SYNTHESIS §2 founding-phase demand math
**Issue:** The 'faster path to profit' engine has no lead source that fits the exclusions. B12/D26 promise founding clients 'from real conversations in month 1… before an audience exists', and REGISTRY says 30–50 conversations. But there is no free community and no podcasts or collabs, DMs route only (D22), every CTA goes to the door (E16), and no decision covers warm outbound. Nothing says where those conversations come from. The project's own demand math puts the founding phase at ~170–2,000 leads (~17k–2M engaged views), which is an audience.

**Fix:** Add a 'Founding Pipeline' decision with four sources: the operator's disclosed warm network; repliers and commenters on the first long-form and short-form pieces; a standing founding-offer page linked from every early asset; and, in the founding phase only, permission-first replies to inbound DMs from age-attested adults. Label research and sales conversations (D32). Reword B12/D26 to 'before a large audience exists'. Set month 1–4 targets as PL ranges of conversations booked and founding clients enrolled. Record a warm-outbound rule: permission-first, capped, never minors, no cold outreach.

### SPE-12 [major] THESES P8, B4, B13, E2; STANDARD 'Vocabulary' and H6; REGISTRY 'Four Hidden Variables of a Stall'; SPEC §7
**Issue:** There are paths for technique teaching to leak in. The product being sold is 'why this person stalled, what to change first' (B4). The stall model names 'technique' as a variable (REGISTRY). E2's recommended topics include 'breathing habits at work'. P8's client lexicon includes 'protocol', the excluded word in SPEC §7 ('techniques/protocols'); it is missing from the Standard's and H6's approved vocabulary, and B13 has the brand 'own… the protocol'. Worked examples, scripts and swipe copy (D7) will need concrete 'what to change first' content, and there is no rule for writing it without teaching technique.

**Fix:** Add a 'Technique Firewall' rule to STYLE and STANDARD. In examples and scripts, method content appears only as bracketed placeholders ('[first adjustment]') or at category level ('a consistency problem, not a technique problem'). Topics appear as titles and angles, never as instructions. Remove 'protocol' from P8's lexicon (use 'plan' or 'block') and reserve the word for the measurement/capture standard. Rename B13's 'the protocol' to 'the capture standard'.

### SPE-13 [major] THESES §0 master thesis, §1 Five Scarcities, E2, E4, E5, E7, E8, E13
**Issue:** The backbone is generic, against SPEC §6 ('never deliberately generic'). The master thesis ('Sell calibrated judgment, delivered in scarce minutes…') and four of the Five Scarcities (minutes, trust, evidence, efficacy) would fit any solo coaching business. E4, E5, E7, E8 and E13 contain no niche mechanism. Every module is meant to locate itself in the Five Scarcities, so the intro and module openers will default to generic framing with niche examples bolted on. Two naming defects come with it. 'Sellable adults' / 'Sellable-Adult Yield' fails the Standard's own Dignity Check and Hostile-Screenshot Test if the operator's language leaks. And E2 calls age mix 'the master profit variable', while B1/D31 make profit per operator hour the master metric.

**Fix:** Rewrite the master thesis around the niche's defining facts: faked before/afters, a discredited vocabulary, slow and bounded outcomes, a teen-heavy audience. Give each Scarcity, and each of E4/E5/E7/E8/E13, one sentence on why it bites harder in this niche (e.g., E8: a grift-fatigued audience reports pressure as spam). Rename to 'Eligible Adults' / 'Eligible-Adult Yield', and retitle E2 'the master ecosystem variable'.

### SPE-14 [major] DECISIONS D49; THESES E2, B7; SPEC §3 buyer groups ('not a 180')
**Issue:** The buyer choice is not a 180, but it over-reaches in a tilt. The sources' core is young men 17–32, with a 19–28 platform focus and a 24-year-old example protagonist. D49 makes the primary buyer the 'employed Struggler, 20–32', routes 'Students 18–21' to the Down-Path by category, and leaves employed 18–19-year-olds unassigned. E2's Age-Up Dial, the 'at 27' and work-photo packaging, and an Optimizer 25–35 premium lane all pull the center of gravity older. Routing students to the Down-Path up front pre-judges affordability. That contradicts the stop rule, which offers the Down-Path only after the buyer says he can't afford it.

**Fix:** Base eligibility on the age fork plus the one affordability question asked of everyone, not on student status. Assign 18–19 explicitly. Keep the 19–28 Struggler as the default protagonist of worked examples and scripts. Limit the Age-Up Dial to reducing minors' share, not aging the adult core.

### SPE-15 [major] THESES B6, B9, B10, E8, E10, E11, E12, E16, P17, §6; LEDGER B (refund/dispute rows), F (complaint thresholds), G (age of majority, statutory cancellation); STANDARD hard line 1; SPEC §3 'AI minimal, legal minimal'
**Issue:** Legal/risk coverage goes beyond 'minimal: flag only the biggest risks'. There are about 20 legal/policy touchpoints across a dozen theses, plus jurisdictional detail in the LEDGER (US state ages, UK/EU cancellation, card-network thresholds). Yet it misses one of the biggest risks: the door collects email and screen answers before or around the age fork, and nothing says that data from under-18s, or health-adjacent answers from anyone, is kept to a minimum. AI goes the other way. There is no house rule at all: the ecosystem research's 'no AI avatar or cloned voice' rule never reached THESES/STANDARD, so modules will improvise.

**Fix:** Consolidate legal into one 'Biggest Risks' register of at most 6 flags, owned by one module and cited in one line elsewhere:
- minors and age
- structural and medical claims
- testimonial and before/after consent and typicality
- refunds, statutory cancellation and disputes
- health-adjacent data in forms and pixels
- health framing on destinations
Cut LEDGER G's jurisdictional detail to 'legal adult where you live'. Add: 'Under-18s give no email or intake answers; the education lane is public content only.' Add one AI line: AI may assist drafting, clipping and transcription under operator claim approval; no AI avatars, cloned voice, or AI-generated or AI-edited faces or results.

### SPE-16 [major] THESES B1, B14, B15, §7 instrumentation list; DECISIONS D31; LEDGER A; THESES E1–E16 (count); business SYNTHESIS module map
**Issue:** Operations and ecosystem get more weight than the spec gives them. SPEC §3 says operations (pricing & unit economics, systems, freelancers, time & energy) are 'in scope but NOT in-depth'. Yet the organizing frame makes the minute the unit, LEDGER A has 12 unit-economics rows, §7 lists ~15 instruments, and D31 adds stage metric sets and guardrails. The business research's module map spends ~5 of 14 business modules on ops: business math, pricing, payments/disputes, operating model, metrics. Ecosystem is the least-focus track (~5 modules) but carries 16 theses, more than Business's 15.

**Fix:** Cap operations at two business modules (unit economics & pricing mechanics; operating week & freelancers), plus stage-note sidebars. Fold payments, guarantees and disputes into one section. Limit instrumentation to at most 6 stage metrics plus guardrails. Define 'energy design' as scheduling mechanics only. Consolidate the E-theses to ~10 (fold E4 into E7/E14, E13 into E5, E8 into E7), and move E2 and E16 (door, eligible-adult yield) into the business track, where they drive profit.

### SPE-17 [major] THESES H4; STANDARD 'Numbers'; DECISIONS D37, D46; THESES P3
**Issue:** The numbers rule contradicts other decisions and over-tightens. H4/STANDARD: 'No number in public copy unless it comes from the operator's own records.' But D37 has the early operator lead with 'research literacy', and D46 describes mechanisms by evidence tier; both usually mean citing outside figures. As written, the operator can't say in a video that habits take a median of about two months to become automatic. That is stricter than the prior standard, which treated research literacy as a form of proof.

**Fix:** Scope the rule: 'No performance, outcome or client number unless it comes from your own records. External research figures may appear only when attributed at their evidence tier, and never as the brand's results.'

### SPE-18 [major] DECISIONS D38; THESES P3, P11; DECISIONS D41; REGISTRY 'Skeptic's Piece'
**Issue:** D38 ('No analysis of non-consenting real faces in any form') is stricter than the prior standard, which banned rating and annotating features. It blocks the anti-grift content the foundation relies on. P3's prebunking and D41's named enemy, 'the grift economy (fake proof…)', both require showing how a published before/after was manufactured, and that is analysis of a non-consenting face.

**Fix:** Reword D38: 'Never comment on the features or attractiveness of a non-consenting real person. You may analyze the capture conditions of a published marketing claim (lens, light, angle, expression, timeline claim), with faces cropped or blurred where possible and the claim, not the person, as the subject.'

### SPE-19 [minor] STANDARD 'Stop rules'; THESES H2, B3, B1; LEDGER B 'Down-Path tool'
**Issue:** The stop rule says ''I can't afford it' ends money talk: offer the Down-Path once'. But the Down-Path is a $27–97 paid tool, so offering it is money talk, and H2 defines reopening money after 'can't afford' as pressure. The Down-Path's 'scheduled human touch' also spends operator minutes on the lowest-yield rung, against B1's minute economy.

**Fix:** Lead the Down-Path with a free branch: public resources plus one batched async check-in. Name the paid tool once, with its price, as an option with no follow-up. Define the human touch as one templated, batched async message, and cap Down-Path volume per week.

### SPE-20 [minor] DECISIONS D41, D22, D46; THESES E9, E15
**Issue:** The exclusions leak at the edges. 'Clinicians are referral partners' (D41) implies a two-way partnership: a collaboration channel, which is excluded, and an implied endorsement, which D46 forbids. E15's 'X is a peer graph' invites co-created threads, live audio and guest swaps. E9's title ('The comment section is the public community') and D22's Instagram 'broadcasts' blur the free-community exclusion in vocabulary.

**Fix:** D41: 'referral destinations (one-way), never co-branded or presented as endorsers'. E15: X engagement is replies and quote-posts on ideas; no co-created content, Spaces or guest swaps. Retitle E9 'The comment section is the public record', and define broadcasts as one-way with no member chat.

### SPE-21 [minor] STANDARD governing test (Informed-Client Test); DECISIONS D45; THESES B11
**Issue:** The Informed-Client Test includes 'and what they got'. That makes it depend on outcomes and impossible to audit at writing time. Read literally, any low-end client fails it, a stricter bar than the prior test (which looked at the mechanism) and the opposite of the spec's loosening. B11 then says continuity's 'real product is the outcome dataset'. A client paying for continuity would not endorse that framing if he saw the playbook.

**Fix:** Restate the test: 'Would a client whose result fell anywhere in the range he was shown, including the low end, still say the persuasion served him?' Reframe B11: 'For the client, continuity's product is long-run measurement and review; for the business, the dataset is a by-product, used publicly only with written consent.'

### SPE-22 [minor] THESES §0 master thesis, P5, P11; DECISIONS D13; REGISTRY 'Research Literacy'; SPEC §3 'write credential-agnostic'
**Issue:** SPEC asks for credential-agnostic writing. The foundation instead writes as if the operator has no credentials: 'The operator is solo, uncredentialed', 'the uncredentialed operator's defensible edge', 'Credentials are replaced by visible standards', 'the credential substitute'. The playbook becomes wrong the day the operator earns any certificate.

**Fix:** Use phrasing like 'trust that never rests on credentials' and 'whatever credentials you hold, visible standards carry the weight'. Keep the scripted qualifications answer as 'what I am and am not', without assuming there are none.

### SPE-23 [minor] LEDGER A 'Revenue needed per delivery hour', B 'Private layer', B 'Payment-plan sales lift', C 'Self-assessment start → lead', E effect-size rows; THESES B5
**Issue:** Several LEDGER rows have errors or break the ranges rule:
- The upper bound of revenue needed per delivery hour doesn't match its own inputs: $75k ÷ 26 h ≈ $2,900, not $2,600.
- The Private total ('≈$4.5–6k') doesn't match its parts: $1.5–3k cohort + $2.5–4k = $4–7k.
- Several rows are single points (~+20%, ~40%, r≈.3, d≈0.47, d≈0.3, r≈0.1), against the 'ranges with context' rule.
- B5's 1.5–3 h per enrollment assumes a 0.75 h all-in slot. If intake review and the written plan push that to ~1.25 h, it becomes ~2.5–5 h and overlaps the free-call range, weakening B5's headline claim.

**Fix:** Recompute the delivery-hour and Private-total rows. Express effect sizes as ranges or qualitative bands marked 'explanation only, never quoted'. State the assessment's all-in time (intake review + session + written plan) and recompute hours per enrollment.

### SPE-24 [minor] REGISTRY ('Taste', 'Floor over Peak', 'Change in Kind, Not Degree', 'Three Carriers of Authority', 'Brand-as-Container', 28 reused ecosystem frameworks); THESES E5, E6; SPEC §5 'refresh then extend, minimal overlap'
**Issue:** The future Glossary carries leftovers from excluded or over-covered material. Self-development aphorisms (Taste, Floor over Peak, Change in Kind) edge toward operator mindset. Three Carriers of Authority and Brand-as-Container were built on the faceless premise ('equity beyond the face'). ## Proposed theses
- In a category built on faked before/afters and a discredited vocabulary, the brand sells the adult who has stalled an honest, measured answer about his own face: what can still move, what cannot, and what to do this week. It is delivered in scarce review minutes and proven by matched-condition records, with force aimed at wasted months and never at his face.
- Genetics and growth set the structure. For an adult, habits and honest measurement move presentation and function (posture, breathing habits, body composition, how he is photographed) and how quickly he learns what is worth pursuing. Structural change is never the promise.
- Bone growth is the category's question, not the brand's promise. The brand answers it by age and evidence tier: growth modification during growth is an orthodontist's domain, habit-driven skeletal change in adults is unproven, and adult presentation and function are measurable. It wins trust by being the one voice in the category that answers the bone question straight.
- A date is an offer attribute when the next start is always purchasable, the date comes from the calendar or the capacity math, and nothing about the week's content or email volume changes as it approaches. Once a window needs its own content sequence, bonuses, prices, extra sends or a closing cart, it has become a launch and is out of bounds.
- The House Standard is a license as well as a limit. Every persuasion module must show each relevant permitted move at full strength in at least one worked script: a direct ask with a price, a firm second probe, a true stake stated plainly, a real date. The looser standard is then demonstrated rather than merely allowed.
- Vulnerability is handled by signals, not scores. Acute distress, fixation language, surgery-seeking or compulsive checking pause the sale for a human conversation, and that conversation, not a questionnaire cut-off, decides between enrolling with adjusted expectations, the Down-Path, or a referral.
- The assessment reads the history, not the face. Photos are captured as a baseline for later matched comparison and never interpreted structurally, and the written plan must be worth its fee to the buyer who never enrolls, including when the recommendation is not to buy.
- Gate the announcement, not the raise: once utilization and outcome milestones have held for two cycles, announce a date, and an announced price rise always happens.
- The paid community sells belonging to adults who measure. By default it is the alumni continuity room built around logged weeks and periodic review, where membership means 'people who keep records'. An open paid membership becomes an option only once moderation minutes are budgeted and the room's standard of evidence is established.
- Non-response is the most common honest outcome in this category, so it is agreed in advance at onboarding. Client and operator settle what a week-6 non-response looks like and what follows: adjust, hold, exit with a partial refund, or refer. The moment where most coaches lose clients and reputation becomes the brand's most credible proof of honesty.
- Identity is earned by evidence, not by appearance or purchase. The in-group is 'people who measure'; each commitment is small, private by default and verifiable; public commitment is offered but never required; and competition inside a cohort runs only on logged process, opt-in.
- The first clients come from the smallest honest audience the operator can reach without collaborations or a free community: his disclosed warm network, the people who reply to his first videos, and a standing founding-offer page linked from every early piece. Month one is measured in conversations booked, not views.
- The top half of the profit band comes from price and leverage, not more hours. Proof-gated cohort pricing, async-first assessments, capped Private seats and a post-graduate digital system each enter only when they raise profit per operator hour.
