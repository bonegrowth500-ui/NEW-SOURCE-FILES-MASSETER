## 1. Executive findings

1. **Diagnosis beats closing technique.** Sales-performance meta-analyses rank selling-related knowledge and adaptiveness among the top predictors (Verbeke 2011; Franke & Park 2006) [STRONG, B2B]. Rackham's call observations (~35,000 calls†) linked classic closing techniques to *lower* success in high-consideration sales. They traced most objections to pitching capabilities before the buyer had stated an explicit need [MODERATE: proprietary, dated].
   - *Implication:* $1.5–3k is ~4–6% of a 22–32-year-old's gross annual pay [B2], a high-consideration purchase. Diagnose the stall, then make one reasoned recommendation. Closing lines are low-yield.

2. **His own words persuade him.** In motivational interviewing (MI), the strength of a client's commitment language predicts behaviour (Amrhein 2003). Confrontational counselling raises resistance and worsens outcomes a year later (Miller 1993) [STRONG clinically; the transfer to selling is DERIVED]. Follow-up questions raise liking (Huang 2017) [STRONG], and talking about oneself is intrinsically rewarding (Tamir & Mitchell 2012) [MODERATE].
   - *Implication:* the Struggler has heard every pitch. He has rarely said out loud what 6–24 months of unmeasured effort cost him. Questions first, your reasoning second, price last.

3. **Autonomy raises yes-rates.** Telling people they are free to refuse increases compliance (Carpenter 2013: 42 studies, ~22,000 people†) [MODERATE]. Threats to freedom trigger reactance through anger and counter-arguing (Rains 2013) [STRONG].
   - *Implication:* a firm ask (N2) plus a sincere "no is a fine answer" is the highest-yield close for a grift-fatigued buyer.

4. **Transparency converts this buyer.** Once people spot a tactic, they discount the whole message. A salient ulterior motive cuts perceived sincerity (Campbell & Kirmani 2000) [STRONG]. Two-sided messages that *refute* the counter-argument beat one-sided ones; two-sided messages that only concede do worse (O'Keefe 1999†) [STRONG].
   - *Implication:* narrate the call. Say you sell. Name and answer "adults can't change" and "another grift".
   - A tactic that only works unseen fails the IC test and backfires commercially here.

5. **Speed and short lead times are the cheapest levers.**
   - Leads contacted within an hour were ~7× likelier to qualify than those contacted an hour later, and ~60× likelier than those contacted after 24 hours. The average firm took 42 hours (2,241 firms) [MODERATE, B2B].
   - Show rates fall from ~81% for next-day bookings to ~60% at day 14 [B1].
   - Clinic no-shows average ~23% and rise with lead time; SMS reminders raise attendance (odds ratio ≈1.5†) [STRONG analogs].
   - *Implication:* same-day replies, slots within 0–3 days, two reminders carrying a pre-call video.

6. **Choice overload is conditional, and this buyer meets the conditions.** The mean effect is ≈0 across 50 experiments (Scheibehenne 2010). It is reliable when options are complex, the task is hard, preferences are uncertain and one option must be chosen (Chernev 2015, 99 observations) [STRONG]. A second attractive option raises deferral (Tversky & Shafir 1992) [MODERATE].
   - *Implication:* one recommendation. Offer the Down-Path only on disqualification or affordability.

7. **Loss aversion is smaller and more conditional than folklore claims; loss framing is not a multiplier.** λ is the loss-aversion coefficient: how much more a loss weighs than an equal gain. Meta-analytic λ runs from ≈1.3† (risky choice; Walasek 2024) to ≈1.96† (607 estimates; Brown 2024). It shrinks with low stakes and expertise (Mrkva 2020) [existence STRONG; size CONTESTED]. Gain- and loss-framed health messages differ by almost nothing (O'Keefe & Jensen) [STRONG].
   - *Implication:* **Honest Stakes**: one true, specific cost-of-inaction line, ideally in his own words.

8. **How people pay shapes how they use.** Payment plans lift sales ~20%, mostly among buyers with weak credit [B1, STRONG]. Usage peaks after each payment and then decays, and prepaid bundles weaken the link between cost and use, so bundle holders skip more (Gourville & Soman 1998; Soman & Gourville 2001) [MODERATE–STRONG, field].
   - *Implication:* **Payment Rhythm**. Offer monthly plans behind an affordability check (HL5). Pay-in-full clients get a monthly "value receipt": what they used and what their measures show.

9. **Reversible decisions sell better but satisfy less.** Lenient refund policies raise purchases more than returns [B1, STRONG]. But people who can still reverse a choice like the outcome less (Gilbert & Ebert 2002) [MODERATE].
   - *Implication:* keep B1's 14–21-day fit window, closed by a **Commit Ritual** at the first review.

10. **Automaticity: a ~2-month median, with a spread from days to most of a year.** Lally 2010: median 66 days, range 18–254 [MODERATE]. A 2024 review (20 studies, 2,601 people): medians 59–66 days, means 106–154, range 4–335 [MODERATE]. One missed day did not derail formation.
    - *Implication:* many habits won't be automatic by week 12. Say so in the **Expectation Contract** and build a Hold/continuity stage. Never quote "21 days" (a myth) or "66 days" as a rule.

11. **Plan, monitor, report: the best-evidenced adherence stack.**
    - **Implementation intentions** (pre-set "if X, then Y" plans): d≈0.65 across 94 tests (2006), smaller in narrower and newer syntheses (physical activity ≈0.31†; a 642-test synthesis smaller still†) [existence STRONG; size CONTESTED].
    - **Goal setting:** d≈0.34 (Epton 2017) [STRONG].
    - **Progress monitoring:** d≈0.40, larger when progress is physically recorded and reported to someone (Harkin 2016, 138 studies) [STRONG].
    - *Implication:* each client leaves onboarding with if-then plans, a daily log the coach reads, and a fixed reporting rhythm.

12. **Measure behaviour often; measure the face rarely and identically.**
    - Body checking raised body dissatisfaction in experiments (Shafran 2007†) [MODERATE].
    - Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) is over-represented among people seeking appearance change [B2, STRONG].
    - A selfie taken at 12 inches makes the nose look ~30% wider than one taken at 5 feet (Ward 2018) [MODERATE].
    - *Implication:* **Two-Track Tracking**. Log behaviour daily; take identical photo sets (distance, lens, light, head position) only at fixed checkpoints (e.g., baseline, weeks 6 and 12), reviewed with the coach. This protects the client and the proof (HL1).

13. **Human review is the adherence engine.**
    - Unguided self-help products leak: mental-health apps keep ~3.9% of users at day 15 (Baumel 2019), and MOOC completion is 3.13% [STRONG].
    - Online fitness coaching retains 94/82/65/45% at months 1/3/6/12 [B1, MODERATE].
    - Guided programs beat unguided ones. People adhere for a reviewer they see as benevolent, competent and trustworthy (the supportive-accountability model; Mohr 2011) [MODERATE–STRONG].
    - *Implication:* the review loop is the product. Async review minutes are the real delivery unit and the honest reason for seat caps.

14. **The working alliance predicts results about as well as anything a coach controls.** Its correlation with outcomes is r≈.32 in coaching (27 samples, N=3,563) and r≈.28 in psychotherapy (295 studies) [STRONG]. Progress feedback helps off-track clients most (Lambert 2018†) [MODERATE].
    - *Implication:* a 60-second **Alliance Check** after every call, plus an off-track alert rule.

15. **Endings sell the next step.** Memory over-weights peaks and endings, although duration is not fully ignored (Kahneman 1993; colonoscopy RCT, Redelmeier 2003; Alaybek 2022 meta-analysis†) [STRONG]. Service sequences that improve and end strong raised repurchase (Dixon & Verma 2013†) [MODERATE].
    - *Implication:* make the final fortnight the peak, and place the continuity decision there, at a measurement moment.

16. **Ask directly, and ask everyone.**
    - People asking for help expect to need about twice as many asks as they do† (Flynn & Lake 2008) [MODERATE].
    - Referred customers are ≥16% more valuable [B2, STRONG].
    - Solicited reviews are more representative (Karaman 2021†) [MODERATE].
    - FTC guidance warns against asking only customers you expect to be positive ("review gating") [STRONG, regulatory].
    - *Implication:* a **Win-Timed Ask** to every client at measured checkpoints. Ask for referrals privately (B2's Discreet Referral).

17. **Identity follows logged behaviour.** Self-identity predicts intentions beyond attitudes (r≈.47†; Rise 2010) [MODERATE]. The "be a voter" noun-label effect failed a large field replication (Gerber 2016) [CONTESTED].
    - *Implication:* tie identity framing (N7) to evidence: "38 of 42 days logged is what taking this seriously looks like."

18. **Expectations sustain effort; fantasies drain it.** Positive *expectations* (probability judgments) predict effort and success; positive *fantasies* (idealized images) predict less (Oettingen & Mayer 2002) [MODERATE–STRONG]. Mental contrasting with if-then plans: g≈0.34† [MODERATE]. Weight-loss patients who expected ~2× the typical result† were disappointed by clinically meaningful losses (Foster 1997); unrealistic expectations are linked to dropout (Dalle Grave 2005) [MODERATE].
    - *Implication:* sell hope as probability ranges tied to things he controls, never as a fantasy face.

## 2. Sales conversation mechanics

### 2.1 What each selling school contributes

| School | Mechanism | Evidence | Where it breaks for this buyer | Keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SPIN** | Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff questions. The buyer turns vague needs into explicit ones; objections are prevented by not pitching early | MODERATE (≈35k calls†; proprietary; B2B) | Too many situation questions feel like an interrogation. Scripted implication questions read as manipulation | Implication and need-payoff questions in *his* words. No program talk before he states an explicit need |
| **Challenger** | Teach a reframe, tailor it, take control | WEAK–MODERATE (survey of 6,000+ reps†; B2B) | "Teaching" can humiliate; "control" slides into pressure | One commercial insight: the stall reframe |
| **Gap selling** | The value is the gap between his current state and the future he wants | ANECDOTAL | Manufactured gaps; shame when the gap is the face itself | Gap stated in controllables: consistency, measurement, feedback, time lost |
| **MI-informed** | Draw out his own reasons, reflect them, don't argue with his hesitation, respect autonomy | STRONG clinically (g≈0.22, 119 studies); the transfer to selling is DERIVED | MI assumes the helper has no stake; you do | Disclose the stake. Explore MI-style, then recommend honestly |
| **Adaptive** | Adjust the approach to each buyer | STRONG (B2B meta-analyses). Customer orientation alone doesn't lift objective sales† | Friendliness is not adaptiveness | Tailor from the application |

### 2.2 What predicts close rates (by strength of evidence)

1. **Pipeline hygiene** [MODERATE; STRONG analogs]: response speed, lead time, reminders, pre-call video.
2. **Diagnostic depth and tailoring** [STRONG, B2B].
3. **One recommendation plus a payment option** [STRONG mechanisms].
4. **Self-selection** [mechanism MODERATE; niche data WEAK]: application effort, 18+ gate, red flags, price.
5. **Buyer talk share** [WEAK, vendor]: top B2B reps talk ~43% of the time and ask ~11–14 questions spread through the call (Gong†).
6. **Warmth** [ANECDOTAL]: content consumed before booking. Track "Start Here" views per applicant.

**Benchmarks:**
- Close rate on held calls: 15–35%; top performers 35–45% [B1, WEAK].
- Assessment → program conversion: 25–50% [B1, ESTIMATE].
- Call length: no outcome data exists. Default to 40–60 minutes for a $1.5–3k decision (matching B1's 0.5–0.75 h assessment) and a 15-minute fit check for warm direct enrollers [ESTIMATE].

### 2.3 The Glass-Box Call (for a $1.5–3k decision)

| Phase (min) | Move | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Frame (2–3) | "Here's how this works: I'll ask what you've tried, tell you honestly whether coaching fits, and if it does, what it costs. If not, I'll tell you what I'd do instead. A no is fine." | Transparency; autonomy |
| Excavate (15–20) | What he tried, for how long, how he measured it, what happened around month 3, what he concluded | Problem questions; follow-ups; tailoring |
| Stakes (5) | "If the next 12 months look like the last 12, what does that cost you?" Reflect his words back | Implication questions; MI discrepancy; Honest Stakes |
| Standards (3–5) | What coaching can and can't do; typical ranges; refer-out flags | Refutational two-sidedness; HL2 |
| Recommendation (5) | One option, with reasons tied to his words. State the price once, then stay silent | One-Recommendation Rule; honest anchors |
| Decision (5) | Ask directly. If he says "I need to think about it", run the Real-Objection Sort (§4) | N2; autonomy reminder |
| Day-one plan (3) | If yes: if-then plans, and the first review booked before hanging up | Implementation intentions; dissonance relief |

If no, give him the Down-Path advice anyway. A well-handled decline feeds B2's Refusal Ledger.

### 2.4 Application design (6–8 questions, 5–10 minutes)

- **Age:** 18+; 20+ for high-ticket [B2]. Hard stop for minors (HL5).
- **History:** what, how long, how measured. This shows his stall pattern.
- **Time:** realistic minutes per day.
- **Red flags:** breathing or sleep complaints, jaw pain, orthodontic history, brief BDD-screen items (preoccupation, time spent, distress). Any flag means decline and refer; validated screeners exist, but niche cut-offs don't.
- **Readiness:** "If we decide it's a fit, are you in a position to invest in the next 30 days?"
- **Why now:** his own reasons, stated before the call.

Some friction helps. Effort justification and the IKEA effect (self-built items valued ~63% more†) raise commitment [MODERATE], and price screening filters for intent [B1, STRONG]. But long forms cut volume [DERIVED].

Evenly spaced external deadlines beat self-set ones (Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002) [MODERATE] | Name the real cohort-start and credit-window dates. ## 4. Objection taxonomy with underlying psychology

| Surface objection | What's usually underneath | Diagnostic move | Response principle | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Value doubt. His reference prices come from free content and $5–15 e-books [B1]; pain of paying | "Compared with what?" | Change the comparison class: he is buying structure, measurement and review, not information. Show the delivery math | No fake value stacks (HL1) |
| "I can't afford it" | A real liquidity constraint | One explicit affordability question | Down-Path, then **stop selling** | HL5 hard stop |
| "I need to think about it" | Decision conflict, anticipated regret, an unvoiced concern, or a polite no | Real-Objection Sort (below) | Resolve the specific issue, or set a real decision date with a yes/no follow-up | N2; no fake deadline (HL3) |
| "I don't have time" | The illusion that future time will be more plentiful than future money [MODERATE–STRONG]; the planning fallacy [STRONG]; low priority in disguise | "Walk me through a normal weekday. Where would 20 minutes go?" | State the true weekly time cost. Make an if-then plan on the call | IC: never understate time |
| "Will it work for me? Adults can't change" | Outcome uncertainty; learned helplessness after a stall | "What would you need to see by week 6 to know it's working?" | Expectation Contract: process claims, contextualized ranges, what is measurable in 12 weeks. Refute; don't concede | HL2 |
| "How do I know you're legit?" | He expects a sales tactic and infers an ulterior motive | "What have you seen that made you skeptical?" | Glass-box transparency, your refusal record, standardized proof, what you *don't* claim | HL6 |
| "I already tried mewing for a year" | He blames stable causes ("genetics"); sunk cost | "What did you do, how did you measure it, and what happened at month 3?" | Re-attribute the stall to controllable variables: measurement, consistency, feedback | HL4: never blame the person |
| "I need to ask my parents / partner" | Shared finances; real authority at 18–21 | "What will they want to know?" | Offer a one-page summary or a short joint call | Under 18: stop (HL5) |
| "What will people think?" | Stigma; he wants to use this privately [B2] | "Who would know, and would it matter?" | Privacy by design: neutral billing name, no face-posting required, private cohort | Permitted |
| "I'll start next month / after exams" | Fresh-start effect (a real boost) or plain deferral | "What changes then?" | Pin the start to a real landmark and make the day-one plan now | N4 |
| "Should I see an orthodontist instead?" | A scope question, often legitimate | "What made you ask?" | A confident boundary. Refer out on flags; coaching works alongside professional care, not instead of it | HL2 |
| "Can you guarantee results?" | Risk aversion. Those least likely to succeed are most drawn to outcome guarantees (adverse selection) | "What would a guarantee need to protect you from?" | Process Guarantee [B1]. Explain why no one can honestly guarantee structural change | HL2 |

**Real-Objection Sort (script core):**
- *Open:* "Fair. Usually it's one of four things: the money, whether it'll work for you, the timing, or someone you need to talk to. Which is it?"
- *Resolve or release:* "If it's X, here's what I'd suggest… If you'd rather sleep on it, let's book 10 minutes on Thursday for a clear yes or no. Either answer is fine."
- *Basis:* decision-avoidance psychology (Anderson 2003) plus the autonomy reminder [MODERATE]. Permitted under N2.

## 5. Calls vs DMs vs async selling

**Evidence**
- **Voice builds trust better than text.** Hearing someone speak makes them seem more thoughtful and competent than reading the same words (Schroeder & Epley 2015). The effect holds even when they disagree with you (Schroeder 2017) [MODERATE–STRONG].
- **Calls are less awkward than people expect.** People predict calls will be more awkward than text, yet calls build stronger bonds with no extra awkwardness (Kumar & Epley 2021) [MODERATE].
- **Asking in person beats asking by email.** Face-to-face requests were ~34× more effective, and requesters overrated email (Roghanizad & Bohns 2017) [MODERATE].
- **Async lowers pressure and scales.** Fewer live asks mean less reactance [DERIVED], and async gets around the cap on revenue from one-to-one calls at 20–25 h/week (B2's Call Ceiling).
- **Benchmarks:** landing-page median conversion 6.6% (wellness 8.2%) [B1]; automated-DM opens 80–90% and click-through 25–45% [B1, WEAK]. DMs carry age risk and "DM closer" grift optics [B1].

**Channel-by-Ticket Map (default picks)**

| Offer [B1 bands] | Default channel | Human layer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY / Down-Path ($97–297) | Sales page → checkout | FAQ video; email replies | A call costs more than the sale earns; low risk |
| Paid assessment ($150–250) | Page → booking | Pre-call video | The assessment *is* the sales conversation |
| Cohort ($1.5–3k) | Assessment → one recommendation. Warm direct enrollers: page plus an optional 15-min fit check | Voice-note answers to questions | High-consideration purchase; voice builds trust; an optional call preserves autonomy |
| Private ($3.5–6k, capped) | Call required | — | Fit and capacity are critical |
| Continuity ($29–79/mo) | Graduation review | The coach's recommendation | Peak-end and measurement moment |

**DM rules**
- Route people; don't close in DMs.
- Answer questions with 1–3-minute voice notes where the platform allows (Voice-Before-Text).
- Confirm age 18+ before discussing any paid offer.
- Link to the page, application or assessment.
- No price pressure in text.
- Reply the same day.

The voice-note rule rests on analog evidence and is untested in DMs.

## 6. Onboarding & post-purchase

**Evidence**
- **Post-purchase dissonance** has three facets: emotional unease, doubts about the wisdom of the purchase, and concern over the deal (Sweeney 2000) [MODERATE].
- **"Spreading of alternatives"** (liking the chosen option more after choosing) has a methodological flaw (Chen & Risen 2010). It survives in smaller form under corrected designs (Izuma & Murayama 2013) [CONTESTED].
- **Preparation reduces dropout.** Explaining in advance what the process involves and how long it takes ("role induction") reduces psychotherapy dropout (Swift et al. 2012 practice recommendations†) [MODERATE].
- **Fresh starts help.** Temporal landmarks such as a new week or month raise aspirational behaviour (Dai 2014) [MODERATE–STRONG].
- **A real head start helps.** Completion was 34% vs 19% (Nunes & Drèze 2006) [MODERATE].

**First-14 Protocol**

| When | Move | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes after payment | Confirmation: what happens next, when, and what he needs | Settles "concern over the deal" |
| Within 48 h | Kickoff (call or async video): Expectation Contract, if-then plans, first review date | Preparation; implementation intentions |
| Days 1–3 | Baseline: standardized photo set and behaviour log. Both count as Stage 1 progress | A real head start; Two-Track Tracking |
| Day 7 | First review, naming one specific thing he did well | Supportive accountability; small wins |
| Days 14–21 | Fit window closes at a review with the Commit Ritual: he restates his reasons | Reversibility paradox |

Start dates fall on landmarks: a Monday, the 1st of the month, or a cohort start.

## 7. Adherence mechanics

(Items already in §1 are not repeated here.)

- **Habit speed.** In the 2024 review, faster formation went with more frequent practice, consistent timing, a self-chosen behaviour and enjoyment† [MODERATE].
  - *Hypothesis:* continuous posture and breathing behaviours may form more slowly than discrete cue-response habits. There is no direct evidence.
- **Plans.** Use 2–3 if-then plans, a "miss plan", and mental contrasting on the likeliest obstacle. In the field, prompts to write down a date and time raised flu vaccination by ~4 percentage points† (Milkman 2011) [STRONG].
- **Goals.** Use specific, hard-but-reachable *process* goals. Public or group-shared goals tended to do better† [STRONG core].
- **Monitoring.** In weight loss, self-monitoring is consistently associated with results (Burke, Wang & Sevick 2011) [STRONG, observational].
- **Stage Gradients.** Run 3–4 short stages, and give weeks 5–8 a fresh sub-goal.
  - Effort speeds up as a reward nears (Kivetz 2006) [MODERATE–STRONG].
  - Motivation sags in the middle (Bonezzi 2011) [MODERATE].
  - Highlight what's done early and what's left late, whichever is smaller (Koo & Fishbach 2008/2012) [MODERATE].
- **Small wins.** Felt progress in meaningful work was the top daily motivator in ~12,000 diary entries, yet managers ranked it last† (Amabile & Kramer 2011) [MODERATE]. Use a Weekly Wins Ledger.
- **Self-determination theory (SDT).** Support for autonomy, competence and relatedness tracks self-driven motivation and health behaviour across 184 datasets [STRONG, correlational]. SDT-based interventions give small real gains across 73 studies [STRONG]. Offer choice within structure, with a reason for every requirement.
- **Groups** (card C23). Sources: "true groups" beat classes and home programs (Burke 2006†) [MODERATE]; step competition beat support arms (Patel 2019†) [STRONG]; competitive networks beat supportive ones (Zhang 2016) [MODERATE]. Compete on logged days, never faces (HL4; BDD).
- **Deadlines.** Evenly spaced external deadlines beat self-set ones [MODERATE]; set weekly submission deadlines.
- **Lapses** (card C24). Sources: abstinence-violation effect (Curry 1987) and dieters' "what-the-hell" collapse (Polivy & Herman 1985) [MODERATE]; one missed day is harmless (Lally); a return-after-miss reward topped a 61,293-person gym megastudy (Milkman 2021) [STRONG].
- **Identity.** When a behaviour fits who someone is, difficulty reads as "this matters", not "this is impossible" (identity-based motivation: Oyserman 2009; possible-selves RCT: Oyserman 2006) [MODERATE].
- **Dropout predictors, ranked:**
  1. No human review [STRONG].
  2. Weak alliance [STRONG].
  3. Younger age (in psychotherapy)† [STRONG].
  4. Unrealistic expectations [MODERATE].
  5. Low response in the first ~2 months, which predicts later outcomes in lifestyle programs (Unick 2015†) [MODERATE].
  6. Usage decaying after an upfront payment [MODERATE].
  - Benchmark: ~50% of exercise starters quit within 6 months (an old, widely cited estimate†) [MODERATE–WEAK].
- **Myths to keep out:**
  - "21 days"
  - "66 days" as a rule
  - "95% with an accountability partner" (untraceable)
  - "the Yale/Harvard 3% goal study" (never happened)
  - "7-38-55%" (a misapplication of Mehrabian)

## 8. Alliance, retention & renewals

- **Alliance.** In 1,895 client–coach pairs†, the alliance was the main predictor of coaching effectiveness as clients rated it; personality matching was not (de Haan 2016) [MODERATE].
- **Coaching effects.** Workplace coaching overall: g≈0.43–0.74 (Theeboom 2014) and δ≈0.36 (Jones 2016) [MODERATE–STRONG].
- **MI.** Effect g≈0.22 across 119 studies: durable, and comparable to other active treatments (Lundahl 2010) [STRONG]. MI beat plain advice in ~3 of 4 RCTs† (Rubak 2005). Use it for plateaus and ambivalence, not as a cure-all.
- **Alliance Check.** Three items after each call: did he feel heard, did you work on what matters, does the approach fit. Modelled on brief session-rating scales [MODERATE]. Any dip triggers a repair conversation.
- **Benchmarks.** Online fitness coaching retains 94/82/65/45% at months 1/3/6/12 [B1]. Paid-community churn is 4–10% a month [B1, WEAK].
- **Renewal design.**
  - Decide at a measurement moment (B1's week-10 reassessment).
  - Sequence the program to improve and end strong.
  - Make continuation a transparent default. Defaults shift choices at d≈0.68 (Jachimowicz 2019) [STRONG]. Require explicit consent and offer one-click exit.
  - Nudge members who go quiet (B1's Usage-Backed Continuity).
  - Treat graduation as success (B2's Graduation Ladder).
- **Legal flag:** auto-renewal rules vary by jurisdiction.

## 9. Referral & testimonial psychology

- **Ask directly.** People underestimate how willing others are to help (Flynn & Lake) [MODERATE]. Referral intentions far exceed referral behaviour (Kumar, Petersen & Leone 2007) [MODERATE]. Make the ask specific and low-effort: a forwardable one-pager or a private link.
- **Timing.** Ask after a measured win, a milestone or graduation; the week-12 review is the natural peak [DERIVED from STRONG peak-end evidence]. Testimonials written close to the experience ("just finished my week-12 review") read as more credible (Chen & Lurie 2013) [MODERATE].
- **Content.** Narratives move intention; statistics move beliefs (Zebregs 2015) [MODERATE]. Narratives persuade reliably (Braddock & Dillard 2016) [STRONG]. Pair his story with a standardized comparison, context (timeline, adherence %, what else changed) and the typical range.
- **Ask-Everyone Rule.** Solicit every completer at fixed points (Karaman 2021†). This avoids gating and stays within the FTC review rule, the Endorsement Guides and the FTC's solicitation guidance [B2, STRONG].
- **Privacy.** Use discreet referral codes. Allow faceless (voice or text) testimonials, with consent. Never publish a face without written consent.
- **Incentives.** Referral rewards work best for weak ties and weaker brands [B2], which fits the early stage. Disclose any incentive attached to a testimonial.

## 10. Mechanism cards

Each card follows the same pattern: *mechanism* · *Ev* = evidence · *Breaks* = where it fails · *Use* = application in this niche · *Std* = Standard Card note.

**C1. Evoked self-persuasion.** People act on reasons they say out loud; follow-up questions raise liking. *Ev:* STRONG clinically; DERIVED for sales. *Breaks:* leading questions read as a script. *Use:* "What did you expect by month 6, and what happened?" *Std:* N2/N9. Disclose your stake (IC); never make the face the problem (HL4).

**C2. Need before capability.** Describing the program before he states a need creates objections; implication questions let *him* state the value. *Ev:* MODERATE (SPIN). *Breaks:* over-leading questions. *Use:* no program talk until he names what a measured, reviewed year would change. *Std:* permitted.

**C3. Stall reframe.** A credible new explanation, pinning the stall on changeable causes, restores motivation and creates the case for help (Weiner 1985; Curry 1987). *Ev:* WEAK–MODERATE. *Breaks:* condescension; "everything you know is wrong". *Use:* "A stall is usually a measurement and feedback problem before it's a genetics verdict." *Std:* N6, aimed at ideas; HL2.

**C4. Pressure backfires.** Threats to autonomy trigger anger and counter-arguing, and he argues for staying put. "You must" language amplifies this; "the choice is yours" partly restores freedom (Miller 2007†). *Ev:* STRONG. *Breaks:* confusing directness with confrontation; evidence-based challenges are fine. *Use:* "You said consistent. How many days did you log?" *Std:* N2; HL4.

**C5. Free-to-Say-No Close.** Explicit autonomy reminders raise compliance. *Ev:* MODERATE. *Breaks:* when paired with pressure. *Use:* "Based on what you've told me, the cohort is the right move. Your call; no is fine." *Std:* N2; passes IC.

**C6. Commitment Read.** End-of-call commitment strength predicts follow-through: "I'll try" is weaker than "I will, Monday". *Ev:* STRONG (MI). *Breaks:* individual speaking styles. *Use:* weak commitment plus $2k = refund and chargeback risk; set a start date or offer the Down-Path. *Std:* protects HL5 and IC.

**C7. Speed and short lead time.** Intent decays fast; distant bookings no-show more; reminders help. *Ev:* MODERATE, with STRONG analogs. *Breaks:* over-reminding. *Use:* same-day replies; slots within 0–3 days; reminders at 24 h and 1 h with a 3-minute video. *Std:* permitted.

**C8. Effort-screened entry.** Effort and price raise valuation and filter out low intent. *Ev:* MODERATE for effort; STRONG for price screening [B1]. *Breaks:* long forms. *Use:* 6–8 questions, 18+ gate, BDD and medical flags → decline and refer. *Std:* N7; hard stop for minors (HL5).

**C9. Glass-box transparency.** Spotted tactics get discounted; salient ulterior motives cut sincerity; refutation beats omission. *Ev:* STRONG. *Breaks:* too much negativity [B2]. *Use:* narrate the call, state your stake, answer "adults can't change" head-on. *Std:* the core of IC.

**C10. One-Recommendation Rule.** Overload and deferral rise when preferences are uncertain and alternatives are close. *Ev:* STRONG (conditional). *Breaks:* expert buyers. *Use:* one recommendation; the Down-Path only on disqualification or affordability. *Std:* N8.

**C11. Honest Stakes.** Losses loom larger than gains (λ≈1.3–2.0, moderated), yet loss framing doesn't win overall. Clarity plus stakes he states himself do the work. *Ev:* size CONTESTED; framing null STRONG. *Breaks:* stacked fear; untrue stakes; loss framing aimed at the price. *Use:* "another year of effort with no baseline." *Std:* N3; HL2 and HL4.

**C12. Payment Rhythm.** Paying hurts; usage peaks after each payment then decays; bundles blur what each session cost; plans lift sales mostly among weak-credit buyers. *Ev:* STRONG/MODERATE. *Breaks:* plans for people who can't afford them. *Use:* plans only after intent, with an affordability question; monthly value receipts for pay-in-full clients. *Std:* N8; HL5.

**C13. Honest reference frames.** People budget by category; anchors pull valuations (weakly for willingness to pay). *Ev:* MODERATE–STRONG / CONTESTED. *Breaks:* fake value stacks; surgery anchors. *Use:* frame it as an investment in skills and measurement; anchor on his own spend and your delivery math. *Std:* N8; HL2.

**C14. Future-time illusion.** Future time feels more plentiful than future money; plans underestimate duration (the planning fallacy). *Ev:* MODERATE–STRONG (time slack); STRONG (planning fallacy). *Breaks:* time that really is squeezed (exams). *Use:* concrete weekly minutes; a start on a real landmark; an if-then plan on the call. *Std:* N4 real dates only (HL3).

**C15. Post-purchase lock-in.** Doubt spikes after a costly purchase, and reversible choices satisfy less. *Ev:* MODERATE; post-choice liking CONTESTED. *Breaks:* no refund at all, which grift-fatigued buyers won't accept. *Use:* instant confirmation, 48-h kickoff, a day-7 win, then the Commit Ritual. *Std:* N8 conditional guarantee.

**C16. Supportive accountability.** People adhere for a reviewer who is benevolent, competent and trustworthy; guided beats unguided. *Ev:* MODERATE–STRONG. *Breaks:* surveillance or shame. *Use:* review every log within 48 h; the loop is the product. *Std:* permitted.

**C17. If-then plans + obstacle planning.** Pre-set cues automate starting; mental contrasting targets the likeliest obstacle. *Ev:* existence STRONG; size CONTESTED (d≈0.27–0.65). *Breaks:* too many plans; plans standing in for motivation. *Use:* 2–3 plans plus a miss plan on day 1. *Std:* permitted.

**C18. Habit window.** Automaticity grows and then plateaus: a ~2-month median, a wide spread, and single misses are harmless. *Ev:* MODERATE. *Breaks:* continuous posture behaviours may differ (untested). *Use:* sets program length, the Hold stage and honest timelines. *Std:* IC; N5 with context.

**C19. Two-Track Tracking.** Recorded, reported monitoring works (d≈0.40); appearance checking raises dissatisfaction; photo conditions distort. *Ev:* STRONG / MODERATE. *Breaks:* daily face checks; unstandardized photos presented as proof. *Use:* daily process logs; Same-Photo Standard at fixed checkpoints. *Std:* HL1; HL4 and HL5.

**C20. Stage Gradients.** Effort speeds up near a goal; real head starts help; motivation sags mid-way; focus on the smaller share. *Ev:* MODERATE–STRONG. *Breaks:* fake progress; multi-year horizons. *Use:* 3–4 short stages, each with its own finish line. *Std:* no fake progress (HL1).

**C21. Weekly Wins Ledger.** Felt progress in meaningful work is the top daily motivator, and managers underrate it. *Ev:* MODERATE. *Breaks:* patronizing trivial wins. *Use:* name one real process win per review ("first week with every log in"). *Std:* permitted.

**C22. Choice within structure (SDT).** Autonomy, competence and relatedness make motivation self-driven. *Ev:* STRONG (correlational); interventions show small effects. *Breaks:* "autonomy" meaning no structure. *Use:* he picks his cue times and one of two weekly focuses; every requirement has a reason. *Std:* permitted.

**C23. True groups, process competition.** Shared norms and interaction raise adherence; competition on behaviour beat support in an RCT. *Ev:* MODERATE / STRONG. *Breaks:* comparing appearance; humiliating whoever is last on the leaderboard. *Use:* cohort streak boards on logged days only. *Std:* HL4.

**C24. Comeback Protocol.** Blaming a lapse on a stable flaw predicts relapse; single misses don't derail habits; rewarding returns worked best at scale. *Ev:* MODERATE–STRONG. *Breaks:* normalizing chronic skipping. *Use:* "never miss twice"; a same-day comeback check-in; zero shame. *Std:* HL4.

**C25. Identity through evidence.** People infer identity from their own behaviour; identity-congruent difficulty reads as importance. *Ev:* MODERATE; noun labels CONTESTED. *Breaks:* identity pressure after a lapse. *Use:* tie identity statements to his logs. *Std:* N7.

**C26. Expectation Contract.** Realistic expectations sustain effort; fantasies sap it; inflated expectations predict disappointment and dropout. *Ev:* MODERATE–STRONG. *Breaks:* realism tipping into pessimism. *Use:* written ranges for weeks 2, 6 and 12 (behaviours, measures), plus what is uncertain (structure) and out of scope (medical). *Std:* N5 with context; HL2.

**C27. Alliance Check.** Bond and agreement on goals and tasks predict outcomes; feedback flags off-track clients. *Ev:* STRONG / MODERATE. *Breaks:* polite rating inflation. *Use:* three quick items after each call; any dip triggers repair. *Std:* permitted.

**C28. Peak-End Graduation.** Memory weights peaks and endings; improving sequences raise repurchase. *Ev:* STRONG with caveats / MODERATE. *Breaks:* manufactured peaks. *Use:* the week-12 standardized comparison and a graduation call, with the continuity decision made there. *Std:* N4; N5 with context.

**C29. Transparent defaults.** Defaults shift choices (d≈0.68). *Ev:* STRONG. *Breaks:* hidden renewal → chargebacks, legal exposure, IC failure. *Use:* continuation as the default at graduation, with explicit consent and one-click exit. *Std:* IC; legal flag.

**C30. Win-timed, ask-everyone proof.** People underestimate others' willingness to help; referral intentions exceed actions; solicited reviews are more representative; testimonials written close to the event are more credible; narrative plus numbers moves both intention and belief. *Ev:* MODERATE. *Breaks:* gating, cherry-picking, undisclosed incentives. *Use:* ask every client at fixed checkpoints; referrals through private links. *Std:* HL1; FTC.

## 12. Open questions

1. **Verification.** Check every † figure and DOI in full text; this session could neither search nor fetch.
2. **Niche benchmarks.** No show-rate, close-rate or assessment-conversion data exist for this niche. Instrument them from the first call.
3. **Call vs no-call.** Does a no-call async path convert as well as a call for $1.5–3k among men aged 20–32? A/B test across cohorts.
4. **Habit speed for posture and breathing.** No data. Track automaticity with a 4-item self-report at weeks 0, 4, 8 and 12.
5. **Photo cadence and BDD.** The safe photo interval is unknown. Get clinical input and watch for checking behaviour.
6. **MI-informed vs scripted selling.** Effect on close rate is untested.
7. **Payment plan vs pay-in-full.** Effect on adherence in coaching: only gym and theatre analogs exist.
8. **Referral under stigma.** Discreet referral mechanics are untested.
9. **Continuity uptake at the peak.** B1 assumes 40–50%; unverified.
10. **Voice notes vs text in DMs.** Untested.
11. **Alliance in async group formats.** Does the alliance–outcome link hold there? Most evidence comes from 1:1 workplace coaching.
