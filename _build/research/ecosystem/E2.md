Spot-check ‡ and † figures before they enter LEDGER. Operator figures are labelled **ESTIMATE** or **DERIVED**, with inputs shown.

**Strength legend.**
- **STRONG:** a platform's binding rule text, a meta-analysis or a large field experiment.
- **MODERATE:** a large vendor dataset, a single good study or an authoritative document.
- **WEAK:** a vendor or aggregator claim, a small or undisclosed sample, or dated data.
- **ANECDOTAL:** a practitioner or a single case.
- **CONTESTED:** sources disagree materially.
- **ESTIMATE / DERIVED:** my own modelling.

**Definitions.**
- **Click rate:** unique clickers ÷ delivered.
- **Bulk sender:** Gmail's term for a sender of about 5,000 or more messages a day to personal Gmail accounts from one primary domain.
- **Health-classified:** a Meta data source placed in the restricted health-and-wellness category.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **An owned, authenticated sending domain is day-one infrastructure.**
   - *Evidence:* Gmail requires SPF or DKIM from all senders. Bulk senders also need SPF, DKIM and DMARC, with the From domain aligned, plus one-click unsubscribe. Bulk status is **permanent**. Since **November 2025** Gmail has been ramping up to "temporary and permanent rejections." Outlook has rejected non-compliant bulk mail ("550 5.7.515") since **5 May 2025**, and Yahoo mirrors Gmail. **STRONG.**
   - *Implication:* get a brand domain and an authenticated email service before the first opt-in. A list of about 7,000–10,000 crosses Gmail's bulk line on one send (DERIVED).
2. **Spam complaints set the ceiling, not list size.**
   - *Evidence:* keep complaints **under 0.10%**, never **0.30%**. Above 0.3%, Gmail withholds mitigation until 7 clean days. Gmail asks for unsubscribes to be honored within **48 h** (a condition of mitigation); Yahoo requires **2 days**. beehiiv's platform average is **0.02%**. **STRONG** (rules).
   - *Implication:* an insecure, grift-fatigued audience reports pressure as spam. Make leaving and pausing effortless, and don't pitch dormant segments.
3. **Opens no longer measure people.**
   - *Evidence:* Apple's own text says MPP loads content "in the background, even when you don't open the message." Google: it "doesn't track open rates." **STRONG.**
   - *Implication:* the scoreboard runs on clicks, replies, complaints, unsubscribes, assessments and enrollments. Sunset rules key on clicks and replies.
4. **Realistic newsletter ranges.** **MODERATE‡.**
   - Reported opens: **35–45%** (MPP-inflated).
   - Clicks: **2–5%** of delivered (beehiiv 3.23% in 2025; passion niches **7.7%+**).
   - Unsubscribes: **0.1–0.4% per send**. Bounces: about **1.5%**.
   - *Implication:* plan for 2–5% clicks. Beating that is a hypothesis to test.
5. **Automations earn far more per recipient than broadcasts.**
   - *Evidence:* Klaviyo welcome flows average **4.92%** clicks and **1.97%** orders (top decile **15.7% / 9.89%**) against campaigns at **1.29% / 0.08%**. Flows are about **5% of sends and 41% of email revenue**. **MODERATE‡** (e-commerce).
   - *Implication:* build the welcome sequence off the quiz result first. Polish the newsletter second.
6. **Segment by declared stage and behavior, not signup date.**
   - *Evidence:* same-sender comparisons show segmented sends get **+100.95% clicks** and **+14.31% opens**. Signup-date segments raise complaints. **MODERATE–WEAK‡** (2017, correlational).
   - *Implication:* use **Readiness Tags** (candidate name, §4.2).
7. **Self-assessments out-capture PDFs and segment as they go, but the evidence is vendor-grade.**
   - *Evidence:*
     - Quiz start → lead about **40%**; completion about **65%**†.
     - Quiz pages convert about **5–8%** of visitors, PDF pages **1–4%**‡.
     - The landing-page median is **6.6%** (wellness **8.2%**). Plain-language pages convert **11.1% vs 5.3%**.
   - **WEAK–MODERATE.**
   - *Implication:* the front door assesses **controllables**. It never rates faces.
8. **Meta blocks the obvious ads for this niche.** From Meta's own text (**STRONG**):
   - Health and cosmetic ads must target **18+**.
   - Ads cannot imply or try to generate "negative self-perception."
   - Ads cannot assert or imply "physical or mental health (including medical conditions)."
   - Lead forms cannot ask health questions without Meta's prior permission.
   - Landing pages are reviewed too.
   - *Secondary reading (MODERATE‡, Sep 2026):* cosmetic before/afters are allowed only at 18+, with no "statements of inferiority" and no promise of a specific outcome by a set date without qualifiers.
   - *Implication:* no "Do you have a weak chin?" ads. Host the quiz on your own site. Keep the Standard stricter than Meta on before/afters.
9. **Meta's health-data restrictions can blind optimization.**
   - *Evidence:* since **Jan 2025**, domains Meta categorizes as health and wellness, which it judges by site topic, can lose URL parameters, lower-funnel events or all events. The server-side Conversions API doesn't bypass this. From **2 Sep 2025**, custom conversions that suggest health conditions are restricted. **MODERATE‡.**
   - *Implication:* condition-heavy pages (sleep apnea, TMJ, malocclusion) invite classification. Honest habit-coaching positioning is compliant and practical. Never engineer workarounds; Meta bans circumvention.
10. **Google and YouTube wall off health personalization.** From Google's own text (**STRONG**):
    - "Invasive medical procedures, which includes cosmetic surgery" and "medical devices for sleep apnea" count as health.
    - Personalized ads may not impose negativity, including "body shaming, negativity related to physical attributes."
    - Misrepresentation rules ban "unrealistic claims."
    - *Implication:* retargeting creative must be aspirational. Condition-led ads can lose remarketing and customer-list targeting.
11. **Leads are affordable. Lead-to-client ratios are the unknown.**
    - *Evidence:* Meta US lead campaigns median **CPL $27.66** (2025). Google Search **CPL $66.69** (2026). Booked high-ticket calls **$80–450**†. **MODERATE‡.**
    - *DERIVED:* paid CAC per $1.9–2.4k client spans roughly **$100–6,000**, depending on two unmeasured rates.
    - *Implication:* ads amplify a funnel that already converts organically. They never originate one.
12. **At solo scale, ad ROI can't be proven, so triangulate.** Field evidence (**STRONG**):
    - Across 15 Facebook RCTs, observational estimates were off by **3× or more in half**, mostly overstating lift.
    - eBay's brand-keyword ads showed "no measurable short-term benefits."
    - Across 25 experiments, the median ROI confidence interval was over **100 points wide**.
    - Retargeting lift is real but modest: **+10.5% purchases** in one campaign.
    - *Implication:* judge ads on blended CAC from your own records, on/off pulses and holdouts. Never on platform ROAS.
13. **Self-reported attribution is the best free signal.**
    - *Evidence:* in one B2B vendor study, software credited **79%** of conversions to search while buyers named search about **3%** of the time. **WEAK‡.** The mechanism is robust.
    - *Implication:* ask an open-text "Where did you first hear about us, and what made you act now?" at the quiz and at checkout.
14. **UTMs survive Apple's link protection; click IDs don't.**
    - *Evidence:* iOS 17+ strips gclid and fbclid in Mail, Messages and Private Browsing, and leaves UTMs alone. **MODERATE‡.**
    - *Implication:* tag every owned link.
15. **AI answers shrink search clicks. The value moves to being cited and to branded demand.** **STRONG** (Pew); **MODERATE‡** (vendors); magnitude CONTESTED.
    - *Pew:* with an AI summary on the page, people clicked a traditional result on **8%** of visits vs **15%** without, and clicked inside the summary on **1%**.
    - *Ahrefs:* position-1 CTR fell **34.5%**, and later updates report about **58%**.
    - *Amsive:* branded queries **gained 18.7%** CTR when an AI Overview appeared.
    - *Implication:* skip head-term blogging. Build a brand hub, experience-based long-tail pages, and YouTube search.
16. **"Search everywhere" complements Google; it doesn't replace it.** **WEAK–MODERATE‡.**
    - **49%** of US consumers used TikTok for search in 2026 (Gen Z **65%**), but the share of Gen Z who *prefer* it to Google fell from **8% to 4%**.
    - LLM referrals are under **1–2% of sessions**, and AI assistants cite Reddit and YouTube heavily but volatilely.
    - *Implication:* answer real questions on YouTube, keep one consistent brand entity, and participate honestly on Reddit.
17. **YMYL plus no credentials means competing on Experience, not Expertise.**
    - *Evidence:* Google applies stronger scrutiny to "health or safety" topics. Its March 2024 spam policies target **scaled content abuse**. **STRONG** (text); **MODERATE** (as ranking inference).
    - *Implication:* publish first-hand process, methodology and refer-out pages. Never mass-produce AI articles.
18. **Minors shape every channel.**
    - *Evidence:* Meta requires 18+ health and cosmetic targeting, and Google disables under-18 personalization. **STRONG.**
    - *Implication:* ask age first in the quiz, and run an **Education-Only Lane** (candidate name) that is excluded from all sales email. This is also a Standard hard line.

## 2. Email mechanics & benchmarks

### 2.1 The metric stack changed: opens are now a machine signal

- **The mechanism.** Apple's Mail UI, current through macOS 26.1, says Mail Privacy Protection "works by hiding your IP address and loading remote content privately in the background, even when you don't open the message." Every pixel in that client fires whether or not a human read the email. **STRONG.**
  - Google says it "doesn't track open rates," "can't verify the accuracy of open rates reported by third parties," and that "low open rates aren't necessarily an accurate indicator of deliverability." **STRONG.**
- **What survives.** **MODERATE–WEAK‡.**
  - Clicks need a human tap.
  - Replies, unsubscribes and complaints are human acts.
  - Purchases happen on your own site.
  - A secondary guide warns that Apple's link protection may erode clicks next.
  - Two corollaries follow:
    - Subject-line tests keyed on opens measure Apple's prefetch.
    - A "non-opener" sunset rule deletes real readers who block images and keeps phantom openers.
- **Exposure varies.** Apple-client share varies by list; one guide cites 40–60%+ for consumer lists, which is **WEAK**. iPhone ownership is not MPP exposure, because Gmail-app users aren't proxied.
  - *Implication:* read your client mix in the email service, and report opens only as a within-list trend.

**Candidate framework: Human-Signal Scoreboard.**

| What it tells you | Metric |
|---|---|
| Interest | Clicks per delivered |
| Relationship | Replies |
| Tolerance | Unsubscribes, complaints |
| Money | Assessments or enrollments per 1,000 delivered |

Opens appear only as a footnote.

### 2.2 Benchmarks: what "normal" looks like in 2025–26

| Metric | Creator newsletters (beehiiv 2025, 28B emails) | All industries (Mailchimp, Dec 2023)‡ | E-com campaigns (Klaviyo 2025, avg / top 10%)‡ | Read for this niche |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reported open rate | 41.24% (37.67% in 2024) | 35.6% | 37.93% / 54.78% | Directional only |
| Click rate | 3.23% (4.74% in 2024); top categories 7.7%+ | 2.62% | 1.29% / 4.74% | Plan for **2–5%** |
| Unsubscribe per send | — | — | 0.35% / 0.11% | Plan for **0.1–0.4%** |
| Spam complaints | 0.02% (0.04% in 2024) | — | — | Keep under 0.1% |
| Bounce / delivery | 1.48% / 98.90% | — | — | Bounces over 2% mean a hygiene problem |

Strength: **MODERATE**. These are large first-party datasets, but audiences and definitions differ. beehiiv attributes its click decline to readers "consum[ing] content within the email itself."

- **Flows (Klaviyo 2025, e-commerce)‡.**

  | Flow | Open | Click | Placed order |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All flows | 48.6% | 4.67% | 1.42% |
  | Welcome (average) | 51.3% | 4.92% | 1.97% |
  | Welcome (top decile) | 74.2% | 15.7% | 9.89% |

  - GetResponse (2024) reports welcome-email opens of 83.6%.
  - **MODERATE** for direction; the absolute values don't transfer to coaching.
- **Derived attrition.** At 4–13 sends a month and 0.1–0.4% unsubscribes per send, the list loses about **0.4–5% a month** to unsubscribes alone (DERIVED).
  - *Implication:* weekly list growth is a KPI, consistent with B2.

### 2.3 Frequency, format and the letter-style email

- **Frequency.**
  - *Evidence:* successful beehiiv creators send **1–3 times a week**. Tuesday and Wednesday, and early morning, perform best; overnight is worst. **MODERATE‡** (observational).
  - *Implication:* one weekly letter, plus automations, plus offer mentions tied to *real* dates (cohort starts, credit windows).
- **Lead with the point.**
  - *Evidence:* in an NN/g eye-tracking study, **67%** of readers made zero fixations inside newsletter intros. **WEAK** (2006, 42 people).
  - *Implication:* the useful idea goes in the first two lines.
- **Personalize by stage, not by name.**
  - *Evidence:* in one Litmus test, first-name personalization gained **5 points** of opens and lost **50%** of conversion. **WEAK‡.**
- **Plain text vs designed.**
  - *Evidence:* no current controlled study was found, and the old vendor tests predate MPP (**WEAK**).
  - The mechanism favors a *letter-style* email with light HTML: one voice, one call to action, few images, and a visible unsubscribe link. It fits an operator-fronted brand, renders reliably, avoids proxy-cached images, and meets Gmail's formatting rules (valid HTML, no hidden content, "visible and easy to understand" links). **ANECDOTAL/ESTIMATE.**
- **Display names and subjects are rule-bound.** Gmail's 2025 display-name guidelines tell senders not to use "TIME IS RUNNING OUT," "URGENT REQUEST" and "Last Chance," the recipient's name, fake verification emoji, and fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subjects. **STRONG.**
  - *Implication:* use "First name | Brand." These rules and the Standard's ban on fake urgency point the same way.

### 2.4 List growth and list-value anchors

- **Newsletter growth.**
  - *Evidence:* the median 2025 beehiiv newsletter had **482 subscribers in month 1** and **8,314 at 12 months**, with **66 days to first revenue**. beehiiv's Recommendations growth tool was associated with **2.75× faster** growth. **MODERATE** (survivorship).
  - *Implication:* treat these as a ceiling reference. Cross-promotion falls under "collabs" and is out of scope.
- **YouTube → email.**
  - *Evidence:* no benchmark exists. B2 and E1 carry **0.2–1% of views** (ANECDOTAL).
  - *My derivation (ESTIMATE):* 0.5–2% of viewers click a description or pinned link, and 25–50% of warm clickers opt in. That gives **1–10 subscribers per 1,000 views**. The low end holds with one generic spoken call to action; the high end when the resource matches that video's exact problem.
- **Short-form and Instagram → email.**
  - *Evidence:* keyword automations report **80–90%** opens and **25–45%** clicks (vendor†, WEAK). E1's **Keyword Door** handles the route.
  - *Implication:* measure opt-ins per 1,000 engaged views.
- **Email-to-purchase anchors.**
  - *Evidence:*
    - Paid-newsletter free-to-paid is **0.62%** median (about $10 a month; beehiiv‡, MODERATE).
    - Klaviyo campaigns place orders at **0.08% average and 0.44% top-decile** per recipient‡.
    - B2 carries **1–3% per campaign for $50–200 products** (WEAK).
    - There is no benchmark for $1.5–3k coaching (§4.3).

## 3. Deliverability 2024–2026

### 3.1 The three mailbox providers' rules

| Requirement | Gmail (personal accounts) | Yahoo | Outlook.com (consumer) | Solo-operator action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who is "bulk" | About 5,000+/day to Gmail from one primary domain (subdomains pooled); **permanent once reached** | "Bulk senders" (no number in the text) | Domains sending over 5,000/day | Assume bulk from the first big send |
| All senders | SPF **or** DKIM; valid PTR; TLS; RFC 5322; spam under 0.3%; don't impersonate gmail.com | SPF or DKIM; spam under 0.3%; PTR; RFC 5321/5322 | Authentication "benefits all senders" | Custom domain + authenticated email service |
| Bulk authentication | SPF **and** DKIM; DMARC (p=none OK); aligned From; DKIM ≥1024-bit (2048 recommended) | SPF & DKIM; DMARC ≥ p=none, aligned | SPF, DKIM pass; DMARC ≥ p=none aligned | DMARC with reports from day one |
| Unsubscribe | RFC 8058 one-click headers (mailto doesn't count) **plus** a visible body link | One-click (POST "highly recommended") + body link | "Functional," visible link | Email service handles headers; add a preferences page |
| Unsubscribe speed | Within **48 h** recommended (missing it forfeits mitigation) | Honor within **2 days** | — | Automate |
| Spam threshold | **<0.10%** target; never **≥0.30%**; calculated daily | **<0.3%** | Hygiene, bounce management | Monitor daily around sends |
| Enforcement | Gradual since Feb 2024; mitigation withheld for spam >0.3%, no DMARC, no one-click, or late unsubscribes. **Nov 2025:** temporary and permanent rejections. New domains on an "accelerated timetable" | Effective first half of 2024 | **5 May 2025:** rejects with "550; 5.7.515" | Warm new domains slowly |
| Monitoring | Postmaster Tools (compliance dashboard) | Complaint Feedback Loop | SNDS (new service in 2026) | Check weekly |

Strength: **STRONG** for all three (each provider's own text, archived).

Other 2025–26 changes (**STRONG**):
- Gmail added display-name rules in April 2025.
- In March 2026, Gmail clarified that quotas apply **per IP and per authenticated domain**. After error **4.7.28**, wait 10 minutes, then resume on a single connection.

### 3.2 What the rules mean at solo scale

- **Bulk-line arithmetic (DERIVED).** A list of *N* sends *N × s* messages to Gmail, where *s* is the Gmail share. The 5,000 line is crossed at *N* = 5,000 ÷ *s*: about **7,100** at s = 0.7, and **10,000** at s = 0.5. These are illustrative; read your own domain mix.
  - *Implication:* the operator becomes a permanent bulk sender early in Year 1, so build as if already there.
- **Complaint Budget (candidate framework, DERIVED).** With 6,000 Gmail recipients on one send day, **6 complaints** reach 0.1% and **18** reach 0.3%.
  - *Implication:* send promotions only to recent clickers. Show new subscribers an unsubscribe link at the top as well as the bottom. Never re-add people who unsubscribed.
- **Separate mail streams (Google, STRONG).** Keep one From address per category, and don't put promotions in receipts.
  - *Implication:* receipts come from the payment processor. Newsletters come from one reply-capable "Name | Brand" address, which Microsoft also recommends.

### 3.3 Hygiene, confirmation and sunsetting under MPP

- **Google's guidance (STRONG).**
  - "Confirm each recipient's email address before subscribing them."
  - Periodically re-confirm, and consider unsubscribing people who don't read.
  - Never buy lists or use pre-checked opt-ins.
  - Easy opt-out "can improve open rates, click-through rates, and sending efficiency."
- **Double opt-in trade-off.** **WEAK/ANECDOTAL.**
  - *Evidence:* old Mailchimp data (about 2011, around 30,000 accounts) found double-opt-in lists got **+72% opens** and **+114% clicks**. The usual confirmation loss is **20–30%**, a heuristic.
  - *Implication:* use double opt-in for paid-social leads. Single opt-in plus an immediate reply ask is fine for warm YouTube traffic. Measure both.
- **Sunset on human signals (ESTIMATE).** After **60–120 days with no click or reply**, send one re-permission email, then suppress. Never sunset on opens.

### 3.4 Platform rules and the Standard converge

Gmail's guidelines rule out fake "Re:" and "Fwd:" subjects, urgency-bait display names, hidden content and misleading links. Microsoft asks for accurate subjects and consented recipients. Every fake-urgency trick the Standard forbids is also a deliverability liability, so compliance and trust are one system. **STRONG** (rules) / DERIVED (convergence).

## 4. Sequences & segmentation

### 4.1 Welcome and onboarding sequence

**Evidence.**
- Welcome flows beat campaigns by about **4× on clicks and about 25× on order rate** (Klaviyo‡, DERIVED from 4.92/1.29 and 1.97/0.08). **MODERATE** (direction), **WEAK** (transfer to coaching).
- Google: "messages sent from an address in the recipient's contacts are less likely to be marked as spam" (**STRONG**). Replies are the natural way to earn that relationship; this last step is my inference.

**Candidate pattern: Result-First Welcome.** An evergreen sequence, not a launch, running about 14 days.

| Email | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Immediately | The result and what it means. One reply question: "What have you tried, for how long, and where did it stall?" |
| E1 | ~Day 1 | The stall mechanism for their pattern |
| E2 | ~Day 3 | How progress is honestly measured (controllables, standardized photos). Process, not technique |
| E3 | ~Day 5 | Proof with context: who, how long, what else changed, typical range |
| E4 | ~Day 7 | The paid assessment: what it is and isn't (no diagnosis), its price, and the real credit window |
| E5 | ~Day 10 | Objections: adult-change skepticism, grift fatigue, cost |
| E6 | ~Day 13–14 | A real deadline reminder, plus the lower-priced Down-Path |

After that, the subscriber moves to the weekly letter. Timings are ESTIMATES. Each email carries one link and is scored on clicks, replies and assessment purchases.

### 4.2 Readiness Tags: segmentation by buyer stage

| Tag family | Values | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Declared stage | DIY time: under 6 mo / **6–24 mo (Self-Taught Struggler)** / over 24 mo; main concern (profile/chin, jawline, mouth breathing, general); belief (skeptical / hopeful) | Quiz | Branch the welcome and newsletter examples |
| Age band | under 18 / 18–24 / 25–32 / 33+ | First quiz question | **under 18 → Education-Only Lane**, no sales email ever |
| Refer-out flags | Pain; sleep or breathing symptoms; recent orthodontic work | Quiz screen | Safety email ("see a dentist/doctor"); hold sales until acknowledged |
| Behavior | Evaluator (clicked pricing); Engaged (3+ clicks in 30 days); Dormant (no click or reply in 60–120 days) | Email-service data | Who gets offers; who gets re-permission |
| Transaction | Assessment buyer / client / graduate / refunded | Checkout | Suppress pitches; move to onboarding or alumni |

Segments help when they are *relevant*, and signup-date or lapsed-activity segments raised complaints (Mailchimp 2017‡). **MODERATE–WEAK.**

**Privacy constraint (STRONG, Meta text).** Symptom answers are health-adjacent. Keep them in the email service and never in ad pixels, URLs or event names. Meta's Business Tools terms prohibit sending health information (§6.2).

### 4.3 Email-to-call and email-to-purchase: known vs unknown

**Known.**
- Call show rates: about **81%** for next-day bookings, falling to about **60%** at 14 days (Chili Piper†, MODERATE).
- Qualified high-ticket close rates: **15–35%**† (WEAK).
- Welcome-flow order rates: **2–10%** at low prices‡.
- Paid-newsletter conversion: **0.62%** median‡.

**Unknown.** There is no public benchmark for list-driven sales of $150–250 assessments or $1.5–3k programs.

**Planning ESTIMATES** (replace within 60–90 days):

| Step | Planning range | Moves up when… | Moves down when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiz lead → paid assessment (60 days) | **2–6%** | Struggler tag, YouTube source, credit window stated | Cold paid traffic; cash-constrained under-25s |
| Paid assessment → program | **25–50%** (B1) | Assessment within 0–3 days; clear plan | Slow scheduling; weak fit |
| Call invite → booked (Evaluator segment) | **1–4%** of recipients | Private tier; next-day slots | "Free strategy call" framing |
| Booked → held | **60–81%**† | Next-day slots; reminders | 14-day lead times |

Measure every ratio per source through UTMs and the self-reported attribution field (§8).

### 4.4 Funnel math (DERIVED, illustrative)

**Worked example:** 20 enrollments a month (inside B1's 150–400 clients a year), all via the paid assessment.
- At 35% assessment-to-program: about **57 assessments a month**.
- At 4% lead-to-assessment: about **1,430 leads a month**.
- At 5 subscribers per 1,000 views: about **290,000 long-form views a month**.

**Sensitivity:** at 6% and 50% the view requirement falls to about **130,000**; at 2% and 25% it rises to about **800,000**.

**Implication:** the conversion ratios, not traffic, are the first lever. Direct enrollment for warm viewers (B1) shortens the path.

### 5.1 Format evidence

| Email-sourced traffic | Visitor → conversion | ~19% | Unbounce† | MODERATE |
| Reading level 5th–7th vs professional | Conversion | 11.1% vs 5.3% (health pages 10.8%†) | Unbounce‡ | MODERATE |

**Synthesis.** Interactive formats win on capture and on data (stage, age, flags). The figures are mostly vendor-made, and their denominators differ. **WEAK–MODERATE.**

### 5.2 The self-assessment as magnet and router: design constraints

1. **Controllables only.** It assesses history, habits, what was tried and for how long, and photo readiness. It never scores faces or reads structure (B1's Anti-Rating Positioning; Standard hard lines).
2. **Age question first**, which routes under-18s to the Education-Only Lane.
3. **Refer-out screening** feeds a safety email.
4. **The results page** states the stall pattern, one next step, and the paid assessment with its price. Direct selling is allowed under the Standard.
5. **Host it on the owned domain, not in Meta lead forms**, which cannot ask health questions (STRONG). Answers stay out of pixels.
6. **Plain language.** The reading-level effect is among the sturdier benchmark findings.

### 5.3 Channel routes into the list

Platform mechanics are covered in E1.
- **YouTube:** spoken call to action, a description link and a pinned link, all UTM-tagged per video. Planning range: **1–10 subscribers per 1,000 views** (ESTIMATE).
- **Shorts, Reels and TikTok:** profile link, or the E1 Keyword Door. Automation hands off to a human and never sells to unverified ages.
- **Instagram Stories:** link sticker to the quiz.
- **X:** profile link or a link in the first reply. No benchmark exists.

## 6. Paid ads (policy, economics, when and when not)

### 6.1 Policy map

| Platform | Rule (from platform text unless marked) | Strength | Niche consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta | "Dietary, health or weight loss or weight gain" and "cosmetic products, procedures or surgeries" ads must target **18+** | STRONG | Paid reach starts at 18, matching the paid-offer floor |
| Meta | No "content implying or attempting to generate negative self-perception" to promote health products | STRONG | No insecurity hooks ("tired of your side profile?") |
| Meta | No content that "asserts or implies personal attributes… physical or mental health (including medical conditions)" | STRONG | No "Do you mouth-breathe / snore / have a recessed chin?" |
| Meta | No ads "meant to degrade or shame" people | STRONG | No circled strangers' faces or celebrity jaw roasts |
| Meta | Lead ads may not request health information without permission | STRONG | Quiz on the owned site |

| Meta | Cosmetic before/afters allowed at 18+ with no "statements of inferiority"; no clickbait or outcome-by-timeframe promises without qualifiers; rule "revised more than once" | MODERATE‡ (Sep 2026) | Allowed isn't wise: the Standard's matched-conditions rule plus grift fatigue favor process proof |
| Meta | 2026 review is multimodal; indirect second-person framing ("for guys dealing with…") treated as direct | WEAK‡ (one vendor blog) | Write in the third person or educationally |
| Google / YouTube | Misrepresentation examples include "misleading or unrealistic claims regarding weight loss or financial gain" | STRONG | No outcome timelines or structural promises |

| Google / YouTube | Under-18s: personalization off, sensitive categories restricted; remarketing lists can't be "overly narrow" | STRONG | Small retargeting pools may not serve |

### 6.2 Meta's health-and-wellness data restrictions

**Evidence.** Meta assigns "data source categories" by a site's topics and offerings. Health and wellness covers "medical conditions or health statuses, provider and patient relationships… health-related products and services."
- Restricted sources get one or more of:
  - **Core setup**, which strips parameters and everything after the domain;
  - blocks on "specific mid and lower funnel" events;
  - **full restrictions**.
- Custom events are blocked until reviewed. Repeated prohibited data can hold a dataset in Core setup for at least 90 days.
- The Conversions API is covered too.
- From **2 Sep 2025**, custom conversions that suggest health conditions are proactively restricted, and flagged audiences (and their lookalikes) can't serve new campaigns.
- Practitioners date the rollout to **mid-January 2025**. In their reading, symptom language was tolerated while condition names and numeric health statuses triggered classification.

Strength: **MODERATE‡** (secondary guide linking each Meta help page) / ANECDOTAL (practitioner).

**Implications.**
- A domain that reads as a sleep-apnea or TMJ site may be limited to click-optimization, which finds clickers, not buyers.
- The fix is **accurate positioning**, not evasion: habits, measurement and adherence coaching that refers medical questions out. A no-credential operator must say this anyway.
- Keep health detail out of URLs, parameters, event names and audience names, and check Events Manager's data categories before spending.

**Candidate rule: Condition-Free Copy Rule.** Ads and ad-destination pages describe habits, process and experiences, never conditions or diagnoses. Refer-out language lives in the post-opt-in safety email and on the policy page.

### 6.3 Economics: benchmarks and derived CAC

| Input | Range | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Meta US lead campaigns (2025 medians) | CTR 2.59%, CPC $1.92, lead-form conversion 7.72%, **CPL $27.66** (+21% YoY); traffic campaigns CTR 1.71%, CPC $0.70 | MODERATE‡ |
| Meta e-commerce (2025, ~35k brands) | CPM $14.19, CTR 2.19%, CPA $38.19 (Health $38.55) | MODERATE‡ (different funnel) |
| Google Search (2026, 13,474 campaigns) | CTR 6.64%, CPC $5.42, conversion 8.18%, **CPL $66.69**; Health & Fitness CPC $6.17; CPL Health & Fitness $82.27, Beauty $61.56 (2024) | MODERATE‡ |
| Paid booked high-ticket call | $80–450 observed (A$40–220 benchmark) | WEAK† |
| Quiz ad case | $0.21 per completed quiz | ANECDOTAL‡ |

**DERIVED CAC per program client.** Gross CAC = CPL ÷ (lead→assessment × assessment→program).

| Case | CPL | Lead→assessment | Assessment→program | Gross CAC | Net of assessment fees |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best | $3 | 6% | 50% | **~$100** | Negative |
| Middle | $10 | 4% | 35% | **~$714** | ~$140 (after ~2.9 × ~$200) |
| Worst | $30 | 2% | 25% | **~$6,000** | ~$5,200 |

- *Where CPL comes from (DERIVED):* $3–12 is the $0.70–1.92 CPC divided by an assumed 15–28% quiz capture (ESTIMATE). $28 is the lead-form median.

**Candidate framework: Self-Liquidating Assessment.** In the middle case, the $150–250 assessment fee recovers most ad spend. The cost is 0.5–0.75 delivery hours per assessment (B1).

### 6.4 When to run ads, and when not to

**Candidate framework: Three Ad Gates.** All three must be open.
1. **Proof.** The organic path has converted for **60–90 days**, with measured ratios and consented, contextualized outcomes. **ESTIMATE.**
2. **Economics.** Net CAC is at most about **20–30% of program price**, with fast cash payback. **ESTIMATE.**
3. **Compliance and measurement.** Creative passes §6.1 *and* the Standard; the domain isn't condition-classified; UTMs, a self-reported attribution field and a pulse or holdout plan exist; and there are **3–6+ genuinely different concepts**.

**Don't run ads when:**
- conversion ratios are unknown;
- the only strong hook is negative-attribute or shock before/after;
- under-18s dominate the audience;
- the only offer is a $97–297 Down-Path (B1's S3 needed about $60–90 blended CAC);
- or you can't spare **2–4 hours a week** for creative and review (ESTIMATE).

**Three good uses:**
1. **Amplify** proven organic winners (promote top long-form videos; boost top Reels to 18+).
2. **Retarget** warm viewers to the quiz.
3. **Research** messages. Hook and CTR tests need impressions, not conversions, so they're measurable at small budgets, even though ROI isn't (Lewis & Rao).

### 6.5 Retargeting

**Evidence.**
- In a ghost-ads experiment, retargeting lifted visits **17.2%** and purchases **10.5%**, far below platform claims. **STRONG‡** (single campaign).
- Retargeting is the most credit-inflated line in any dashboard. **MODERATE‡.**

**Implications.**
- Prefer on-platform engagement audiences (50%+ video viewers, profile engagers) over pixel audiences, and name them neutrally.
- Hold out **5–10%** to measure lift (heuristic‡).
- Google's narrow-list and health rules may leave small YouTube pools unservable.
- Creative: "what 12 tracked weeks look like," not "still stuck with that profile?"

### 6.6 Creative rules for this niche

**Mechanism.** Meta's retrieval system (Andromeda, global rollout October 2025) uses the **creative itself as the main targeting signal** and treats near-duplicates as one concept. **MODERATE‡.**

Practitioner heuristics (**WEAK‡**): a 3-second hook rate of **25–35%** is strong, a hold rate of **40–50%** is average, and concepts need refreshing every **30–45 days**.

**What works:**
- The operator on camera, one myth per ad.
- Process demonstrations (measurement, check-ins).
- Testimonials with context and disclosure.
- Anti-grift contrasts aimed at ideas, never faces.
- Public prices.

**What fails:** attribute callouts, statements of inferiority, timeframe promises, unmatched before/afters, structural or medical claims, and clinic-like styling that implies credentials.

### 6.7 Budget ranges

**Learning-phase math.**
- Meta's learning phase needs about **50 optimization events per ad set per 7 days** (Meta help, recalled; corroborated‡, MODERATE).
- At a $5–15 CPL, one lead-optimized ad set needs **$250–750 a week (about $1,100–3,300 a month)**.
- Optimizing for assessment purchases (CPA $50–1,500) would need **$2,500–75,000 a week**, which is infeasible.
- So optimize on leads, and judge on assessments from your own data. **DERIVED.**

**Default plan (ESTIMATE):**
- A **$1–3k a month test for 6–8 weeks**, once the gates are open.
- Retargeting capped by pool size, watching for frequency over about 3‡.
- Scale in steps of 20% or less, since bigger budget changes can reset learning‡. Scale only while net CAC holds.

## 7. Website & SEO in 2026

### 7.1 The owned hub's six jobs

1. **Deliverability.** A custom domain is needed for DMARC alignment (§3). STRONG.
2. **Conversion.** Quiz, assessment checkout, application, program pages and public prices, with the page matching the ad. STRONG.
3. **Trust.** About (no implied credentials), methodology, "who this is not for," refer-out policy, and an outcome library with context. YMYL signals, MODERATE.
4. **Measurement.** UTMs, first-party records and the self-reported attribution field (§8).
5. **Resilience.** Immune to platform bans and algorithm resets (B2).
6. **Search and AI legibility.** One entity name, clear claims, question-shaped pages.

### 7.2 Landing pages

- **Plain language:** 5th–7th-grade copy converted **11.1% vs 5.3%**. MODERATE‡.
- **Mobile first:** mobile converts about 8% worse on about 5× the traffic. MODERATE‡.
- **Speed:** 0.1 seconds faster lifted retail conversions **8.4%** (Google/Deloitte 2020). MODERATE, sponsored and dated.
- **Message match per door:** required by Meta's relevance rule. STRONG.
- **One call to action per page:** practitioner consensus. WEAK.
- *Implication:* one quiz page per major door, each UTM-tagged and each echoing that door's promise, with the same claim ladder as the ad.

### 7.3 AI Overviews and the click squeeze

| Finding | Number | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clicks with vs without an AI summary | **8% vs 15%** of visits; 1% clicked inside the summary; 26% vs 16% ended the session | Pew (900 adults, 68,879 searches, Mar 2025) | STRONG |
| Searches showing an AI summary | ~18% (Pew, Mar 2025); 6.5% → ~25% → ~16% over 2025 (Semrush) | Pew; Semrush‡ | STRONG / MODERATE |

**Synthesis.** Google informational clicks are shrinking, by a contested amount. Being *cited*, and being *searched by name*, both move in the operator's favor.

### 7.4 YMYL, E-E-A-T and spam policy: the no-credential constraint

- **Scrutiny.** Google gives "health or safety" topics stronger scrutiny. It says E-E-A-T is not itself a ranking factor, but its systems favor content that shows experience, expertise and trust. **STRONG** (Google text); MODERATE as ranking inference.
- **Spam policy.** The March 2024 update added policies against **scaled content abuse**, expired domain abuse and site reputation abuse. **STRONG.**
- *Implication:* head terms are held by dental and medical publishers, Reddit and YouTube, and AI answers summarize the consensus.
  - The operator can't out-*expert* them, and must never imply credentials.
  - The operator can own **Experience**: first-hand client-process documentation, measurement methodology, and honest "what's plausible for adults" pages that cite the literature.
  - Citing statistics, quotations and sources raised generative visibility by up to **40%** in the GEO benchmark.
  - Mass AI articles are both a spam-policy risk and a brand risk.

### 7.5 Niche strategy: Brand-Demand SEO and the Search-Everywhere Map

**Candidate framework: Brand-Demand SEO.** Target three query types, in order:
1. **Branded** ("brand," "operator name," "brand reviews," "brand price"). These *gain* CTR under AI answers‡ and grow with YouTube reach.
2. **Experience long-tail.** Stall stories, realistic timelines, how to measure, how to choose help.
3. **Evaluation** ("is [approach] legit," "[brand] vs DIY").

Head informational terms go to YouTube. No independent study isolates "topical authority" as a ranking cause (practitioner-level, WEAK). Size the page cluster to the *real* question set that quiz answers and comments reveal, not to a page-count target.

**Candidate framework: Search-Everywhere Map.**

| TikTok search | Discovery for under-30s | 49% searched on TikTok in 2026, but only 4% of Gen Z prefer it to Google, down from 8% (Adobe‡, WEAK) |

### 7.6 Visibility in AI assistants (brief)

**Data.** **MODERATE‡.**
- ChatGPT reported **800M weekly users** in October 2025.
- AI referrals to top sites reached **1.13B visits** in June 2025, up 357%.
- LLM referrals are still **under 1–2% of sessions** (13-month GA4 study), and their conversion rate is disputed.
- Citations over-index on Reddit, Wikipedia and YouTube. **Reddit's share of ChatGPT citations fell from about 60% to about 10% within about 6 weeks**.

**Implication.** For the first 9 months, AI visibility is a by-product, not a channel. The inputs are:
- one consistent brand entity everywhere;
- About and methodology pages that state what the brand does and doesn't do;
- question-shaped FAQ pages with contextualized claims and citations;
- YouTube transcripts and chapters;
- third-party mentions.

Track branded search, and "where did you hear" answers that mention AI.

## 8. Attribution for a solo operator

### 8.1 Why platform numbers mislead

| Study | Finding | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky (2019): 15 Facebook RCTs | Observational methods "frequently fail" to recover RCT lift. In **half the studies they were off by 3× or more**, mostly overestimating (one: true 2.4% vs estimated 1,306%). No method dominated. Better on registration and page-view outcomes than on purchases | STRONG |
| Blake, Nosko & Tadelis (2015), eBay | Returns "a fraction of conventional non-experimental estimates"; brand keywords "no measurable short-term benefits"; non-brand returns negative on average. Naive ROI +4,173% vs experimental −63%‡ | STRONG / MODERATE‡ |
| Lewis & Rao (2015), 25 experiments | Median ROI confidence interval over 100 points wide | STRONG‡ |
| Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer (2017), ghost ads | Retargeting +17.2% visits, +10.5% purchases (one campaign) | STRONG‡ (single) |
| Refine Labs (B2B, 620 conversions) | Software gave 79% to search; buyers said ~3%; 98% of revenue from "dark social" | WEAK‡ |

**More erosion.**
- Apple strips click IDs in Mail, Messages and Private Browsing, while UTMs survive; Safari 26 blocks fingerprinting by default (MODERATE‡).
- Meta reportedly retired its 7-day and 28-day view-through windows in January 2026 (WEAK–MODERATE‡).
- Health-classified domains can't report lower-funnel events to Meta (§6.2).

### 8.2 The Triangulated Attribution stack (candidate framework)

1. **UTMs on every owned link:** descriptions, pinned comments, bio, story stickers, emails and ads.
2. **Self-reported attribution in open text** at the quiz and at checkout: "Where did you first hear about us?" and "What made you act now?"
   - Its limits (SparkToro): it can't reveal channels you aren't on, people conflate first and last touch, and famous channels get over-named.
   - Open text beats dropdowns, which anchor answers‡.
3. **First-party records as truth.** The email address joins quiz, assessment, program and revenue by source.
4. **Pulses and holdouts for paid.** Turn spend on and off for 2–4 weeks and watch blended assessments; hold out 5–10% of retargeting‡.
5. **Disagreement is information.** Software measures what *captured* demand; self-report measures what *created* it.

### 8.3 UTM convention (ESTIMATE)

| Parameter | Values |
|---|---|
| `utm_source` | youtube / instagram / tiktok / x / email / meta-ads / google-ads |
| `utm_medium` | description / pinned-comment / bio / story / dm-keyword / newsletter / welcome / paid-social / paid-video |
| `utm_campaign` | asset slug (e.g. `lf-2026-09-adult-change-myths`) |
| `utm_content` | placement or call-to-action variant |

Use lowercase and keep one sheet of approved values.

### 8.4 One-Page Scoreboard (candidate framework)

**Weekly (15 minutes):**
- Views by platform.
- New subscribers by source, and subscribers per 1,000 views.
- Quiz starts, finishes and leads.
- Assessments and enrollments by source.
- Cash collected.
- Email clicks per delivered, replies, unsubscribe % and complaint %.
- If ads run: spend, CPL and blended cost per assessment.

**Monthly:** a source cohort table (lead → assessment in 60 days → program in 90 days → revenue per lead), with self-reported attribution tallies beside the UTM tallies.

**Quarterly:** one pulse test, one sunset pass, and one Meta data-category check.
