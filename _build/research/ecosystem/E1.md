## 1. Executive findings

1. **Wording decides distribution more than topic does.**
   - Meta's Community Standards (Sep 2026) restrict three kinds of content to adults 18+:
     - "statements of inferiority about physical appearance";
     - cosmetic-procedure content "with the intention to treat, or restore function or structure of people's faces or bodies";
     - health clickbait with "promises of specific health-related results within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers" [P, STRONG].
   - *Implication:* "Fix your recessed chin in 90 days" gets age-gated. "What 18 stalled months usually have in common" does not.
   - Candidate framework: **Visibility Tax Ladder** — every rung up the claim ladder costs reach before it costs trust.
2. **Comparison and rating content is the niche's most policy-exposed format, on every platform.**
   - TikTok bans "harmful body comparisons" and "degrading remarks about appearance" [P].
   - Meta protects private adults and minors from content that circles or highlights their features or "ranks them based on physical appearance" [P].
   - YouTube's harassment examples include "Look at this creature's teeth…" [P]. It also limits repeated teen recommendations of videos that compare physical features (†, MODERATE).
   - *Implication:* "never circle strangers' flaws" is a distribution rule as well as an ethical one. Analysis content uses consenting clients compared with their own past, never rankings of people.
3. **YouTube's medical-misinformation policy now has two categories: prevention and treatment misinformation** that contradicts local health-authority guidance [P, STRONG].
   - The standalone COVID-19 rules are retired.
   - The policy explicitly covers links and "verbally directing users to other sites".
   - Coaching isn't named. The risk is framing coaching as *treatment* (sleep apnea, malocclusion) or discouraging orthodontic or medical care.
   - *Implication:* the sales page counts as part of the video's compliance surface. State "adjunct, not substitute" everywhere you sell.
4. **YouTube ranks for satisfaction, not clicks.**
   - Its published ranking research moved from expected watch time (2016) to combined engagement-plus-satisfaction objectives: likes, dismissals, survey ratings (2019).
   - YouTube describes "valued watch time" as trained on viewer surveys (2021) (†, STRONG for mechanism, dated).
   - Test & Compare picks thumbnail winners by watch-time share, not CTR (†, MODERATE).
   - *Implication:* dignified packaging costs nothing. Bait is penalised twice: through the satisfaction signals, and through the "malicious clickbait" and misleading-thumbnail rules [P].
5. **YouTube's 2025–26 enforcement favours an original human on camera** [P, STRONG]:
   - "Inauthentic content" (renamed 15 Jul 2025) can't be monetised, and neither can AI personas giving health advice.
   - The spam policy lists "off-platform diversion", "scraped content" and AI mass-production.
   - About 12.5M channels were terminated Jan–Sep 2025 (*via B2*, MODERATE).
   - *Implication:* offer CTAs are fine inside a useful video. A video made only to push a link is a spam risk.
6. **Shorts are a discovery surface, not a conversion surface.**
   - Shorts can run 3 minutes (Oct 2024 †).
   - Since 31 Mar 2025 every start or replay counts as a view, while "engaged views" keep the old logic (†, MODERATE).
   - Description and comment links have been unclickable since Aug 2023 (†).
   - *Implication:* judge Shorts by subscribers, related-video clicks and opt-ins per 1,000 *engaged* views.
7. **TikTok US is run by "TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC"** (Terms of Service, 15 Jul 2026; the Creator Rewards terms cite Executive Order 14352) [P, STRONG].
   - The algorithm was retrained on US data after the January 2026 deal (*via B2*, MODERATE).
   - *Implication:* TikTok is a volatile feeder, never the spine.
8. **TikTok's August 2026 guidelines apply directly to a coaching business** [P, STRONG]:
   - Disclosure is required when "promoting your own business, product, or service".
   - The For You feed (FYF) and DMs are 16+.
   - The FYF avoids repeating "extreme fitness or dieting… overgeneralized mental health advice".
   - "Unoriginal or reused material" is ineligible.
   - Accounts that post a lot of ineligible content become "harder to find".
9. **Instagram's stated top signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**, with sends the most important for reaching non-followers (†, MODERATE, platform self-report).
   - Trial Reels test a reel on non-followers first (†).
   - Aggregator reposts are demoted (†), and unoriginal content can't be monetised [P].
   - *Implication:* design "send-worthy" recognition content ("this is literally you at month 8") and never cross-post watermarked files.
10. **Instagram DMs are the highest-intent surface and the riskiest.**
    - Keyword flows report 80–90% opens and 25–45% click-through (*via B1*, WEAK vendor).
    - Automation only runs inside user-initiated windows (†).
    - Teens are present, and "DM closer" optics carry the grift smell.
    - *Implication:* DMs route people, they don't close. The path is keyword → resource or self-assessment (email plus an age check) → a human conversation only with adults who show intent.
11. **X's published 2026 weights reward conversation among mutual follows and shareable ideas** [P, STRONG]:
    - Copy-link share 20; reply 5 (+15 between mutual follows since Jul 2026); quote 5; DM share 5; follow 4; like 0.5; open link 0.2.
    - Report −234; mute −58.8; not interested −43.2; block −31.2.
    - Candidates older than 48 h are dropped.
    - *Implication:* grow through replies and peer relationships, and avoid anything that makes viewers mute or block. With a ~2-day post life, route every post to owned channels.
12. **Links on X carry no explicit penalty in the published weights** (open-link weight is small and positive), but link posts still have to win on replies, shares and dwell [P].
    - Link-only posting, and editing a link after it earns engagement, count as spam [P].
    - Premium is required for monetisation [P]. Its reach effect is confounded (WEAK). No Premium multiplier appears in the two weight and scoring files I inspected.
13. **A face drives engagement; a brand name buys transferability.**
    - Photos with faces were about 38% more likely to be liked and 32% more likely to get comments, across 1.1M Instagram photos (*via P1*, MODERATE; 2014, correlational).
    - Default architecture: a brand handle with the operator's name in the display name on YouTube, Instagram and TikTok. On X, a personal handle, because X ranks through the person graph (mutual-follow boost, PageRank credibility) [P].
14. **Teen protections cluster around appearance content.**
    - YouTube estimates users' ages in the US (Aug 2025 †).
    - Instagram Teen Accounts default to PG-13 content (Oct 2025 †).
    - TikTok limits the FYF and DMs to 16+ [P].
    - Australia banned accounts for under-16s (Dec 2025 †).
    - *Implication:* build adult-first (18+ offers, an age screen at every door) and treat lost teen reach as a feature.
15. **Without a free community there are four pathways:**
    - YouTube → email → paid (the anchor);
    - Instagram → keyword DM → assessment or call;
    - X → newsletter;
    - TikTok/Shorts → profile → YouTube or email.

    The ranges are thin:
    - views → email 0.2–1% (*via B2*, ANECDOTAL estimate);
    - landing-page median 6.6% cold (*via B1*, MODERATE);
    - show rate 60–80%, close rate 15–35% (*via B2*, WEAK–MODERATE).
16. **Organic before/afters are allowed but can't be advertised.** Meta ads ban them (†), and X Shopping bans "deceptive 'before and after'" [P].
    - *Implication:* build proof formats that don't depend on them.
17. **Policy drifts constantly.** The archived YouTube Community Guidelines recorded 12 changes Jan–Jul 2026; Meta's recorded 10 May–Sep 2026 [P].
    - *Implication:* a quarterly policy review and a living claims library are part of the operating rhythm.

## 2. YouTube long-form

### 2.1 Recommendation signals: satisfaction, returning viewers, CTR × AVD

- **Mechanism (†, STRONG, dated).**
  - The 2016 recommender paper ranked by expected watch time because ranking by CTR "often promotes deceptive videos that the user does not complete ('clickbait')".
  - The 2019 multitask ranker added satisfaction objectives (likes, dismissals, ratings) and corrected for position bias.
  - In 2021 YouTube described 1–5 viewer surveys that train a model to predict satisfaction for every video ("valued watch time"), alongside likes, dislikes, shares and "not interested".
- **2024–26 emphasis (†, MODERATE).**
  - Discovery leads say the system "follows the audience" toward long-term satisfaction.
  - In 2025 Studio replaced "returning viewers" with new, casual and regular segments (verify the labels).
  - Growth in regular viewers is the practical proxy for satisfaction.
- **How CTR and AVD interact.**
  - CTR normally falls as impressions widen to less-matched viewers (†).
  - Over-sold packaging lifts CTR but cuts retention and satisfaction, which limits its own spread.
  - The system approximates watch time per impression. Candidate metric: **Impression Yield** (DERIVED: CTR × average view duration). Test & Compare's winner rule works the same way.
- **Niche implication.**
  - A Struggler's stall is diagnosed across several sessions, so build series and playlists around one problem arc.
  - Judge the channel by regular-viewer growth plus opt-ins, not single-video CTR.
  - A calm "why you stalled" package draws fewer, better-matched clicks, which is exactly what the system rewards.

### 2.2 Test & Compare

- **How it works (†, MODERATE).**
  - Available in desktop Studio since 2024.
  - Up to 3 thumbnails, shown to randomised viewer groups.
  - The winner is decided by **watch-time share**, and results read "Winner", "Preferred" or "Performed the same" within about two weeks.
  - Title testing was announced in 2025; verify that it has rolled out.
- **Implications.**
  - Test *concepts*: face plus stall-map diagram; a text-led question ("Too late at 25?"); a consented client photographed under matched conditions.
  - Never test a shame variant. A shame "winner" still breaks the Standard Card, and on Meta it would be age-gated as an inferiority statement [P].
  - Low-traffic channels will often get "Performed the same" (ESTIMATE), so the tool pays off only after impressions build.

### 2.3 Search and Google's AI surfaces

- **Evidence.**
  - YouTube videos appear in Google results and are cited in AI Overviews and AI Mode. 2025 SEO-vendor analyses rank YouTube among the most-cited domains (†, WEAK; figures not captured).
  - YouTube health queries surface **health source panels and shelves** reserved for licensed professionals and organisations (†, MODERATE). The operator can't qualify.
  - TikTok also says its search tries "to highlight content from reliable sources at the top" [P].
- **Niche implication.**
  - Skip clinical queries (sleep apnea treatment, TMJ, overbite correction).
  - Own **decision and experience queries**: "mewing not working after a year", "too late as an adult?", "mewing vs braces: what can change", "how to take consistent jaw photos".
  - Say the plain answer in the first minute and use chapters; transcripts are what text systems read (†, WEAK).

### 2.4 Channel memberships

- **Rules (†, verify).**
  - The Partner Program's "fan funding" tier (2023) needs 500 subscribers and 3 uploads in 90 days, plus either 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3M Shorts views in 90 days.
  - The creator keeps 70% of membership revenue, net.
- **Options.** (a) Skip; (b) a cheap "backstage" tier; (c) memberships *as* the paid community. Option (c) breaks in three places:
  - no email or buyer data;
  - a 30% fee;
  - client-style analysis reaching anonymous, possibly minor, members.
- **Default pick.** Skip it as an offer. Keep continuity where you own the relationship, and revisit (b) only as a late "supporter" tier (ESTIMATE).

### 2.5 Packaging with dignity, face on camera

- **Boundaries [P, STRONG].**
  - Thumbnails can't mislead viewers "to think they're about to view something that's not in the video".
  - "Malicious clickbait" counts as spam.
  - "Prolonged insults… based on… physical attributes" count as harassment.
  - Cross-posted to Meta, inferiority language gets age-gated and circling someone's features breaks the bullying rules.
  - Surgery-shock imagery earns limited or no ads under the shocking-content rules.
- **Why faces work (*via P1*, MODERATE; 2014, not re-tested under 2025 feeds).** Faces lift likes and comments, and face impressions form in under a second.
- **Dignified archetypes (derived).**
  1. The operator's face plus a process visual (stall map, "4 variables" diagram).
  2. A text-led question on a calm face.
  3. A consented client, same person, matched angle, light and distance, captioned with what changed *and* what didn't.
  4. The operator demonstrating a photo or measurement protocol.
- **Retire these archetypes.**
  - Red circles on a stranger's face.
  - Rating numbers ("3/10 → 7/10").
  - "Ugly/weak" vocabulary.
  - Surgery shock.
  - Unmatched-angle before/afters (hard line 1).
- **Candidate framework — the Dignity Packaging Test.**
  1. Would the person pictured endorse this?
  2. Would a Struggler feel *seen*, not judged?
  3. Does the video fully pay off the promise?

  A package goes into Test & Compare only if it passes all three.

### 2.6 Medical-misinformation policy and a creator with no credentials

- **Text [P, STRONG].**
  - The policy bars content "that contradicts local health authority (LHA) guidance about specific health conditions and substances" and poses "a serious risk of egregious harm".
  - Treatment misinformation includes promoting "harmful substances or practices that have not been approved… as safe or effective". The worked examples cover discouraging approved treatment and recommending "alternative treatments in place of approved treatments" (the examples are about cancer).
  - It applies to descriptions, comments and links, including verbal redirects.
  - Context from education, documentary, science or art "may" be allowed if it includes countervailing views. Firsthand experience is allowed, but "a pattern of promoting medical misinformation" is removed.
  - A first violation brings a warning plus optional training; 3 strikes in 90 days can end the channel.
  - The 2024-era "denial" category is gone (†).
- **What it means here (inference, MODERATE).** Appearance coaching is outside the named examples. Exposure starts when content does any of these:
  - frames oral posture or mouth taping as treatment for a diagnosable condition (obstructive sleep apnea, malocclusion, TMJ disorders, children's crossbites);
  - advises skipping orthodontic or medical care;
  - promotes DIY appliances.
  - The linked sales page is exposed too.
- **The credential gap.**
  - No health-source panel is possible (†), and "clinical" framing risks both policy action and hard line 6.
  - The "countervailing views" exception doubles as a trust asset: a steelman segment ("what orthodontists say, and where I agree").
- **Niche implication.**
  - Write the claim library at process, educational, observational and contextualised-outcome level.
  - Add a standing scope line to descriptions and sales pages: "coaching on habits and presentation; not diagnosis or treatment".

### 2.7 Spam, AI and monetisation [P, STRONG unless noted]

- **Monetisation.**
  - Inauthentic content (15 Jul 2025) can't be monetised, including "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates". AI used "to edit your video scripts" is fine.
  - AI personas posing as health experts can't be monetised ("An AI 'doctor' providing… wellness remedies"); the July 2026 clarifications also cover this (*via B2*).
- **The spam policy** covers engagement manipulation ("It's okay to ask viewers to like, comment, or subscribe"), off-platform diversion, detection evasion, mass-production, scraping and malicious clickbait. Since 10 Mar 2025, ad-suitability checks may add a human review of up to 24 hours.
- **Niche implication.**
  - No AI avatar or cloned voice.
  - Offer segments are allowed, but each video must be complete on-platform.
  - Back up the archive and grow email weekly.

## 3. Short-form: TikTok, Reels, Shorts

### 3.1 TikTok in the US, 2026

- **Primary facts [P, STRONG].**
  - The Terms of Service (15 Jul 2026) name "TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC… the operator of the Platform in the United States".
  - The Creator Rewards terms (20 Jul 2026):
    - are "provided by TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC";
    - route payments through "TT Commerce & Global Services" (BD TikTok USA LLC);
    - process data "in accordance with… Executive Order 14352".
- **Context (*via B2*, MODERATE).**
  - The deal was finalised on 22 Jan 2026 (NPR).
  - The US recommendation algorithm is being retrained on US data.
  - By Feb 2026 there was no mass exodus of users (CNBC). The managing investors are Oracle, MGX and Silver Lake (per that URL; names also †).
  - Background (†): the 2024 divest-or-ban law was upheld in Jan 2025, and executive orders delayed enforcement.
- **Niche implication.** Rules and reach may shift while the retrained model settles, and US policy can diverge from the rest of the world. Keep TikTok a native-posted feeder measured by opt-ins.

### 3.2 Ranking signals and For You eligibility

- **Signals.**
  - TikTok's 2026 text: the system "looks at things like what you like, share, comment on, and search for, as well as what's trending" [P].
  - Its 2020 explainer (†, MODERATE) weights interactions above video information and device settings. It calls finishing "a longer video from beginning to end" a strong signal.
  - A document leaked in 2021 (NYT †, MODERATE, dated) described a value formula built from predicted likes, comments, playtime and plays.
- **Eligibility [P, STRONG].**
  - Ineligible for the FYF:
    - "unoriginal or reused material without anything new";
    - misinformation that "may cause moderate harm";
    - "lower-severity degrading behavior";
    - manipulating people into "giving gifts or inflating likes or follows";
    - undisclosed commercial content (visibility reduced).
  - TikTok avoids *repeatedly* recommending "extreme fitness or dieting… or overgeneralized mental health advice".
  - Accounts that post a lot of ineligible content can be made "harder to find".
  - Creator Rewards pays only for videos of 1 minute or longer.
- **Unknown.** It is unstated whether dispersion covers appearance-optimisation loops, though it is plausible given the "harmful body comparisons" ban (WEAK). Treat looksmaxxing-adjacent binge reach as unreliable.
- **Niche implication.**
  - 60–90-second diagnosis formats ("the 3 stall patterns") fit both completion weighting and the 1-minute rule.
  - No "cure" or "fix in X days".
  - Keyword-comment CTAs sit near the engagement-bait line, so always deliver the promised resource (risk flag).

### 3.3 Instagram Reels

- **Signals (†, MODERATE; self-reported by Instagram's head, Jan 2025).** Watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers.
- **Trial Reels (Dec 2024 †).** A reel goes to non-followers first, with an optional auto-share to followers if it performs.
- **Originality (Apr 2024 †).** Aggregator reposts are swapped for the original. Repeat reposters lose eligibility for recommendations; the reported trigger was 10+ reposts in 30 days (verify). Meta also refuses to monetise "unoriginal content… reproduced without making meaningful enhancements" [P].
- **Other changes (†).** Reels up to 3 minutes and a 3:4 grid (Jan 2025); the Edits app (Apr 2025).
- **Niche implication.** Strugglers send each other "this is you" content. Design for *recognition* (the month-8 doubt, the photo-angle trap) over spectacle, and use Trial Reels as a free lab for hooks.

### 3.4 YouTube Shorts

- **Rules (†, MODERATE).**
  - Length up to 3 minutes since 15 Oct 2024.
  - Since 31 Mar 2025 views count every start or replay. "Engaged views" keep the old method and drive Partner Program eligibility.
  - Description and comment links stopped being clickable on 31 Aug 2023; one related-video link is allowed.
- **Shorts → long-form: no public benchmark.**
  - Mechanism: a lean-back swipe state, and separate recommendation for Shorts and long-form.
  - YouTube staff say Shorts don't hurt long-form (†, MODERATE). Creators report Shorts-only subscribers (ANECDOTAL). **CONTESTED.**
- **Niche implication.**
  - Cut Shorts from the long-form's decision moments and link each to its parent.
  - Measure related-video CTR and whether Shorts subscribers go on to watch long-form. Don't read raw views as demand.

### 3.5 Cross-posting realities

- **Penalties [P, STRONG].**
  - TikTok: reused material without anything new is ineligible for the FYF.
  - YouTube: reused content can't be monetised, and scraping others' material is spam.
  - Meta: unoriginal content can't be monetised.
- **Watermarks (†, MODERATE).** Instagram has said since 2021 that reels visibly recycled from other apps are less discoverable. YouTube guidance on Shorts is similar (WEAK).
- **Ambiguity.** The rules target *others'* content. Whether your own identical file trips TikTok's "reused" clause is unstated (open question).
- **Default.**
  - Export a clean master and upload natively to each platform.
  - Re-cut the first 1–2 seconds and the on-screen text for each platform.
  - Use native captions and a platform-specific CTA.
  - Toggle TikTok's disclosure on offer posts.

## 4. Instagram beyond Reels

### 4.1 DMs as a sales channel: keyword DM flows

- **Mechanics (†, MODERATE).**
  - Automation only works after the user starts the conversation: a DM or a comment.
  - A standard 24-hour window applies; a private reply can answer a comment; human-agent follow-up has a longer window (about 7 days).
  - Keyword "comment → DM" tools run on these rules.
- **Performance.** 80–90% of DMs opened and 25–45% click-through (*via B1*, WEAK vendor).
- **Age.** Teen Accounts limit who can message teens (†). TikTok DMs are 16+ [P]. Whether automated flows reach 16–17-year-olds is an open question.
- **Standard.** The rule "Route, don't close in DMs" (P2) stands. The vulnerability hard line applies in private channels.
- **Candidate framework — Keyword Door.** Comment keyword → the promised resource → one adulthood-and-fit question → self-assessment (email capture) → a human conversation only after an adult signals intent. No price talk in automated DMs.
- **Niche implication.** DMs are where the Struggler tells the real story. Treat it as sales intelligence handled with care, not pressure.

### 4.2 Broadcast channels, Stories, Close Friends

- **Formats (†).**
  - Broadcast channels are one-to-many: only the creator posts, and members react or vote.
  - Stories reach mostly followers and carry link stickers.
  - Close Friends is a private list.
- **Uses.**
  - Real enrollment windows and cohort dates (notch 4).
  - Case notes.
  - Assessment slots.
  - Close Friends as a perk for clients and alumni.
- **Guardrail.** None of these allows member-to-member interaction, so none recreates the excluded free community. Keep it that way.
- **Evidence gap.** No performance benchmarks were captured.

### 4.3 Carousels

- **Rules (†).** Up to 20 slides (Aug 2024). Public posts from professional accounts can be indexed by Google (10 Jul 2025, with an opt-out).
- **Performance (WEAK, figures not captured).** Vendor studies consistently place carousels among the highest-engagement formats per impression, while Reels lead reach.
- **Niche implication.** Carousels work as saveable, sendable *decision tools*, e.g. "Posture problem or structure question? Six checks before you spend money". Keep them non-diagnostic, add a referral line, and write captions and alt text as search copy.

### 4.4 Subscriptions

- **Rules (†, verify).** Roughly 18+ and 10k followers. App-store fees apply, and subscriber data stays with the platform.
- **Default pick.** Skip. It duplicates the paid community on worse terms.

### 4.5 Profile and search

- **Rules (†).**
  - The name field and username are searchable.
  - Up to 5 bio links and 3 pins.
  - Hashtags carry little weight; a 5-per-post cap was reported in Dec 2025 (WEAK–MODERATE, verify).
- **Default setup.**
  - Display name: "Operator Name | Brand".
  - Bio: who it's for, what you don't do, one next step.
  - Links: self-assessment first, "Start Here" long-form second.
  - Pins: intro; process proof; who this is and isn't for.
  - Use "coach/education" words. Never "specialist", "therapist" or "clinic" (hard line 6).

### 4.6 Teen Accounts

- **Rules.**
  - Teen Accounts are private by default, with restricted messaging and the strictest sensitive-content setting (Sep 2024 †). PG-13 has been the teen default since Oct 2025 (†).
  - Meta's 18+ restrictions [P] cover cosmetic-procedure content, weight-loss content, health clickbait and inferiority statements.
- **Implication.** The most looksmaxxing-coded content is invisible to teens by rule. For an adult-offer business, that aligns with the goal.

## 5. X (2025–2026)

### 5.1 For You ranking in the published 2026 code [P, STRONG]

- **Pipeline.**
  - Candidates come from followed accounts (Thunder) and from out-of-network retrieval (Phoenix embeddings plus SimClusters).
  - A transformer ("Phoenix") predicts the probability of each action for each viewer.
  - `Final Score = Σ (weight × P(action))`. The weights scale *predicted probabilities*, "not the raw engagement counts".
- **Weights (production defaults, Aug 2026).**
  - Positive: share via copy link 20; reply 5; quote 5; share via DM 5; follow author 4; share 2; repost 1; like 0.5; click 0.4; open link 0.2; video open 0.07; photo expand 0.05; dwell 0.05; continuous dwell time 0.004; profile click 0.
  - Negative: report −234; mute −58.8; not interested −43.2; block −31.2; not dwelled −0.02. A report is ">1000x" rarer than a like.
- **Mutual-follow reply boost.**
  - Tested at 5–20 from 10 Jul 2026, launched at 20 on 13 Jul, and set to 15 on 24 Jul.
  - It boosts predicted replies between accounts that follow each other.
- **After scoring.**
  - Author-diversity decay.
  - An out-of-network discount.
  - A new-author boost (parameters: 1,000-impression threshold, 1,000-follower cap, 48-hour post age, lift toward feed slot 15–16).
  - A diversity reranker that gives up "a little score for less similarity between neighbours".
- **Filters and labels.**
  - Candidates older than 48 hours are dropped.
  - Visibility filtering is separate from ranking. It includes "DoNotAmplify" rules for non-followers, spam and malicious-URL drops, and underage or no-stated-age "sensitive viewer" drops.
  - `agatha/` labels accounts from "blocks, reports and spam reports relative to favorites".
  - `user-cred-v2/` runs PageRank over follow and engagement edges.
  - The "Under the Hood" tool (Aug 2026; legal-withholding info added 18 Sep) shows the labels on your own account.

### 5.2 Change versus the 2023 release [P, STRONG]

| Weight | 2023 (5 Apr 2023) | 2026 (Aug 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Reply | 13.5 | 5, plus the mutual-follow boost |
| Reply engaged by the author | 75 | Not in the published 2026 weights |
| Good profile click | 12 | Profile click 0 |
| Good clicks | 11 and 10 | — |
| Share via copy link / via DM | Not in the published 2023 weights | 20 / 5 |
| Negative feedback | −74 | Split into not interested, mute and block |
| Report | −369 | −234 |

- **What moved.** Distribution-like actions (sharing a link off X, sending via DM) and replies between mutual follows now carry the weight. Author-engaged replies and profile clicks lost their premium.
- **Caveat.** Weights are defaults that change with experiments, and learned features (author details, account labels) can carry effects the weights don't show.

### 5.3 Long posts, Premium, links

- **Long posts and articles (†, MODERATE).**
  - Premium allows posts of about 25,000 characters, and articles are available on paid tiers.
  - Dwell carries only small weights [P], so a long post ranks through replies, quotes and shares.
  - *Niche use:* a steelmanned contrarian essay ("adult change: what's plausible, what's grift") that invites replies, with the newsletter link in the first reply.
- **Premium [P].**
  - Monetisation requires:
    - Premium (Premium, Business or Organizations);
    - 18+ and an account at least 3 months old;
    - a complete profile, a verified email and 2FA;
    - X Money or Stripe;
    - identity verification.
  - Creator subscriptions also need 2,000+ active Premium followers, 5M organic impressions in 3 months, and a post in the last 30 days.
  - Premium's perks include reply prioritisation (†, MODERATE).
  - Vendor analyses report much higher median impressions for Premium accounts (†, WEAK), but the comparison is confounded by who buys Premium. **CONTESTED.**
  - No Premium multiplier appears in `param.rs` or `scoring.rs`. That only shows its absence from two files.
  - *Default:* buy Premium only once X is a deliberate channel.
- **Links.**
  - The open-link weight is small and positive, and copy-link sharing is the largest weight [P]. There is no explicit penalty, but link posts must still win on replies and dwell.
  - Link-only posting and editing a link's destination after it earns engagement are spam [P].
  - Leadership has said at points that link posts get less reach (†, WEAK–MODERATE).
  - *Default:* a native post that stands alone, with the link in the first reply or the profile.

### 5.4 Audience fit

- **Evidence.**
  - X reaches about a fifth of US adults, skewed male, far below YouTube (†, MODERATE; Pew). YouTube reaches about 95% of US 18–29s (*via B2*, STRONG).
  - About two-thirds of 3,000+ young men aged 16–25 regularly engage with masculinity content, and regular viewers report more feelings of worthlessness (*via B2*, Movember 2025, MODERATE, cross-sectional).
  - Appearance talk on X is often ironic or hostile. The mute, block and report weights, and the labels from blocks relative to likes, punish content that repels people [P].
- **Niche implication.** X is secondary, with three jobs:
  1. A peer and credibility graph of mutual follows with adjacent educators and clinicians.
  2. A test lab for YouTube topics and contrarian takes.
  3. A newsletter feeder for 22–35-year-old optimisers.

  It is not the front line for 18–24-year-old Strugglers.

## 6. Account architecture for a person-fronted brand

| Option | What it is | Wins when | Breaks when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Brand handle, human face** | @Brand; display name "Operator Name \| Brand"; operator on camera | You want a sellable, extensible container for products | The name and face drop out of the content and the brand reads as faceless |
| **B. Personal handle, brand as offer name** | @operatorname; the brand appears only on offers and site | Maximum trust; fits X | Only partly meets the named-brand-from-day-1 spec; harder to extend |
| **C. Dual track** | @Brand plus @Operator on one platform | A team can staff two voices | Doubles work at 20–25 h/week and splits signals (sends, mutual-follow replies) |

- **Default pick.**
  - Option A on YouTube, Instagram and TikTok.
  - On X, a personal handle with the brand in the bio, because X ranks through the person graph [P].
  - One primary account per platform.
- **Mechanism.**
  - Faces lift engagement (*via P1*, MODERATE). Parasocial trust attaches to the person, while the brand holds the offers, the site and the email identity.
  - Real inputs (†, ANECDOTAL):
    - Athlean-X: a company brand fronted by its founder in almost every video.
    - Renaissance Periodization: a company channel fronted by a founder who also has a personal presence. Dual tracks work when there are staff.
    - The Try Guys leaving BuzzFeed: the audience followed the people.
  - *Where it breaks:* in creator businesses, brand equity is mostly person equity. The name buys packaging and extensibility, not independence from the operator.
- **Operations.**
  - Run YouTube as a Brand Account so freelancers get Manager or Editor roles without passwords (†). Use Meta business roles on Instagram (†).
  - TikTok business accounts are limited to the commercial music library (†), which matters little for talking-head education. Either account type must disclose self-promotion [P].
  - X monetisation requires identity verification [P].
- **Naming constraint.**
  - No "clinic", "institute", "ortho", "therapy" or "Dr." (hard line 6).
  - TikTok reserves some health-service marketing for "licensed and regulated medical entities with a verified business account" [P].
  - Candidate name: **Face-Forward Brand Handle**.

|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube | Medical misinformation (prevention/treatment), including links and verbal redirects | Posture or taping as *treatment*; discouraging orthodontic or medical care; DIY devices | Removal; warning, then strikes |
| YouTube | Spam: malicious clickbait, off-platform diversion, scraping, AI mass-production | Timeframe promises the video doesn't deliver; link-only videos | Removal, demonetisation, termination |
| YouTube | Harassment of physical attributes; misleading thumbnails | Face roasts, breakdowns of strangers | Removal or strike |
| YouTube † | Teen limits on repeated feature-comparison content; US age estimation | Comparison-heavy series | Lower teen distribution |
| TikTok | "Harmful body comparisons"; "degrading remarks about appearance" | Rating or comparison formats | Removal or FYF ineligibility |
| TikTok | Misinformation (moderate harm); unoriginal content; dispersion of "extreme fitness or dieting" | Overclaiming structure; reposts; binge loops | Lost FYF; account "harder to find" |
| TikTok | Disclosure for your own business; FYF and DMs 16+; monetisation 18+ | Offer posts; teen commenters | Reduced visibility; feature limits |
| Meta (Instagram) | 18+ restriction: cosmetic procedures incl. "restore… structure of people's faces"; weight loss; health clickbait with timeframes; inferiority statements | "Mewing vs jaw surgery" results talk; "fix X in 90 days"; "weak chin" | Visibility restricted to adults |
| Meta (Instagram) | Bullying: circling or highlighting features; ranking by appearance | Face breakdowns of non-consenting people | Removal |
| X | Spam (link-only, link edits); X Shopping bans deceptive before/after; health and cosmetic services restricted in Shopping | Link dumps; bait-and-switch posts | Reach limits, labels, suspension |
| US regulators † | FTC fake-reviews rule (2024); Endorsement Guides on typical results; health-claim substantiation guidance (2022) | Unrepresentative testimonials; health-benefit claims | Civil penalties (biggest-risk flag only) |
| Youth laws † | Australia's under-16 ban (Dec 2025); UK Online Safety Act child duties (Jul 2025) | Teen-heavy audiences | Platform age-gating |

**Candidate framework — Visibility Tax Ladder.** It maps the Standard's claim ladder onto platform consequences:

| Rung | Claim type | Platform tax |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Process and educational | None |
| 1 | Observational, or outcome with context (who, timeline, what else changed, typical range) | Low, provided it's true |
| 2 | Timeframe promises, inferiority vocabulary, procedure talk | Meta 18+ gating; FYF risk; satisfaction risk |
| 3 | Rating third parties, or claiming coaching replaces treatment | Removal and strikes |

The ladder shows the Standard's hard lines and the platforms' distribution rules pointing the same way.

**Before/afters.** Organic before/afters are allowed. The constraints:
- Meta ads ban them (†).
- X Shopping bans deceptive ones [P].
- Procedure depictions are 18+ on Meta [P].
- The FTC's typical-results logic applies (†).
- Playbook rule: matched conditions, consent and the context stack, and never the only proof type.

## 8. Content-to-offer pathways without a free community

### 8.1 Door Map (candidate name)

| Surface | Clickable doors | Allowed ask | Next step | Age gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube long-form | Description and pinned-comment links; spoken CTAs. External end-screen and card links have historically needed Partner Program status (†, verify) | Direct offer segments when warmth justifies them; public prices | Problem-specific resource or self-assessment → email | At resource and checkout |
| YouTube Shorts | Related-video link only (†) | "Full breakdown in the linked video" | Parent long-form | — |
| TikTok | Profile link (eligibility varies †); disclosure toggle [P] | Resource; disclosed offers | Profile → email | DMs 16+: no selling |
| Instagram | 5 bio links, Story links, keyword DMs, broadcast channel (†) | Keyword → resource; real cohort dates | Self-assessment → email → call (adults) | Teen Accounts; confirm age in the flow |
| X | Link in first reply or profile; long posts (†) | Newsletter; occasional direct offer | Newsletter → email offers | Underage viewers are filtered from sensitive posts [P]; confirm age at opt-in |

### 8.2 Pathways and ranges

1. **YouTube → email → paid (the anchor).**
   - Views → subscribers: 0.2–1% (*via B2*, ANECDOTAL estimate). Higher with a specific, named resource offered mid-video *and* in the pinned comment; lower with generic "free guide" asks.
   - Landing pages: median 6.6% cold; wellness 8.2%; health pages written at a 5th–7th-grade reading level 10.8% (*via B1*, MODERATE, 41k pages). Warm video traffic should beat the median, but there's no niche benchmark (ESTIMATE).
   - List → purchase: 1–3% per campaign at $50–200 (*via B2*, WEAK).
   - DERIVED: 1,000 views ≈ 2–10 subscribers, so 50k views a month ≈ 100–500 new subscribers.
2. **Instagram or TikTok → keyword DM → self-assessment → call.**
   - Opens 80–90%, clicks 25–45% (WEAK). DERIVED: 100 keyword comments ≈ 20–40 clicks, before opt-in.
   - Show rate 60–80% (80%+ with qualification, reminders and deposits); close rate 15–35% (*via B2*, WEAK–MODERATE).
3. **X → newsletter → offers.** No reliable benchmark. With a 48-hour post life, email is the only durable capture.
4. **Shorts or TikTok → YouTube or email.** Multi-hop, so every hop leaks. Measure the hop each surface controls: related-video CTR and profile-link clicks.

### 8.3 Measurement

- **Candidate metric — Revenue per 1,000 Qualified Views, by door.** Built from:
  - UTM-tagged links per door and per video;
  - a "where did you find us?" field at opt-in and at checkout;
  - a monthly roll-up.
- **Why.** It replaces view counts, which have been especially misleading since the 2025 Shorts change.
