## 1. Executive findings

1. **Delivery hours, not audience size, are the binding constraint ("Delivery-Hour Ceiling", candidate name).** With about 6–10 h/week for delivery, $25–50k/month profit needs **$700–$2,600 revenue per delivery hour** (DERIVED). That is **6–12× the average coach's annual revenue** (~$49k; $234/hr global, $297/hr North America; 11.6 coaching h/week — ICF 2025, MODERATE†). *Implication:* one-to-many delivery carries the revenue; 1:1 is a capped premium tier.
2. **1:1 alone cannot reach the target at 20–25 h/week.** At $3,000–$6,000 per 12–16-week engagement and 2.7–3 h per client per month, 30–40 delivery h/month caps revenue at **~$11k–$22k/month** (DERIVED from ESTIMATE hours). *Implication:* 1:1 becomes a "Private" upgrade with 6–10 seats.
3. **Default front door: a paid, credited, non-diagnostic assessment ("Assessment-First Front Door", candidate name).** Price screens for high-use buyers (field RCT, STRONG in its domain). $50–$250 consults credited within 30–90 days are normal in medical aesthetics (MODERATE). Niche analogs sell reads and analyses at $60–$250 (MODERATE). It answers the Struggler's "why did I stall?", earns revenue from non-converters and filters tire-kickers. *Hard line:* it assesses habits, history and standardised photos; it never "diagnoses" or reads structure "from a glance".
4. **A paid community is a weak front door and a good continuity layer.** Skool median paid price is **$27–$49/mo**, median paid MRR **$2.8–4.2k**, top decile >$25k (WEAK–MODERATE). Typical churn is 5–10%/mo (WEAK). A community alone needs **370–1,200+ members** at $49–$79 to carry the target (DERIVED), competes with free forums, and adds moderation load and minors.
5. **Guarantees help good providers, not unknown ones charging high prices.** Across 109 articles, guarantees lift purchase intent for good providers but not poor ones (MODERATE–STRONG). At high prices, generosity raises believability **only for well-known sellers** (MODERATE). Leniency lifts purchases more than returns, and longer windows *reduce* returns (meta-analysis, STRONG, retail). *Implication:* a **conditional "Process Guarantee"** on controllables (completion → continued coaching or partial refund); never structural change.
6. **Payment plans lift sales ~20%, mostly among low-creditworthiness buyers** (field, STRONG). BNPL also raises total spending ("liquidity flypaper", STRONG). *Implication:* with men aged 17–32, plans lift enrollments *and* affordability-harm, default and chargeback risk. Guardrails: 18+, modest premium, explicit affordability check, and a cheaper **"Down-Path"** (candidate name) instead of pushing.
7. **Decoys are fragile; the middle-option pull is modest for price-quality tiers.** Only 11 of 91 attraction-effect attempts were reliable, and the effect fades with verbal or pictorial options (CONTESTED). Extremeness aversion is robust across 142 observations but weaker for price-quality trade-offs (STRONG). *Implication:* three honest tiers (DIY / Cohort / Private) for segmentation, not decoys.
8. **Price endings barely matter.** Just-below prices: g = 0.13, ~0 after publication-bias correction (k = 69, N = 40,541, STRONG). The round-price "feels right" effect failed preregistered replication (N = 588, CONTESTED). *Implication:* round prices ($1,900, not $1,997), chosen for anti-grift brand consistency.
9. **"Pay more, commit more" is mostly selection, not causation.** Higher prices screened for high-use households with no consistent sunk-cost effect (field RCT, STRONG). Full-price theatre subscribers attended more only early in the season (MODERATE). Usage peaks after each payment, then decays (field, MODERATE–STRONG). *Implication:* price for delivery depth and readiness; adherence comes from structure (check-ins, stage calls, reassessments).
10. **Flat, all-in prices win.** Buyers choose flat tariffs even when pay-per-use is cheaper (insurance, taximeter and overestimation effects). Flat-rate bias does **not** raise churn; pay-per-use bias does (field, STRONG). *Implication:* all-in program fee, flat continuity, no metered reviews or messages.
11. **Continuity can quietly earn from non-use.** Gym members on $70+/mo contracts attended 4.3×/month, paying $17+ per visit when $10 was available; they forecast ~9.5 visits against ~4.2 (STRONG). *Implication:* **"Usage-Backed Continuity"** (candidate name) — monthly reassessment, usage nudges, one-click cancel — so it passes the Informed-Client Test.
12. **Self-paced products starve the proof engine.** MOOC completion was 3.13% (2017–18); 52% never start (STRONG). Cohort platforms claim 75–95% completion (WEAK, vendor). *Implication:* digital-first engines yield few measured results and few honest testimonials; cohorts feed the **"Proof Flywheel"** (candidate name).
13. **The niche is a barbell with an empty middle.** Cheap end: $5–$15 e-books, $3.99–$6.99/week apps, $80–$250 one-off analyses. Clinical end: $1,200–$3,500 myofunctional programs, ~£12.5k orthotropic courses, $2.6–3.6k chin-augmentation surgeon fees, $20–50k+ jaw surgery (MODERATE). *Implication:* **measured, honest, coached adherence for adults at $1,500–$3,000** is largely unoccupied.
14. **Credibility vacuum at the top.** The public face of "mewing" was erased from the UK dental register (Nov 2024; appeal dismissed May 2026, MODERATE†). The AAO says there is "no scientific evidence" for jaw reshaping (Jan 2024, MODERATE). An Aug 2026 NGO report calls looksmaxxing a "grift economy" (216 influencers, 14.6B views, MODERATE). *Implication:* the market is primed for an anti-escalation, process-first brand, and structural or medical claims carry reputational and regulatory exposure.
15. **Face-rating is the dominant mass monetisation, and it is ethically loaded.** UMAX: ~$3.99/week, self-reported $350–500k/month (WEAK). Psychologists quoted in the press link rating apps to harm in teenage boys (MODERATE). *Implication:* **"Anti-Rating Positioning"** (candidate name) — assess controllables, never attractiveness.
16. **Minors are a structural risk.** Contracts with minors are voidable; the minor can disaffirm and recover payment (STRONG legal principle; jurisdiction varies). *Implication:* all paid offers 18+; under-18s get free education and a "see a dentist or orthodontist" route.
17. **Chargebacks can end a high-ticket solo business.** Visa's merchant "excessive" threshold fell to **1.5%** in April 2026 (MODERATE†); one dispute among 30 monthly transactions is 3.3%. *Implication:* clear terms, recorded onboarding confirmation, a conditional guarantee claimed through a conversation, and no enrollment of anyone in affordability distress.
18. **"100 true fans" math fits better than "1,000".** $300–600k/year profit means about **150–400 program clients/year at $1,500–$3,000**, plus continuity (DERIVED). Graduation *is* success, so acquisition never stops. *Implication:* a "200 True Clients" frame (candidate name) backed by a permanent demand engine.

**Which mixes reach $25–50k/month profit at 20–25 h/week (DERIVED):**

- **Mixes that work:** 60–85% of revenue from one-to-many delivery (cohort, community or digital), 1:1 capped at 6–10 seats at a premium, a front-end revenue line (paid assessments or DIY), and continuity at 5–15% of revenue. Editing and design are outsourced, and the operator's content time stays at roughly 6.5–7.5 hours per week.
- **Mixes that fail:**
  - 1:1-only: the ceiling is about $11–22k.
  - Low-price community-only: needs 600–1,200 members at $49, or about 42–84 joins per month at 7% churn.
  - Low-ticket-only: needs thousands of buyers a month and paid acquisition, and it starves the proof engine.

## 4. Unit economics & capacity scenarios (steady-state, evergreen, no launches)

**Global assumptions (ESTIMATES unless sourced):** processing ~3%; refunds and chargebacks 3–5% (coaching) and 10–15% (DIY, ANECDOTAL anchor); freelancers (long-form edit, shorts, thumbnails) $2.5–4k/mo; software $400–650/mo (incl. Skool Pro $99 where used); miscellaneous (accounting, insurance, legal templates) $400–550/mo. Payment plans: ~half of buyers take 3 instalments at +10%, and ~5% of later instalments go uncollected (ESTIMATE; failed-payment churn runs ~0.9–1.7% per billing cycle in large subscription data, MODERATE). Cohorts: 12 weeks, rolling monthly starts. **"Stage Calls"** (candidate name): one weekly call per stage (Month 1/2/3) however many cohorts overlap, so calls stay at ~3 h/week. Content: 6.5–7.5 h/week with editing outsourced; early months need more (see stage note).

| **Sunk cost vs screening** | Higher price screened for users, no consistent sunk-cost effect (RCT); theatre sunk-cost effect faded late-season | Screening STRONG; sunk-cost weak | Price for delivery depth and readiness, not "to make them commit" | — |
| **Real scarcity, staged increases** | Oversubscription: demand above capacity, waiting list, rising prices | ANECDOTAL (practitioner) | Cap monthly cohort seats at ~15–20 (justified by review minutes); raise prices on announced milestones that actually happen; grandfather existing clients | Real scarcity permitted; fake forbidden |

**Pricing under outcome uncertainty — "Price the Path, Not the Face" (candidate name).** Adult facial change is uncertain, slow and confounded by adherence. Outcome pricing invites **adverse selection** (those least likely to change seek guarantees) and **moral hazard** (results depend on client behaviour), and structural guarantees are a hard line. Price the **"Four Priceables"** (candidate name) instead: **Assessment** (information), **Structure** (plan, stages, protocol packaging), **Access** (reviews, calls) and **Measurement** (standardised photo protocol, tracked markers). Show outcomes as contextualised evidence (who, timeline, what else changed, typical range); never promise them.

The closest ethical precedent is IVF "shared-risk" refund programs. An ethics committee permits them only if the success criterion is set in advance, clinic-specific odds are disclosed, exclusions are itemised and patients understand there is no guarantee. It warns that high premiums or low refunds make such programs serve the clinic, and that early successes overpay (MODERATE, normative). **For this niche:** any outcome-linked refund must use process or measurement markers defined in advance, carry a modest premium, and screen eligibility (adults, no orthodontic or medical red flags).

## 6. Productization patterns

1. **Named signature system (packaging, not technique).** A named, staged process makes the method legible and ownable and keeps persuasion on the process and education rungs of the claim ladder (practitioner, ANECDOTAL). *Niche rule:* stage names (Audit → Build → Hold); avoid pseudo-medical names like "Bone Remodeling Protocol", which imply structural claims.
2. **Diagnostic-first ("Stall Audit", candidate name).** Draws on diagnose-before-prescribe, paid discovery in services, and credited aesthetic consults. *Tension:* one practitioner school says never sell the diagnostic apart from the prescription, but in B2C the assessment must also stand alone as useful (a written plan) because many buyers won't continue. *Resolution:* "Phase 0" of the program, credit expiring in 14–30 days.
3. **DIY / DWY / "Done-Around-You" (candidate name).** Results depend on the client's daily habits, so true done-for-you is impossible. The honest top tier is done-for-you *planning, tracking set-up, environment design, check-in cadence and referral navigation*. Tiers: DIY system $97–$297, cohort $1,500–$3,000, Private $3,500–$6,000 with capped seats (ESTIMATES anchored to the scan).
4. **Fixed-scope productized reviews.** Quarterly progress review at $150–$250 (anchored to $80–$250 niche analyses): standardised photo comparison, marker review and an updated plan in 30–40 minutes. An add-on for DIY buyers and alumni that fits the flat-rate bias (a fixed product, not metered time).
5. **Rolling cohorts with Stage Calls.** Monthly start dates are real deadlines. Weekly calls run by *stage*, not by cohort, so call hours stay flat as cohorts overlap. Async review minutes set the capacity cap and are the honest reason for seat limits.
6. **Usage-Backed Continuity ("Hold" phase).** Alumni-only, paid ($39–$79/mo): monthly reassessment prompt, monthly group session, quarterly review discount, one-click cancel. Retention comes from use, not from forgetting to cancel (the gym-economics warning).
7. **Renewal ("Phase 2").** Triggered by the week-10 reassessment and offered only to clients with measurable momentum; priced below Phase 1 (ESTIMATE 50–70%). Usage decays after payment, so timing the renewal decision to a measurement moment is both useful and honest.
8. **Measurement kit as a product feature.** A standardised photo protocol (angle, lighting, distance, time of day) plus tracked habit markers. It guards against misleading before/afters (hard line), creates honest proof, and anchors the Proof Flywheel.

## 8. Model critiques (mechanism → where it breaks here → salvage)

- **Value Equation (dream outcome × perceived likelihood ÷ time delay × effort).** *Mechanism:* move four levers to raise perceived value. *Breaks:* pushing "perceived likelihood" above real likelihood is the core grift move and collides with the structural-guarantee hard line; time delay is biological and can't honestly be compressed; effort *is* the product. *Salvage:* raise likelihood only with process proof, standardised measurement and contextualised cases; reframe delay as **time to first measurable win** (habit and marker wins in 2–4 weeks); cut *friction*, not effort.
- **Grand Slam Offer (stacked bonuses, guarantees, urgency, naming).** *Breaks:* inflated "value stacks" read as scams to grift-fatigued buyers (persuasion-knowledge theory, well established, not re-sourced here); an unconditional guarantee from an unknown high-priced seller isn't believable (MODERATE). *Salvage:* stack real delivery units (reviews, calls, reassessments), use conditional or process guarantees and real deadlines only. An honest "anti-guarantee" ("no one can guarantee bone change") can differentiate.
- **Value Ladder (bait → front end → middle → back end).** *Breaks:* every rung leaks. Low-ticket buyers rarely finish (MOOC 3%), so they rarely ascend; cheap rungs cheapen positioning against a $5–$15 floor; the free-community rung is off the table; long ladders tend to be launch-built (excluded). *Salvage:* a **Short Ladder** (candidate name) of 2–3 rungs pivoting on the paid assessment.
- **1,000 / 100 True Fans.** *Mechanism:* a direct relationship with a small base, 1,000 × $100 or 100 × $1,000 per year. *Breaks:* fans of a *transformation* graduate, so churn is success; $1,000+/year needs a "step-function" increase in value (the author's own framing), which young men pay only for visible structure and support. *Salvage:* **200 True Clients** (~150–400 per year) plus a permanent demand engine.
- **Smallest Viable Market / Audience.** *Mechanism:* specificity creates word of mouth. *Breaks (hypothesis):* appearance insecurity is private, and earlier audience research recorded "what will my friends think", so public referral will be weak. *Salvage:* private referral mechanics and results-led content. Untested; needs testing.
- **Oversubscribed.** *Mechanism:* visible demand above capacity, a waiting list, rising prices. *Breaks:* the temptation to fake it (hard line). *Salvage:* publish the *real* capacity arithmetic (review minutes per client) and use waiting lists for cohort starts.
- **Value-based fees / Choice of Yeses.** *Breaks:* ROI maths ("20:1 return") doesn't fit appearance and turns manipulative when aimed at insecurity. *Salvage:* keep the options mechanic, drop the ROI rhetoric.
- **Diagnose-before-prescribe / paid discovery.** *Breaks:* "diagnosis" is medical language, reading structure from photos is a hard line, and the practitioner source has no conversion data. *Salvage:* call it an **assessment or audit**, base it on habits and history, and build in refer-out triggers.
- **Productized services (fixed scope, price and process).** *Breaks:* outcomes vary with adherence, so scope can't be outcome-defined. *Salvage:* productize *process units* (reviews, stage calls, reassessments).
- **Flat-rate memberships (gym economics).** *Breaks:* profiting from non-use fails the Informed-Client Test. *Salvage:* Usage-Backed Continuity.
- **Face-rating app model.** *Breaks:* a scoring loop that feeds insecurity (shame hard line), with the harm debate on record, and it needs a development team. *Salvage:* none as a business model; useful only as the idea to position against.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Assessment-to-program conversion in this niche.** There is no public data. Run a 60–90-day test of the paid assessment against a free 20-minute fit call, measuring enrollments, no-shows, refunds and revenue per lead.
2. **Audience age and income mix.** What share of the audience is under 18? What can 18–24-year-olds actually afford? These drive price points, Down-Path design and payment-plan risk.
3. **Payment-plan leakage and chargebacks in $1.5–3k coaching.** No credible public benchmarks exist. Track this from the first client; Visa's 1.5% ratio is the hard ceiling.
4. **Fitness, skin and body-transformation coaching price bands for 2024–26 could not be verified** before the search budget ran out. The niche band here is triangulated from looksmax coaching, myofunctional programs, cohort-course and coaching-hourly data. Verify before the LEDGER.
5. **Churn in appearance-adjacent paid communities.** No niche data exists; the general benchmarks are WEAK.
6. **DIY completion.** Do built-in check-ins or optional reviews raise completion enough to protect the Proof Flywheel?
7. **Ad-platform policy constraints** on appearance claims, before/after imagery and targeting young men. Not researched here, and it affects S3's viability.
8. **Consumer-protection rules for testimonials and outcome claims** (endorsement disclosures, UK and US advertising standards). Not researched here; flagged as the biggest legal risk after minors.
9. **Is "anti-rating, anti-escalation, honest" a premium driver** or only a trust driver? Test messaging variants.
10. **Referral behaviour in a private-insecurity niche.** Do clients refer, and do private or anonymous referral mechanics work?
11. **Continuity join rate and Phase 2 uptake.** The join rate is modelled at 40–50% of graduates with 7–9% monthly churn. Phase 2 uptake is not modelled; its price is assumed at 50–70% of Phase 1. All are untested assumptions.
12. **Spot-check all † figures** against full text before they enter `LEDGER.md`, because this sweep read search extracts only.
