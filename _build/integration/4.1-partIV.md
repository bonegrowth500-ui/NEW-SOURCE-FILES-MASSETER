## 4.2 nominations (per module)
- 14 · weakest part: §6 The Canon (about 555 words) — the reader gets seven finished claims and a table of bracketed cadences. He doesn't learn how to write his own: how a claim earns its tier and fixed words, how to choose 5–7 from his link tally, what "one proof asset a skeptic can check" looks like per claim, or how a claim is retired by dated correction. No case runs the rotation, for example Cole building his Canon from his first 30 link cards, or a resurfacing myth moved to the front and read afterward. Rotation changes have no signs → read → default. Parts V–VI apply the Canon on every surface, so it is under-built beside §3 · strongest part: §3 Finding the Broken Link — a sign table with one checking question per link. Adrian's live read suspects Vehicle from an early sign, confirms it, clears Now, and repairs from his own months. Then the pushback variant, the line never said, and the burned-buyer and stop-rule limits. The Sam hard case ranks record over words and repairs the earlier link first.
- 15 · weakest part: §3 Sell in the Open, and Presence over Pedigree (about 572 words) — the surface table works, but presence over pedigree is four instructions (on camera, personal replies, a composite review walk-through, a fixed publishing day) resting on one evidence line. There's no composite case, no signs of over- or under-disclosure beyond one sentence, and no decision on what carries presence once replies can't stay personal (Growing to Scaling, templates, routing help). The stake line ("I earn money when you join…") is never run on a skeptic · strongest part: §2 Costly vs Hygiene Signals — its two-cost mechanism (keeping a signal true, being caught faking) sorts the signals into two lists. A proves / can't-prove test table follows, and Theo tests one corrective-week entry against the terms' date. It ends on an operating rule with LEDGER thresholds.
- 16 · weakest part: §1 Why Proof Fails Here (about 452 words after 4.1) — it asserts the audit mindset and gives a formats table, but has no composite, no operating rule, and nothing to do this month. "Volume reads as selection" is one paragraph. It never shows what one dramatic pair costs with the silent skeptics who read the comments. As written it is setup for §2–§6 · strongest part: §2 The Claim Ladder and the Context Stack — the ladder is shown on Sam's own sentence, and a full-stack caption names confounders, a denominator and the men at zero. Three named confounders follow, then a hard case that turns a striking but weight-explained pair into a body-composition Case without burying it.
- 17 · weakest part: §6 The Identity Threshold, and the Self-Assessment as a Commitment (about 636 words, the shortest) — the threshold is named and Theo gets a paragraph, but the moves (privacy, crossing with the log, threshold questions first) never run on a buyer crossing. There are no signs → read → default for a confession crossing against a record crossing, and no checkout or recap wording. The self-assessment subsection is mostly evidence caveats and ends at "[8–10] questions" · strongest part: §5 Quiet Commitments — reasons come before rules: a reader keeps the monitoring effect without shame. Each board rule carries its reason (self-reported days are excluded because they decide refunds), and the day-12 "all in" picture shows why nothing sits near refund rights. An options table with a default and the ownership-settled hard case close it.
