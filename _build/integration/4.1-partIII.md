## 4.2 nominations (per module)

- 09 · weakest part: §1 Why You Sell Before You Have an Audience (387 words, the shortest deep section). It asserts that conversations decide month 3 from the band waypoints, but gives no signs → read → default move for the week-1 choice between posting and starting conversations, and no run on a case. Its third subsection, "Founding revenue pays for the editor", is a reserve-and-editor cost note that doesn't serve the section's claim. · strongest part: §6 The Month-3 Gate. It reads two legs in a fixed order, uses a replay-driven fix table ("what the replays show → it points at → the one change"), sets a founding-price row for every outcome, and ends on Reid's hard case, where praise, the signal that costs a buyer nothing, loses to the fix order.
- 10 · weakest part: §5 The Training-Partner Seat and Coming Back. It joins two topics loosely. The seat gets rules (one door each, no discount, no minor) but no mechanism beyond "a partner notices a skipped week" and no cap or minute arithmetic. Re-enrollment is one Sam vignette with no signs → read → default → left alone and no decision table, so the reader can't tell when a returning graduate should get the Hold, a restart, or the Program. · strongest part: §2 Round Two, the Maintenance Format. Measured momentum is a printed reading test with a reason for each part, the format is priced from its cadence, full intensity is split by whose miss it was, and Adrian's hard case lets the sheet written before money outrank three buying signals.
- 11 · weakest part: §6 The Risk Register. It's mostly a table. The reader gets six flags and advice triggers but no mechanism for how each risk arrives in this niche (a dispute from a charge he didn't recognize, an age restriction after a comparison short), no run on a case, and a closing subsection of two sentences. · strongest part: §2 The Protected Content Minimum and the De-Scoping Order. It gives the lag mechanism (a skipped video costs next month's leads), a fixed cut order with a reason at each step, a never-cut list, "two weeks at step 5 means capacity binds", and the breakout hard case.
- 12 · weakest part: §3 The Operator Review (558 words, the shortest deep section). The levels table is clear, but "a decision is any change that touches a joint" stays abstract. The section never runs one review on a case (the worked example carries that) and says nothing about a review where every number sits in range. · strongest part: §2 Small Numbers Lie and Denominator Discipline. It defines an event and a window precisely, reads single-digit rates through their stages, and runs two Cole cases: a page change read by stage, and a tool's denominator break that he dates and never splices.
- 13 · weakest part: §5 Round Two and the Hold at Capacity (348 words). After the LEDGER alignment it's a recap of 10's arithmetic plus the Seat Math-line rule and intensity creep. It has no case and no capacity decision of its own, such as how Seat Math splits minutes between back-end seats and Program Async seats at the $50k configuration. · strongest part: §3 The Parity Rule and the Premium Lane. Reid's "precision plan" runs through the arithmetic, the async-first seat is costed line by line to about $10k at parity, the weekly-call seat is refused on the credibility ceiling, and the Optimizer-surge hard case goes by the minutes.
