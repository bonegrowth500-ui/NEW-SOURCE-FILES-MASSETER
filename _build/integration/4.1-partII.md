## 4.2 nominations (per module)

- 04 · **Weakest part: §6 Packaging the Method, and the New-Creations Index.** As the target reader, I want to package my own offer: what to call the Program and its deliverables, and what goes on the page. The section gives one naming rule and one formula line (baseline day → … → graduation). Then two tables: an index of other modules' offers and a never-list. There is no run on a case (for example, Cole naming his rungs and deliverables, with one name rejected by the Hostile-Screenshot Test), no signs → read → default, and little mechanism for why a visible journey outsells a curriculum here.
  **Strongest part: §3 One Flagship, Two Buffers, One Net.** It builds slot, rung, and tier as a working vocabulary, then runs the three-question placement test. Three graduates' records pick their rungs, with measured momentum stated properly. The template-pack hard case prices the fold against one enrollment a month and ends on a default and what buyers keep.
- 05 · **Weakest part: §6 The One Recommendation (467 words, the shortest deep section).** It lists the four recommendations and recaps the Path and Timeline Card, the Warmth Ladder, the DM Handoff, and the Async Arc. It never shows how to choose between neighboring recommendations when a buyer sits on the line: Program vs Starter Path for a "not now" with a half-real constraint, or Private vs the priority tier for an Optimizer. Nor does it show how the recommendation is worded so it stays one while every tier is on the page. A reader who has just built the door gets its ending as a table.
  **Strongest part: §4 The Fit Check and the Affordability Question.** Mechanism comes first: a check after the money only decides refunds. The signal-pause table says what each part protects. The serial purchaser is told apart from the burned buyer. The owner text of the pause route is now complete. The hard case, a warm buyer who answers no, turns the answer into the rule and the unfinished-checkout note into its test.
- 06 · **Weakest part: §6 Seat Math and the Container.** The formula and its planning ranges are here, but the run on numbers lives only in the extras worksheet. The section has no signs → read → default for the moment Seat Math matters most early: measured minutes run over the line, turnaround starts slipping, and a waiting buyer asks for a seat. It also doesn't say what happens to the start already announced. The container table is sound but generic next to the rest of the module.
  **Strongest part: §5 Milestones and Markers.** Marker design has six rules, each with its reason, a passing sheet, and a failing sheet. The week-6 hard case runs the three verdicts with the misdirected standard. The week-12 table now covers every adherence and marker combination, so a reader can settle any verdict and any refund without an argument.
- 07 · **Weakest part: §6 Refunds, Disputes, and Cash (463 words).** It gives sound rules (reply the same day, set a discreet descriptor, read claims by layer, hold reserves, keep other income) but no case. For an early operator, one dispute can breach a processor ratio. The reader needs one worked dispute: the charge, the evidence from his checkout acceptance and baseline-day record, and whether to contest or refund. He also needs a signs → read → default for when a refund beats a contest. The niche grounding is thin next to §5.
  **Strongest part: §5 The Layered Guarantee.** Each layer states its trigger, decider, claim, payout, and timeline before payment. The three stops outside the windows now match LEDGER D and the trigger rule. The Collectability Test takes apart a real-world "extend until you see results" clause and rewrites it into one a burned buyer can collect. Theo's case shows the low end getting paid.
- 08 · **Weakest part: §5 The Fill History and Seat Status (692 words).** The status-threshold rule (state the count once seats left reach your usual last-fortnight intake) is good but never run on numbers in the section. The founding-seats subsection leans on 09 for how the group runs. The section's hard case is really about §4's step gate and the cash reserve rather than status or history. As the target reader in Early, with a history of [1] of [4], I want to know how to present a thin record so it builds trust rather than doubt.
  **Strongest part: §2 The Launch Line.** The problem comes first (every date pulls toward a launch), then the mechanism (information vs pressure), then the five tests shown before the name. A verdict table pairs slips with passes, and Reid's true Price Step fails three tests before the fix keeps every fact and changes the frame.
