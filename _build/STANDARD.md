# STANDARD CARD v3 — internal calibration (never quoted in the playbook)

Internal audit tool for drafting. It is calibrated one to two notches less strict than the source standard, and it records that calibration. **The playbook never refers to the calibration.** The playbook-facing text is `HOUSE_STANDARD.md`. Briefs and Standard Checks cite House Standard items by name ("Sell directly", "Close", "Name the stakes", "Use real dates", "Name the destination boldly", "Fight ideas, not people", "Build identity on evidence", "Present the price", "Say the true thing"), never "notch N". Internally these nine are called **licenses**. v3 applies DECISIONS R2.

## 1. Governing test — ceiling and floor

**The Informed-Client Test.**
- **Ceiling:** a client whose result fell anywhere in the range shown, including the low end, would still say the persuasion served him after seeing exactly what was done and why.
- **Floor:** letting a screened, able-to-pay, good-fit buyer talk himself out of the right decision fails him too.
- The floor applies only after the fit check and while no stop rule has fired. **Stop rules override the floor.**
- **Burned-buyer exception:** his "I need to think" is due diligence. Support it with the verification kit (sample plan, sample weekly review, Verify Page), make one firm recommendation, and let him choose the date.

Drafting consequence: an ambiguity doesn't default to restraint. It resolves to the license's default, and the stronger variant is used wherever its license holds.

## 3. Hard lines (no license moves these)

1. **Fabricated or borrowed proof**: undisclosed paid or invented testimonials; AI-generated or doctored results; unmatched before/afters presented as evidence; invented numbers; the operator's own face as evidence for the method.
2. **Universal, guaranteed, structural, or medical claims**: never claim or imply that habits produce visible adult skeletal change; never promise specific results to everyone; never imply coaching replaces dental, medical, or orthodontic care; never give a medical or dental opinion.
3. **Fake scarcity or urgency**: fake timers, invented seat counts, deadlines that don't hold.
4. **Shame and ridicule**: mocking real faces, circling strangers' "flaws", making a person's worth the problem.
5. **Exploiting vulnerability**: any selling to minors; pushing people who can't afford it after they've said so; selling into acute distress or fixation, including by automation; recovery pitches built on sunk spend.
6. **Credential misrepresentation**: implying qualifications the operator doesn't hold.

## 4. Claim ladder

Process → Educational → Observational → Outcome → Universal/Guaranteed/Medical.
- The backbone is process and educational.
- Observational and outcome claims carry the full context stack: who, what else changed, timeline, capture conditions, range, denominator, "observed, not caused".
- Universal, guaranteed, or medical: never.
- The adult-bone answer (the Outcome Map) is canon claim #1.

## 5. Force vs pressure — stop rules

- **Force (required where the floor applies):** a stake in his words, a confident recommendation, an explicit ask, resolving each real objection (up to two probes each), a real decision date.
- **Stop rules (override everything):**
  - A clear no ends the ask for that conversation.
  - "I can't afford it" ends money talk. Hand over the Starter Path once as a concrete next step, then stop.
  - Acute distress, fixation, or "everything is ruined" language means no sale and a referral conversation.
  - A minor ends it.
  - Follow-up follows the LEDGER rule.
  - **Stop rules bind automation.** The pause route stops sales sequences and date sends for 60–90 days, then re-permission.
- **Defensiveness changes the route, not the destination:** reflect, ask permission, then challenge with his own evidence.
- **Buyer-state routes:** burned buyers take the verification-first route; insecurity-led buyers take the dignity route.

## 6. The Launch Line

A date is an offer attribute, and allowed, only when all of these hold:
- the next start is always purchasable;
- the date comes from the calendar or the capacity math;
- content and email volume don't change as it approaches (one announcement + one reminder per start date, engaged segments only, never paused leads);
- there are no window-specific bonuses, prices, or lead-in sequences;
- no cart "closes" without a next date.

Anything else is a launch, and launches are excluded. There are no free live enrollment sessions; an optional *paid* group decision session is named for what it is ("cohort walkthrough and Q&A; the offer and price are inside").

## 7. Deliberate tightenings (stricter than the 1–2-notch posture, on purpose)

| Tightening | Why it stays strict |
|---|---|
| Fit Check tier 2 at booking of any paid step, plus the signal pause (no same-day payment, written adjusted expectations, ≥72 h cooling-off, no plan) | Appearance-concern prevalence among appearance-change seekers (hard line 5) |
| Affordability question before every paid step, in every channel | Hard line 5 |
| Pause route: stop rules bind automation | Hard line 5; the disclosure must not be harvested |
| Path and Timeline Card and Expectation Document before payment | Informed-Client ceiling: the full path, total cost, and review method are known before paying |
| Minors: education lane only; no email or intake answers from under-18s | Hard line 5; data minimization |
| The operator's face is never proof; no aspirational thumbnails using his own profile | Hard line 1 |
| Public numbers only from the operator's records | Hard line 1 |
| Plain-Language Rule (no diagnose/treat/protocol/clinical titles), also in the playbook's own analysis | Hard lines 2 and 6; vocabulary leaks into client talk |
| Surgery or filler never an anchor | Hard line 2 |
| No countdown widgets even for real dates | Grift-coded for this audience |
| No decoys | Informed-Client ceiling |
| Sunk spend never an anchor, and never near the price | Hard line 5 (recovery-scam pattern) |
| Credit windows are never deadlines | A sunk-fee deadline fails the ceiling for the low-end client |
| Commitments private by default; consistency devices, testimonial asks, and written reasons never timed to refund rights | Evidence (public identity announcements can reduce follow-through); refund integrity |
| Review disclosure (how reviews are made; where tools help) | A ceiling failure if discovered later |
| Photos coach-only; pseudonymous, camera-optional cohorts; free baseline stays on his device; keywords by DM | Privacy is part of the product |
| No free live enrollment sessions | Launch Line (spec exclusion) |

## 8. Sub-checks

- **Dignity Check**: after this framing, does he feel more capable or more defective?
- **Hostile-Screenshot Test**: would this line survive being screenshotted by the category's harshest critic?
- **Payoff Test**: does every curiosity device pay off in the same asset?
- **Collectability Test**: could a client who did the work actually collect on the guarantee (fit window, exit right, non-response clause, plan-usefulness refund)?
- **Launch Line test**: section 6.

## 9. Module audit questions

- **Ceiling:** would a client anywhere in the range shown, including the low end, endorse this in hindsight?
- **Floor:** is each license used at its default wherever it applies, and its stronger variant wherever licensed? Did any good-fit buyer in the examples or scripts leave un-asked, un-challenged, or with the stake unnamed (burned buyers excepted, as defined)?
- **Hard lines:** zero instances, including in automation.
- **Aim:** is force aimed at the situation and the wasted months, never at his worth or his face?
- **Launch Line:** passed.
- **Numbers:** every number matches LEDGER; public-facing examples use bracketed placeholders.
