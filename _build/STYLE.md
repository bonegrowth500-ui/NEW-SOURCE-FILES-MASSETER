# STYLE v2 — House rules for every module

## Voice (blend)
- Second person, operator-to-operator ("you"). Calm authority with conviction; direct, never hypey; optimistic because the plan is sound, not because outcomes are promised.
- Explain *why* before *what*; every recommendation carries its reasoning.
- Scannable on top: clear `##`/`###` headers, bolded named frameworks, bullets for variants and options, tables where comparison is the point, short paragraphs (2–5 sentences). No walls of text, and no bullet-only skeletons either.
- Plain language. Niche vocabulary is used correctly and defined once in each module, at first use.
- Theory plus application: name the psychological or economic mechanism, then show exactly how it works in this niche.

## Default cast (for examples, scripts, and composites)
- **Default protagonist:** the employed Self-Taught Struggler, mid-20s (range 19–28): months of solo effort, nothing measured, grift-fatigued, able to pay from monthly income.
- **Other buyer states** (route by state, not script): the **Optimizer** (25–35; speed, precision, privacy, status); the **Burned Struggler** (has bought before and been let down; verification first); the **Ambivalent** (sees the need, keeps deferring).
- Eligibility is the age fork plus one affordability question, never student status. Women and buyers over 30 appear as welcome, not targeted. Minors appear only in the education lane and referral scenarios.
- Composites are labeled as composites. Numbers inside examples use bracketed placeholders or LEDGER ranges, never invented results.

## Niche grounding and the Technique Firewall
- Every module is about a facial-development coaching business: the buyers, the category's grift history, the adult "is it too late?" question, stalls, skepticism, photos, measurement, presentation.
- **Technique Firewall:** the method is never taught. In examples and scripts, method content appears only as bracketed placeholders ("[first adjustment]", "[weekly habit block]") or at category level ("a consistency problem, not a technique problem"). Topics appear as titles and angles, never as instructions. Packaging the method (naming, structure, deliverables, capture standard) is in scope.
- **The Outcome Map governs every outcome sentence.** Adults: habits, body composition, posture and breathing *habits*, grooming, presentation, and how they're photographed are changeable and measurable. Visible jaw or profile change from habits is debated, and is presented at its evidence tier. Habit-driven skeletal change is never claimed. "Bone growth" and "mewing" are category search terms the brand explains; they are never its promise or its identity.
- Referral triggers are named where relevant (sleep or snoring signs, jaw pain, bite concerns, distress or fixation, minors) and never elaborated into health advice.

## Named frameworks
- Frameworks get memorable, distinct names (Title Case, bolded at first use), registered in `_build/REGISTRY.md`, which becomes the Glossary.
- **Glass-box naming:** every name must pass the Hostile-Screenshot Test. A buyer who saw it would find it accurate and unoffensive (eligible-adult yield, Starter Path, Pushback Signal, Plain-Language Rule — never "sellable adults", "down-path", "defense meter", "control lexicon").
- Present frameworks as **options plus a default pick** wherever a real choice exists: the variants, when to pick each, then the recommended default.

## Numbers and evidence
- Ranges with context, always: "typically X–Y; higher when…, lower when…". No false precision, and no single-point statistics except fixed process facts (cadence, turnaround, number of reviews). Every number matches `_build/LEDGER.md`.
- Planning ranges are presented as planning ranges, to be replaced by the operator's own data after ~30 events. None is ever framed as a result the brand has achieved.
- Research is synthesized in the playbook's own words: no citations, no study names, no author names. Weak or contested effects are described as small or conditional. No pop neuroscience.
- Outcome language follows "observed, not caused": no causal verbs for appearance change.

## Banned phrasing
- **Source-relative comparisons.** No sentence may compare this playbook, its standard, or its advice to any earlier material. Grep before every commit for: "anymore", "no longer", "newly", "than before", "looser", "unlike before", "this time", "as you learned", "previously", "the old way". A hit is allowed only when it describes the buyer or the business itself (for example "no longer second-guessing himself").
- No mention of the source files or any "prior program", course, or blueprint.
- Clinical or therapeutic vocabulary (diagnose, treat, therapy, healing, patient, protocol) is out. Use assess, review, baseline, decision, plan, block, record, habit, presentation, capture standard.

## Module template (hybrid) — finalized in Step 2.6
1. Title plus a one-paragraph opening frame: what this module changes in how you think or operate.
2. Deep numbered `##` sections, each running mechanism → reasoning → application in the niche.
3. Named frameworks with variants and a default pick.
4. Worked examples: realistic composite niche cases walked end to end.
5. Extras chosen per module type: scripts and swipe copy for persuasion, sales, and content modules; checklists and templates for business and ops modules; action steps in every module.
6. **Stage Notes**: Early / Growing / Scaling (as defined in LEDGER A2), covering what changes at each stage and which constraint binds.
7. **Standard Check** (brief): how the module's tactics sit against the House Standard (ceiling and floor, relevant licenses, lines, stop rules).
8. **Quick Reference**: summary, key takeaways, and a framework cheat sheet.

## Hard rules
- Cross-references between modules are allowed ("see Module 9"), sparingly. Each module stays self-contained.
- The exclusions never appear as recommendations: faceless strategy, launch campaigns, operator mindset or self-help, podcasts and collabs, techniques and protocols, a free community.
- AI tools: minimal (the House Standard line). Legal and risk: minimal, with one owning module holding the biggest-risk flags and one line elsewhere where relevant.
- Operations stay a floor: at most two business modules plus sidebars; at most six metrics per stage plus guardrails.
- Length ~7,000 words (audit band 6,300–7,700).
