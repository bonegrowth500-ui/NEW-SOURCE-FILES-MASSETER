# The House Standard (playbook-facing text) · v2

Source text for every module's Standard Check and for the Intro's statement of the playbook's standard. Written natively and in absolute terms. It never mentions a calibration, a notch, or any earlier standard. Modules cite its items by name (for example, "Build identity on evidence" or "the line on structural claims"), never by number. STANDARD.md stays internal.

---

## Why a standard is a growth asset

In this category, trust is the scarcest thing you sell against. Buyers have seen faked before-and-afters, devices that did nothing, and a vocabulary that promised bone and delivered nothing measurable. So they audit everything. A standard they can watch working is your moat: it lets you sell harder than the grifters, because a skeptic can see exactly what you're doing and why.

## The test: the Informed-Client Test

Every tactic in this playbook passes one test, which works in both directions.

- **The ceiling.** Picture a client whose result landed anywhere in the range you showed him, including the low end. He sees exactly what you did and why. Does he still say it served him? If a tactic only works while he can't see it, it's out.
- **The floor.** Suppose a screened, able-to-pay, good-fit buyer talks himself out of the right decision because you wouldn't challenge his story, name the stake, or ask. You failed him, just as surely as pressure would have.

The floor applies only after the fit check, and only while no stop rule has fired. Stop rules always win. With a buyer who has been burned before, the floor means something specific. His "I need to think" is due diligence, so support it: give him the verification kit, make one firm recommendation, and let him choose the date.

## Force, and where it's aimed

Force is welcome here. Aim every ounce of it at the situation: the wasted months, the guessing, the advice sold without a measurement system. Never aim it at his worth or his face.

- **Force:** a stake he states in his own words; a confident recommendation; an explicit ask; resolving each real objection he raises (up to two probes each); a real decision date.
- **Pressure (never):** asking again after a clear no; reopening money after "I can't afford it"; selling into distress or fixation; invented urgency; shame.

When he gets defensive, change the route, not the destination. Reflect his case for waiting, ask permission to challenge it, then challenge it with his own evidence: his months, his record, his words.

## Nine things we do on purpose

1. **Sell directly.** Every warm asset names the offer, who it's for and who it isn't for, the public price, and one next step. Go further with dedicated offer content and pitch sections in decision-stage videos. Once the door has verified age and fit, a direct message may carry one recommendation and a checkout link. Never close an unverified contact in DMs.
2. **Close.** Close by contract: agree the structure and a clear yes-or-no outcome up front, excavate in his words, summarize his goal, cost, and need back to him, recommend one option, state the price once and hold the silence, ask, resolve each real objection, agree a decision date, and book day one before hanging up. Go further when it's earned: challenge his story with his own evidence, and name what "I need to think" is hiding. With a burned buyer, "I need to think" is due diligence, not a hiding place.
3. **Name the stakes.** Ask the implication question and let him name the cost. Reflect his months, the money at stake from here on, and his missed moments back to him, plainly. What he has already spent is part of understanding his situation; it never appears when you recommend or price. Future-pace the record, not the face: the year that repeats the last twelve months of guessing, set beside the year where he knows. Choose the frame for accuracy, never for fear. Stakes about anatomy, structure, or how he looks are out. With an insecurity-led buyer, take the dignity route: keep stakes to time, money from here on, and guessing, stop the laddering at the destination, and keep the recommendation firm.
4. **Use real dates.** Monthly starts, seat caps from real capacity, announced price steps. State them plainly and turn them into his personal decision date on the call. State seat status as fact when it's informative, and publish the fill history after each start closes. Never use countdown widgets or hype. A credit he has already paid for is never a deadline.
5. **Name the destination boldly.** He wants to be taken seriously, to be at ease in photos and rooms, to know instead of hoping. Say so vividly, then name the obstacle and the plan in the same breath. Every asset that names the destination also carries one line placing it beyond the face: presentation, how he's photographed, knowing, and the parts of life no program touches. Outcome claims come only from your own records and carry their range, context, and denominator. Never imply his face is the cause of what he wants.
6. **Fight ideas, not people.** Go after the grift patterns (manufactured proof, device grift, endless tips) and the "it's all genetics" shrug, pointedly if you like. Steelman first. Teach the honest-evidence test so buyers can judge anyone, you included. Never name or mock individuals, never comment on the features of people who haven't consented, and treat clinicians as referral destinations, not enemies.
7. **Build identity on evidence.** The group is **adults who measure instead of guess**. Membership is a practice: logs kept, captures on schedule, decisions made from the record. Anyone on the Starter Path who keeps that practice is a member. The rituals are the review and the decision at baseline, the re-captures, and graduation. Every label rests on his own record, and standards, not price, signal seriousness. Commitments go to his reviewer and stay private by default. Sharing, and leaderboards on logged process, are opt-in and stay inside the closed cohort. Never "real men don't quit", never mockery of outsiders.
8. **Present the price.** Show the real tiers, premium first. Recommend one. State the price once without apology. Itemize what he actually gets instead of inventing a "total value". Every tier is real and bought by someone. Every price step names what was added, and none takes a published deliverable away. Never anchor on surgery, filler, hourly rates, or what he has already spent.
9. **Say the true thing.** Name the desire and the frustration plainly, and validate plainly. When it's earned, say the hard thing with force: "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem, not you." Never turn insecurity into shame.

## Six lines we never cross

1. **No fabricated or borrowed proof.** No undisclosed paid or invented testimonials, no AI-made or doctored results, no unmatched before-and-afters, no invented numbers. Your own face is never evidence for the method.
2. **No universal, guaranteed, structural, or medical claims.** Never claim or imply that habits produce visible skeletal change in adults. Never promise the same result to everyone. Never imply coaching replaces dental, medical, or orthodontic care. Never give a medical or dental opinion.
3. **No fake scarcity.** No fake timers, invented seat counts, or deadlines that move.
4. **No shame or ridicule.** No mocking faces, no circling strangers' "flaws", no making a person's worth the problem.
5. **No exploiting vulnerability.** No selling to minors. No pushing people who've said they can't afford it. No selling into distress or fixation. No recovery pitches built on money he has already lost.
6. **No borrowed credentials.** Never imply qualifications you don't hold.

## The claim ladder

Process → educational → observational → outcome → universal/guaranteed/medical.
- Build on process and educational claims.
- Observational and outcome claims carry the full context: who, what else changed, the timeline, capture conditions, the range, and the denominator. They are observed, not caused.
- The top rung is never used.

## Stop rules

- A clear no ends the ask for that conversation.
- "I can't afford it" ends money talk. Hand over the Starter Path as a concrete next step, once, then stop.
- Distress, fixation, or "everything is ruined" language means no sale and a referral conversation, before or during the program.
- A minor ends it.
- Follow-up is fixed: a written recap within 24 hours, one check-in on the agreed decision date, and one close-the-loop message.
- **Stop rules bind automation too.** A distress answer, "I can't afford it", or a fit-check pause sets a route that stops every sales sequence and date announcement for that person. The route records no reason.

## Real dates, not launches

A date is part of the offer when the next start can always be bought, the date comes from the calendar or the capacity math, nothing about your content or email volume changes as it approaches, there are no window-only bonuses or prices, and nothing "closes" without a next date. That's the only kind of deadline this playbook uses.

## House rules

- **Minors.** An age fork at the door ("legal adult where you live"). Under-18s get public education and an orthodontist referral, nothing else: no email capture, no intake, no calls, no offers, no parent-facing pitches.
- **The fit check.** Before any paid step, a short, plain-language fit check. Any signal means:
  - no payment that day;
  - adjusted expectations in writing;
  - at least 72 hours to cool off;
  - no payment plan.

  The possible outcomes are to enroll with adjusted expectations, the Starter Path, or a referral. For checking or fixation, it's a referral and reading-only content, with no capture tools. It is never a label and never a clinical instrument.
- **The affordability question.** Before every paid step, in every channel: "Is this comfortable from your own income or savings, without new credit or buy-now-pay-later?" A "no" ends money talk.
- **The path before payment.** Before he pays, he sees the whole path: what the program reliably delivers, when visible change tends to show, what the full path is likely to cost, and how reviews are made.
- **Your face is not the proof.** Your appearance is never evidence for the method. Publish what your face does and doesn't prove, and answer appearance attacks with the measurement system.
- **Numbers.** Public numbers about performance, outcomes, or clients come only from your own records. Research figures appear only when attributed at their evidence level, and never as your results.
- **Observed, not caused.** Published ranges say "observed among clients", list the confounders, sit beside a comparison where one exists, and never use causal verbs.
- **Measure behavior often, appearance rarely.** Behavior is logged and reviewed weekly. Standardized photos are taken at baseline, around week 6, at week 12, and then quarterly, and never in between. A free baseline taken before purchase stays on his own device.
- **Privacy is part of the product.** Before purchase, your site says who sees photos, where they're stored, which tools touch his check-ins, and how deletion works. Photos are seen only by the coach. Cohorts can be pseudonymous and camera-optional. Fit-check answers stay out of marketing tools. Keywords arrive by direct message, never as public comments. Anything is deleted on request. Marketing use needs separate, revocable consent. Sender and billing names are discreet.
- **Plain language.** The vocabulary is assess, review, baseline, decision, plan, block, record, habit, presentation. Clinical words ("diagnose", "treat", "protocol", "patient") and clinical titles are out, and so are analyzing structure and scoring faces.
- **AI assists behind the scenes, never speaks as you, and clients are told where it helps.** No AI avatars, cloned voices, or AI-made or AI-edited faces or results. Client consent comes before any tool processes a check-in.
- **Guarantee what you control, and make it collectable.** Statutory rights come first. Then:
  - **Fit window.** A full refund. The conversation about it is optional and feedback-only, and there's no re-pitch.
  - **Service guarantee on your inputs.** Review turnaround, the written week-6 read, the week-12 re-assessment, and the Hold and Private deliverables.
  - **Week-6 exit right.** A client who did the work and asks to stop gets a pro-rata refund.
  - **Week-12 non-response clause.** A cash refund when pre-agreed markers haven't moved despite his effort.
  - **The assessment.** Refunded if its plan wasn't useful.

  Never guarantee appearance. Never time a commitment device, a testimonial ask, or his written reasons to his refund rights.

## The five checks

Run these on every asset, script, and offer:
1. **Informed-Client Test:** ceiling and floor.
2. **Dignity Check:** does he leave more capable or more defective?
3. **Hostile-Screenshot Test:** would it survive the category's harshest critic?
4. **Payoff Test:** does every curiosity device pay off in the same asset?
5. **Collectability Test:** could a client who did the work actually collect on the guarantee?
