# The House Standard (playbook-facing text)

Source text for every module's Standard Check and for the playbook's own statement of its standard. It is written natively and in absolute terms. It never mentions a calibration, a notch, or any earlier standard. Modules quote or paraphrase it freely; STANDARD.md stays internal.

---

## Why a standard is a growth asset

In this category, trust is the scarcest thing you sell against. Buyers have seen faked before-and-afters, devices that did nothing, and a vocabulary that promised bone and delivered nothing measurable. So they audit everything. A standard they can watch working is your moat: it lets you sell harder than the grifters, because a skeptic can see exactly what you're doing and why. Selling in the open is the only persuasion that compounds here.

## The test: the Informed-Client Test

Every tactic in this playbook passes one test, which works in both directions.

- **The ceiling.** Picture a client whose result landed anywhere in the range you showed him, including the low end. He sees exactly what you did and why. Does he still say it served him? If a tactic only works while he can't see it, it's out.
- **The floor.** Suppose a screened, able-to-pay, good-fit buyer talks himself out of the right decision because you wouldn't challenge his story, name the stake, or ask. You failed him, just as surely as pressure would have. Under-selling a good-fit buyer is a failure of care, not a virtue.

The floor applies only after the fit check, and only while no stop rule has fired. Stop rules always win.

## Force, and where it's aimed

Force is welcome here. Aim every ounce of it at the situation: the wasted months, the guessing, the advice sold without a measurement system. Never aim it at his worth or his face. What separates force from pressure:

- **Force:** a stake he states in his own words; a confident recommendation; an explicit ask; resolving each real objection he raises (up to two probes each); a real decision date.
- **Pressure (never):** asking again after a clear no; reopening money after "I can't afford it"; selling into distress or fixation; invented urgency; shame.

When he gets defensive, change the route, not the destination. Reflect his case for waiting, ask permission to challenge it, then challenge it with his own evidence: his logs, his timeline, his words.

## Nine things we do on purpose

1. **Sell directly.** Every warm asset names the offer, who it's for and who it isn't for, the public price, and one next step. Go further with dedicated offer content and pitch sections in decision-stage videos. Once the door has verified age and fit, a direct message may carry one recommendation and a checkout link. Never close an unverified contact in DMs.
2. **Close.** Close by contract: agree the structure and a clear yes-or-no outcome up front, excavate in his words, summarize his goal, cost, and need back to him, recommend one option, state the price once and hold the silence, ask, resolve each real objection, agree a decision date, and book day one before hanging up. Go further when it's earned: challenge his story with his own evidence, and name what "I need to think" is hiding.
3. **Name the stakes.** Ask the implication question and let him name the cost. Reflect his months, money, and missed moments back to him, plainly. Future-pace the record, not the face: set the year that repeats the last twelve months of guessing beside the year where he knows. Choose the frame for accuracy, never for fear. Stakes about anatomy, structure, or how he looks are out.
4. **Use real dates.** Cohort starts, credit windows, seat caps with a public fill record, announced price steps. State them plainly and turn them into his personal decision date on the call. Seat status is stated as fact ("this start: 5 of 8 seats taken"), never with countdown widgets or hype.
5. **Name the destination boldly.** He wants to be taken seriously, to be at ease in photos and rooms, to know instead of hoping. Say so vividly, then name the obstacle and the plan in the same breath. Outcome claims come only from your own records and always carry their range, context, and denominator. Never imply his face is the cause of what he wants.
6. **Fight ideas, not people.** Go after the grift patterns (manufactured proof, device grift, endless tips) and the "it's all genetics" shrug, pointedly if you like. Steelman first. Teach the buying test only an honest operator passes. Never name or mock individuals, never comment on the features of people who haven't consented, and treat clinicians as referral destinations, not enemies.
7. **Build identity on evidence.** The group is **adults who measure instead of guess**. Being adult, fit-checked, and logging is the status. The rituals are baseline day, the re-captures, and graduation. Every label rests on his own record. Commitments go to his reviewer and stay private by default; sharing inside the closed cohort is opt-in. Never "real men don't quit," never mockery of outsiders.
8. **Present the price.** Show the real tiers, premium first. Recommend one. State the price once without apology. Itemize what he actually gets instead of inventing a "total value." Every tier is real and bought by someone. Never anchor on surgery, filler, hourly rates, or what he has already spent.
9. **Say the true thing.** Name the desire and the frustration plainly, and validate plainly. When it's earned, say the hard thing with force: "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem — not you." Never turn insecurity into shame.

## Six lines we never cross

1. **No fabricated or borrowed proof.** No undisclosed paid or invented testimonials, no AI-made or doctored results, no unmatched before-and-afters, no invented numbers. Your own face is never evidence for the method.
2. **No universal, guaranteed, structural, or medical claims.** Never claim or imply that habits produce visible skeletal change in adults. Never promise the same result to everyone. Never imply coaching replaces dental, medical, or orthodontic care. Never diagnose.
3. **No fake scarcity.** No fake timers, invented seat counts, or deadlines that move.
4. **No shame or ridicule.** No mocking faces, no circling strangers' "flaws," no making a person's worth the problem.
5. **No exploiting vulnerability.** No selling to minors. No pushing people who've said they can't afford it. No selling into distress or fixation. No recovery pitches built on money he has already lost.
6. **No borrowed credentials.** Never imply qualifications you don't hold.

## The claim ladder

Process → educational → observational → outcome → universal/guaranteed/medical.
- Build on process and educational claims.
- Observational and outcome claims carry the full context: who, what else changed, the timeline, capture conditions, the range, and the denominator. They are observed, not caused.
- The top rung is never used.

## Stop rules

- A clear no ends the ask for that conversation.
- "I can't afford it" ends money talk: name the Starter Path once, then stop.
- Distress, fixation, or "everything is ruined" language means no sale and a referral conversation, before or during the program.
- A minor ends it.
- Follow-up is fixed: a written recap within 24 hours, one check-in on the agreed decision date, and one close-the-loop message.

## Real dates, not launches

A date is part of the offer when the next start can always be bought, the date comes from the calendar or the capacity math, nothing about your content or email volume changes as it approaches, there are no window-only bonuses or prices, and nothing "closes" without a next date. That's the only kind of deadline this playbook uses. The first group is your founding group, sold through the standing door.

## House rules

- **Minors.** An age fork at the door ("legal adult where you live"). Under-18s get public education and an orthodontist referral, nothing else: no email capture, no intake, no calls, no offers, no parent-facing pitches.
- **The fit check.** Before any paid step, a short, plain-language fit check. A signal pauses the sale for a human conversation, with three possible outcomes: enroll with adjusted expectations, the Starter Path, or a referral. It is never a label and never a clinical instrument.
- **Your face is not the proof.** Your appearance is never evidence for the method. Publish what your face does and doesn't prove, and answer appearance attacks with the measurement system.
- **Numbers.** Public numbers about performance, outcomes, or clients come only from your own records. Research figures appear only when attributed at their evidence level, and never as your results.
- **Observed, not caused.** Published ranges say "observed among clients," list the confounders, sit beside a comparison where one exists, and never use causal verbs.
- **Measure behavior often, appearance rarely.** Behavior is logged and reviewed weekly. Standardized photos are taken at baseline, around week 6, at week 12, and then quarterly, and never in between.
- **Privacy is part of the product.** Photos are seen only by the coach. Cohorts can be pseudonymous and camera-optional. Fit-check answers stay out of marketing tools. Anything is deleted on request. Marketing use needs separate consent that can be revoked. Sender and billing names are discreet.
- **Plain language.** The vocabulary is assess, review, baseline, decision, plan, block, record, habit, presentation. Never diagnose, treat, analyze structure, score faces, or use clinical titles.
- **AI assists; it never appears.** AI can help draft, clip, transcribe, and summarize under your claim approval. No AI avatars, cloned voices, or AI-made or AI-edited faces or results.
- **Guarantee what you control.** Statutory rights first. Then a fit window with a full refund after a short conversation. Then a service guarantee on your inputs (review turnaround, the written week-6 read, the week-12 re-assessment), and a cash non-response clause tied to markers agreed in advance. Never guarantee appearance.

## The five checks

Run these on every asset, script, and offer:
1. **Informed-Client Test:** ceiling and floor.
2. **Dignity Check:** does he leave more capable or more defective?
3. **Hostile-Screenshot Test:** would it survive the category's harshest critic?
4. **Payoff Test:** does every curiosity device pay off in the same asset?
5. **Collectability Test:** could a client who did the work actually collect on the guarantee?
