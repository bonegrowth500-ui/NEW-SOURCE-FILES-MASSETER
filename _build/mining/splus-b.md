## 3. Positions & conflicts

Each item gives the position, where it sits, what it conflicts with, and the decision it forces. (a) marks a conflict with the other source; (b) marks a conflict with the new spec.

1. **Faceless-first, graduate later (6.1, 6.7, 6.8, 6.12).** "Faceless-then-graduate produces a stronger creator than face-from-day-one."
   - (a) The other source is face-first ("your own visual presentation is a walking testimonial"; "bring 20% more energy").
   - (b) The spec puts the operator on camera from day 1 and excludes faceless strategy.
   - **Decision:** retire all faceless and graduation content. Salvage the Three carriers, the Appearance-is-not-the-credential rule, and the calm-authority presence standard. Settle the energy question fresh: calm conviction as the default, more force when the stakes are true, never a hype register.

2. **Your face is not your credential (6.8) vs "walking testimonial" (other source).** The slice says never let your appearance become the basis of authority. The other source treats the coach's own look as proof.
   - This matters more now: a day-1 on-camera operator in a face niche will be judged on their face.
   - **Decision:** state a rule for what the operator's face and own results may prove. Suggested default: your face shows there is a person; it never proves the method unless it passes the Integrity Checklist with context. Also set a policy for appearance-based challenges in comments and on calls.

3. **YouTube's only conversion job is the free community; never sell coaching or the paid tier on YouTube (8 intro, 8.2, 8.13, 9.7).**
   - (a) The other source runs lead magnet → email → application → a 30-45 minute sales call for ~$2,500 packages, with no free community.
   - (b) The free community is permanently gone, and notch 1 permits selling paid offers straight from content.
   - **Decision:** design a new front door and nurture engine. Options: email-first with a lead magnet; a low-ticket digital product; a paid-community entry tier; an application to a call for 1:1 and group. Pick a default, and decide what each content type routes to.

4. **"Never pitch the deep offer cold" and "don't spend proof-trust on a cold high-ticket ask" (7.8, 8.2, 8.9).** Still sound for cold traffic, but written as a near-blanket rule, with the paid pitch always downstream.
   - (b) Notch 1 permits "explicit offer pitches in content when the audience's warmth justifies it".
   - **Decision:** define warmth signals that license a direct paid ask. Examples: a Struggler-state video, returning viewers, email subscribers, a completed application. Define which assets may pitch which offer.

5. **CTAs promise a process or place, "never an outcome" (8.11); "say slightly less than the image appears to show" (7.10).**
   - (b) Notch 5 permits bolder outcome framing with context, testimonial-led content, and naming deeper desires such as confidence or liking photos of yourself.
   - **Decision:** split the rule. Visual and structural outcomes stay under-claimed with Level 2-3 integrity. Experiential outcomes (confidence, clarity, consistency, being taken seriously) may be framed confidently with context: who, what else changed, timeline, typical range. Guaranteed, universal and structural outcomes stay forbidden.

6. **"Any form of pressure" is forbidden at the ask; "No pressure — it's there if it's useful" (8.1, 8.11).**
   - (a) The other source runs launch urgency and a close-oriented call structure.
   - (b) Notches 2-3 permit asking for the decision, surfacing the real objection behind "I need to think about it", challenging self-stories, and true cost-of-inaction framing.
   - **Decision:** define legitimate force vs pressure. Legitimate force is true stakes, direct asks, and confident recommendations. Pressure is fake scarcity, shame, exploiting distress, or pushing after an "I can't afford it". Write sales-call scripts that pass the Informed-Client Test.

7. **Hype editing is emotional overclaiming; never let pace climb toward the payoff; restraint is the mechanism of conversion (6.2, 6.10, 8.11).**
   - (a) The other source's "+20% energy" and its power-word lists (urgency, "don't miss", "before it's gone").
   - (b) The looser card adds force.
   - **Decision:** keep the ban on edit-manufactured urgency and on register breaks. Allow energy and conviction proportional to true stakes. Treat "restraint converts better" as a testable hypothesis, not doctrine.

8. **Visual proof goes late, sparing, heavily contextualized; "the grifter's order" is to lead with transformations (7.1, 7.4, 7.9).**
   - (a) The other source leans heavily on before/afters: "Dramatic before/after transformations (visual proof, stops scroll)"; thumbnails with "arrows pointing to features, side profiles"; "Analyze facial structure — celebrities, before/afters".
   - **Decision:** under the looser card, testimonial-led content is allowed. Before/afters must meet Level 1 at minimum and Level 2 by default. Arrows and annotations that exaggerate are banned (overclaim-by-annotation). Celebrity or stranger face analysis falls under hard line 4 and should be retired.

9. **Proof of thinking beats proof of results (7.3, 7.6, 7.9); "prove the judgment always".**
   - (a) The other source treats client results as "the most powerful credibility builder" and models positioning such as "documented over 100 client transformations".
   - **Decision:** settle the proof mix per stage. Early: judgment plus research literacy plus honest limits. Growing: add contextualized case and outcome ranges. Scaling: aggregated results with ranges. Flag the other source's "100 transformations" style as usable only if real and documented.

10. **Scarcity appears only as a forbidden move (false scarcity on a free offer) (8.1, 8.6, 8.11).**
    - (a) The other source has a launch module with a real-vs-fake urgency section; launch campaigns are excluded in the new spec.
    - (b) Notch 4 permits real scarcity: capacity limits, cohort dates, enrollment windows, real price increases.
    - **Decision:** write evergreen real-scarcity mechanics (solo capacity math, cohort calendars, waitlists) without drifting into launch-campaign content.

11. **The gravitational center is YouTube long-form feeding the community (9.7); the success metric is community joins (8.2, 10.2 Lens C).**
    - (a) The other source says "Your email list is your most valuable asset. Build it from day one with a compelling lead magnet."
    - (b) Spec: no community, business-led, email in scope, $25-50k/month profit target.
    - **Decision:** choose the new center, e.g. YouTube long-form as the trust consolidator plus the email list as the owned conversion center. Replace "joins per qualified view" with revenue- and profit-linked north-star metrics.

12. **Email capture is just one optional bridge variant, "for colder traffic ... or as insurance against platform risk" (8.7).**
    - (a) The other source makes email central.
    - (b) Without a community, email is the main owned nurture channel.
    - **Decision:** promote email capture from an option to the likely default bridge for everything except High-Intent traffic.

13. **Shorts never convert directly; that is "a category error" (9.1, 9.4).**
    - (a) The other source routes about 15% of short-form to lead magnets ("Free guide in bio").
    - **Decision:** keep "no high-ticket ask from a Short". Permit low-friction asks (lead magnet, follow, email) and offer mentions to warm followers under notch 1.

14. **"Pushing paid before free violates the ecosystem sequence" (8.9).**
    - (b) There is no free tier any more; the ladder is a blank slate and could open with a paid front door (low-ticket product or paid community).
    - **Decision:** retire this anti-pattern and replace it with "never ask for a rung the buyer isn't standing on", which is warmth-based, not price-based.

15. **Offer-readiness as a late graduation trigger ("your community and coaching are live") and the long-game patience thesis (6.7, 10.9).** Revenue comes late, after the audience.
    - (b) The spec is stage-agnostic, lists "faster path to profit" as a success criterion, and excludes operator mindset.
    - **Decision:** sequence offers so 1:1 revenue can start early, before audience scale, while content compounds. Present patience as asset math, never as a mindset lesson.

    - (b) The spec is solo plus freelancers, with no team-building.
    - **Decision:** adapt it to a freelancer-supported cadence. Keep brand-as-container for asset value, but don't plan a team.

17. **"Plus track record and credentials" as results-rich competence proof (7.3).**
    - (b) The operator has no credentials; hard line 6 forbids implying qualifications.
    - **Decision:** remove credentials from all proof ladders. Use research literacy, demonstrated judgment, transparent method and documented client work as the competence stack.

18. **The faceless amplifier: a restrained faceless voice is "believed more readily" (6.6, 6.10, 7.10, 8.11, 9.10).**
    - (b) Its premise is gone.
    - **Decision:** replace it with named accountability. Claims now carry the operator's face and brand name permanently, which raises the reputational stakes of any overclaim.

## 4. Gaps for the new direction

What a business-led solo operator (no free community, on camera from day 1, hybrid offers, $25-50k/month profit, persuasion mastery including sales conversations and client transformation) needs that this slice does NOT provide.

**Business & offers (the lead theme; almost entirely absent)**
1. **Offer ladder design.** The slice treats offers as "downstream, not YouTube's job". There is nothing on what the rungs are (1:1, group, paid community, digital products, new creations), how they connect, or what the hybrid profit engine looks like.
2. **Front door without a free community.** The whole conversion architecture assumes a free-community nurture layer. There is no replacement design: email nurture sequences, a lead magnet, a low-ticket product, a paid-community entry tier, a free assessment or application. There is also no guidance on which content routes to which door.
3. **Pricing and pricing psychology.** No prices, anchoring, tiering or decoys, payment plans, conditional guarantees, value stacking or premium framing (notch 8). The "parasocial premium" line (6.7) is the only pricing idea, and it is undeveloped.
4. **Unit economics and profit math.** No CAC, LTV, contribution margin, capacity math (hours per client at 20-25 hrs/week), freelancer cost lines, or path-to-$25-50k/month profit. No benchmark conversion rates at all: view-to-lead, lead-to-call, show, close.
5. **Real scarcity mechanics.** Only fake scarcity is covered. Missing: honest capacity limits for a solo operator, cohort start dates, waitlists, and real price increases (notch 4) without launch-campaign content.
6. **Signature system / packaging.** No guidance on naming and packaging the method into a signature offer (light scope in the spec), or on the brand-as-IP moat.
7. **Operations.** Only "delegate editing, thumbnails, clips + SOPs". No time budget by activity (content vs sales vs delivery), freelancer hiring, briefs, QA or cost control, and no automation of intake, booking or payments.
8. **Legal and risk flags (minimal but needed).**
   - Testimonial and endorsement disclosure rules.
   - Consent and releases for client photos and video.
   - Health- and structural-claim exposure.
   - A minors policy: the core audience starts at 17, and hard line 5 forbids pressuring minors, so sales need an age gate or parental policy.
   - Refund terms.

**Persuasion (sales conversations and client transformation absent; content persuasion partial)**

   - Application and qualification design.

   - Surfacing the real objection behind "I need to think about it".
   - Ethical closing lines, and follow-up without pressure.
   - DM-to-call conversational selling.

12. **Client transformation.** No onboarding, adherence and habit systems, progress tracking, expectation-setting with honest ranges, handling a client's plateau live, retention/renewal, referral engineering, or a testimonial-collection pipeline.
