# THESES v2 — First-principles foundation (Step 1.4, revised after 5-lens critique)

Working file (removed at ship). The intellectual backbone every module stands on. v2 integrates the adversarial critique (operator, spec, evidence, buyer, persuasion-force lenses — see `critique/` and DECISIONS R1). Nothing here is cited in the playbook; everything is rewritten natively.

Evidence tags: **S** strong · **M** moderate · **W** weak · **D** derived · **C** contested · **E** estimate (the operator's own data replaces it) · **H** hypothesis to test. "Transfer" = evidence from an adjacent domain applied by analogy.

---

## 0. Master thesis

**In a category built on faked before/afters, a discredited vocabulary, and slow, bounded outcomes, the stalled adult wants what no one will give him: the end of guessing. Sell him that — a straight answer about what can and can't move for him, a plan he can verify, and weekly review that keeps him on it — delivered in scarce minutes, proven by honest measurement, and asked for with conviction. Aim every ounce of force at the wasted months, never at his face.**

- **He buys a destination.** Being taken seriously, ease in photos and rooms, no longer second-guessing himself, knowing instead of hoping. The vehicle (judgment, structure, measurement, review) is *why he believes this path reaches it*. Name the destination plainly; frame it experientially, never structurally.
- **Desire is abundant but misdirected.** Fantasies of an idealized face sell clicks and drain effort; concrete expectations tied to things he controls sell programs and sustain effort. The persuasion job is redirection: name the desire, then the obstacle and the plan in the same breath. Never claim his face is the cause of what he wants.
- **The core promise is one the operator controls:** by the end of the work, from his own measured record, he will know what is moving, what isn't, and what to do next. That promise can be guaranteed; a new face cannot.

## 1. Organizing models

### 1a. The Five Scarcities (and why each bites harder in this niche)

| Scarcity | What's scarce | Why it bites harder here | Master move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | Eligible adults (18+, fit, able to pay) who know the brand exists | The category's raw attention skews young; comparison and rating content pulls the least eligible viewers fastest | Package for adults; measure eligible-adult yield, not views; reply personally while volume is low |
| **Trust** | Belief that this operator is straight | Faked before/afters, device and course grift, and a discredited vocabulary have trained buyers to audit every claim | Make standards visible and costly to fake; calibrate every claim; answer the bone question straight |
| **Minutes** | The operator's live, one-to-one time | Review of an individual's record is the product; it doesn't scale by itself | Convert live one-to-one minutes into group and async forms without losing proactive human contact |
| **Evidence** | Proof that means something | Outcomes are slow, variable, confounded (weight, growth into the early 20s, camera distance), and easy to fake | Measure everyone from day one; behavior often, appearance rarely; ranges with denominators; "observed, not caused" |
| **Efficacy** | His belief that effort can work for him | The core buyer has tried for months and seen nothing measurable | Let him succeed at something verifiable early; raise self-efficacy hard, belief in the method only as far as evidence allows |

### 1b. The Constraint Sequence (which scarcity binds, by stage)

The binding constraint moves in a fixed order — **reach → conversations → care minutes**. Door design, price gates, call policy, and the north-star metric are chosen for the constraint that binds *this month*, not the one that binds at maturity.

| Stage (trailing 3-mo avg revenue + volume signal) | Binding constraint | North star | Door default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early** (< ~$8k/mo; < ~50 eligible leads/mo; < ~15 concurrent clients) | Reach, then conversations | Held qualified conversations per week + cash collected | Disclosed fit conversation (free or small credited fee); personal reply to every adult lead |
| **Growing** (~$8–30k/mo; ~50–400 eligible leads/mo; ~15–35 concurrent) | Conversion and sales minutes | Enrollments per month + eligible lead → enrollment | Fit conversations for uncertain buyers; call-optional enrollment for warm ones; paid decision assessment once calls bind |
| **Scaling** (~$30k+/mo; 400+ eligible leads/mo; 35+ concurrent) | Care minutes | Profit per operator hour | Paid/async decision assessment; capped live calls; seat caps from measured review minutes |

### 1c. The Belief Chain (the persuasion model)

Every yes is a completed chain of six beliefs; every no, objection, or stall names the broken link. Every asset, call segment, email, and renewal conversation is assigned the link it repairs.

1. **Cause** — "My stall has a findable cause, and it isn't my worth."
2. **Range** — "Some of what I dislike is set and some is movable — and I don't yet know which is which for me."
3. **Vehicle** — "Measurement plus outside review is what I was missing, and I can't supply it myself."
4. **Guide** — "This operator's standards fit me, and he'd tell me if they didn't."
5. **Self** — "With this structure, I will actually do it."
6. **Now** — "Waiting costs something real, and there's a real date."

Past the purchase: **Hold** ("what I built is worth protecting until it holds on its own" — renewal) and **Share** ("recommending this makes me look rigorous, not insecure" — referral).
Objection map: price → Vehicle/Self · "adults can't change" → Range · "is this legit?" → Guide · "free on YouTube" → Vehicle · "why not an orthodontist?" → Range/Guide (scope) · "no time" / "I've failed before" → Self · "I need to think" → Now or a hidden link.

## 2. The Outcome Map (the bone question, answered straight)

Bone growth is the category's question, not the brand's promise. The brand wins trust by being the one voice that answers it straight — by age and evidence tier — and every honest "no" about structure travels with an honest "yes" about something he can move.

| Column | What sits here (adults) | Evidence posture |
|---|---|---|
| **Changeable and measurable** | Habits and consistency (logged); body composition (visibly changes the face); head/neck posture and how he holds himself; breathing *habits* in daily life; grooming and presentation; how he is photographed (distance, lens, angle, light, expression); knowing what's worth pursuing | Established that these move presentation and are measurable; state plainly |
| **Debated / unestablished** | Visible change in jawline or profile from sustained oral-posture habits in adults; muscle-driven contour change from chewing habits (with known trade-offs) | Present at its evidence tier: plausible for some, unproven, varies by person; never promised |
| **Never claimed for adults** | Habit-driven skeletal change; "restoring" or "reshaping" bone; fixing asymmetry; replacing orthodontic, surgical, or medical care | Hard line 2 |
| **During growth (minors)** | Growth modification is a clinical domain | Education only + referral to an orthodontist; no paid offers |

Rules:
- **Never claim or imply that habits produce visible adult skeletal change**; when asked, answer at the evidence tier.
- The adult-bone answer is **canon claim #1**: repeated wherever the category keyword brings people in, on the verify page, and in a dedicated "honest answer" asset that answers before it pitches.
- **The brand's public descriptor names what it delivers** (assessment, plan, weekly review, measurement, habits and presentation). "Bone growth" and "mewing" are category search terms the brand explains, never its identity or its promise.
- **The null-result plan exists now.** If visible-change ranges in the operator's own data come back small, the offer stays worth buying: a verdict that stops wasted money and months, the habit and presentation levers, and measurements the client owns.
- **Bounded agency is an accusation against guessing, not a concession to genetics:** genes set the range of what shows; guessing wastes it; measurement tells him where he is in it — with "what shows" always meaning the changeable column, never bone.

## 3. Business theses

**B1. The Constraint Sequence governs every business decision.** (D) See §1b. Early, spare minutes and too few adults: maximize conversations and learning per lead. Later, conversations bind: route by uncertainty and go call-optional for warm buyers. At scale, care binds: convert live minutes into group/async forms. Profit per operator hour is the *Scaling* north star, not an early gate.

**B2. Rhythm, not format, buys results; proactive beats available.** (M transfer from lifestyle behavior change: group ≈ individual at equal intensity; proactive contact raises outcomes; extended low-intensity contact protects maintenance.) The flagship is a group/cohort format with weekly, short, coach-initiated review; "message me anytime" is not a delivery unit. Private attention is sold on speed, customization, and privacy — never on "better results".

**B3. One flagship, two buffers, one floor — activated by stage, not all at once.** (D) The **cohort** is the flagship. The **decision assessment** buffers the front once calls bind; **continuity** buffers the back; the **Starter Path** is the humane destination for "not now / not a fit / can't afford" (free branch first; a low-cost tool named once, never pushed). **Rung Activation Schedule:** months 0–3 door v0 + founding 1:1 + free Starter Path · months 3–6 founding group as founding 1:1 sunsets · months 6–9 continuity at first graduation; paid assessment when call load binds · Private only when care minutes are measured and seats are spare · paid self-serve products only after enough graduates have produced proof and a stall taxonomy.

**B4. Sell the decision, not the diagnosis — and make the plan worth its fee even to a non-buyer.** (D; hard line 2) Face "analysis" is commoditized and sits on the diagnosing line. Judgment — why *this* person stalled (three verdicts, P5), what to change first, whether coaching fits, whom to see instead — is commodity-*resistant* while it is tied to a human who reviews him weekly. **Baseline, not reading:** photos are captured for later matched comparison, never interpreted structurally; reviews comment on history, habits, logging, capture quality, and fit. The written plan is useful without buying anything, and the recommendation may be "don't buy". **The free/paid line:** give away the why and the decision framework in public; sell judgment applied to him, week after week.

**B5. The door is stage-gated.** (D; H) Early default: a disclosed fit conversation (free or small credited fee) — purpose, price, and "one recommendation or I'll point you elsewhere" stated on the booking page. Switch to the paid decision assessment when held calls exceed ~6–8/week, show rates fall, or most calls are no-fit. The paid door trades lead-efficiency (fees cut take-up) for hour-efficiency; which wins is an empirical question measured on profit per operator hour per 100 eligible leads. At volume the assessment goes async-first (recorded review + written plan + optional short live slot).

**B6. The Founding Sprint produces the first conversations without an audience.** (D) Sources that fit every exclusion: the operator's disclosed warm network; people who reply to or comment on the first pieces; a standing founding-offer page linked from every early asset; native short-form as the main early reach source; permission-first replies to adults who engage publicly; a personal reply to every adult door completion within hours. Targets: 3–6 held conversations a week; honest expectation 0–2 clients in month 1, 3–6 by month 3. Month-3 gate: fewer than 3 clients from 25+ held conversations → fix offer or positioning before building the group. First cash may slip if none of these works; the plan says so.

**B7. Founding clients are R&D, priced near the real price, with more access instead of a discount.** (D) Founding 1:1 builds the stall taxonomy, the check-in instrument, and the capture standard fastest. Price near the flagship's opening band, disclose "you're among my first N clients; here's what that means", state the next price, sunset on schedule into the founding group. The public price history never shows one-to-one cheaper than the group.

**B8. Plan on the audience you'll probably have.** (D) Every scenario states the eligible leads (and engaged views) it needs. Reach bands (LEDGER A) include a band where the channel never breaks out; that band still reaches first profit through price, renewal, conversion, and personal lead handling. In middle bands the $25k floor is honestly a year-2 outcome; the top half of the band comes from price and leverage, not more hours.

**B9. Screen before any paid step — by signals, not scores.** (S law/prevalence; D design) Two tiers. **Free door:** age fork (legal adult where he lives) + one or two unscored impairment/distress items routed automatically to non-labeling resources plus an opt-in human conversation; under-18s give no email or intake answers (education lane = public content only). **Paid step:** a brief **fit check** at booking or checkout (distress, fixation language, surgery-seeking, compulsive checking, functional impairment). A signal pauses the sale for a human conversation with three outcomes: enroll with adjusted expectations, Starter Path, or referral; only acute signals end it. Never a clinical instrument, never a label, minimal data.

**B10. Price sits inside three ceilings — and at capacity, price is the profit lever.** (D; M) Cash (measured in months of take-home pay, not annual gross), capacity (revenue per delivery hour must clear the target), credibility (raised by the persuasion system: guarantee, standards, written plans, contextualized cases, felt familiarity). When demand binds, a raise that loses a smaller share of buyers than its percentage flows almost entirely to profit; when seats bind, price and minutes-per-client are the only levers left. **Gate the announcement, not the raise:** once proof milestones and a conversion or utilization signal have held for two cycles, announce a date ≥30 days out and the raise always happens; justify it by what was added, never by demand; grandfather alumni; never discount below recent buyers.

**B11. Anchor honestly.** (M + hard line 2 + Informed-Client Test) Honest anchors: the real premium tier shown first; the delivery math; the value of months not wasted. Never surgery or filler (implies substitution; recruits the riskiest buyer). Never his own sunk spend as a price anchor (it's the recovery-scam pattern) — prior spend is used diagnostically only. Executive-coaching hourly rates stay internal.

**B12. Payment plans are priced at cost and end before delivery does.** (M; W premium evidence) Plans lift sales mainly among buyers least able to absorb risk. Premium 0–5% (processing + expected leakage), stated as a total, or routed through a regulated lender; all installments inside delivery; **cancel-forward** (a client who exits after the fit window stops future payments); one scripted affordability question ("comfortable from your monthly income without new credit?"); no plan when the answer signals borrowing; serial appearance purchasers get extra care (no plan, a cooling-off gap).

**B13. Guarantee what you control, loudly — and make it collectable.** (M; costly signal) Three layers: statutory rights first; a fit window (full refund through a short conversation); a **service guarantee** on the operator's inputs (every review inside the stated window, the written week-6 read, the week-12 re-assessment — missed = credit or refund); plus a **non-response clause** with markers agreed in advance and a cash partial refund, not "more weeks of what didn't work". **Collectability Test:** could a burned client who did the work actually collect? Publish the claim rate once data exists. Consistency devices are never timed to, or used against, refund rights. Never guarantee structural or appearance outcomes.

**B14. Calibration is margin — calibration means accuracy, not modesty.** (S expectation effects) Expectations color perceived results and fuel effort; overpromises return as refunds, disputes, lost renewals, and screenshots. Set expectations the program reliably meets and state them with confidence. The expectation document states the time commitment (minutes a day, check-in effort, weeks) and the pre-agreed non-response markers.

**B15. The back end must reach the months when outcomes appear.** (M maintenance; D) A 12-week flagship ends before most habits are automatic. **Phase 2 renewal** (offered at the week-10/12 measurement to clients with measured momentum) and a disclosed **Hold** continuity layer earn from months 4–9, when visible progress, proof, and referrals are born. For the client, continuity answers "did it hold?"; for the business, the consented dataset is a by-product shared back with alumni.

**B16. Paid community is an option set with a default.** (spec; D) Default: the **alumni room** — continuity designed as a paid community of people who keep records (measurement, periodic review, belonging). Option: an in-cohort peer space. Option (Growing/Scaling): an open paid membership for verified, fit-checked adults once moderation minutes are budgeted. Never a front door or a nurture layer. Room rules: no face-photo sharing, no appearance comparison, camera-optional, pseudonymous handles, opt-in leaderboards on logged process only.

**B17. Two lanes for two buyers.** (D) Primary: the employed Self-Taught Struggler (19–32; default protagonist mid-20s). Premium: the Optimizer (25–35) — needs speed, precision, privacy, status. Give him a real lane: a **Private variant** with every group surface optional (recorded calls, async review, start any week) priced to hourly parity, or a one-off **priority review** as his entry product; a fast lane that embeds the fit check and holds one call that is both assessment and enrollment. Eligibility = age fork + one affordability question asked of everyone, never student status. Women and 30+ welcome, not targeted; minors education-only; the Burned Struggler is a state within the core buyer, handled by route (P13).

**B18. The brand owns the method, the capture standard, and the client library; the face carries trust; no name is a claim.** (D; owner-reputation evidence favors a visible "Name | Brand" link) Product names use process language ("restore", "remodel", "reshape" are out). Decide naming before the first group. **Trust is credential-agnostic:** visible standards, demonstrated judgment, and scope honesty carry it with or without credentials; a credential never widens scope, and its absence is stated plainly, never disguised.

**B19. Every per-person minute is priced, batched, or templated; content hours are a protected minimum.** (D) Unpriced per-person work (screen-flag conversations, Starter Path touches, DMs, follow-ups) is a leak that grows with the audience. Content gets a protected batch block; seat caps are set so client work cannot eat it (cutting content today removes leads a month from now). Design the week to ~20 hours so ~5 hours of slack absorbs variance; a de-scoping order says what goes first when a week runs past 25.

**B20. Small numbers lie.** (S statistics) At solo volumes, monthly ratios are mostly noise. Decide by thresholds set in advance and qualitative signal (call transcripts, refund reasons, stall notes); pool across longer windows; a ledger range yields to the operator's own ratio only after ~30 events; change course only on large differences. Five weekly numbers early: eligible leads, held conversations, enrollments + cash, pieces published, client check-in completion.

**B21. Operations are a floor, not a craft.** (spec) Freelancers own production derivatives (editing, clipping, thumbnails, design, scheduling) and, at Growing/Scaling, a task-billed scripted routing/moderation role (routing and policy moderation only — no selling, no claims, no client conversations, no contact before age is confirmed). The operator keeps claims approval, selling, review minutes, client communication, and public replies. Live work is batched into two or three fixed weekly windows; no-shows and reschedules follow one protocol whose time cost is budgeted. Open the payment processor early with small charges, hold ~2–3 months of costs in reserve, and keep other income until trailing profit covers personal costs.

**B22. Starts run on a calendar.** (D) One founding group with a fixed start date; then rolling starts (monthly once volume supports it; every 6–8 weeks before that), each with a real seat cap from measured care minutes. Group calls stay combined until ~12–15 concurrent clients.

## 4. Persuasion theses

**P1. The Informed-Client Test has a ceiling and a floor.** (Standard) Ceiling: would a client whose result fell anywhere in the range he was shown — including the low end — still say the persuasion served him? Floor: letting a screened, able-to-pay, good-fit buyer talk himself out of the right decision because you wouldn't challenge his story, name the stake, or ask, fails him too. Stop rules always override the floor.

**P2. Split efficacy: raise self-efficacy hard; raise belief in the method only as far as evidence allows.** (S efficacy; S–M EPPM distinction) Self-efficacy ("I can do the steps") rises through mastery (small verifiable wins) and similar others' stories with ranges. Response efficacy ("the steps work for adults") is capped by the Outcome Map; promise clarity ("you'll know within N weeks"), not change.

**P3. The strongest stake is the one he says out loud.** (M; accuracy over loss framing — framing effects are negligible) Ask the implication question, hold the silence, reflect his own months, money, and missed moments back to him — then spend most of the minutes on a step he can verify. Lived costs framed as what he does or misses (dodging photos, holding back) may be reflected plainly; anatomy and structure, never. Future-pace **the record, not the face**: the year that repeats the last twelve months of guessing vs the measured year where he knows.

**P4. Don't evoke the defense — and when it comes, change the route, not the destination.** (M–S clinical; D transfer) Excavate before you rebut; raise the top objection yourself (two-sided, refutational messages beat one-sided). When he argues for the status quo: reflect it, ask permission to challenge, then challenge with his own evidence (logs, timeline, words). Only a clear no, "I can't afford it", distress, or a minor ends the persuasion. Burned buyers are the exception route: their "I need to think" is due diligence to be supported (P13).

**P5. Three honest verdicts for a stall.** (D; replaces single attribution) A stall was (1) never measured, (2) measured but misdirected, or (3) the lever doesn't move for this person. The assessment and the week-6/week-12 reviews must be willing to return verdict 3 and say "stop". Give his past failure an honest culprit where true — advice sold without a measurement system — never "you weren't consistent" as a default alibi.

**P6. Identity is earned by evidence and signaled by standards.** (M; public identity announcements can backfire; identity-noun framing failed replication) The in-group: **adults who measure instead of guess**. Entry criteria (adult, fit-checked, logging) are the status; rituals are baseline day, the week-6 and week-12 re-captures, graduation into the alumni room; every label rests on his own record ("38 of 42 days logged is what doing this properly looks like"). Commitments are reported to the reviewer, private by default, opt-in inside the closed cohort; never broadcast, never "real men don't quit", never mocking outsiders. For young male buyers, **adults-only, fit-checked, and premium read as seriousness** — the standards are the status signal.

**P7. Let him succeed before he pays.** (S mastery; D) The best micro-commitment is a piece of the real work: a free 7-day log plus one standardized baseline capture after the age fork. It rebuilds efficacy through mastery, screens intent, and turns the recommendation into a continuation (the back-dated baseline makes his DIY months dataset zero). Every rung is real work, never a request-sequence trick. After the yes, a **Commit Ritual** (baseline day, his written reasons, the expectation document signed off) and a **First-14** sequence (a verifiable win inside the first fortnight, a reviewer touch in the first 72 hours) answer buyer's remorse with evidence, inside the fit window.

**P8. Close by contract, not by technique.** (M adaptive/consultative selling) Open with the contract (structure, your stake, price at the end, a clear yes or no is the goal and both are fine) → excavate in his words → summarize his goal, cost, and need in his words → recommend one option with reasons he gave you → state the price once and hold the silence → ask → resolve each real objection he raises (the **Real-Objection Sort**) until yes, a clear no, a stop rule, or a decision date he agrees → on yes, book the day-one plan before hanging up → either way, send the written plan (his words, one recommendation, price, date). Follow-up is a fixed rule (LEDGER).

**P9. Price is presented, not revealed.** (M) Show real tiers — premium first as an honest anchor — then recommend one; file the spend in the right mental account (an investment in skill, measurement, and review — never treatment, never cosmetic); state the price once without apology; itemize real delivery units with no invented "total value". **No decoys:** every tier is real and bought by someone.

**P10. Real dates end deferral; the Launch Line keeps them offer attributes.** (M deferral; C urgency) Deadline force equals the cost of waiting for the next real opportunity. Design the calendar so every assessed buyer meets one real decision point within ~2–4 weeks (credit window, cohort start with a real seat cap, announced price step), with starts pinned to real landmarks. Announce real dates publicly in warm assets; turn them into his personal decision date on the call. The **Launch Line** (H13) separates this from an excluded launch.

**P11. Show the work, not the minutes.** (W–M; H) Lead with the deliverable: a sample written plan, the turnaround, what gets reviewed, the number of reviews and re-captures, seat caps as plain facts with a public fill history (under-filled cohorts included; never "X spots left"). Any fee breakdown includes the operator's own pay and profit. Publishing raw review minutes is a test, not a default (it invites per-minute pricing).

**P12. Numeric ranges beat vague hedges; points state process facts.** (M uncertainty communication; W–M range-vs-point) Ranges cost little credibility and beat verbal hedging; they beat point promises mainly when buyers compare formats — so show the contrast with how the category phrases results, keep ranges tight, and state fixed process facts (cadence, turnaround, number of reviews) as points. Ranges for variable outcomes are required on ethical grounds regardless.

**P13. One standard, four routes.** (S adaptiveness) Route by buyer state, not by script: **Struggler** → Cause and Self links; **Optimizer** → Vehicle and Guide plus speed, precision, privacy, status; **Skeptic / Burned Struggler** (door tag: "what have you already bought or tried?") → Guide link with a verification-first path (verify page, sample deliverable, outcome data with denominators, guarantee terms up front), no stakes questioning, a decision date *he* chooses; **Ambivalent** → Now link with his own reasons and a real date. Insecurity-led buyers get the dignity route; any acute signal ends persuasion on every route.

**P14. The call's arc must survive without the call.** (D; H) Profit at scale needs most enrollments without a live call. The self-assessment result is the first sales conversation (names his stall pattern in his words, re-attributes it, gives one verifiable step, recommends the next rung, names the next real date); a recorded walkthrough branches by that result; stage-matched sales emails carry the arc; replies go by voice note. Build async assets only from arcs proven live; count the learning value of early calls. A live call is justified when its close-rate lift × price per call-hour beats the hour's current opportunity cost.

**P15. Install the buying test only you pass.** (M–S inoculation/prebunking) Teach the specific tells of manufactured proof (lens distance, lighting, angle, flexing, water weight, timeline tricks) and the honest-evidence standard (matched captures, ranges with denominators, published refusals, stated scope). Every competitor is then judged by criteria your offer was built to meet. Prebunking binds you: build the capture standard before publishing anti-grift content.

**P16. Four stories carry the brand, each with its context stack.** (M narrative) **Origin** (why the standard exists — never implying credentials or an undocumented transformation) · **Stall told back** (his story, better than he tells it, with a better cause) · **Transition** (a founding client's change of belief — usable before any outcome data exists, consented) · **Case** (a measured arc with its range and denominator). Composites labeled.

**P17. Trust signals: hygiene vs costly.** (M) Hygiene (policy pages, stated limits — cheap talk, but required): pair every stated limit with why it exists and what to do instead. Costly (a grifter can't afford them): guarantees visibly honored with a published claim rate, ranges that include poor outcomes and non-responders, published fit-decline counts in aggregate (never clinical reasons or individuals), a long consistent record, a pre-committed publication schedule for outcome ranges whatever they show. Sincerity is not efficacy; only the last two speak to "does it work?".

**P18. Testimonial rights come from the dataset — process testimonials from week two.** (S) Outcome/appearance claims ship with range, context, and denominator; consent in writing; incentives disclosed; never gate or suppress reviews. Experience and process testimonials (responsiveness, clarity, "I logged 80 of 84 days") make no appearance claim and are usable from the founding phase. Contextualized testimonial clips may appear in organic short-form with range/denominator on screen; none in ads.

**P19. Every plateau is a re-sale — and sometimes an honest exit.** (M lapse/relapse; D) Pre-announce plateaus in the expectation document; re-attribute to controllables with his own data; re-plan within 48 hours; reward the comeback, not the streak ("never miss twice"); raise an off-track alert on early non-response; at week 6, apply the pre-agreed markers — adjust, hold, exit with the non-response refund, or refer. Rising photo-checking or distress mid-program pauses persuasion and triggers referral.

**P20. Sell the renewal at the measured peak with a true stake.** (M peak-end; M habit timelines) Make the final fortnight the program's peak; put the Phase 2 / Hold decision inside the week-12 review; argue it from his own adherence data (many habits aren't automatic by week 12); offer alumni status at graduation; time every ask (renewal, referral, testimonial, upgrade) to a measurement moment. Continuity default = opt-in at the review; option = a disclosed Hold phase agreed at purchase with a reminder before the first charge and one-click exit. Never a hidden default (defaults work — that's why hidden ones invite disputes).

**P21. Ask everyone, privately, at measured peaks.** (M; referred customers are more loyal) People underestimate how often a direct, specific, low-effort ask works; public referral is capped by impression management. Asks: a private link, the training-partner seat (pair enrollment, which also helps adherence), or a shareable that makes the sharer look rigorous. Plan acquisition without referrals; never skip the ask; never reward posting faces.

**P22. The working alliance is a deliverable.** (S alliance ↔ outcome) He feels heard and the work matches his goals — checked briefly after every call and in weekly reviews. Plain validation is allowed ("that's frustrating, and it makes sense"); the **Plain-Language Rule** bans therapeutic/medical framing ("healing", "treatment"), not warmth. Action and data language (baseline, review, plan, block, record, decision) frames help-seeking as taking command.

**P23. Selling skill is trained on a loop.** (operational) Record calls with consent; score one a week against the close arc and the stop rules; keep an objection log that feeds content and the FAQ (the **Conversation-to-Content Loop**); track refunds and disputes by the promise and path used; change one variable per cycle.

**P24. Belief change is a subscription.** (S no-backfire; M decay) Each exposure is small; repetition within a coherent canon compounds. A canon of 5–7 calibrated core claims (adult-bone answer first) repeats across long-form and email; email is the belief-maintenance channel between purchases, with a slow lane for silent verifiers.

## 5. Ecosystem theses (consolidated)

**E1. Nurture is five jobs; each needs an owning asset.** (M; D) Repeated exposure (serialized long-form + weekly email), visible social proof (the comment section as public record + a consented outcome library), identity rehearsal (recurring formats and vocabulary), a Q&A archive (pinned replies + FAQ pages), and a warm pool for offers (the stage-tagged list). Any job without an owner is a leak.

**E2. Packaging sets the audience's age mix — a major, testable lever.** (S reach/policy; M mechanism; W minor share) Ratings, comparisons, and idealized-jaw content pull the youngest viewers. ## 6. House rules (operational; the playbook-facing text lives in HOUSE_STANDARD.md)

**H1. Minors and data minimization.** Age fork at the door (legal adult where he lives). No paid offers, DM selling, calls, or plans with minors; under-18s give no email or intake answers; education lane = public content only. A minor's "I need to ask my parents" ends it. No parent-facing paid offers; "younger bodies respond better" never appears in selling or marketing (the education lane may state true age dependence with a referral).
**H2. Force vs pressure.** Force: a stake in his words, a confident recommendation, an explicit ask, resolving each real objection (up to two probes per objection), a real decision date. Pressure: re-asking after a clear no, reopening money after "I can't afford it", selling into acute distress or fixation, invented urgency, shame. Follow-up follows the LEDGER rule.
**H3. Face-as-proof and the implicit halo.** The operator's appearance is never evidence for the method; self-results enter proof discipline as one case at matched conditions. Publish "what my face does and doesn't prove"; no thumbnails using the operator's profile or jaw as an aspirational before/after; appearance attacks get a calm redirect to the measurement system.
**H4. Numbers.** No performance, outcome, or client number in public copy unless it comes from the operator's own records; external research figures may appear only attributed at their evidence tier and never as the brand's results; templates use bracketed placeholders; every range names its denominator.
**H5. Observed, not caused.** Published appearance ranges say "observed among clients", carry confounders (weight change, age under ~23, capture deviations), sit beside a comparison where one exists (e.g., waitlisted applicants measured over the same interval), and never use causal verbs.
**H6. Measure behavior often, appearance rarely.** Behavior is logged and reviewed weekly; standardized appearance capture only at baseline, ~week 6, week 12, then quarterly; clients are asked not to take interim comparison photos; checking frequency is a fit-check and in-program signal.
**H7. Privacy is part of the product.** Photos are coach-only (never in group spaces, never visible to freelancers); participation can be pseudonymous and camera-optional; fit-check and health-adjacent answers stay out of email tags, pixels, and freelancer-visible tools, with short retention; deletion on request; separate, revocable consent for any marketing or dataset use; discreet sender and billing names that are still recognizable.
**H8. Plain language and the Technique Firewall.** Vocabulary: assess, audit, review, baseline, decision, plan, block, record, habit, presentation. Never diagnose, treat, analyze structure, score faces, use clinical titles, or "protocol". In the playbook's examples and scripts, method content appears only as bracketed placeholders ("[first adjustment]") or at category level ("a consistency problem, not a technique problem"); topics appear as titles and angles, never as instructions.
**H9. Evidence tiers inside the playbook.** No pop neuroscience; weak or contested effects are presented as small or conditional.
**H10. Glass-box naming.** Every internal name must pass the Hostile-Screenshot Test (eligible-adult yield, Starter Path, Pushback Signal, Plain-Language Rule — never "sellable adults", "control lexicon", "defense meter", "down-path").
**H11. AI.** AI may assist drafting, clipping, transcription, and templated summaries under the operator's claim approval. No AI avatars, cloned voice, or AI-generated or AI-edited faces or results.
**H12. Biggest-risk flags (legal: minimal, one owning module, one line elsewhere).** (1) Minors and age of majority. (2) Structural/medical claims and health framing on destinations. (3) Testimonials, endorsements, and before/after consent and typicality. (4) Refunds, statutory cancellation, disputes, auto-renewal consent, and payment-plan credit rules. (5) Health-adjacent data in forms and pixels; no software extraction of facial geometry. (6) Platform health and ad policies.
**H13. The Launch Line.** A date is an offer attribute when: the next start is always purchasable; the date comes from the calendar or the capacity math; nothing about content or email volume changes as it approaches (one announcement + one reminder per start date, engaged segments only); there are no window-specific bonuses, prices, or lead-in sequences; no cart "closes" without a next date. Anything else is a launch and is out. No countdown widgets (grift-coded for this audience); state real dates plainly.
**H14. Vulnerability during delivery.** Rising photo-checking, distress in check-ins, or "everything is ruined" language mid-program pauses persuasion and triggers a referral conversation.
