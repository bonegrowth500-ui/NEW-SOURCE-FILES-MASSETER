Format and rules as in `part-1-2.md`. Selling and transformation modules carry scripts and swipe copy as their main extras, so they use the **Part V budget**:
- opening ~200
- deep sections ~3,800–4,000
- worked example ~1,000
- extras ~1,500–1,700
- Stage Notes ~200

That totals ≈7,130–7,530, inside the band. Every script uses placeholders for numbers and method content, and uses the fixed script format with a debrief (VOICE). Every sales unit ends at the decision.

## 18 · Content That Sells

**Argument.** With no free community to hold people, content has to sell, but only at the warmth it has earned. The default failure is respect without demand: good content that creates no pull. The rules:
- **The Warmth Ladder** decides which asset may ask for what. Warmth signals license direct selling with public prices.
- Every asset repairs one Belief Chain link and makes one ask.
- **The Offer Pieces** carry the arc on warm surfaces, including "price with delivery math", which itemizes deliverables and the seat cap and never shows minutes.
- **The Stake-to-Step Ratio** keeps force honest: one true stake, then most of the minutes on a step he can verify.
- Every destination line comes with one perspective line.
- **The Pre-Publish Card** is run before anything ships.
- The Conversation-to-Content Loop keeps content aimed at real objections.

**Belief shift.** "Content builds an audience and selling is separate", or "everything gets a pitch" → "Warm assets sell directly with public prices, and every asset repairs one link and makes one ask."

**Opening frame.** A channel with a loyal audience and a quiet checkout: every video respected, none acted on. The fix is not more pitching; it's matching the ask to the warmth.

**Sections.**
1. **Respect without demand, and the Warmth Ladder** (~800). Why good content doesn't sell. The warmth signals: returning viewer, subscriber, completed self-assessment, offer-page visit, reply. What each rung licenses, as the single rule the surfaces (23–27) map onto.
2. **One ask per asset, and the belief sentence** (~500). One primary ask and at most one ranked secondary. Every asset is tagged with its link and its before/after belief.
3. **The Offer Pieces and the Page Sequence** (~1,000):
   - The five selling formats: the offer video; who it's for and who it isn't; price with delivery math; the objection piece; the case piece.
   - When each runs.
   - Every offer piece recaps the Path and Timeline Card: what 12 weeks deliver, and the likely total path (06).
   - The Page Sequence: promise → frame → path → trust → next step.
4. **Stake-to-Step, the perspective line, and the Click Contract** (~700). Force in content: name the destination vividly, add one perspective line placing it beyond the face, then give the plan. Hooks as promises; the Payoff Test.
5. **Direct selling in practice** (~500). Pitch segments in decision-stage long-form; pinned offers; public prices; each real date announced once and reminded once, never to paused leads (08); DMs follow the handoff (20).
6. **The Conversation-to-Content Loop and the Pre-Publish Card** (~400). The objection log becomes content placed earlier in the journey, harvested weekly from calls, check-ins, and exits. The card itself is at most ten yes/no lines.

**Frameworks.**
- **Owns ★:** The Warmth Ladder.
- **Owns ◆:** The Offer Pieces, The Stake-to-Step Ratio, The Pre-Publish Card.
- **Owns ○:** One Ask per Asset, the belief sentence, the Click Contract, the Page Sequence, the Conversation-to-Content Loop, respect without demand, price with delivery math.
- **Recaps ↺:** the Belief Chain and Fantasy to Expectation (14), the Launch Line and Decision Points (08), the Path and Timeline Card (06), Surface Types (23), the Payoff Test and the Dignity Check (Intro).

**Worked example** (~1,000). A month of content for a Growing operator: eight assets with their link tags, warmth rungs, and asks, plus one offer video. It shows which asset sold and why, and which one broke the ladder by asking too early.

**Extras** (~1,600).
- Scripts with placeholders, each with a debrief:
  - the offer video;
  - "who this is for and who it isn't";
  - price with delivery math;
  - an objection piece;
  - a case piece with its context stack.
- The Pre-Publish Card, in full.
- Hard case: a warm audience that bristles at any pitch.

**Stage-note angles.** Early: selling happens in conversations, so content points to the founding page and the door. Growing: the Offer Pieces go live. Scaling: offer content is refreshed from the objection log, and async enrollment carries it (20).

**Licenses in play.**
- *Sell directly:* every warm asset names the offer, who it's for and not, the price, and one step → offer pieces and pitch segments in warm long-form → did a warm viewer finish without knowing what's sold, what it costs, and what to do next?
- *Name the destination boldly:* destination plus perspective line → vivid future-pacing, then obstacle and plan → did we describe what he actually wants?
- *Present the price:* price with delivery math → the value of months not wasted as an anchor → did we hide the price on a warm surface?

**Standard Check focus.** Every date mention passes the Launch Line. Dignity in every stake. The Click Contract is kept. No minutes are published.

**Quick Reference.** The warmth rungs; the one-ask rule; the five Offer Pieces; the stake-to-step rule; the Pre-Publish Card.

**Dependencies.** THESES P3, P11, E1; HOUSE_STANDARD (Sell directly); LEDGER C, E, F.
**Research for 3.1.** Persuasion knowledge and transparent formats; two-sided messages (have).
**Guardrails.** Surface mechanics belong to 23–27; no platform tactics here.

## 19 · The Sales Conversation

**Argument.** Closing is neither pressure nor luck; it's a contract. **Close by Contract** runs in order:
1. Agree the structure and a clear yes-or-no outcome up front.
2. Excavate in his words; the implication question lets him name the stake.
3. Summarize.
4. Recommend one option.
5. State the price once and hold the silence.
6. Ask.
7. Sort and resolve each real objection.
8. Agree a decision date.
9. Book day one.
10. Send the written recap.

When he pushes back, change the route, not the destination. The route follows his state: a burned buyer's "I need to think" is due diligence, so he gets the verification kit and chooses his own date; an insecurity-led buyer takes the Dignity Route and still hears a firm recommendation. Stop rules end it. The floor matters as much as the ceiling: letting a good-fit buyer talk himself out of the right decision fails him. This module is where you say the hard true thing, with force.

**Belief shift.** "Closing is pressure or luck" → "Close by contract, with force aimed at the situation, and routes for burned and insecure buyers."

**Opening frame.** Transcript fragment: "I need to think about it." What most operators say next (nothing, or a discount), and what a contract-based close says instead.

**Sections.**
1. **The contract and the arc** (~600). Why an upfront contract lowers defenses; the full arc; the Informed-Client floor in live selling.
2. **Excavation and the Implication Question** (~700). Questions in his words; the Destination Ladder live; the implication question and the silence after it; the summary he agrees with. Prior spend stays in excavation as data. It never returns in the price segment.
3. **Recommendation, price, and the spoken affordability line** (~600):
   - One option, with the reasons he gave you.
   - Premium First, Price Once, then silence.
   - The Path and Timeline Card is recapped before the price.
   - The spoken affordability line, verbatim; "no" ends money talk.
   - Plans and the guarantee introduced correctly (07).
   - The straight answer to "how much of your time does this get?"
4. **The objection map** (~1,000). Fourteen deciding objections, each by link:
   - "Adults can't change"; "it's free on YouTube"; "why not an orthodontist?"; "I've seen faked before/afters"; "what if nothing changes?"; "I need to think";
   - time for money; privacy; "how many minutes a day?"; "I always quit"; timeline and total cost; "can I stop paying?"; "I need to ask my parents" or partner (as an adult);
   - "I need to ask my parents" from a minor stops the conversation.
   - The Real-Objection Sort allows up to two probes per real objection.
   - The Pushback Signal and the route change: reflect → ask permission → challenge with his own evidence.
5. **State Routing, the true thing, and stop rules** (~600):
   - What changes in the arc for each state.
   - The burned route: due diligence, the verification kit, his date.
   - The Dignity Route in practice (15, 02).
   - Saying the hard true thing with force.
   - Stop rules as spoken lines.
6. **Close, decision date, and follow-up** (~400). Booking day one on the call; the written recap; the Decision Date tied to the next real point (08); the Follow-Up Rule; the Selling-Skill Loop (recording with consent, scoring, an objection log, refunds traced to the promise and path).

**Frameworks.**
- **Owns ★:** Close by Contract.
- **Owns ◆:** The Implication Question, The Real-Objection Sort, The Pushback Signal, State Routing, Premium First, Price Once.
- **Owns ○:** the objection map, the Decision Date, the Follow-Up Rule, the Selling-Skill Loop.
- **Recaps ↺:**
  - from 02: Buyer States, the Destination Ladder, the affordability question (policy);
  - from 07: Plans at Cost, the Layered Guarantee;
  - from 08: Decision Points;
  - from 15: the Dignity Route;
  - from 06: the Path and Timeline Card;
  - from the Intro: Stop Rules.

**Worked example** (~1,000). An annotated composite call with Dan, from contract to booked day one. Two short excerpts follow: Theo raising the same objection (the burned route, where he chooses his date), and a stop rule firing mid-call.

**Extras** (~1,700).
- The full call outline with verbatim load-bearing lines: contract, implication question, recommendation, price, affordability line, ask, stop rules. With a debrief.
- Objection responses for all fourteen, by link.
- Templates: the recap and follow-up.
- The call scorecard.
- Hard case: the good-fit buyer who says "I need to think" twice.

**Stage-note angles.** Early: dual-purpose founding conversations (09). Growing: fit conversations under the Call Cap; the Decision Assessment call. Scaling: calls reserved for Private and high-uncertainty buyers (20).

**Licenses in play.**
- *Close:*
  - default: Close by Contract;
  - stronger: challenge with his evidence, two probes, name the hidden link (except for burned buyers);
  - under-use check: did a good-fit buyer leave un-asked?
- *Name the stakes:*
  - default: the implication question;
  - stronger: name the stake directly once he's described it;
  - under-use check: did we let him treat waiting as free?
- *Present the price:*
  - default: premium first, price once;
  - stronger: the value of months not wasted;
  - under-use check: did we apologize for the price?
- *Say the true thing:*
  - default: name the frustration;
  - stronger: "You've spent eight months doing this without knowing whether it works. That's the problem, not you.";
  - under-use check: did we talk around it?
- *Use real dates:*
  - default: a decision date tied to the next real point;
  - under-use check: did he leave without one?

**Standard Check focus.** The line on vulnerability. The floor, with the burned-buyer exception. Stop rules verbatim. Prior spend never near the price.

**Quick Reference.** The arc on one line; the objection map; state routes; stop rules; the Follow-Up Rule.

**Dependencies.** THESES P1, P3, P4, P8, P9, P13, P23; STANDARD §1, §2, §5; LEDGER C, E, G.
**Research for 3.1.** Evidence on consultative and adaptive selling; implication questioning; reflective listening and sustain talk (verify tiers).
**Guardrails.** No manipulation scripts: no embedded commands, false takeaways, or "assume the close" tricks. Money talk ends at "I can't afford it".

## 20 · Selling Without the Call

**Argument.** Calls are the scarcest selling resource, and at scale most enrollments must happen without one. **The Async Arc** carries the call's structure: his stall, a better cause, your standard, one recommendation, the price, the ask, and a real date. It travels through:
- the **result page**, the first sales conversation, which branches by state (a burned buyer gets the verification kit first; an insecurity-led buyer gets the Dignity Route);
- a **Branched Walkthrough**;
- stage-matched sales emails;
- voice-note replies;
- a **DM Handoff** after verification;
- a checkout with the affordability question and the Fit Check built in.

The pause route is honored everywhere. **The Call Economics Test** says when a live call is worth its hour.

**Belief shift.** "High-ticket needs a call" → "The call's arc survives without the call, and calls are spent where they change the decision."

**Opening frame.** Two buyers enroll on the same day. One took a 40-minute call; the other watched a walkthrough on his phone, asked one question by voice note, and bought. Both were right.

**Sections.**
1. **Why async** (~500). Capacity, call economics, buyers who want control, and when a call genuinely helps.
2. **The Async Arc and threshold continuity** (~600). What the arc must carry, and why every surface must feel like the same mind.
3. **The result page as the first sales conversation** (~800). His stall pattern in his words, re-attributed; one verifiable step; the next rung; the next real date. Branches by state: burned, dignity, Optimizer (the Premium Lane), Ambivalent. The pause-route result carries no sales content.
4. **The Branched Walkthrough, sales emails, and voice notes** (~1,000). Segments and branches; a five-email stage-matched sales sequence (the content; plumbing is 26's); voice-note replies (length, tone, one recommendation).
5. **The DM Handoff, checkout, and the paid group session** (~700):
   - The DM rule: unverified contacts go to the door; verified adults may get one recommendation and a checkout link; Private needs a call.
   - Checkout: age attestation, the affordability question, and the Fit Check.
   - The optional paid group decision session, named honestly, with anonymous attendees.
6. **The Call Economics Test** (~300). The formula in words, examples, and who always gets a call.

**Frameworks.**
- **Owns ★:** The Async Arc.
- **Owns ◆:** The Branched Walkthrough, The DM Handoff, The Call Economics Test.
- **Owns ○:** the result page, threshold continuity, the paid group decision session.
- **Recaps ↺:** the Door, the Fit Check, and the pause route (05); Close by Contract (19); Readiness Tags and the Welcome Arc (26); the Keyword Route (25); the Premium Lane (13).

**Worked example** (~1,000). Adrian and Theo both enroll asynchronously over ten days: every touch, what each repaired, and the single human moment in each path. Theo's path runs through the verification kit.

**Extras** (~1,600).
- Templates:
  - the result page, by state;
  - the walkthrough segment outline;
  - the five-email sales sequence, with a debrief;
  - voice-note guidelines;
  - DM handoff lines;
  - the paid session's honest description.
- Hard case: a buyer who pauses mid-sequence, then returns.

**Stage-note angles.** Early: calls teach you the arc, so don't automate yet. Growing: build async from arcs proven live. Scaling: most enrollments are async; calls are kept for Private and high uncertainty.

**Licenses in play.**
- *Sell directly:* one recommendation and a checkout link for verified adults → did an async buyer finish without a clear next step?
- *Use real dates:* the next real date on the result page → did he meet no decision point?

**Standard Check focus.** The same House Standard on every async surface. The affordability question and the Fit Check before paid steps. The pause route binds sequences. No fake scarcity in sequences.

**Quick Reference.** The arc's elements; the result-page structure and branches; the sequence map; the DM rule; the call test.

**Dependencies.** THESES P14, E13; BUSINESS §4; LEDGER C.
**Research for 3.1.** Evidence on video sales pages and asynchronous decisions (principle level); response-speed effects (have).
**Guardrails.** Email plumbing belongs to 26 and routing structure to 05.

## 21 · Onboarding, Adherence and the Plateau

**Argument.** Results depend on design more than discipline.
- **The Commit Ritual** is there for clarity: the review and decision at baseline, his written reasons (never quoted back in any exit), and the Expectation Document signed off.
- **The First-14** delivers a verifiable win in the first fortnight and a reviewer's touch in the first 72 hours.
- Proactive review keeps the alliance, with an Alliance Check after every call and review. Never Miss Twice rewards the comeback, not the streak.
- **The Plateau Plan** pre-announces plateaus, re-plans within 48 hours, and asks the verdict question first at week 6.
- Every plateau renews the decision to continue, adjust, or exit, and sometimes the honest move is an exit under the exit right or the non-response clause.
- Rising distress pauses persuasion and triggers a referral.

**Belief shift.** "Results depend on the client's discipline" → "Adherence is designed. Every plateau renews the decision, and some end in an honest exit."

**Opening frame.** Week 5. A composite client's logs drop from six days to two, and his check-in says "I don't think this is working." What happens in the next 48 hours decides the refund, the renewal, and the referral.

**Sections.**
1. **Why adherence is designed** (~600). Monitoring effects; proactive vs available contact; the small effects of if-then plans; habit timelines (never cited as a promise of outcomes).
2. **The Commit Ritual and the First-14** (~700). The ritual's job is clarity, not consistency pressure. The first win; the 72-hour touch; remorse inside the fit window.
3. **Running proactive review** (~700). The psychology behind the Review Rhythm (06); the Alliance Check; plain language; earned labels in feedback; Never Miss Twice. Routing help never sees check-ins.
4. **The Plateau Plan and the renewed decision** (~900):
   - The pre-announcement.
   - Re-attribution to controllables, using his data.
   - The 48-hour re-plan.
   - At week 6, the verdict question first, then markers and Stall Verdicts.
   - The renewed-decision conversation.
   - Saying the hard true thing at the plateau.
5. **The Honest Exit and vulnerability during delivery** (~600). The exit right in practice; the non-response clause; the referral; rising photo-checking or distress means persuasion pauses and the scope boundary applies.
6. **Adherence data as the renewal case** (~300). What to capture now for week 10/12 (22).

**Frameworks.**
- **Owns ★:** The Plateau Plan.
- **Owns ◆:** The Commit Ritual, The First-14, Every Plateau Renews the Decision, The Honest Exit.
- **Owns ○:** the Alliance Check, Never Miss Twice.
- **Recaps ↺:** the Review Rhythm, the Measurement Calendar, and markers (06); Stall Verdicts (14); the Layered Guarantee and its clauses (07); Earned Labels and Quiet Commitments (17).

**Worked example** (~1,000). The week-5 client, end to end: the check-in, the 48-hour re-plan, the week-6 read (verdict question, then markers), the decision, and his week-12 result as a range. A second, shorter thread follows a client who takes the exit right at week 6.

**Extras** (~1,500).
- Scripts, each with a debrief: the Commit Ritual; the plateau conversation; the honest-exit conversation.
- Checklists: the First-14; the Alliance Check.
- Template: the plateau pre-announcement for the Expectation Document.
- Hard case: a client whose logging is perfect but who is checking photos daily.

**Stage-note angles.** Early: founding clients teach you where plateaus hit. Growing: templated plateau responses. Scaling: templates and flags, with you handling every plateau conversation.

**Licenses in play.**
- *Say the true thing:* name the frustration → the hard true thing at the plateau → did we soften the read?
- *Build identity on evidence:* earned labels in feedback → did we praise what his record doesn't show?
- *Close:* the renewed decision is asked for plainly → did we leave him drifting at week 6?

**Standard Check focus.** The line on vulnerability during delivery. The honest exit. No consistency pressure tied to refund rights. Written reasons are never quoted back. Dignity.

**Quick Reference.** The ritual; the First-14; the Plateau Plan's steps; the week-6 order; the exit criteria.

**Dependencies.** THESES P19, P22, H14; LEDGER D.
**Research for 3.1.** Lapse and relapse models; the alliance–outcome link; habit formation timelines; monitoring meta-analyses (verify tiers).
**Guardrails.** No method content in re-plans (placeholders only); no therapy framing.

## 22 · Renewal and Referral

**Argument.** Renewals and referrals are earned at **measured peaks**, not asked for at random. A measured peak is a measurement moment that shows progress on his record. Nothing is asked for at a plateau, a flat read, or an exit.
- Design the last fortnight as the program's peak.
- Put the Round Two or Hold decision inside the week-12 review, and argue it from his record against the timeline he accepted before paying, with an honest "you don't need it" when true.
- Ask every client privately for a referral at a peak, and never plan on it.
- Give him shareables that make him look rigorous.
- Make the Training-Partner Seat ask.
- Ask for testimonials only after the fit window, at a peak.
- Graduation confers alumni status.

**Belief shift.** "Renewals and referrals happen if clients are happy" → "Asks are timed to measured peaks, renewal is argued from his record, and every client gets a private referral ask."

**Opening frame.** Two graduations. One ends with "thanks, good luck". The other ends with his week-12 re-capture, his logged days, a straight recommendation, and one private question.

**Sections.**
1. **Peaks and measured moments** (~600). Peak-end memory; why asks belong to measurement moments; what counts as a peak here and what never does.
2. **The Peak-End Finish** (~600). Designing the final fortnight: the re-capture, the written read, the graduation ritual.
3. **The Renewal Case** (~900). Round Two vs the Hold vs graduating clean; the argument from his record against the Path and Timeline Card; the true stake; the honest "you don't need Round Two"; the script.
4. **Measured-Peak Asks** (~600). The testimonial ask, with timing here and rules in 16; the upgrade ask to Private (13); wording.
5. **Ask Everyone, Privately; shareables; the Training-Partner Seat** (~900). Why direct asks work more often than operators expect; private links; shareables that make the sharer look rigorous; the pair-enrollment ask; never rewarding face posts.
6. **Alumni status and graduation** (~300). Alumni identity; the room (10).

**Frameworks.**
- **Owns ★:** Measured-Peak Asks.
- **Owns ◆:** The Peak-End Finish, The Renewal Case, Ask Everyone, Privately.
- **Owns ○:** identity-safe shareables, alumni status.
- **Recaps ↺:** Round Two, the Hold, and the Training-Partner Seat (10); testimonial rules (16); Adults Who Measure (17); graduation (06); the Path and Timeline Card (06).

**Worked example** (~1,000). Weeks 10–12 of a cohort of ten, with every client accounted for:
- three renew into Round Two;
- two join the Hold;
- three graduate clean, one of them told "you don't need Round Two";
- one took the exit right at week 6 and gets no ask;
- one has a flat read: no ask now, a re-plan, and an ask only if a later read shows progress.

Referral asks go to all eight clients at a peak, and two testimonials are given. All as ranges and placeholders.

**Extras** (~1,500).
- Scripts, each with a debrief: the renewal conversation; the private referral ask; the testimonial ask; the Training-Partner Seat ask; the graduation outline.
- Template: shareables.
- Hard case: a client at a peak who asks for a discount to renew.

**Stage-note angles.** Early: ask founding clients for process testimonials and referrals at their peaks. Growing: renewal and the Hold at cohort scale. Scaling: alumni referrals are a steady channel, never a planned one.

**Licenses in play.**
- *Close:* the renewal recommendation is made plainly → did a graduate with measured momentum leave without an offer?
- *Build identity on evidence:* alumni status is earned → did we confer it without the record?
- *Sell directly:* the private ask of everyone at a peak → did we skip someone we should have asked?

**Standard Check focus.** No hidden renewals. The honest "you don't need it". No asks at plateaus, flat reads, or exits. Testimonial consent and timing. Referrals never reward posting faces.

**Quick Reference.** The measured moments; the renewal case skeleton; the referral ask; the shareable rules.

**Dependencies.** THESES P20, P21, B15; LEDGER D, E.
**Research for 3.1.** Peak-end evidence; referral value, and how people underestimate compliance with direct asks (verify tiers).
**Guardrails.** Offer design belongs to 10 and testimonial rules to 16.
